**What the exposure is.** An *exercise snack* (ExSn) means
«incorporating short bursts of high-intensity physical activity into daily routines» —
brief, vigorous bouts spread across the day rather than one continuous session. In the 14 trials
Wan pooled, the dose was **1-2 min per bout** (occasionally up to \~10 min), intensity set relative to
each person's fitness / age / max heart rate, in three delivery forms: **sprint-cycle** (bouts from
\~20-30 s), **stair-climbing** (\~2-4 min), and **strength-based** bouts; interventions ran **4-12
weeks**. [@wan2025]

This page **opens the exercise-snacks cell** (provisional type-C; no prior snacks source was held). Its
closest neighbour is the intensity-over-duration principle on
[[Measuring and Raising Cardiorespiratory Fitness]] — snacks are, mechanically, distributed HIIT — and
the minimum-dose question on [[Physical Activity Dose and Mortality]]. Distinct from
*breaking up sitting* ([[Sedentary Behaviour and Chronic Disease Risk]]): a snack is a brief *vigorous*
effort, not merely a standing/walking interruption of sitting.

## What moves, and by how much (Wan 2025 SR+MA)

Evidence base: **12 RCTs + 2 non-RCTs, 483 adults** (13 studies in the meta-analysis), apparently
healthy, >=18 y; comparator = usual lifestyle. Risk of bias: of the RCTs, 2 high / 10 some concerns;
of the non-RCTs, 1 moderate / 1 serious. All effects are **standardized mean differences** (SMD) —
*surrogate / fitness endpoints, not patient-important outcomes* ([[Surrogate Outcomes]]).
[@wan2025]

| Outcome | Effect vs control (SMD, 95% CI) | Studies / n | I2 | GRADE certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VO2max (CRF) | **+1.43** (0.61 to 2.25), p<0.001 | 10 / 378 | 76.6% | **VERY LOW** |
| Peak power output | **+0.68** (0.00 to 1.36), p=0.050 | 5 / 119 | 65.7% | MODERATE |
| Total cholesterol | **-0.65** (-1.18 to -0.11), p=0.018 | 4 / 89 | 28.3% | LOW |
| LDL-C | **-0.65** (-1.22 to -0.09), p=0.023 | 3 / 67 | 6.4% | MODERATE |
| HDL-C | +0.16 (-1.60 to 1.93), ns | 4 / 89 | 89.7% | LOW |
| Triglycerides | +0.13 (-0.43 to 0.68), ns | 3 / 72 | 21.2% | MODERATE |
| Body fat % | +0.16% (MD; -0.22 to 0.54), ns | 7 / 301 | 0.6% | LOW |
| Body weight | no significant difference | 8 / -- | -- | LOW |

[@wan2025]

The headline summary: snacks
«can significantly improve cardiometabolic health by enhancing VO2max and PPO and decreasing TC and
LDL-C». The gains sit in **cardiorespiratory fitness and two lipid fractions**; **body composition
does not move**, and HDL-C and triglycerides are null.
[@wan2025]

**The VO2max estimate is fragile — read it with its caveats.** The raw pool was **non-significant**
(SMD +0.91, -0.95 to 2.78, I2 = 87.3%); the significant +1.43 appears **only after a leave-one-out
sensitivity analysis excluded one high-risk-of-bias study**, and the authors themselves graded it
**VERY LOW** certainty on 10 small trials with high heterogeneity. The CI (0.61 to 2.25) is wide, so
the magnitude is identified poorly even where the sign is. Treat *snacks raise VO2max* as directionally
supported, not quantified. [@wan2025]

## For whom, and at what bout length — the effect-modification signals

The benefit **concentrates in the physically inactive**:
«the benefits of ExSn on cardiometabolic health are more pronounced in physically inactive adults than
in physically active adults». But the statistical warrant is uneven across outcomes —
«Significant group differences were observed only in PPO based on participants' physical activity levels
and the duration of each ExSn bout». For VO2max and TC the inactive-subgroup point estimates were
larger, yet the *between-group* difference was **not** significant (the review attributes this to small
n). So the inactive-benefits reading is **route-(a)-flavoured** (lower baseline, higher adaptive
reserve) with only **one outcome (PPO) showing a route-(b) interaction** — and that on 5 tiny trials.
[@wan2025]

On dose: «ExSn sessions lasting more than 2 min are more effective in improving cardiorespiratory
fitness» — PPO rose significantly **only when each bout exceeded 2 min**. Meta-regression found age, bout
duration, and trial duration were **not** significant continuous moderators of VO2max, and age was only
marginally related to PPO (beta = -0.3482, p=0.086) — so the >2-min signal is a subgroup split, not a
fitted dose-response. [@wan2025]

## Body composition does not respond

Neither body fat nor body weight moved. The authors read this as consistent with prior work that
«short-duration, high-intensity exercise interventions are inefficient for the optimization of body
composition» — the total exercise *volume* in a snack regimen (sessions «no more than 10 min») is simply
too low to create the energy deficit fat loss needs. Anyone adopting snacks **for weight or fat loss is
targeting the wrong outcome**; the case for snacks is fitness and lipids.
[@wan2025]

## Are snacks a sufficient minimum, or only a floor above nothing?

The authors are explicit that snacks are **not a substitute** for meeting activity guidelines:
«it is unlikely that physically inactive individuals will meet the recommended physical activity levels
solely through this approach», and therefore
«ExSn should be considered a complementary or alternative strategy for improving health in individuals
unable to meet traditional physical activity guidelines, rather than a replacement for these
recommendations». [@wan2025]

**Decision reading (Layer 1).** For a **near-inactive** person — where the inactivity big rock is
present — snacks are a high-value *entry lever*: they
«can be easily integrated into daily routines, offering a practical solution for sedentary and inactive
individuals or those with limited time», they carry the adherence advantage of being short and
distributable, and the evidence that they move CRF and LDL-C is where the effect concentrates. Snacks
plausibly beat *nothing* for someone who will not do structured exercise (adherence is part of the
effect). But the evidence does **not** license calling them a sufficient *replacement* for the full
activity dose: the endpoints are surrogates, the trials are <=12 weeks, and the authors disclaim
sufficiency. The honest position is **floor-above-nothing and complement, not demonstrated sufficient
minimum**.

## Gaps this evidence cannot close (type-G)

- **No patient-important outcomes.** Every endpoint is a surrogate (VO2max, lipids) or a body-composition
  marker; there is **no mortality, CV-event, incident-disease, or function/QoL evidence**, and no
  long-term follow-up (trials <=12 weeks). Whether raised CRF from snacks transmits to hard outcomes is
  assumed, not shown. long-term / hard-outcome exercise-snacks evidence.
- **Very thin, low-certainty base.** Per-outcome n is small (PPO 119, LDL-C 67), heterogeneity is high
  on the fitness outcomes, and GRADE is VERY LOW to MODERATE throughout. A single gold SR with a hard
  endpoint would move this page materially.
- **No frequency / total-dose optimization.** Bout length (>2 min) is the only dose feature with a
  signal; snack *frequency* per day and weekly total were not analysable (limited reporting). The
  minimum effective *regimen* is undefined.
- **Modality heterogeneity unresolved.** Sprint-cycle vs stair-climbing vs strength-based were pooled;
  which form is most effective is open, and matters for the deconditioned joint (impact).
- **Companion source deferred.** — a second gold
  snacks SR (inactive-only, Br J Sports Med 2025) is staged but deferred at triage as a saturation pair;
  it could upgrade or contest the inactive-stratum reading here.
