**The decision this page serves.** A person asks whether to take fish oil "for health". That question
has no answer as posed. Marine omega-3 *supplementation* (the isolate — capsules of EPA/DHA or
prescription EPA, not fish as food) moves different outcomes in opposite directions, and the sign flips
with the dose. This page owns the cross-outcome picture that each instance page holds only one slice of;
every figure below is extracted on the linked page and delegated here, not re-sourced.
 — the cross-outcome join and the two decision rules are this page's; each magnitude is
quoted on its instance page.

The fourth column of a cross-source table normally asks "same quantity?"; here the answer is **NO in
every pairing by construction** — these are distinct outcomes, so the table is a type-B disambiguation,
not a contrast. That is the point: the single word "omega-3" names objects that a decision must keep
apart.

## The 4 g/d decision is a joint benefit-harm weigh, not "supplement: yes/no"

The benefit and the harm sit at the **same end of one dose axis**, and REDUCE-IT is the case that makes
it undeniable: at 4 g/d icosapent ethyl, in a statin-treated high-CV-risk population, the **same trial**
reports a CV-event reduction (HR 0.75, NNT 21) **and** an increase in atrial fibrillation (RR 1.35
[1.11-1.65]) — the benefit held on [[Vitamin and Mineral Supplements for Disease Prevention]], the harm
on [[Omega-3 Supplementation and Atrial Fibrillation]]. Gencer's pooled dose-response puts the AF harm at
its steepest in exactly this >1 g/d region (1.49). So a high-risk person choosing 4 g/d weighs a large
ischemic benefit against a smaller-absolute but real arrhythmia harm on one dose — a net-effect
judgement, not a supplement-or-not one. The low dose (1 g/d, VITAL) resolves the weigh trivially the
wrong way: null CV benefit, and only the mild AF slope remains. — the join across the two
pages is this page's; both magnitudes are extracted on them.

## The dose is a curve, not a two-point pair — but it bends only for the high-CV-risk stratum

The VITAL-null / REDUCE-IT-benefit contrast reads as two points; a pooled dose-response now sits behind
them. Across 13 CVD-outcome RCTs (n=127,477, dose range 376-4000 mg/d), a meta-regression found a
**linear, non-plateauing** clinical dose-response — each +1000 mg/d lowering risk — that held *with and
without* the 4 g REDUCE-IT outlier for exactly two outcomes: **total CVD (\~17% per +1000 mg/d in the
outlier-excluded analysis; \~9% with the 4 g trial) and major vascular events**; the MI / total-CHD slope
appeared only when the single 4 g trial was included, and
stroke stayed null throughout (Hu 2019, figures held on [[Vitamin and Mineral Supplements for Disease Prevention]]). Two decision-relevant reads:

- **It replaces "does fish oil work?" with "how much, for whom?"** — the ischemic benefit rises with
  dose, so 1 g/d and 4 g/d are different bets, not the same supplement at two strengths. This is why the
  4 g/d weigh above is a genuine dose decision.
- **The pooled benefit even EXCLUDING the 4 g outlier is a high-CV-risk average, not a usual-risk one.**
  Pooling the mostly \~840 mg/d trials without REDUCE-IT still lowered MI, CHD death, total CHD, CVD death
  and total CVD — but those trials are dominated by secondary-prevention / established-CVD populations,
  and VITAL, the only usual-risk trial, stayed null on its composite. So the curve bends for the
  high-risk stratum; it does not license generic low-dose supplementation for a healthy general-prevention
  adult (the row below). And the slope is Hu's own «highly exploratory», 4-g-outlier-driven estimate, not
  a settled law.

 — the "curve not a pair" framing and the high-risk-only reading are this page's; every RR,
slope and caveat is Hu 2019's, quoted on the instance page. Hu is a type-F composite over the same trial
base (VITAL, REDUCE-IT et al.) and shares an author with VITAL — it refines confidence in the dose axis,
it is not independent corroboration.

## "Should I take fish oil" is ill-posed — name the outcome and the dose

For a **general-population adult with no established CV disease**, the held evidence offers no outcome
with a benefit at the doses sold over the counter: CV events null at 1 g/d, cancer null, preterm and
neurodevelopment null, depression *prevention* null. What remains at that dose is the mild AF slope. So
generic low-dose supplementation "for general health" targets no outcome the fabric can show it moves,
while carrying a small harm — a null-or-net-negative bet, not a neutral one. The decision becomes
positive only when it names an outcome **and** a dose that has evidence: high-dose EPA for a high-CV-risk
statin-treated person (REDUCE-IT, and weigh the AF harm), or EPA for someone being *treated* for
depressive symptoms (small effect, fragile to EPA-type and dose).

## Two firewalls

- **Isolate is not fish.** Every finding here is the supplement. The fish-as-food case is separate — it
  carries a matrix (iodine, selenium), a mercury tradeoff, and a CHD-mortality signal the isolate RCTs
  do not reproduce at supplemental doses. REDUCE-IT's own author warns the result "should not be
  generalized to... dietary-supplement preparations of n-3 fatty acid mixtures".
  -> [[Fish and Seafood Consumption]], [[Is the Food Category Doing Any Work]]
- **The benefit may concentrate in the deficient.** The isolate RCTs pool Western-replete populations,
  which cannot resolve whether a baseline-omega-3-deficient person benefits where the replete do not.
  The repletion-vs-enhancement cross-cut is owned on [[Deficiency Repletion vs Enhancement]]; the
  pregnancy and depression pages each flag it as their live route-(b) gap.
