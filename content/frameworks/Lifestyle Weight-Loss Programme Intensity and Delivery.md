**The decision this page changes.** Someone trying to lose weight, or a clinician referring them, has
to pick a *format*: a few check-ups with a GP, a monthly group, weekly sessions with a dietitian, a
phone or web programme, a commercial programme. The single held source answers which formats move
weight and by how much, and its answer is a contact-frequency gradient. Which diet goes inside the
programme matters far less (-> [[Named Diet Programs Compared]]).

The source is the 2013 AHA/ACC/TOS adult obesity guideline (Jensen and colleagues, NHLBI Expert Panel).
Its programme question, CQ4, rests on 51 trials (72 articles) rated good or fair after 74 poor-quality
publications were excluded. Each CQ4 evidence statement (ES) carries a High / Moderate / Low grade (the
guideline's *insufficient evidence* statements, elsewhere, carry none).
[@jensen2014obesity]

The systematic search ran January 1998 to December 2009, with large RCTs (>100 per arm) published after
2009 added. [@jensen2014obesity]

**Everything here is weight, a surrogate.** The CQ4 statements report kilograms lost and regained, not
diabetes, events or function -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]. What weight loss does to those outcomes is a
separate question -> [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]],
[[Lifestyle vs Metformin for Diabetes Prevention]].

## What "comprehensive" means: three components, all required

CQ4 ES1 (High) defines an effective high-intensity, on-site programme as diet plus physical activity
plus behaviour therapy, all three included
[@jensen2014obesity]:

- **Diet:** an energy deficit of ≥500 kcal/d, often set as 1200-1500 kcal/d for women and 1500-1800
  kcal/d for men.
- **Activity:** aerobic activity such as brisk walking ≥150 min/wk; about 200-300 min/wk to keep weight
  off beyond a year.
- **Behaviour therapy:** a structured programme with regular self-monitoring of food, activity and
  weight; for maintenance, weighing weekly or more often -> [[Self-Weighing for Weight Loss]].

The grade describes what effective programmes *contained*. It does not separate how much each component
contributes. [inferred from @jensen2014obesity]

## The intensity gradient — contact frequency is the dose

| Intensity (on-site, comprehensive) | Mean loss in the programme arm (vs usual care as each ES states it) | Grade | ES |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low: less than monthly sessions | does not consistently produce weight loss vs usual care | Moderate | ES9b |
| Moderate: 1-2 sessions per month | 2-4 kg in 6-12 months; *generally* greater than usual care | High | ES9a |
| High: «frequent (ie, initially weekly)» on-site treatment (the ≥14-sessions-in-6-months figure is ES9c's and rec 4b's definition, not ES2a's) | up to 8 kg at 6 months, about 5-10% of starting weight; greater than usual care | High | ES2a |
| High, continued weekly-to-monthly for another 6 months | up to 8 kg at 1 year | Moderate | ES2b |
| Comparing the three | high intensity gives greater **net-of-control** loss than low or moderate (the only kilogram ranking net of control; ES9b is a null against usual care) | Moderate | ES9c |

The kilogram cells are mean losses within the programme arms, not net-of-control differences, so the
gap between the moderate and high rows is not itself a measured effect of the extra sessions; ES9c is
the statement that compares net losses. [inferred from @jensen2014obesity]

ES9a's own wording on the usual-care contrast is hedged: «These losses generally are greater than those
produced by usual care (ie, minimal-intervention control group).»
[@jensen2014obesity]
ES2a's population is adults «in whom weight loss is indicated and who wish to lose weight»: the
high-intensity figures are for people who want to lose weight and have a reason to, not for everyone
with a high BMI. [@jensen2014obesity]

[@jensen2014obesity] (ES2a-b);
[@jensen2014obesity] (ES9a-c)

The two key statements, verbatim: «ES9b (Low-Intensity Interventions): Low-intensity, on-site
comprehensive lifestyle interventions, which provide less-than-monthly treatment sessions, do not
consistently produce weight loss when compared with usual care.» and «ES9c (Effect of Intervention
Intensity): When weight loss with each intervention intensity (ie, low, moderate, and high) is compared
with usual care, high-intensity lifestyle interventions (≥14 sessions in 6 months) typically produce
greater net-of-control weight losses than do low-to moderate-intensity interventions.»
[@jensen2014obesity]

The recommendations built on this are both Grade A (Strong): rec 4a, take part for ≥6 months; rec 4b,
«Prescribe on-site, high-intensity (ie, ≥14 sessions in 6 mo) comprehensive weight loss interventions
provided in individual or group sessions by a trained interventionist.»
[@jensen2014obesity]

**Read the 14 sessions as a category edge, not a located knee.** The three intensity bands are bins the
panel compared, so the evidence shows more contact giving more loss across low, moderate and high. No
statement locates a point above which extra sessions stop paying, and none tests anything above "high".
The guideline's own gap list treats the ≥14-contact programme as «the standard for behavioral weight
loss interventions» [@jensen2014obesity], a reference
point rather than a measured optimum. The studied range is the three bands; past high intensity there is
no data here. [inferred from @jensen2014obesity]

**Who delivers it.** A "trained interventionist" in the trials was mostly a health professional
(dietitian, psychologist, exercise specialist, health counsellor) following a formal protocol, and in a
few cases a trained lay person in a programme validated in published trials.
[@jensen2014obesity]

## Primary care on its own: not shown to work

«ES5: In studies to date, low- to moderate-intensity lifestyle interventions for weight loss provided to
overweight or obese adults by primary care practices alone have not been shown to be effective.»
(High) [@jensen2014obesity]

This is the most decision-relevant line on the page for the common case. ES5 concerns structured low-
to moderate-intensity programmes run by practices, and the trials have not shown them to work; routine
advice at ordinary visits is closer to the usual-care comparator than to these programmes. The
guideline's algorithm (Box 10) says «All patients for whom weight loss is recommended should be offered
or referred for comprehensive lifestyle intervention (Box 11a and 11b).»
[@jensen2014obesity]
Read together, the clinician's job is to get the person into a structured programme, in the practice or
by referral, rather than to counsel at routine visits. The ES says "not shown to be effective" for the
trials reviewed; it is not a claim that no primary-care format could work.
[inferred from @jensen2014obesity]

## Delivery mode — on site generally ahead of internet/email; telephone similar in one Low statement, and the guideline is split on remote delivery

| Mode | Weight result | Grade | ES / rec |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual vs group sessions (high-intensity, on site) | no substantial difference | Low | ES10 |
| Telephone vs face-to-face (both with prepackaged meals or web support) | similar, about 5 kg net of usual care at 6 and 24 months | Low | ES4 |
| Electronic (web) with personalised feedback from an interventionist, programmes developed in academic settings | up to 5 kg at 6-12 months (programme arm), more than minimal web or print advice | Moderate | ES3 |
| On site vs internet/email | on site generally greater | Low | ES11 |
| Commercial, in person | 4.8-6.6 kg at 6 months (2 trials, conventional food); 6.6-10.1 kg at 12 months (2 trials, provided food) | Low | ES6 |

[@jensen2014obesity] (ES3);
[@jensen2014obesity] (ES4, ES6, ES10, ES11)

Both remote and commercial options are Grade B (Moderate) recommendations. Rec 4c: «Electronically
delivered weight loss programs (including by telephone) that include personalized feedback from a
trained interventionist† can be prescribed for weight loss but may result in smaller weight loss than
face-to-face interventions.» Rec 4d allows commercial programmes «provided there is peer-reviewed
published evidence of their safety and efficacy».
[@jensen2014obesity]

The commercial figures rest on two trials per row, all graded Low, and the 12-month row includes
provided food, so they do not show that a commercial programme on its own matches a clinic programme.
The mode comparisons are all Low: on site came out generally ahead of internet or email (ES11), while
telephone matched face-to-face in the trials that tested it (ES4). Rec 4c hedges (remote delivery *may*
give smaller losses). The web figure comes from programmes built in academic settings,
which says little about whether a commercial app reaches it.
[inferred from @jensen2014obesity]

*Added 2026-11-09.* The algorithm text (Box 11b), unlike rec 4c, states the smaller loss without the hedge: «As found
in CQ4, emerging evidence supports the efficacy, albeit with less weight loss, of electronically
delivered interventions (eg, by Internet or telephone) that provide personalized feedback by a trained
interventionist† and of some commercial programs that provide counseling (face-to-face or telephonic)
with or without prepackaged meals.» (Box 11b; the next sentence makes referral to these options an
expert-opinion recommendation, conditional on evidence of safety and efficacy)
[@jensen2014obesity]
So the document reads three ways on remote delivery. ES4 (Low) finds telephone similar to
face-to-face. Rec 4c, the graded recommendation (Grade B), says electronic delivery, telephone included,
*may* give smaller losses. Box 11b, algorithm text, states the smaller loss without the hedge. The page
cannot settle which reading the panel meant. On the held text, *smaller loss* is a possibility in the
graded recommendation and a plain statement only in the algorithm, and *telephone equal* rests on one
Low statement whose arms also had prepackaged meals or a web programme.
[inferred from @jensen2014obesity]

## The trajectory: loss peaks at 6 months, then drifts back

The guideline is explicit that a programme's peak is temporary. With continued contact after year one,
«ES2c (Long-Term Weight Loss): Comprehensive lifestyle interventions that, after the first year,
continue to provide bimonthly or more frequent intervention contacts, are associated with gradual weight
regain of 1 to 2 kg/y (on average) from the weight loss achieved at 6 to 12 months. Long-term (>1 y)
weight losses, however, remain larger than those associated with usual care.» (High)
[@jensen2014obesity]
The diet question shows the same shape: 4-12 kg at 6 months, 4-10 kg at 1 year, 3-4 kg at 2 years (CQ3
ES3, High). [@jensen2014obesity]

How many keep a meaningful loss: «ES8b: Of overweight/obese adults who participate in a high-intensity
long-term comprehensive lifestyle intervention, 35% to 60% maintain a loss of ≥5% of initial body weight
at ≥2 years' follow-up (after randomization).» (Moderate)
[@jensen2014obesity]

Averages hide a wide spread. The algorithm text puts the expected average from a high-intensity
programme at about 8 kg and says «The observed average weight loss of approximately 8 kg includes people
who have variable weight loss (ie, some more and some less than average), so accurate prediction of
individual weight loss is not possible.» [@jensen2014obesity]
That sentence sits in Box 11a, part of the treatment algorithm, and restates the evidence statements
rather than adding a graded one.

The physiology behind the regain (appetite and energy-expenditure defence) is on
[[Weight-Loss Maintenance and Metabolic Adaptation]].

## Maintenance: at least a year, and the optimum is unknown

Rec 4f (Grade A): people who have lost weight should take part long term (≥1 year) in a comprehensive
maintenance programme. Rec 4g (Grade A) sets its content: contact monthly or more often, face to face or
by phone, with 200-300 min/wk of activity, weighing weekly or more often and a reduced-calorie diet.
[@jensen2014obesity]

The evidence under it is Moderate. ES8a: a maintenance programme for up to 2.5 years after the initial
loss reduces regain against minimal intervention, and «The optimal duration of weight loss maintenance
programs has not been determined.» [@jensen2014obesity]

So "≥1 year" is a floor inside a studied range of up to 2.5 years. It does not mean a year is enough;
the trials did not test stopping points. [inferred from @jensen2014obesity]

## Very-low-calorie diets inside a programme

| Statement | Result | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| ES7a: medically supervised VLCD (<800 kcal/d, meal replacements) in a high-intensity programme | 14.2-21.0 kg over 11-14 weeks | High |
| ES7b: after the VLCD programme stops | regain of 3.1-3.7 kg over the next 21-38 weeks | High |
| ES7c: moderate-intensity exercise (e.g. brisk walking 135-250 min/wk, aerobic or resistance) added to maintenance therapy after a VLCD | does not reduce regain vs maintenance therapy alone | Low |

[@jensen2014obesity]

Rec 4e reserves VLCDs for «limited circumstances» under medical supervision. It is Grade A on the
NHLBI scale, but the panel mapped it to ACC/AHA Class IIa rather than Class I. The footnote gives the
reason in two sentences: «‡There is strong evidence that if a provider is going to use a
very-low-calorie diet, it should be done with high levels of monitoring by experienced personnel; that
does not mean that practitioners should prescribe very-low-calorie diets. Because of concern that an
ACC/AHA Class I recommendation would be interpreted to mean that the patients should go on a
very-low-calorie diet, it was the consensus of the Expert Panel that this maps more closely to an
ACC/AHA Class IIa recommendation.» [@jensen2014obesity]
The bariatric rec 5a carries a parallel footnote (Grade A, mapped to IIa) on the same ground: «The
ACC/AHA criterion for a Class I recommendation states that the treatment/ procedure should be
performed/administered.» [@jensen2014obesity]
*Revised 2026-11-09.* Read with both footnotes, the IIa is a translation between two
recommendation-strength scales. The NHLBI grade is a strength of recommendation, not an evidence
level: Table 2 is headed *NHLBI Grading of the Strength of Recommendations*, and Grade A reads
«There is high certainty based on evidence that the net benefit† is substantial.»
[@jensen2014obesity]
The evidence level sits in the separate ACC/AHA Level of Evidence column, A for rec 4e, and it is
unchanged too. Only the class moved: the NHLBI Grade A stands, and Class I would have meant *should
be performed*, which the panel did not intend for a supervision-conditional recommendation. An
earlier reading here called it a concern about how clinicians would read it rather than about the
evidence. That is true, but it is a thin instance for [[Which Objective Moved This Recommendation]]: no
non-health objective moved the recommendation, only its label on the second scale.
[inferred from @jensen2014obesity]

ES7c is about moderate exercise after a VLCD. A later trial of *vigorous* supervised exercise after an
8-week low-calorie diet is held on [[Weight-Loss Maintenance and Metabolic Adaptation]]; the two differ
in intensity, comparator and timing, so they are not compared here.

## Where drugs and surgery enter (comparator thresholds only)

The panel did not run an evidence question on drugs, because when the questions were set orlistat was
the only approved weight-loss drug. [@jensen2014obesity]
Its drug recommendation is expert opinion: «The Expert Panel did not review comprehensive evidence for
pharmacotherapy for weight loss. On the basis of expert opinion, the panelists recommend that for
individuals with BMI ≥30 kg/m2 or BMI ≥27 kg/m2 with ≥1 obesity-associated comorbid condition(s) who are
motivated to lose weight, pharmacotherapy can be considered as an adjunct to comprehensive lifestyle
intervention to help achieve targeted weight loss and health goals.»
[@jensen2014obesity]
The BMI cutpoint itself (≥30, or ≥27 with comorbidity) is not the panel's: the algorithm footnotes
attribute it to the FDA, «BMI cutpoint determined by the FDA and listed on the package inserts of
FDA-approved obesity medications.» [@jensen2014obesity]
(Box 10 ‡; the same footnote is Figure 1 †, chunk 06). The expert opinion is the decision to adopt it.
Bariatric referral (rec 5a, Grade A) is for BMI ≥40, or ≥35 with an obesity-related condition, after
behavioural treatment has not reached the target; for BMI <35 there is no recommendation (5b, N).
[@jensen2014obesity]

The drug landscape has changed since: the class comparison that now sizes the drug alternative is on
[[Comparing Obesity Drugs]]. This guideline's drug position predates it and should not be read as a
current comparator.

effectiveness and cost effectiveness of total or partial meal replacements, intermittent fasting,
plant-based and low carbohydrate diets, in achieving and maintaining weight loss in adults living with
overweight or obesity?» [@nice]
It does not ask about programme intensity, contact frequency or delivery mode [searched: "contact

## Limits

- **Dated.** Trial search to 2009 plus large later RCTs (>100 per arm). Remote and app
  delivery has grown since, so the remote-delivery rows are the most likely to have moved. Any
  divergence from newer guidance has lag as a live reading.
- **US setting.** The guideline was written for US primary care. The held text does not say which
  trials sit behind ES5 or ES6, or where they ran, so how far the primary-care null and the commercial
  rows transfer to another health system is untested here.
- **Weight only.** No CQ4 statement reports a patient-important outcome.
- **Bands, not a curve.** The intensity effect is a comparison of three bands, each against usual care,
  not a dose-response fit.
- **Packages, not components.** Each statement grades a whole programme; the share owed to contact,
  diet, activity or weighing is not separated.
- **Single source.** One guideline family; the held NICE review does not ask this question (above).

[inferred from @jensen2014obesity]

## Decision relevance

- **Pick a format with at least monthly contact; the graded target is weekly at the start, ≥14
  sessions in 6 months (rec 4b, Grade A).** Less than monthly did not reliably beat usual care;
  1-2 sessions a month gave 2-4 kg (High); weekly-start programmes gave up to about 8 kg at 6 months
  (High). These are programme-arm means; the net advantage of high intensity is ES9c (Moderate).
- **Routine GP advice alone is not a programme.** Ask for referral to a structured one.
- **Telephone matched face-to-face in the trials that tested it (both arms also had prepackaged meals
  or a web programme); web with personalised feedback gave up to about 5 kg, generally less than on
  site.** Both mode comparisons are Low. Rec 4c (Grade B) says electronic delivery, telephone
  included, *may* give smaller losses; only the algorithm text (Box 11b) states it without the hedge.
  Prefer on site where it can be kept up.
- **Plan for maintenance from the start.** Keep monthly or more frequent contact for at least a year
  after the loss, with 200-300 min/wk of activity and weighing weekly or more (rec 4g). Expect 1-2 kg/y
  regain even with continued contact, and 35-60% to keep ≥5% off at 2 years (Moderate).
- **Group or one-to-one: whichever is easier to keep attending.** The difference was not substantial.
