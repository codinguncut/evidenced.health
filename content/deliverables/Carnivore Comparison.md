**A stratum, not a person, and not everyone.** This critiques two animal-based patterns for one reference
class — the **drifting-median, metabolically-impaired adult**: late 40s, BMI ~30 with central/visceral
adiposity, hypertension, dysglycemia-leaning (elevated triglycerides, low-grade inflammation, toward
metabolic syndrome and MASLD). This person is **not low-risk**, and that fact does most of the work below.

**The two patterns, both run at a matched ~10% caloric deficit:**

| | Clean Carnivore | Medium-Fat Animal-Based |
|---|---|---|
| Macros | ~30% protein / ~70% fat / ~0 carb | ~50% protein / ~35% fat / ~15% carb; up to 3.5 g/kg protein |
| Foods | ribeye (fatty muscle meat, high SFA) | medium-lean beef (round roast) + steamed carrots & broccoli; hydration |

**Why the matched deficit matters.** Holding energy balance equal across both arms controls the
weight-loss variable *by design*, so this is not a contest over which diet makes you eat less — it
isolates the **composition** differences.

## Start here: the deficit hands both arms the biggest lever, roughly equally

For this stratum the single largest win is **visceral-fat loss**, and a matched ~10% deficit delivers a
sustained loss in *either* arm. A ~5% loss reduces liver fat and 7-10% improves liver inflammation
([[Fatty Liver MASLD and Weight Loss]]); the same loss improves glucose, blood pressure and function.
The honest bound, refined by the meta-analysis: dietary/lifestyle weight loss does **not** cut hard
cardiovascular *events* (the largest trial null at HR 0.95; a 54-RCT meta-analysis confirms CV events
null, RR 0.93), but it **does** lower **all-cause mortality** (RR 0.82, high quality, ~6 fewer per 1000)
— by a *non-cardiovascular* route ([[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]]). So the deficit is
worth pursuing for the liver, glucose, blood pressure, function *and* a real (non-CV) mortality benefit —
just not a promised heart-attack reduction.

**Because the deficit controls weight, the decision is no longer *which one makes you lose more*.** It is
which *composition* is better for an already-at-risk person, and which the person can *sustain* — and
both high-protein patterns carry a satiety advantage that helps at a deficit. The live discriminators are
the composition axes below.

## Axis 1 — Saturated fat and LDL: the difference that actually matters here

This is where the two patterns genuinely diverge, and for *this* stratum it is not a footnote.

- **Clean carnivore maximises saturated fat** (70% fat, fatty ribeye) and adds a second LDL-raising
  push: very-low-carbohydrate/ketogenic eating *itself* raises LDL on top of the SFA effect
  ([[Low-Carbohydrate vs Balanced-Carbohydrate Diets]]). Reducing SFA lowers LDL with **high certainty**
  and cumulatively — the more SFA is cut, the more LDL falls ([[Saturated Fat Intake and Replacement]]) —
  so the carnivore arm moves LDL in the wrong direction on two counts at once.
- **Medium-fat explicitly cuts SFA** (35% fat, lean round roast), so LDL runs lower.
- **The deficit partially offsets the carnivore's rise** — weight loss lowers LDL — so the net-LDL
  comparison is *deficit-lowering vs SFA-raising* in the carnivore arm and *both-lowering* in the
  medium-fat arm `[INFERRED — the direction is held on each page; the net in a combined deficit-plus-
  high-SFA diet is the wiki's composition, not a measured value]`.

**How much does the LDL difference matter?** On its own, cutting SFA buys a **small** hard-outcome effect:
reducing SFA lowers cardiovascular events by about **RR 0.83 (15 fewer per 1000)** at Moderate
certainty (the pooled figure across replacements — not to be conflated with the replacement-specific
rows), and shows **no clear effect on mortality** ([[Saturated Fat Intake and Replacement]]). But two
things load it *up* for this stratum: absolute benefit scales with baseline risk
([[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]], [[SCORE2 Baseline Risk and the ESC Treatment Thresholds]]), and this person is at **elevated** risk — so the same relative effect is a larger absolute
one than it would be for a lean 30-year-old, where the low-risk ceiling
([[Cardiometabolic Interventions and Hard CV Outcomes in Low-Risk People]]) would make it genuinely
second-order. **And the causal picture is now held, not hedged:** LDL/apoB *causes* atherosclerotic
disease — evidenced by genetic + Mendelian-randomization + RCT convergence, and risk is *cumulative*
(magnitude x duration) ([[LDL ApoB and Cumulative Exposure]]) — so a sustained diet-induced rise is not a
moved marker but added lifetime exposure. **For THIS stratum the concern is worse than the LDL-C number
shows:** in metabolic syndrome / insulin resistance LDL-C *under-states* the atherogenic **apoB** particle
burden (small-dense LDL packs more particles per unit cholesterol), so the carnivore arm's LDL-C rise
likely understates its apoB rise — the number to trust here is **apoB (or non-HDL)**, not LDL-C. The
residual uncertainty is narrower than before: how *much* apoB a high-SFA carnivore pattern raises in a
given person (the hyper-responder magnitude), not whether the rise matters.

**Verdict on this axis:** for an already-at-risk person, the carnivore arm's LDL/apoB maximisation is a
real cost — sharpened, not softened, now that the causal transmission is held — and the medium-fat arm
sits closer to the evidence. Symmetric standards, though: *the deficit
causing weight loss does not excuse a large LDL rise here — and the LDL worry does not erase the shared
weight-loss benefit.*

## Axis 2 — Protein: the medium-fat arm overshoots, but the deficit gives it a partial defence

At *maintenance*, the medium-fat arm's **3.5 g/kg is gratuitous** — muscle gains from protein plateau
near **~1.6 g/kg/day** ([[Protein and Resistance Training for Muscle and Strength]]), and above that the
extra protein buys no more muscle (the supplemental effect is modest to begin with: ~+2.5 kg on a 1RM,
~+0.3 kg lean mass). **Under the deficit the rationale improves:** adequate-to-high protein preserves
lean mass while fat is lost, and the useful target rises somewhat in a deficit — but the defensible
figure is *high, ~1.6-2+ g/kg*, still short of 3.5. Both patterns are animal-based, so protein
*quality* is a non-issue (both high on the DIAAS scale, [[Protein Quality and the DIAAS Score]]). A
quiet synthesis point: raising protein is *part of how* the medium-fat arm lowers its fat fraction, so
Axis 2 and Axis 1 are linked. **The upper safety bound of ~3.5 g/kg is a gap the wiki does not hold** —
named, not invented.

## Axis 3 — Carbohydrate, fibre, vegetables

- **Carbohydrate restriction helps the dysglycemic arm of this stratum** — both patterns are lower-carb,
  and restriction raises short-term type-2-diabetes remission (HbA1c <6.5%: risk difference ~0.32 at 6
  months, Moderate), but the effect **attenuates by 12 months** (to ~0.10) and **collapses toward zero
  once *no diabetes medication* is required** ([[Carbohydrate Restriction and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]]). Between a low- and a balanced-carb diet the weight difference is ~1 kg, judged not
  clinically important ([[Low-Carbohydrate vs Balanced-Carbohydrate Diets]]).
- **Fibre and vegetables:** the carnivore arm has essentially none; the medium-fat arm's steamed
  carrots/broccoli supply fibre (a modest, cohort-level benefit, [[Whole Grains Refined Grains and Pulses]]) plus potassium (Axis 5) and micronutrient diversity. Steaming is low-heat, so it forms fewer
  advanced glycation end-products — though that lever is small: low-AGE diets move some surrogate markers
  but are null on weight, glucose, HbA1c and blood pressure, with no hard-outcome evidence
  ([[Dietary AGEs and Cooking Method]]). The brassica *goitrogen* worry is defused by cooking and normal
  intake ([[Antinutrients in Plant Foods]]).
- **Carnivore forgoes the fibre/micronutrient channel entirely;** whether muscle-meat-only meets
  micronutrient needs is a gap the wiki does not hold.

## Axis 4 — Red meat: shared, so it does not decide anything

Both patterns are beef-heavy, so the contested red-meat question applies **equally** to both and cannot
discriminate them: one guideline family says limit red/processed meat, another says current intake is
defensible on the same evidence — the live disagreement is the standard of proof and whose values set
the threshold, not whether meat causes cancer ([[Should Adults Reduce Red and Processed Meat]]). One
marginal difference: high-temperature searing (ribeye) forms more heterocyclic amines / PAHs than gentler
roasting ([[Red and Processed Meat and Cancer]]) — a small point favouring the medium-fat arm's cooking,
not a headline.

## Axis 5 — Sodium and potassium: live because this stratum is hypertensive

Reducing sodium lowers blood pressure with **high certainty** (~3.4 mmHg resting, up to ~5.5 mmHg on
ambulatory measurement, [[Sodium Intake and Blood Pressure]]), and the effect is larger in the
hypertensive. Both patterns are meat-heavy, so **salted or processed cuts are the sodium risk to watch**;
the medium-fat arm's vegetables also add **potassium**, which the zero-plant carnivore pattern forgoes.
A small axis, but it runs the same direction as Axis 1 — toward the medium-fat pattern — for this
hypertensive stratum.

## Where this lands (net, stratum-weighted, not a dogmatic verdict)

- **The shared weight loss is the main event**, and the matched deficit delivers it in either arm — so
  whichever pattern the person will *sustain* pulls the biggest lever this stratum has.
- **Among the differences, the SFA/LDL axis is the one that genuinely matters here**, because this person
  is already at elevated risk; on it the medium-fat pattern sits closer to the evidence, and the clean
  carnivore maximises the one exposure with an evidenced (if small) harm direction.
- **The medium-fat arm's 3.5 g/kg protein is an overshoot** — defensible as *high* for lean-mass
  preservation at a deficit, not at 3.5.
- **Neither pattern has hard-outcome evidence**, and no attainable diet-composition difference at this
  risk level is large against the shared weight-loss benefit. Read the whole comparison at the margin.

## Open questions (surfaced, deliberately NOT adjudicated)

These mechanistic hypotheses are stated so a reader is not misled by their absence; the wiki holds no
adjudicating evidence and takes no position (routed to `ingest-queue.md`):

- **No-fibre and cholesterol clearance.** Does a zero-fibre pattern impair enterohepatic bile-acid /
  cholesterol excretion, so circulating cholesterol runs higher than the SFA effect alone predicts, while
  the medium-fat arm's fibre adds a bile-acid-binding LDL-lowering channel? Held only at the effect level
  (a daily pulse portion moves LDL-C ~0.2 mmol/L); the mechanism and the reuptake half are unheld.
- **No-fibre and mobilised-toxin clearance during fat loss.** During active fat loss, lipophilic
  compounds stored in adipose are released into circulation — does fibre aid their faecal clearance, so a
  zero-fibre pattern permits reuptake? Entirely unheld.
- **High dietary fat during fat loss and hepatic/circulating-lipid load.** When adipose is mobilising
  fatty acids during a deficit, does a high dietary-fat intake add to circulating lipids and hepatic
  lipid handling relative to a lower-fat arm, even though net weight loss improves liver fat? Unheld.

## Gaps (stated honestly; routed for acquisition)

- **No hard-outcome (event/mortality) trial of EITHER pattern** — both are assembled from component
  evidence. The open loop, front and centre.
- **Protein upper-safety bound above ~2 g/kg** — unheld; the wiki holds the 1.6 g/kg muscle plateau, not
  a safety ceiling at 3.5.
- **Diet-induced LDL responder tail** on very-low-carb + high-SFA (large *hyper-responders*) — unheld.
- **Micronutrient adequacy of a muscle-meat-only diet** — unheld.
- **Diet tolerability / sustainability at a deficit** — the live discriminator the wiki cannot measure.

## The honest limits

- **Open loop:** this grades what the evidence holds and how faithfully, never whether either diet
  improves a real outcome. Coherence, not validity.
- **Appraise, do not prescribe:** a large diet-induced LDL rise may warrant lipid monitoring or a
  prescriber's input, which this cannot do.
- **A stratum, not a person:** how much the SFA/LDL axis weighs is set by the individual's actual
  cardiovascular risk and outcome priorities, elicited per case.
- **Symmetric standards, both diets and both directions:** neither the carnivore's ancestral framing nor
  the medium-fat diet's SFA-reduction rationale earns a discount; where the evidence is small or absent,
  this says so.
