Nucleus of the `fluoride` cluster. Fluoride is unusual among the wiki's exposures: it is
**deliberately added** to drinking water and toothpaste for a real benefit (caries prevention) *and*
is a suspected **developmental neurotoxicant** at higher doses. The whole decision is a dose-stratified
benefit-versus-harm balance. **Every arm has a held source** — the caries-benefit pole (2024
Cochrane) joins the child-IQ and skeletal harm-side arms — so the nucleus carries the induced
**dose-stratified benefit/harm synthesis** (below), not a list of gaps.



## The child-IQ finding: higher fluoride, lower child IQ — above 1.5 mg/L

The US National Toxicology Program's 2024 OHAT systematic review concluded, at **moderate confidence**,
that higher fluoride exposure is associated with lower IQ in children — but the exposure bound is the
whole decision and is routinely stripped off in secondary reporting. Quoted intact:

- «This review finds, with moderate confidence, that higher estimated fluoride exposures (e.g., as in
  approximations of exposure such as drinking water fluoride concentrations that exceed the World
  Health Organization Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality of 1.5 mg/L of fluoride) are consistently
  associated with lower IQ in children.»
  [@ntp2024fluoride]
- Below that level the review does **not** find a settled effect: «Associations between lower total
  fluoride exposure [... concentrations ... lower than the WHO Guidelines ... of 1.5 mg/L ...] and
  children's IQ remain unclear.»
  [@ntp2024fluoride]
- The same summary paragraph bounds that *unclear* stratum by **total** exposure, not by the water
  number. The next sentence: «However, because people receive fluoride from multiple sources (not just
  drinking water), individuals living in areas with optimally fluoridated water can have total fluoride
  exposures higher than the concentration of their drinking water.» Five sentences later: «The
  moderate confidence conclusions may also be relevant to people living in optimally fluoridated areas of the United States depending
  on the extent of their additional exposures to fluoride from sources other than drinking water.»
  [@ntp2024fluoride]
- NTP names one under-estimated case: «It is worth noting that there are circumstances wherein
  typical children's water consumption considered with water fluoride levels may substantially
  underestimate total fluoride exposure. One example is bottle-fed infants wherein nutrition is
  provided by powdered formula that is rehydrated with fluoridated water (Till et al. 2020).» It then
  relays a held recommendation, made for fluorosis rather than IQ: «To decrease an exclusively
  formula-fed infant’s exposure to fluoride, for the purpose of reducing risk of dental fluorosis, the
  Centers for Disease Control and Prevention recommends using low-fluoride bottled water to mix with
  infant formula (CDC 2015).»
  [@ntp2024fluoride]

A bare *fluoride lowers IQ* with no exposure bound **overstates this source**. The low-*total*-exposure
stratum is an [[The Insufficient-Evidence Statement|insufficient-evidence]] state, not a demonstrated
null and not a demonstrated harm. But US community-water fluoridation at 0.7 mg/L is a *water*
concentration, not a total exposure: most people on it sit in the unclear stratum, while someone on
0.7 mg/L water with large other intake (NTP's example is the formula-fed infant on reconstituted
fluoridated water; NTP also names toothpaste habits and black tea as sources) may sit in the stratum
NTP's moderate-confidence finding *may* cover. The dose-threshold decision, the study table, and the
transportability appraisal live on [[Fluoride and Child IQ]].
[inferred from @ntp2024fluoride]

*Corrected 2026-11-10:* this page earlier placed the whole 0.7 mg/L water stratum in the
insufficient-evidence cell. NTP's summary paragraph says its moderate-confidence finding may apply to

## The exposure map — where the decision-relevant doses sit

| Exposure | Fluoride (drinking water) | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| US community water fluoridation | 0.7 mg/L | PHS-recommended added dose (historically 0.8-1.2) |
| WHO water guideline | 1.5 mg/L | NTP's «higher»-exposure referent; anti-fluorosis, not anti-IQ |
| EPA secondary (non-enforceable) | 2.0 mg/L | severe dental-fluorosis protection |
| EPA MCL (enforceable) | 4.0 mg/L | skeletal-fluorosis protection |

Every value and purpose in the table is stated in NTP's background section (PHS 0.7 mg/L, historically
0.8 to 1.2 mg/L; WHO 1.5 mg/L against dental and skeletal fluorosis; EPA 2.0 mg/L secondary standard
against severe dental fluorosis; EPA 4.0 mg/L MCL set against skeletal fluorosis).
[@ntp2024fluoride]

Total fluoride exposure is not the water number: even in optimally fluoridated areas it «can vary
widely ... based on personal habits in the use of dental products and consumption of beverages such as
black tea» [@ntp2024fluoride]. \~1.9 million US
residents (0.59%) are on community water naturally ≥1.5 mg/L.
[@ntp2024fluoride] That is a small stratum, and
the one where the moderate-confidence association sits by water concentration alone; people below it by water
but high by total intake are the other stratum it may reach (above).

## The benefit/harm menu — one axis, arm by arm

The telos's **one-axis (health)** rule governs here: this is a health-outcome menu, not a
fluoridation-policy verdict. Name each arm and its status; do not net them into a scalar.

**Child IQ / neurodevelopment (harm side; an association):** lower IQ associated with exposure at
moderate confidence above 1.5 mg/L, unclear at lower *total* exposure, and possibly relevant to people
on 0.7 mg/L water with high other intake (above).

**Caries prevention (BENEFIT):** the reason fluoride is added at all — NTP explicitly excluded it
(«Benefits of fluoride with respect to oral health are not addressed in this monograph»
[@ntp2024fluoride]). Held from the 2024 Cochrane review, low
certainty: «Based on contemporary evidence (afer 1975), the initiation of CWF may lead to a slightly
greater change in dmf over time (mean diﬀerence (MD) 0.24, 95% confidence interval (CI) -0.03 to
0.52; P = 0.09; 2 studies, 2908 children; low-certainty evidence). This equates to a diﬀerence in
dmf of approximately one-quarter of a tooth in favour of CWF; this eﬀect estimate includes the
possibility of benefit and no benefit.»
[@iheozor2024fluoridation] The dmf figure is
the primary dentition; the contemporary permanent-dentition (DMFT) evidence is one the review was
«very uncertain» about, and contemporary studies come «with smaller eﬀect sizes than pre-1975
studies», the large familiar benefit being a pre-toothpaste-era number.
[@iheozor2024fluoridation] Full arm,
era-attenuation and substitution mechanism on [[Fluoride and Caries Prevention]].

**Dental fluorosis (mild, harm side; prevalence modelled at 0.7 ppm):** «The previous
version of this review found low-certainty evidence that fluoridated water may be associated with
dental fluorosis. With a fluoride level of 0.7 parts per million (ppm), approximately 12% of
participants had fluorosis of aesthetic concern (95% CI 8% to 17%; 40 studies, 59,630
participants), and approximately 40% had fluorosis of any level (95% CI 35% to 44%; 90 studies,
180,530 participants).»
[@iheozor2024fluoridation] Two scope limits.
The 12% and 40% are modelled *prevalences* at 0.7 ppm (the summary-of-findings table lists
«Comparison: n/a»), not an excess over non-fluoridated water; the odds of aesthetic-concern
fluorosis rise by a factor of 2.90 (95% CI 2.05 to 4.10) per 1 ppm.
[@iheozor2024fluoridation] That interval
excludes 1. And the 2024 update did not re-review fluorosis, for a stated reason: «Because the
association of fluoridated water with dental fluorosis is widely accepted, we did not update the
evidence on this occasion.»
[@iheozor2024fluoridation] So the source holds
both readings: a low GRADE certainty rating, and an association its authors call widely accepted,
with a dose-response odds ratio whose interval excludes 1. A cosmetic/enamel
outcome, below the IQ/fracture patient-important tier. Detail on [[Fluoride and Caries Prevention]].

**Skeletal fluorosis / bone fracture (harm side; associations, dose- and stratum-dependent):** the
EPA 4.0 mg/L MCL is set against skeletal fluorosis [@ntp2024fluoride].
Held at low confidence from one dose-response MA. Its overall curve (4 studies, all fracture sites,
no sex-specific analysis possible) is flat up to about 1.4 mg/L and rises above it: RR 1.06 (95% CI
0.80-1.41) at 2.0, 1.19 (0.87-1.64) at 3.0 and 1.35 (0.94-1.95) at 4.0 mg/L, every CI crossing 1.0.
[@mazzoli2025fluoride] (The abstract rounds the threshold to
about 1.5 mg/L.) [@mazzoli2025fluoride] The sex-specific
fragility-fracture analyses work at lower doses. Across all ages, the female curve is U-shaped and
non-significant: «Specifically, the RR was 0.84 (95% CI 0.67– 1.07) at 0.34 mg/L, 1.00 (95% 0.79–1.27)
at 0.9 mg/L, and rose to 1.13 (95% CI 0.93–1.37) at 1.1 mg/L.»
[@mazzoli2025fluoride] The male curve runs the other way:
«In contrast, the curve for males (seven studies) was generally indica­ tive of a decreased risk with
increasing fluoride expo­ sure with Little indication of a U-shaped pattern, and RR ranging from 0.83
(95% CI 0.72–0.97) at 0.57 mg/L to 0.87 (95% CI 0.48–1.58) at 1.8 mg/L.»
[@mazzoli2025fluoride] The significant harm estimate comes
from a narrower stratum: in a sensitivity analysis of females over 50, fragility-fracture risk rises
from about 0.5 mg/L, RR 1.26 (95% CI 1.10-1.46) at 1.0 mg/L.
[@mazzoli2025fluoride] Mazzoli's abstract describes this as
an effect in females «but little or nothing in males», and concludes «an enhanced susceptibility in
postmenopausal females». [@mazzoli2025fluoride]
BMD and fracture **dissociate** (a BMD rise is not a bone benefit). Full arm, U/J adjudication and
BMD-surrogate trap on [[Fluoride and Bone Health]]. The overall curve's \~1.4-1.5 mg/L bend sits near
the NTP IQ referent, but that alignment holds only for the non-significant overall curve; for women
over 50 the signal starts inside the historic 0.8-1.2 mg/L US fluoridation range.
[inferred from @mazzoli2025fluoride; @ntp2024fluoride]

**Adult cognition:** low confidence, «a lack of evidence of an effect» (two studies).
[@ntp2024fluoride]

**Other child neurodevelopmental (ADHD, infant neurobehavioral):** direction adverse, confidence low.
NTP's low risk-of-bias studies «provide some evidence of an association» with lower infant
neurobehavioral scores, other cognitive effects and attention-related disorders including ADHD, but
heterogeneous outcomes and few comparable studies leave «low confidence».
[@ntp2024fluoride]

*Corrected 2026-11-10:* earlier versions called dental fluorosis *established* at 0.7 mg/L without its
low GRADE rating and read the 12% as attributable to fluoridation; the source gives a prevalence with
no comparator, rates the evidence low certainty, and calls the association widely accepted. They also
tied the fracture arm to the 1.5 mg/L line, omitting the sex-specific fragility signals.

## The cluster-closing synthesis: the balance is DOSE-STRATIFIED, not a single benefit-vs-harm clash



With every arm held, the benefit<->harm relationship is addressable — and the honest
structure is a **dose-stratified distinction, not a `[[tension]]`**. Running the not-joined checks
(Cooper, ii): for most people the caries benefit and the serious harm-side associations **hold mostly
at different exposures**, so they are consistent once matched by dose — filing a blanket *benefit vs
harm* tension would compare non-joined positions (a fake tension). The exceptions are two strata where
a harm-side signal reaches the fluoridation range: high-total-intake children and women over 50.
 Veneri's urinary slope from about the 0.7 mg/L water equivalent is a population-level
pointer rather than a stratum, and its interval crosses zero (−2.15, 95% CI −4.48 to 0.18 IQ points).
[@veneri2023fluoride] What the fabric holds
instead is a single balance whose sign **changes along the fluoride axis**:

| Exposure | Benefit (caries) | Harm (IQ · skeletal · fluorosis) | Net decision structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **\~0.7 mg/L** (US CWF) | modest, **low certainty** (dmf MD 0.24, CI crosses 0; primary dentition), era-attenuated | IQ **insufficient-evidence** at low total exposure, but NTP's moderate-confidence finding *may* reach people with high other intake (e.g. formula-fed infants); overall fracture **insufficient-evidence**, except a low-certainty fragility-fracture signal in women over 50 from \~0.5 mg/L; mild **dental fluorosis** prevalence \~12% aesthetic / \~40% any (low certainty, no comparator) | decision under **deep uncertainty** on the serious-harm side for most people; stratum-specific signals for high-total-intake children and women over 50 |
| **>1.5 mg/L** | caries dose-response **not estimated** in held fabric | **lower child IQ associated** at **moderate confidence**; overall **fracture** association rises (CIs cross 1.0) | balance **tips toward mitigation**, carried by the IQ association; the benefit side's shape here is unknown |
| **>3-4 mg/L** | not estimated | overall fracture RR 1.35 (0.94-1.95) at 4.0 mg/L, still CI-crossing; EPA MCL 4.0 set against skeletal fluorosis | mitigation (defluoridation / alternative source) |

[inferred from @iheozor2024fluoridation; @ntp2024fluoride; @mazzoli2025fluoride]

The load-bearing insight (the `hidden_insight` a tension would have carried): **at the fluoridation
dose there is no joined clash for most people** — the serious harm-side associations are insufficient-evidence rather
than demonstrated, so the 0.7 mg/L decision is *under uncertainty*, not *between two knowns*.
 Two strata are the exception: children whose total intake is high at 0.7 mg/L water, to
whom NTP says its moderate-confidence IQ finding may apply, and women over 50, where a single-lab
sensitivity analysis finds fragility-fracture risk rising from \~0.5 mg/L. The male decreased-risk
estimate at 0.57 mg/L is not carried as a benefit stratum: Mazzoli's abstract reads the male effect as
little or nothing, and the male curve's interval crosses 1.0 by 1.8 mg/L.
[inferred from @ntp2024fluoride; @mazzoli2025fluoride] The clearer
trade-off appears **above \~1.5 mg/L**, where the moderate-confidence IQ association sits (see
[[Fluoride and Child IQ]], [[Fluoride and Bone Health]]). No held source estimates a
caries dose-response above 0.7 mg/L, so whether the benefit has flattened there cannot be cited;
mitigation above 1.5 mg/L rests on the IQ association alone. Below it, for most people, the
decision is dominated by uncertainty and by the person's weighting of a small caries gain against a
mild cosmetic fluorosis risk (layer 3).

This is **one axis (health)**: it is a benefit/harm *map*, not a fluoridation-policy verdict. Cost,
environmental load and equity are separate axes the caries source touches (named on
[[Fluoride and Caries Prevention]]) and are not priced in here; the health-outcome weighting stays the
person's.

## Independence: NTP and Taylor 2025 are ONE backing (VERIFIED), not two

NTP's dose-response rests on its **own companion** meta-analysis («DTT Meta-analysis, Taylor et al.
2024») — «The NTP authors of this monograph conducted a companion systematic review and meta-analysis»
[@ntp2024fluoride]. That companion is now held as
the peer-reviewed primary **Taylor 2025** (JAMA Pediatrics 179(3):282-292), and the one-backing framing
is **CONFIRMED, not assumed**: its ref 12 IS this NTP monograph, its authors are the NIEHS Division of
Translational Toxicology (Taylor, Rooney, Bucher — shared with the monograph), and the funder
statement reads in full: «Role of the Funder/Sponsor: NIEHS had no role in the design and conduct of
the study; collection, management, analysis, and interpretation of the data; or preparation of the
manuscript. NIEHS did have a role in the review approval of the manuscript and the decision to submit
the manuscript for publication.»
[@taylor2025fluoride] The one-backing verdict rests on the shared
authorship and affiliation, not on the funder role. So Taylor 2025 does **not** raise
confidence by type-E convergence — it is the same stream's evidence in peer-reviewed form (a type-F
refinement of the harm arm, landed on [[Fluoride and Child IQ]]), counted as **one line with NTP**.
Confidence stays `low` on the below-1.5 stratum accordingly.

## The third line (Veneri 2023): qualified-E, not independent backing — and a friction

A different stream did arrive — Veneri, Vinceti, Filippini et al. (CREAGEN / University of Modena), an
SR + cubic-spline dose-response
[@veneri2023fluoride]. It clears the **necessary** conditions for a type-E independent line: **no author shared** with
NTP/Taylor, **independent funding** (Veneri: «This research did not receive any specific grant»
[@veneri2023fluoride]), and **different analytic
instruments** (ROBINS-E RoB tool; one-stage cubic-spline).
[@veneri2023fluoride] But independence of team is
not independence of evidence — Veneri pools a **near-fully-shared primary-study base** (its 33 studies
sit almost entirely inside NTP's 72 IQ studies / Taylor's 59 group-level studies; the fluoride-IQ
census is near-closed, sharing every marquee cohort — ELEMENT/Bashash, MIREC/Till+Farmus,
Dunedin/Broadbent).
**Two MAs pooling the same primary cohorts agree on the pooled direction by construction.** Veneri's
water-fluoride dose-response is «−3.05 (95% CI −4.06; −2.04) IQ points per 1 mg/L up to 2 mg/L,
becoming steeper above such level», and urinary fluoride gives «A weaker and substantially linear
decrease of −2.15 (95% CI −4.48; 0.18) IQ points with increasing urinary fluoride emerged above 0.28
mg/L (approximately reflecting a water fluoride content of 0.7 mg/L).»
[@veneri2023fluoride] Its conclusion runs both
ways: «Overall, most studies suggested an adverse effect of fluoride exposure on children's IQ,
starting at low levels of exposure. However, a major role of residual confounding could not be ruled
out, thus indicating the need of additional prospective studies at low risk of bias to conclusively
assess the relation between fluoride exposure and cognitive neurodevelopment.»
[@veneri2023fluoride] That directional
concordance is **shared-data robustness, NOT the independent second backing** that would raise
confidence by convergence. This is a **qualified / partial E** (robust to team + analytic choices),
and it is **NOT** marked `[E-independent]` — doing so would be laundered-E. Confidence stays `low`.


Two forces cut *against* even a confidence bump. First, Veneri's low-RoB and cohort-only strata do
**not** reproduce the association, though both are imprecise rather than null. The low-RoB stratum is
a single cross-sectional study (Feng 2022): «a MD of 1.11 (95% CI −0.67; 2.89) emerged for the only
low RoB study». The cohort-only pool (n = 3) gives MD −0.74 (95% CI −2.90 to 1.42), a negative point
estimate whose interval includes meaningful harm.
[@veneri2023fluoride] NTP reads its own data the
other way: «The inverse association between estimated fluoride exposure and IQ in children was
consistent across different study populations, study locations, study quality/risk-of-bias
determinations, study designs, exposure assessment measures, and types of exposure data (group-level
and individual-level).» [@ntp2024fluoride] The
friction (**type-D**, filed on [[Fluoride and Child IQ]]) traces partly to the RoB instrument:
Veneri's ROBINS-E table rates Bashash 2017 *high* overall (high on confounding)
[@veneri2023fluoride], while NTP counts the three
Mexican and Canadian prospective cohorts, Bashash among them, as «considered to reflect a low risk for
bias». [@ntp2024fluoride] Second, the same shared-data property means the two families cannot
*independently* confirm each other on the low-dose stratum at all — that stays a genuine gap. A truly
independent line would need a **new primary cohort at fluoridation-relevant exposure**, ideally a US
one (none exists yet).

Note also the **halo-across-a-lab** caution: the Modena/Vinceti group **also supplies the now-landed
skeletal harm-side arm** (Mazzoli 2025, on [[Fluoride and Bone Health]]) — different outcome, so no
laundering, but the cluster now rests on **two arms (IQ, skeletal) from the same lab**, and that is
not independent evidential breadth. Independent replication of *either* arm by a different group is a
named gap; the skeletal arm's `low` confidence is held there for exactly this reason.

## Why this is a decision-change, not a restatement

Public discourse collapses to *fluoride is/ isn't safe*. The fabric's move is the **stratum split**: a
moderate-confidence harm association above \~1.5 mg/L water fluoride, and *unresolved* at low total
exposure. Someone on a private well testing >1.5 mg/L faces a different decision (the IQ association
applies; mitigation is on the table) than most people on optimally fluoridated 0.7 mg/L municipal
water, for whom the serious harm arms are insufficient-evidence, mild dental fluorosis is an
association the review authors call widely accepted though GRADE-rated low certainty, and the caries benefit is small and low-certainty in the contemporary era
([[Fluoride and Caries Prevention]]).
Two groups on 0.7 mg/L water sit apart from that majority. Infants fed formula reconstituted with
fluoridated water are NTP's named case of under-estimated total exposure, so its moderate-confidence
finding may reach them. Reconstituting formula with low-fluoride water is a held recommendation (CDC,
relayed by NTP), made to reduce dental fluorosis; extending its rationale to IQ is this page's
inference. Women over 50 carry a low-certainty, single-lab fragility-fracture signal from \~0.5 mg/L.
[inferred from @ntp2024fluoride; @mazzoli2025fluoride]
The exposure number, and total intake, not the word *fluoride*, carry the decision — and at the
fluoridation dose the menu is small effects held at low certainty, on both sides.
