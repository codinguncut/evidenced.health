## Where FATmax-intensity training fits (not a seventh modality)

*Train at FATmax, in the fat-burning zone* names an **intensity-prescription method, not a modality**:
individually-measured maximal-fat-oxidation intensity, applied within aerobic exercise. In adults with
overweight/obesity it is prescribed at \~42-49% VO2max (34.5-46% observed), i.e. at or below the floor of
the AE-M (45-65%) band ranked above — so it is individualised light-to-low-moderate aerobic, folded into
this page's aerobic category rather than ranked beside it -> [[FatMax Training for Body Composition and Cardiometabolic Health]] [@jiang2026] (observed range: chunk 02).

The FATmax meta-analysis (Jiang 2026) reports a much larger body-weight reduction (MD -3.66 kg vs
non-exercise/diet-only control) than this NMA's \~1 kg across modalities. That is a **distinction, not a
contradiction** — the comparator (vs control, not within a modality network), the training volume, the
inclusion of diet co-intervention in several trials, and the population (BMI>=25 incl. adolescents and
metabolically-ill, vs BMI>=30 adults here) all differ. It does **not** overturn the finding below that
exercise alone is not a weight-loss lever; the FATmax number is a confounded against-control effect, and
its own prediction intervals for BMI/body-fat/waist cross zero
[@jiang2026].
[inferred from @odonoghue2020]

**The decision.** An adult with obesity decides to exercise. Does it matter *which kind* — aerobic,
resistance, or both — for body composition and fitness? O'Donoghue 2021 is a gold-tier network
meta-analysis (45 RCTs, 3566 adults with BMI >=30) that ranks six modalities head-to-head against each
other and against no-exercise control, across four anthropometric outcomes plus CRF. It is the
integrating comparison the component exercise pages do not hold: [[Measuring and Raising Cardiorespiratory Fitness]] has HIIT-vs-MICT, [[Exercise vs Caloric Restriction for Visceral Fat]] has exercise-vs-diet,
but neither ranks aerobic-vs-resistance-vs-combined for body composition in this stratum.
[inferred from @odonoghue2020]

The six modalities (F.I.T.T.-defined): **AE-V** aerobic vigorous (>65% VO2max), **AE-M** aerobic moderate
(45-65%), **R-HI** resistance high load (>75% 1RM), **R-LM** resistance low-moderate (50-75% 1RM),
**COM-HI** combined high-intensity, **COM-LM** combined low-moderate
[@odonoghue2020].

## The robust finding: combined on top, low-load resistance-only on the bottom

«Our main findings indicate that in subjects with a BMI >=30 kg m−2, a combined exercise intervention
consisting of aerobic and resistance training (COM-HI or COM-LM) is the most promising for reducing WC
and %BF, as well as increasing CRF, despite no substantial weight loss. Out of the six interventions
investi­gated, low-to-moderate load resistance training was deemed to be the least effective.»
[@odonoghue2020] The top three overall were
COM-HI, COM-LM and AE-M.

What survives as a decision is coarse: **aerobic-containing and combined programmes rank above
*low-moderate-load* resistance-only, and combining aerobic with resistance ranks at or near the top of every
anthropometric outcome.** For BMI the source states the aerobic-component arms «were superior
to resistance only interventions» (near-identical wording for waist circumference; not stated for weight) [@odonoghue2020].
High-load resistance (R-HI) is not separated from aerobic: its Table 3 point estimates vs control are
similar (weight −0.63 vs AE-V −0.65 kg; %BF −1.81 vs AE-M −1.70 / AE-V −1.27) with wider CIs that cross
zero (as AE-V's %BF CI also does), and it outranks both aerobic arms on %BF (P-score .58)
[@odonoghue2020]. The fine ordering among the
leaders does not survive (next section). (corrected 2026-10-06: *aerobic beats resistance-only, consistent
across outcomes* -> scoped to low-moderate-load resistance; self-critique)

## Why the fine ranking is a ranking of noise

The P-scores order the modalities, but **most of the between-modality differences behind them are not
statistically significant** — the superiority that *is* significant is almost always *aerobic-containing
vs control* or *aerobic-containing vs low-moderate-load resistance-only*, not one leader over another. In the CRF network
the problem is total: **no modality reached significance at all.** «Although all interventions resulted
in an increase in absolute VO2max, there were no statistically significant improvements in fitness found;
in accordance with the P score, COM-HI was the intervention ... most likely to increase VO2max.»
[@odonoghue2020] So the CRF ranking (COM-HI .73
on top) orders a set of null results.

The authors supply the governing caveat themselves: «such scores should be interpreted cautiously unless
there are actual clinically meaningful differences between interventions.»
[@odonoghue2020] Read the ranking at the
resolution the evidence supports: **combined and aerobic beat low-moderate-load resistance-only; aerobic vs
high-load resistance is not separated; the precise order among the top arms is not established.**
[inferred from @odonoghue2020]

**A different stratum is silent on between-mode CRF differences** — Khalafi (post-menopausal women; CRF
analysis «35 intervention arms from 25 studies» of a 129-study review) finds every mode raises CRF vs control
(aerobic SMD 1.21, resistance 1.26, combined 1.47, all significant) but runs no head-to-head test between modes
[@khalafi2023], so it neither confirms nor refutes this
NMA's non-separation. Versus control the two disagree: Khalafi finds significant CRF gains for every mode,
this NMA finds none; O'Donoghue's use of absolute (L/min) VO2max is a candidate explanation, not yet checked
. (corrected 2026-10-06: *129 RCTs ... converging* -> 25-study CRF analysis, silent on
between-mode order; self-critique) The cross-stratum synthesis (matched-parameter table, the low-stakes
modality-for-CRF decision) is worked on [[Measuring and Raising Cardiorespiratory Fitness]].

## Exercise alone is not a weight-loss lever — regardless of modality

The single most decision-relevant number is how *small* the weight effect is. «Overall, weight loss was
minimal, with mean values ranging from −0.05 to −1.01 kg.»
[@odonoghue2020] across every modality. The
authors find this similar to earlier meta-analyses of aerobic and combined exercise, which they report
as concluding «for weight loss, exercise alone is not an effective therapy, a hypocaloric balance is
necessary» [@odonoghue2020] — that sentence is
O'Donoghue citing prior work (their refs 18-19), not a conclusion pooled in this NMA; the NMA's own
finding is the \~1 kg ceiling above (corrected 2026-10-06: *authors conclude* -> authors report earlier
MAs' conclusion, ODonoghue chunk 02).

This is the modality-resolved version of the vault's held picture on [[Exercise Energy Compensation]]
(energy expended is partly offset by compensatory intake) and [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]] (exercise disappoints as a scale lever). O'Donoghue adds that **no choice of modality rescues
exercise as a weight-loss tool** — the \~1 kg ceiling holds across aerobic, resistance and combined. The
body-composition effects (%BF, WC) are the better-responding outcomes, and %BF is argued to be more
responsive than body weight.

## Magnitudes and the surrogate boundary

The anthropometric effects are small in absolute terms: best-arm reductions of roughly 1 kg (weight),
\~2.8 cm waist (COM-LM), \~2.8% body fat (COM-HI; -2.82, CI -5.50 to -0.14 [@odonoghue2020]). CRF rose 7-15% but
non-significantly. The BMI estimates (COM-HI −2.79, CI −5.95 to −0.36; AE-M −1.76) are incompatible
with the same arms' \~1 kg weight changes at the sample's mean (90.5 kg, BMI 32.3: −2.79 kg/m2 is \~7.8 kg),
and the COM-HI CIs are asymmetric [@odonoghue2020];
treat the BMI magnitudes as unreliable — the weight figure is the coherent one. (corrected
2026-10-06: \~2.8 BMI units dropped from the *small* list; self-critique) **Every outcome here is a surrogate** — no mortality or CV event is measured
-> [[Surrogate Outcomes]].

The paper re-expresses its surrogates as risk reductions, but **those figures are borrowed coefficients,
not measured outcomes.** «an increase of 1 cm in WC resulted in a 2% increase in CVD risk» (a separate
meta-regression) is applied «in reverse» to claim COM-LM's WC loss buys a 5.5% CVD-risk decrease; and «an
improvement in fitness of between 8% and 10% is asso­ciated with a 12% decrease in mortality» is imported
to argue the CRF gains matter [@odonoghue2020].
These multiply this NMA's surrogate effects by effect sizes from *other* studies — treat them as the
source's cited context, never as evidence that a modality prevents disease here.
[inferred from @odonoghue2020]

## Decision relevance

- **If an adult with obesity is going to exercise for body composition, combined (aerobic + resistance)
  is the best-supported choice** and low-moderate resistance *alone* the weakest. The best-supported
  part is *don't choose low-load resistance alone*; aerobic vs high-load resistance is not separated, and
  the fine order among leading arms is not established.
- **Do not prescribe exercise as a weight-loss intervention.** At \~1 kg across all modalities, the scale
  barely moves; weight loss needs a dietary energy deficit, with exercise combined in for its
  body-composition, fitness and muscle-sparing edge -> [[Exercise vs Caloric Restriction for Visceral Fat]], [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]].
- **Rank this within Layer 1.** For an inactive adult with obesity the big rock is *doing regular
  activity at all*; modality choice is a second-order refinement once that rock is being pulled
  -> [[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]]. The one place modality choice earns weight is
  where low-moderate-load resistance-only would be chosen *instead of* an aerobic-containing programme —
  the NMA ranks that the weakest option for body composition.
- **Out of scope (appraise, not prescribe):** the specific sets/reps/intensity to program, and managing
  exercise around comorbidity. For resistance-training *dose*, see [[Resistance Training Prescription - Load Sets and Frequency]]; for the muscle/strength->function->mortality case for including resistance,
  [[Muscle-Strengthening Activity and Mortality]].

## Limits

- **Single gold source, `confidence: low`.** One NMA carries the ranking; the cross-links to
  compensation, visceral fat and surrogate honesty are the wiki's weave, not replication of the ranking.
- **High risk of bias.** «more than 70% (n = 33) of the included trials were judged as being at moderate
  to high ROB ... the results of this present NMA should be interpreted in a conser­vative manner»; only
  27% used intention-to-treat [@odonoghue2020].
- **Sparse data for the winner.** Aerobic dominated; resistance and combined each contributed only
  \~10-18% per network, and no study reported COM-HI for waist circumference — so the combined-training
  advantage rests on few head-to-head comparisons and the authors warn these are less reliable.
- **Surrogate outcomes only; small absolute effects; short interventions** (mean 20 weeks, mostly
  3x45 min/week) — the ranking cannot be extrapolated beyond that studied dose or to non-obese adults.
- **The CRF ranking orders non-significant results** and must not be read as establishing a
  fitness-modality order.
