**The diseases of ageing share a modifiable core, though not every lever reaches every disease.**
Cardiovascular disease, cancer, and dementia draw much of their modifiable risk from one short list
-- body fatness, tobacco, physical inactivity, and heavy alcohol reach across all three; blood
pressure, blood glucose, and blood lipids drive cardiovascular disease and dementia but are not
established cancer levers. Where a lever does overlap, pulling it buys protection against more than
one disease at once -- but those gains sit on different outcome scales and cannot be added into a
single number.

**Physical activity is the single lever that reaches almost every disease on the list.** It is the one
exposure with credible signal against cardiovascular disease, several cancers, dementia, Parkinson's,
frailty, and falls together. It is rarely the largest effect for any one disease, but it is the widest.

**The disease-specific add-ons are mostly small, contested, or absent.** Once the shared cardiometabolic
levers are pulled, the extra items that dominate public discussion -- eggs, red meat, individual
supplements, single "superfoods" -- move little, move uncertainly, or (for one supplement) do harm.

**For some diseases the honest finding is a low ceiling.** Parkinson's has few credible modifiable
levers and mostly low-quality evidence. Several prevention bundles move a marker without yet preventing
the disease. Reporting that there is little left to optimise is itself a result.

**The ranking shifts with age, not the levers.** In midlife the cardiometabolic levers carry the risk
for later disease; in later life falls, frailty, and muscle loss become the outcomes that decide
independence. Absolute benefit rises with age because baseline risk does.

## The diseases of ageing share a common core of levers

Read across the disease-specific evidence and the same handful of exposures keeps reappearing. The
Lancet Commission on dementia estimates that 14 modifiable risk factors carry a population-attributable
fraction of 45.3% -- «nearly half of all cases of dementia being associated with 14 potentially
modifiable risk factors» [@livingston2024] -- and most of
those 14 (hypertension, diabetes, obesity, high LDL-C, smoking, physical inactivity, excess alcohol)
are the cardiometabolic big rocks already ranked for cardiovascular disease
[@livingston2024]. The cancer evidence lands the
same way: the leading diet-adjacent cancer exposures are body fatness, alcohol, and physical activity,
not specific foods [@wcrf2018ter].

This is the practical payoff of the shared core: pulling a cardiometabolic lever buys a *second* or
*third* patient-important outcome on top of the one it was ranked for. The figure is a modelled,
communality-discounted population fraction, not a per-person guarantee -- the 14 factors overlap, so
their individual attributable fractions do not sum, and the relative risks are borrowed from
observational data assumed causal [@livingston2024]. The
levers below are the shared big rocks that the intervention hierarchy ranks
([[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]]); this deliverable ranks them by cross-disease
breadth and holds the disease-specific
increments they do not cover.

## Physical activity is the widest lever across the diseases of ageing

Ranked by how many age-related diseases it touches -- not by the size of any single effect -- physical
activity is first. The evidence, disease by disease and honest about its grade:

- **Dementia:** physical activity is protective, RR 0.80 (0.77-0.84, n=257,983) in a meta-analysis the
  Commission cites [@livingston2024].
- **Cancer:** WCRF grades physical activity a *convincing* protector against colon cancer and *probable*
  for endometrial and postmenopausal breast: «the greater the amount of physical activity, the greater
  the benefit» [@wcrf2018ter].
- **Parkinson's:** the most credible protective signal in the whole PD evidence base -- RR 0.79
  (0.68-0.91), prospective, dose-dependent by intensity, though null in women
  [@chen2021pd].
- **Frailty:** physical-activity interventions in identified prefrail/frail older adults improve
  mobility, function, and frailty status at moderate certainty (below).
- **Falls, muscle, cardiovascular disease:** the physical-activity lever, detailed under
  [[Exercise for Preventing Falls in Older Adults]] -- balance-and-functional exercise is a
  HIGH-certainty lever against falls; the mortality signal is softer and observational.

Two honest limits keep this from over-selling. First, physical activity's *mortality* evidence is
observational and far softer than its RCT evidence on falls or frailty. Second, the effective *type* is
outcome-specific -- balance training prevents falls, not walking; aerobic and strength work carry the
cardiometabolic and muscle signals. "Move more" is not a single prescription
[inferred from @livingston2024; @chen2021pd; @wcrf2018ter].

## Blood pressure and the cardiometabolic cluster are the biggest shared levers by magnitude

If physical activity is the widest lever, the blood-pressure/glucose/lipid cluster is the heaviest --
it carries large, well-graded effects on both cardiovascular disease and dementia at once. In the
dementia evidence: untreated hypertension HR 1.42 (1.15-1.76), diabetes HR 1.24 (1.06-1.46) per five
years' earlier onset, and high LDL-C +8% per 1 mmol/L (1.08, 1.03-1.14), each acting in *midlife*
[@livingston2024]. These are the same exposures
that dominate cardiovascular risk, so a person who lowers midlife blood pressure or glucose is buying
down two diseases on one action.

A surrogate caution binds the lipid lever. LDL/apoB is a well-evidenced causal input to atherosclerotic
cardiovascular disease, but it is a weaker proxy for *all-cause* mortality, and a marker moving the
right way is not itself the patient-important outcome -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]],
[[LDL ApoB and Cumulative Exposure]]. The decision-relevant timing rule is the Commission's own:
«it is never too early or too late to reduce» dementia risk, but the risk is carried by *midlife*
exposure sustained -- «decrease risk factor levels early ... and keep them low throughout life»
[@livingston2024]. Late-life onset of the same conditions
carries less of the risk, partly a shorter-exposure and reverse-causation artifact.

For cardiovascular disease itself, this same list *is* the disease-specific answer: the big rocks
(blood pressure, lipids, glucose, smoking, adiposity) simply *are* the CV risk factors, so CVD adds no
separate increment beyond them. That informative null is the finding -- once the shared levers are
pulled, there is no distinct CVD lever left to hunt for.

## Weight and visceral fat drive cancer risk across a dozen sites

Body fatness is the shared lever that bites hardest on cancer. WCRF grades greater adult body fatness a
*convincing or probable* cause of many cancers and presents it first: «12 of the 17 cancers reviewed by
the CUP are linked to greater body fatness»
[@wcrf2018ter] -- oesophagus, pancreas, liver,
colorectum, kidney, endometrium, and postmenopausal breast among them, with IARC adding thyroid,
myeloma, and meningioma. WCRF warns that on current trends «overweight and obesity are likely to
overtake smoking as the number one risk factor for cancer»
[@wcrf2018ter]. Risk rises even inside the "healthy"
BMI range, so the exposure is graded, not a threshold at BMI 25
[@wcrf2018ter].

Two caveats keep the lever honest. The mechanism (hyperinsulinaemia, sex hormones, chronic
inflammation) informs *direction*, not proof -- the grades rest on cohort epidemiology, not the
pathways [@wcrf2018ter]. And the causal grade is on
*body fatness -> incidence* in populations; whether an *individual's* weight loss reduces *their* cancer
is not established -- the survivor evidence is «limited»
[@wcrf2018ter]. The weight-management decision itself
turns on [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]].

Alcohol carries a cancer-specific feature the mortality axis does not: no lower threshold. Where the
all-cause-mortality harm begins at a *dose*, WCRF grades the cancer risk as having no safe floor --
«there is no level of consumption below which there is no increase in the risk of at least some
cancers» [@wcrf2018ter] -- and IARC classes
alcoholic drinks a Group 1 carcinogen. The one protective arm (kidney cancer) is instantly outweighed
by the whole-body cancer ledger: the *net* effect, not the intended one, is what decides
-> [[Alcohol and Mortality and Vascular Disease]].

## Muscle and strength are the musculoskeletal core -- measure function, not the scale

The late-life musculoskeletal decline (sarcopenia, frailty) is where the disease-specific evidence is
richest and the levers are clearest, but every one is a *predictor* not yet proven as a *target*. The
sarcopenia consensus inverts three decades of practice: «muscle strength comes to the forefront, as it
is recognised that strength is better than mass in predicting adverse outcomes»
[@cruzjentoft2019], demoting muscle mass
to a confirmatory role and physical performance to grading severity. So screen on strength (grip,
chair-stand), not the scale or the DXA number.

Mass still carries independent prognostic signal. A gold-tier meta-analysis (9 cohorts, n=10,028,
non-frail >=65) finds those who died carried \~0.18 SD less appendicular muscle mass (ASMI SMD -0.18,
-0.23 to -0.12), a link «not explained by differences in muscle strength» -- though that
strength-independence rests on an underpowered null, so it is *consistent with* an independent signal,
not proof of one [@santana2021]. The mass-mortality
link is strongest in the overweight/obese older adult (sarcopenic obesity), the stratum with no agreed
operational cut-off (a G-gap).

The levers are the ones already ranked -- resistance training plus adequate protein, and
muscle-strengthening activity -> [[Protein and Resistance Training for Muscle and Strength]],
[[Muscle-Strengthening Activity and Mortality]], [[Grip Strength and Mortality]]. The line that binds
the whole cluster: low mass and low strength *predict* death, but no RCT shows that *raising* them cuts
mortality -- the loop stays open [inferred from @santana2021; @cruzjentoft2019].
The muscle levers are worth pulling for function, independence, and sarcopenia in their own right.

Osteoporosis fits the same shape. In replete, community-dwelling adults, vitamin D (with or without
calcium) does not prevent fractures -- the repayment is confined to the deficient or institutionalized
(where calcium is the active co-ingredient) -- so the disease-specific lever for everyone else is
weight-bearing and balance exercise, not a supplement (the fracture numbers sit in the supplements
section below).

## Frailty is a lever, not a fate -- but dose the exercise down for the already-frail

Frailty is both a potent prognostic marker and a modifiable target, which is what makes identifying it
decision-changing: the identified stratum has high baseline risk *and* a lever that moves the outcome.
Physical-activity interventions in identified prefrail/frail adults produce «Significant effects were
found for mobility (standardized mean difference [SMD] 0.60, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.37 to 0.83),
activities of daily living (SMD 0.50, 95% CI 0.15 to 0.84), cognitive function (SMD 0.35, 95% CI 0.09 to
0.61), quality of life (SMD 0.60, 95% CI 0.13 to 1.07) and frailty (SMD -1.29, 95% CI -2.22 to -0.36;
risk ratio 0.58, 95% CI 0.36 to 0.93), with moderate certainty of evidence»
[@racey2021frailtypa].

Three cautions. The effective categories are muscle-strengthening and mixed (aerobic + strength), and
some frailty-reversal subgroup figures rest on single small trials -- read the tight intervals with
care. The general activity guideline does *not* transport to this stratum: it «may be too advanced or
too intense for a frailer population», so effective programmes were supervised and dosed down
[@racey2021frailtypa]. And in the
already-frail, activity did *not* significantly reduce falls -- the high-certainty fall-prevention
result is for the general older adult, before deep frailty -> [[Frailty]],
[[Exercise for Preventing Falls in Older Adults]].

## Menopause shifts which levers matter without adding new ones

Menopause is a life-stage stratum, not a disease -- it changes *which* levers matter without adding one
the wiki does not already hold. The intuitive story ("menopause makes women gain weight") is the wrong
emphasis: total fat gain across midlife tracks aging, «with menopause having no significant additional
influence» on the *quantity* [@ambikairajah2019].
What more likely shifts is fat *distribution* -- centrally and viscerally -- but the source hedges this
as a «possible change», and its longitudinal estimates put the visceral-fat gain at roughly half the
cross-sectional figure [@ambikairajah2019].
Central fat is the cardiometabolic driver, so measure the waist, not the BMI, which is confounded
post-menopause by bone and muscle loss.

Hormone therapy is a symptom-and-bone treatment, not a body-composition, muscle, or anti-ageing lever.
It has no beneficial or harmful association with muscle mass, and although it «may help attenuate
abdominal adipose accumulation and weight gain associated with the menopause transition, the effect is
small» [@nams2022]. What it does well is
relieve vasomotor symptoms and reduce fracture. Formulation is a real lever: estrogen-plus-progestin
carries a breast-cancer signal that estrogen-alone does not, and oral (not transdermal) oestrogen
carries the stroke signal -> [[Hormone Therapy After Menopause]]. Two gold guideline bodies (NAMS,
NICE) disagree on whether early initiation adds a cardiovascular/mortality benefit -- but both agree
HRT does *not* change all-cause mortality, so the decision rests on symptom relief and bone against a
rare harm, not on a longevity gain [inferred from @nams2022].

## The dementia-prevention bundle moves the marker but has not yet prevented dementia

The most-studied dementia-prevention strategy is a multidomain lifestyle bundle (diet, exercise,
cognitive training, vascular monitoring), and its evidence is a cautionary tale about surrogates. FINGER
(n=1260, at-risk elderly) moved a cognitive-*composite* surrogate by a real but small amount
(between-group 0.022/year, Cohen's d 0.13, p=0.030)
[@ngandu2015]. MAPT, testing the same kind
of surrogate with a similar bundle, was *null* at the whole-population level
[@andrieu2017]. preDIVA, the one trial to
measure the *hard* endpoint over 6.7 years, found no reduction in dementia incidence (HR 0.92,
0.71-1.19) [@mollvancharante2016].

The responder hope -- "it works if you target the high-risk" -- was tested directly and failed on the
hard endpoint. Pooling the MAPT + preDIVA individual-participant data (n=5205, up to 12 years), the
intervention had no effect on all-cause dementia (HR 0.98, 0.80-1.21), with no effect in any of 11
pre-specified subgroups and none found by a data-driven search
[@coley2025]. The authors' reading:
«the potential impact of multidomain interventions for dementia prevention at the individual level, even
among those at greatest risk of dementia, appears modest at best»
[@coley2025].

The decision-change: the bundle's levers are the cardiometabolic big rocks worth pulling for their
*proven* outcomes -- do not promise a person a cognitive or dementia payoff on top
-> [[Multidomain Lifestyle Intervention and Cognitive Decline]]. The Mediterranean-diet -> Alzheimer's
association (RR 0.89, 0.84-0.93 per adherence point) is observational and probably substantially
mediated by those same cardiometabolic levers, not additive to them
[@garciacasares2021].

## Hearing, vision and head injury are the dementia-specific levers worth naming

Beyond the shared cardiometabolic core, dementia carries specific modifiable levers the other diseases
do not. The largest is hearing: hearing loss is associated with dementia (meta-analysis HR 1.37,
1.00-1.87 -- the lower bound touching the null), and correction is available for most cases, so uptake
is the gap. But the one hearing-aid RCT (ACHIEVE) found «no overall effect on the primary outcome,
cognition at three-year follow-up (difference -0·002, -0·08 - 0·08)»; a pre-specified analysis found a
benefit only in the higher-risk ARIC subgroup (difference 0·19, 0·02-0·36)
[@livingston2024] -> [[Hearing Loss and Dementia]].
Uncorrected vision impairment (RR 1.47, 1.36-1.60) and traumatic brain injury (RR 1.66, 1.42-1.93) are
the other two -- one correctable late in life, one preventable
[@livingston2024]. These are genuine
disease-specific increments, not the shared big rocks under another name.

## Parkinson's sits at the thin end -- few credible levers, mostly weak evidence

Parkinson's is the deliberate contrast to dementia: it has no comparable attributable-fraction estimate
and far weaker evidence. In the umbrella review of 46 systematic reviews, «none of the included SRs were
judged to be of high or moderate quality», and 39 of 46 (\~85%) were «of critically low methodological
quality»
[@chen2021pd]. Of 50 statistically
significant associations, «only 19 of these were supported by evidence from prospective studies»
[@chen2021pd].

The one credible, shared lever is physical activity (RR 0.79, above). Several headline *protective*
signals are artifact-suspect: PD's years-to-decades prodrome lowers activity, coffee, and smoking
*before* diagnosis, manufacturing protective associations, and the protective-drug signals (statins,
CCBs) are confounded by indication [@chen2021pd]
-> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]]. The one dietary *risk* worth naming is dairy/milk (RR
\~1.4-1.6), of uncertain mechanism. The honest finding is a low ceiling: little to optimise, and the
most-discussed factor (pesticides/occupational exposure) rests largely on bias-prone case-control data
[@chen2021pd].

Named gaps (acquisition-gated, so the leg is stated at the strength held): the *ranked* Parkinson's
modifiable-risk menu and the physical-activity effect-size meta-analysis are not yet held, so no ranked
menu is inferred here; and PD's absence of any population-attributable or absolute-risk framing is a
structural limit -- the reverse-causation arms need Mendelian-randomization evidence the corpus does not
yet hold [inferred from @chen2021pd].

## Supplements are a near-null lever for the well-nourished -- and one does harm

For a generally-nourished adult, supplementing vitamins, minerals, multivitamins, or marine omega-3 to
prevent cardiovascular disease, cancer, fractures, or death is a null lever -- and for beta carotene a
harmful one. USPSTF recommends *against* beta carotene and vitamin E (grade D), and finds the evidence
*insufficient* for multivitamins and single nutrients (grade I)
[@uspstf2022supp]. The harm is real: two
trials in smokers or asbestos-exposed workers found «a significantly increased risk of lung cancer with
beta carotene supplementation (risk ratio [RR], 1.18 [95% CI, 1.03-1.36])», and a pooled analysis
showed increased cardiovascular-disease mortality
[@uspstf2022supp]. This is the
*more-is-better* fallacy realised on a hard outcome -> [[Vitamin and Mineral Supplements for Disease Prevention]].

The load-bearing distinction is enhancement-in-the-replete versus correcting a deficiency -- two
different exposures. The nulls above are all *adding* a supplement to already-adequate people; a
confirmed-deficient stratum is a separate question with real repletion benefits (vitamin D on
respiratory infection, iron and iodine on cognition), and periconceptional folate cleanly prevents
neural-tube defects. So supplements are not uniformly a distraction -- they are a near-null *for the
well-nourished adult on chronic-disease endpoints* -> [[Deficiency Repletion vs Enhancement]].

## Eggs and red meat are small or contested disease-specific items

The two most-discussed diet-specific items for cardiovascular and cancer risk are small-effect cases,
and the attention they attract is an anti-signal. Eggs are near a non-lever for the general population:
the largest dose-response meta-analysis (\~2 million people) lands on «There is no conclusive evidence on
the role of egg in CVD risk», every general-population estimate within a few percent of 1.00 at GRADE
low [@godos2020]. Two stratified
exceptions: in *diabetic* cohorts the direction reverses (CVD SRR 1.22, 1.08-1.39 at \~1 egg/day, low
certainty), and heart failure shows a monotone risk signal at high habitual intake
[@godos2020].

Red and processed meat is a modest colorectal-cancer signal read oppositely by two guideline families.
WCRF grades processed meat a *convincing* and red meat a *probable* cause of colorectal cancer; NutriRECS
grades the same cohort evidence low-to-very-low certainty and re-pools it into absolute terms -- a handful
of fewer cancers per 1000 over a lifetime for a realistic 3-servings/week reduction
[@wcrf2018].
The disagreement is about grading and standpoint, not the numbers -> [[Should Adults Reduce Red and Processed Meat]].
Red meat is otherwise a shared big-rock question treated in depth under the Big Rocks deliverables.

## The intervention hierarchy shifts by age stratum, not the levers

The levers do not change with age; their ranking does, and it is driven by two facts
[inferred from @livingston2024; @mollvancharante2016; @santana2021].

- **Midlife is when the cardiometabolic levers carry the future-disease risk.** Dementia's vascular
  risk factors are *midlife* exposures; late-life vascular care may simply come too late -- one reason
  preDIVA (unselected 70-78-year-olds) was null. So a younger adult's highest-value move is to pull the
  shared big rocks early and keep them low, banking a benefit that compounds over decades.
- **Later life is when falls, frailty, sarcopenia, and osteoporosis become the deciding outcomes.**
  These are function-and-independence endpoints, not length ones, and they carry their own strong
  late-life levers (balance exercise, resistance training, adequate protein). The shared cardiometabolic
  levers still apply, but their marginal *cognitive/CV* payoff from late initiation shrinks.
- **Absolute benefit rises with age because baseline risk does (route a).** The relative effect of a
  working lever is roughly constant, so the same intervention averts more events per person in a higher-
  risk older stratum -- legitimate stratification that needs no subgroup claim
  -> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]].

## Length and function are separate axes -- do not collapse them

The diseases of ageing are measured mostly as death and disease *events*, but the outcome many people
weight most heavily is the *trajectory*: full function then a rapid end (compressed morbidity) versus a
long decline. Falls, mobility, cognition, and independence are patient-important outcomes in their own
right -- often critical-rated, and for many people weighted above a marginal change in length of life.
The healthier-life finding in dementia is partly a *trajectory* one: it pushes onset back further than
life expectancy extends, compressing the years lived with dementia
[@livingston2024]. Trials rarely measure the trajectory, so a
clean mortality or incidence result can be silent on the outcome a person values
[inferred from @livingston2024]. This deliverable names which outcomes each lever
moves; the weighting of length against function stays the person's.

## What this evidence still cannot tell you

The shared-core framing hides three real limits, and naming them is itself a result.

- **The cross-disease overlap is co-occurring separate effects, not one poolable number.** Blood-pressure
  lowering against cardiovascular disease, visceral fat against cancer, and the dementia bundle against
  cognition are each estimated on their own outcome scale -- a cardiovascular hazard ratio, a cancer
  relative risk, a cognitive marker. The wiki holds no common metric to add them into a single
  "diseases of ageing" effect, and none of the sources supplies one, so "one lever, several diseases"
  is a statement about *which* outcomes move, never a summed total.
- **The mechanism behind the shared core is not established here.** That the same exposures track
  several diseases is epidemiological co-membership -- evidence they cluster, not a tested common
  pathway. The geroscience claim that a few ageing "hallmarks" are the shared upstream cause is a
  hypothesis this corpus does not hold, and must not be read into the overlap.
- **Several of the largest post-tobacco modifiable cancer causes sit outside this corpus** -- ultraviolet
  and solar exposure (skin cancer), and the oncogenic infections HPV (cervical and other sites),
  hepatitis B and C (liver), and *H. pylori* (stomach). They are modifiable and large, but the wiki
  holds no source on them; their absence here is a coverage gap, not a finding that they are small
.

## The bottom line

- **Pull the shared cardiometabolic levers first** -- blood pressure, body fat/visceral adiposity,
  blood glucose, blood lipids, not smoking, and holding alcohol down. Blood pressure, glucose and lipids
  buy risk reduction across cardiovascular disease and dementia; body fat, tobacco and alcohol reach
  several cancers too. Midlife is when they matter most.
- **Make physical activity the default lever** -- it is the one exposure with credible signal against
  the widest set of these diseases. Match the *type* to the target (balance for falls, strength for
  muscle, aerobic/mixed for cardiometabolic risk); "move more" alone is underspecified.
- **In later life, screen muscle *function* (strength, gait), not the scale**, and train resistance +
  adequate protein; identify frailty early and dose its exercise down.
- **Do not spend attention on eggs, red meat, or a supplement stack** for a well-nourished person --
  they are small, contested, or null, and beta carotene does harm. Correct a *confirmed* deficiency; do
  not supplement to enhance the replete.
- **Treat several "prevention" bundles as unproven on the disease** -- the multidomain dementia bundle
  moves a marker, not yet dementia incidence; pull its levers for their proven cardiometabolic outcomes,
  not for a promised cognitive payoff.
- **Menopause: watch the waist, not the BMI**; use hormone therapy for symptoms and bone, not as a
  body-composition or anti-ageing lever, and weigh formulation.

### Caveats that bind every claim above

- **The loop is open.** This deliverable grades coherence and source-fidelity, never validity. No
  operation here checks any lever against a realized outcome; a clean audit is not a validated
  recommendation.
- **It appraises evidence; it does not prescribe.** Selecting, dosing, screening contraindications, and
  managing interactions are prescriber acts needing this person's labs, history, and medications.
- **Estimates are population-level by default; stratify per person.** Personalize beyond the population
  estimate only on positive effect-modification evidence (as in the diabetic-egg reversal), not on
  mechanistic plausibility. Route-(a) baseline-risk stratification -- scaling absolute benefit by age or
  risk -- needs no subgroup claim.
- **One health axis only.** This holds a health axis (longevity, function, absence of deficiency). It
  carries no environmental, cost, or ethical axis and does not price one; where such a trade-off exists
  it is named, never netted against the health finding.
