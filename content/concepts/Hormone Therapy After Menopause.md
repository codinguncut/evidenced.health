HRT is the **realistic pharmacotherapy comparator** for the menopause stratum — the drug against which the
lifestyle levers (-> [[Menopause and the Shifting Levers]]) are judged. The wiki appraises what it does; it
does not prescribe it. The whole page turns on one distinction the popular *is HRT safe?* question hides:
**the answer depends on *which outcome*, *which formulation*, and *when it is started* — and two gold
guideline bodies reading overlapping but differently-filtered evidence diverge on the last (NAMS: benefit;
NICE: no age variation in mortality, too little data to judge CHD timing).** (corrected 2026-10-22: was
*disagree on the last*; revised again 2026-10-22: was *a largely shared evidence base* and *NICE: not shown,
underpowered* for both outcomes — NICE invokes lack of statistical power only for CHD timing, and excluded
whole reviews NAMS cites; see the tension section)



**The reframing that reorganizes everything.** The 2002 WHI (the trial that made *HRT is risky* common
knowledge) enrolled women of **mean age \~63 (63.2 E+P, 63.3 placebo), «mostly healthy», testing one oral
regimen** [@rossouw2002], with «limited
enrollment of women with both- ersome vasomotor symptoms (VMS; hot flashes, night sweats) who were aged
younger than 60 years or who were fewer than 10 years from menopause onset»
[@nams2022] (corrected 2026-10-22: the quote
stopped at *vasomotor symptoms*, dropping the age/time scope), and its own verdict was scoped to
*primary prevention of chronic disease*:
> «the results indicate that this regimen should not be initiated or continued for primary prevention of CHD.»
> [@rossouw2002]

(corrected 2026-10-22: was *mostly asymptomatic* — not in the WHI text, which says «mostly healthy»; the
quote is re-lifted from the abstract, which has no *the* before *primary prevention*)

It explicitly did **not** test the use HRT is now primarily indicated for:
> «This trial did not address the short-term risks and benefits of hormones given for the treatment of
> menopausal symptoms.»
> [@rossouw2002]

So *WHI showed HRT is harmful* is an over-generalization from an older, mostly healthy population with few
young symptomatic women, and one formulation, to a younger, symptomatic one and all formulations.
(corrected 2026-10-22: was *largely non-symptomatic population* — NAMS says enrollment of symptomatic women
<60 / <10 y was limited, not that the cohort was non-symptomatic.) NAMS names exactly this gap: WHI had
«limited enrollment of women with bothersome vasomotor symptoms ... who were aged younger than 60 years or
who were fewer than 10 years from menopause onset—the group of women for whom hormone therapy is currently
primarily indicated.» [@nams2022]


## The hard-outcome base — WHI, in absolute terms

[@rossouw2002]
The one large hard-outcome RCT. Continuous oral CEE 0.625 mg + MPA 2.5 mg vs placebo, 16,608 women 50-79,
stopped at mean 5.2 y. State it in **absolute** terms — the decision unit (-> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]):

> «Absolute excess risks per 10000 person-years attributable to estrogen plus progestin were 7 more CHD
> events, 8 more strokes, 8 more PEs, and 8 more invasive breast cancers, while absolute risk reductions
> per 10000 person-years were 6 fewer colorectal cancers and 5 fewer hip fractures. The absolute excess
> risk of events included in the global index was 19 per 10000 person-years.»
> [@rossouw2002]

Relative: CHD HR 1.29 (1.02-1.63), stroke 1.41 (1.07-1.85), PE 2.13 (1.39-3.25), breast cancer 1.26
(1.00-1.59), hip fracture 0.66 (0.45-0.98); **total mortality 0.98 (0.82-1.18) — unaffected.**
[@rossouw2002] The excesses are rare in
absolute terms (all <10/10,000 py individually), and mostly appeared or grew over the trial (CHD/PE early,
stroke from year 2, breast cancer after \~4 y).


## *HRT* is two different exposures — E+P vs E-alone (parameter table)

The single most decision-relevant distinction, hidden by the umbrella term. Estrogen-progestin (EPT, for
women with a uterus) and estrogen-alone (ET, after hysterectomy) diverge on the outcome that drove the WHI
alarm — **in the WHI RCTs, breast cancer runs in opposite directions** (observational data differ; see below).

| Parameter (breast cancer) | Estrogen + progestin (EPT) | Estrogen-alone (ET) | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| WHI intervention-phase effect | **increased**, +9 cases/10,000 py | **nonsignificant reduction**, 7 fewer/10,000 py; HR 0.79 (0.61-1.02) | **No — opposite sign (ET NS)** |
| At \~20 y cumulative follow-up | HR 1.28 (1.13-1.45), sustained | HR 0.78 (0.65-0.93), significant reduction | **No** |
| Breast-cancer mortality (20 y) | HR 1.35 (0.94-1.95), NS | **HR 0.60 (0.37-0.97), reduced** | **No** |
| Endometrial protection | the reason progestogen is added | N/A (no uterus) | different indication |

[@nams2022] (corrected 2026-10-22: ET intervention-phase
effect was «a nonsignificant reduction in breast cancer risk», not *reduced*; all table values are in chunk 02, not 01)
Source: «After 20 years of median cumulative follow-up, CEE alone was associated with significantly lower
breast cancer incidence (HR, 0.78; 95% CI, 0.65-0.93) and breast cancer mortality (HR, 0.60; 95% CI,
0.37-0.97) ... In contrast, CEE plus MPA was associated with significantly higher breast cancer incidence
(HR, 1.28 ...) but no significant difference in breast cancer mortality»
[@nams2022]. **So in the WHI RCTs the breast-cancer
excess appears only in the trial whose arm contained the progestogen** — a between-trial contrast (hysterectomized
vs intact-uterus populations, different durations), so it does not isolate the progestogen as the cause
; NAMS itself hedges: «Potential differences of the effects of ET, EPT, and CEE plus BZA on breast
tissue may exist.» [@nams2022] (corrected 2026-10-22:
was *the progestogen, not the estrogen, carries the breast-cancer signal*, untagged between NAMS tags). The
contrast is still one the undifferentiated *HRT* hides. It is
RCT-scoped: in pooled observational data (Collaborative Group, starters aged 45-54) ET risk also rises with
duration — «for ET, the HRs increased from 1.23 (95% CI, 1.11-1.35) for 1 to 4 years of use, to 1.29 (95% CI,
1.21-1.37) for 5 to 9 years, to 1.44 (95% CI, 1.35-1.53) for 10 to 14 years, and to 1.61 (95% CI, 1.49-1.74)
for 15 or more years» [@nams2022] — and NAMS's own
key point is that the effect «may de- pend on the type of hormone therapy, duration of use, regi- men, prior
exposure, and individual characteristics. (Level II)»
[@nams2022]. NICE: «There is very little or no increase in breast cancer risk with oestrogen-only HRT.»
[@niceng23]. Net reading for ET: **small or
no increase (RCT: possibly reduced)**; EPT carries the larger signal in both designs. NICE
concurs on direction: breast-cancer incidence «consistently greater with combined HRT than with oestrogen-only
HRT» [@niceng23] (shared evidence -> F, not
independent E). (corrected 2026-10-22: the opposite-sign claim was stated unscoped; it holds only for the WHI
RCT, and observational data show an ET duration gradient)

**Absolute size, in perspective (EPT, WHI, mean age 63):** «less than one additional case of breast cancer
diagnosed per 1,000 users annually ... similar to the risk reported with obesity and low physical
activity» [@nams2022] — and duration-dependent
in **observational** data on starters aged 45-54 (EPT HR 1.66 at 1-4 y rising to 2.68 at 15+ y; ET 1.23 -> 1.61
over the same bands) [@nams2022]. (corrected
2026-10-22: the 1.66 -> 2.68 trend is Collaborative Group observational data in starters aged 45-54, not the WHI
mean-age-63 EPT arm.) NICE adds it «started increasing in the first year of use» and persists >=10 y after
stopping. [@niceng23]


## The four evidence-states, by outcome

[inferred from @rossouw2002; @nams2022; @niceng23]
Kept distinct: benefit · harm · no meaningful effect · insufficient/contested. This is the honest menu the
*safe or not?* question collapses.

| Outcome | State | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Vasomotor symptoms** | **BENEFIT** (large) | HT «reduce[s] weekly symptom frequency by 75%» (NAMS); NICE «Offer HRT to people with vasomotor symptoms» — the gold-standard indication |
| **Bone / fracture** | **BENEFIT** | HT reduces fracture in healthy postmenopausal women (NAMS Level I); WHI 6 fewer hip + 6 fewer vertebral/10,000 (all ages; **null for hip fracture in the 50-59 subset** — «neither CEE plus MPA nor CEE alone was associated with decreased risk of hip fracture», attributed partly to lower baseline risk); benefit abates on stopping — BMD benefits «abate rapidly when treatment is discontinued» (BMD back to pretreatment within 1-2 y) and «there was a return of fracture risk to levels seen in women who had received placebo, with no excess fracture risk observed after discontinuation of hormone therapy» (no rebound excess) [@nams2022]; NICE: benefit «is maintained during treatment but decreases once treatment stops» [@niceng23] (corrected 2026-10-22: an earlier same-day fix read *BMD falls ... but no excess in fractures*, which overstated persistence of fracture protection; protection is lost, without rebound) |
| **Genitourinary symptoms** | **BENEFIT** (low-dose vaginal) | vaginal ET effective, minimal systemic absorption (NAMS) |
| **Breast cancer** | **HARM (EPT) / small-or-no increase (ET; RCT: possibly reduced)** | see the E+P vs ET table above — opposite signs in the WHI RCTs; observational data show an ET duration gradient |
| **Stroke / VTE** | **HARM**, route- and timing-modified | WHI stroke HR 1.41, PE 2.13; rare in <60 initiators; NICE: combined HRT raises stroke with **oral** but not **transdermal** oestrogen |
| **CHD** | **CONTESTED** (see [[Does Timing Modify HRT Cardiovascular and Mortality Effect]]) | WHI +7/10,000 (older, mostly healthy); NAMS: reduced in <60 initiators; NICE: CHD not increased overall; timing effect not shown (underpowered) |
| **All-cause mortality / life expectancy** | **NO MEANINGFUL EFFECT overall** | WHI total mortality HR 0.98; NICE 1.6.1 «unlikely to affect life expectancy»; a 50-59 subgroup reduction is disputed ([[Does Timing Modify HRT Cardiovascular and Mortality Effect]]) |
| **Cognition / dementia** | **NO benefit; HARM if started >65** | neutral early; WHIMS +23 dementia cases/10,000 when started >65 (NAMS); NICE: «might» increase if started later |
| **Type 2 diabetes** | **BENEFIT** (not an indication) | EPT 16 fewer T2D cases/10,000 py (NAMS) — noted, not government-approved for it |


## The tension — does timing modify the cardiovascular effect? (type-D, two gold bodies)

**Filed as a standalone tension** -> [[Does Timing Modify HRT Cardiovascular and Mortality Effect]] (this
section is the nucleus's summary of it).

[inferred from @nams2022; @niceng23]
This is a genuine joined-issue: **the same question — does age / time-since-menopause modify HRT's effect
on CHD and mortality? — answered *benefit* by NAMS; by NICE, *no significant age variation* for mortality
(a joined disagreement) and *too little data to tell* for CHD timing (insufficient evidence) — two gold
guideline families reading overlapping but differently-filtered evidence.** It is the guidance-null defeated
by showing *why* they diverge, not by picking a side. (corrected 2026-10-22: was *answered oppositely*;
NICE's CHD-timing state is insufficient evidence, not no effect; revised again 2026-10-22: was *not shown:
underpowered* for both outcomes and *a largely shared trial base* — NICE's lack-of-power wording is for CHD
timing only, and NICE excluded the Boardman and Kim reviews NAMS cites, see below)

| | NAMS 2022 (endorses the window) | NICE NG23 2024 (does not endorse it) |
|---|---|---|
| CHD, <60 / <10-y initiators | **reduced** — 2015 Cochrane RR 0.52 (0.29-0.96); 2019 meta-regression OR 0.61 (95% CI 0.37-1.00), attenuated after excluding open-label trials. **Counter-passage — is this joined, or are they answering different questions?** Checked NAMS's own text: the
window rests substantially on subgroup and open-label evidence, and NAMS itself records the fragility — «the results
for CHD, cardiac mortality, and all-cause mortality were all attenuated after excluding open-label trials»
[@nams2022]. The diagnosis splits by outcome
. **For all-cause mortality** it is partly a different evidence base (guidance-difference reason
2): NICE says «The multitude of confounding factors that may affect life expectancy meant that evidence had to
be restricted to randomised controlled trials (RCTs).»
[@niceng23]; NAMS admits observational +
open-label. **For CHD timing it is partly a different evidence base too**, though not by design type: «The
committee based their recommendations on evidence from RCTs and observational studies.»
[@niceng23] But NICE's CVD review excluded,
as whole reviews, the two meta-analyses NAMS sets against each other — the Cochrane review (Boardman 2015,
NAMS ref 236: CHD RR 0.52 in <10-y initiators) and the null 2020 MA (Kim 2020, NAMS ref 248)
[@nams2022]:

> «• they combined data for participants who received combined HRT and oestrogen-only HRT (Boardman et al. 2015) or»
> [@niceng23]

> «• some individual studies included within a review did not match NICE's 2024 criteria and therefore the
> entire systematic review could not be included» (Kim 2020 and Salpeter 2006)
> [@niceng23]

The reviews were excluded as wholes; their studies were re-screened and those matching were included: «The studies included in these systematic
reviews were checked and any that matched the relevant criteria were included in NICE's 2024 evidence
review.» [@niceng23] NICE adds that the
reviews' differences did not change one conclusion: «Although there were differences observed in certain
areas, none of them challenged the overall conclusion that HRT does not increase coronary heart disease risk.»
[@niceng23] — a statement about overall CHD
risk, not about timing. A second strand sits on top: NICE looked at subgroup differences and declined because
there were «no statistically significant subgroup differences in the RCT evidence and» «inconsistent results
between different observational studies.» [@niceng23]
So on CHD two candidate mechanisms sit in the held text: (i) **inclusion criteria** — no pooling of combined
with oestrogen-only HRT, no review with any off-criteria study — which remove NAMS's favourable Cochrane
estimate and (probably) Salpeter's 2006 younger-vs-older CHD meta-analysis, NAMS ref 239 (inferred: NAMS also cites a
2006 Salpeter metabolic-syndrome meta-analysis, ref 193, but 239 fits a CVD-review exclusion); the held text gives no Kim 2020 CHD estimate (NAMS cites Kim as null
on mortality, stroke and VTE). Their effect on the timing verdict is not shown; and (ii) a
different **inferential standard for subgroup claims**, behind one of the reasons NICE gives (no significant
RCT subgroup differences; it also cites inconsistent observational results and, for its research
recommendation, too little data and «lack of statistical power») — NICE requires a significant between-subgroup
difference; NAMS accepts within-stratum estimates and meta-regression.
(corrected 2026-10-22: was *partly a different evidence base (NICE restricts to RCTs ...)* applied to the whole
tension; NICE's RCT-only restriction is scoped to mortality; also was *rests substantially on evidence NICE
excludes*. Revised again 2026-10-22: was *For CHD timing it is not a different evidence base* — NICE names
Boardman 2015 and Kim 2020 among excluded reviews. Revised a third time 2026-10-22: was *the favourable
Cochrane estimate and the null Kim estimate alike* for CHD — no Kim CHD estimate is held, and the removed
reviews lean to NAMS's side: Cochrane with a held CHD estimate, probably Salpeter cited by NAMS for the
younger-women direction with no estimate held.) On the 50-59 mortality signal the held text does **not**
show the two bodies reading the same analysis: NAMS cites the WHI E+P and ET trials **pooled** (HR 0.69, P for trend .01); NICE discusses an
**ET-only** 50-59 subgroup and reads no significant variation by age of starting. So that part is also a
difference in which analysis each body weighed (reason 2), and whether they would disagree on the *same*
WHI subgroup (reason 3) is **not established** from the held sources. (corrected 2026-10-22:
was *a genuine disagreement on the same WHI subgroups (reason 3)*; NICE's subgroup is ET-only, NAMS's figure
is pooled.) It is not a fake tension of different scopes — both answer *does starting earlier change the
CV/mortality outcome?* — but the diagnosis of the divergence rests on reason 2 for mortality and, for CHD,
on reason 2 (inclusion criteria) plus a different subgroup-inference standard.
The no-pooling rule also fits the pooled-vs-ET-only split: NAMS's HR 0.69 pools the WHI E+P and ET trials,
which NICE's criterion would not admit as one estimate, and NICE's mortality discussion is formulation-specific
(an oestrogen-only subgroup). The held text states that rule for the CVD review only, so applying it to the
mortality analysis is the wiki's reading.

**Hidden insight:** the critical-window hypothesis is **carried by subgroup and open-label-sensitive
evidence** — nominally significant in Cochrane 2015 and the pooled WHI, attenuated after excluding open-label
trials, null in a 2020 RCT MA (mortality/stroke/VTE), and with a non-significant raised WHI E+P 50-59 CHD HR.
Two candidate strands: **what it admits** — NICE's criteria drop whole reviews that pool
combined with oestrogen-only HRT (the Cochrane review) or hold any off-criteria study, which removes the
favourable Cochrane estimate (and, *if* the criteria also governed the mortality analysis, which the held
text does not state, the null Kim 2020 estimate alike; on CHD Cochrane, with a held estimate, and probably
Salpeter 2006, cited by NAMS for the younger-women direction without an estimate, both lean to NAMS's side), and leaves NAMS's 2019 meta-regression unnamed
in NICE's text; its effect on the timing verdict is not shown, and NICE gives non-significant RCT subgroup
differences, inconsistent observational evidence and, for its research recommendation, limited data as its
own reasons; and **what it requires of a subgroup** — a body
that requires a significant interaction test will not endorse it, a body that accepts within-stratum estimates
and meta-regression will. (corrected 2026-10-22: was *carried by the weaker evidence ... non-significant RCT
subgroup trends; a body that weights RCT evidence hardest will not endorse it* — two cited RCT-derived
estimates are nominally significant, and NICE read observational CHD evidence too. Revised again 2026-10-22:
was the subgroup-standard strand alone; NICE's named exclusion of the Boardman and Kim reviews adds the
inclusion-criteria strand.) This is
exactly the telos's case where
*guidance families disagree because the evidence does not determine the answer* — and the U/J-shaped-artifact
discipline applies to a subgroup signal too: NICE reads its ET-only 50-59 mortality benefit as significant
only in isolation, with «no identifiable pattern of change in risk as people aged»
[@niceng23] — on that reading a candidate
artifact, not an established effect modifier; NAMS's pooled analysis reports an age trend (P = .01), so the
age-pattern question is itself contested between the two analyses -> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]], [[Certainty of Evidence vs Strength of Recommendation]].

**Where they AGREE (shared evidence -> F):** HRT does not raise all-cause mortality overall; EPT raises
breast cancer more than ET (ET: small or no increase; corrected 2026-10-22, was *ET does not*); oral oestrogen raises stroke and transdermal does not; HRT is not indicated
for CVD or dementia prevention. Agreement across two families on shared trials strengthens the null on those
points but is **not** independent corroboration (both rest on WHI and overlapping trials; NICE excluded the
Cochrane review as a whole but re-screened its trials individually).


## The surrogate note — estrogen's BMD->fracture chain HOLDS (contrast with testosterone)

[inferred from @nams2022]
A clean contrast with the sibling page. In [[Testosterone Adiposity and Muscle]], the credentialed surrogate
inverted: testosterone raised bone density but *increased* fractures (TRAVERSE HR 1.43). With estrogen, the
BMD->fracture chain **transmits correctly** — HRT raises BMD *and* reduces fractures in the same trials
(WHI: 5 fewer hip fractures/10,000 py; NAMS Level I) — all ages pooled; in the 50-59 subset WHI showed no
hip-fracture reduction (low baseline risk; NAMS ch 01). So the lesson is not *BMD is always a bad surrogate*
but *a surrogate earns a target only if its transmission to the outcome is itself evidenced* -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]: estrogen passes that test on fracture, testosterone fails it. The BMD benefit reverses within
1-2 y of stopping HRT — a durable target requires continued exposure.


## Guideline vs underlying evidence (the R15 lens)



NAMS and NICE are **guidelines resting on trials**, not independent evidence. For an effect size, cite the
trial (WHI) not the guideline; the guidelines' value here is (a) the E+P/ET disaggregation, (b) the timing
appraisal, and (c) the *disagreement* itself. NICE makes its own appraisal method explicit (RCT-restriction
for mortality, no reviews pooling combined with oestrogen-only HRT in the CVD review, route-stratified stroke,
research recommendations where power is lacking) — a worked
[[Certainty of Evidence vs Strength of Recommendation]] instance. Which non-health objective moved either
body's framing is not visible here -> [[Which Objective Moved This Recommendation]] (not charged, only noted).


## Decision relevance



- **The question is never *is HRT safe?* but *for which outcome, which formulation, started when?*** For a
  symptomatic woman <60 or <10 y from menopause with no contraindication, both bodies support HRT for VMS
  and bone; the disputed part is a *bonus* CV/mortality benefit (NAMS) vs no age variation in mortality and
  too little data on CHD timing (NICE) — NICE finds no mortality difference and NAMS a reduction under 60, neither an increase, so the decision rests
  on symptom relief and bone against a rare breast-cancer/stroke/VTE harm, not on a life-expectancy gain.
  Bone: NAMS grades a reduced absolute fracture risk in women <60 Level I — «The absolute risks are reduced for
  all-cause mortality, fracture, diabetes mellitus (EPT and ET), and breast cancer (ET) in women aged younger
  than 60 years (Figure 1).9 (Level I)» [@nams2022]
  — while WHI showed no **hip**-fracture reduction in the 50-59 subset (low baseline risk, NAMS ch 01 — route
  (a)); the held text gives no absolute magnitude for the <60 fracture reduction, and it is not shown for hip
  fracture. (corrected 2026-10-22: added the 50-59 hip-fracture null; NICE position was *no effect*; revised
  again 2026-10-22: was *the in-stratum absolute fracture gain is small or undemonstrated* — the 50-59 null is
  hip fracture only, and NAMS reports reduced absolute fracture risk <60 at Level I)
- **Formulation is a real lever, not a detail.** ET (no uterus) carries a small-or-no breast-cancer increase
  (RCT: possibly reduced; observational: rises with duration) vs EPT's larger signal (corrected 2026-10-22:
  was *avoids*);
  transdermal oestrogen avoids the oral-oestrogen stroke signal. These change the harm side materially.
- **Age/timing shifts the harm side regardless of the CV dispute:** starting >60 / >10 y raises absolute
  CHD/stroke/VTE/dementia risk (NAMS: all four, as greater absolute risks; NICE concurs on stroke — rising with age at start for
  oral-oestrogen HRT taken 5-9 y — and on dementia as possible for combined HRT started at 65 or over,
  majority decision; NICE found no clear age-at-start CHD pattern and ties no held line
  on VTE to timing) — so *late initiation is worse* whether or not early initiation *helps*. (corrected
  2026-10-22: was *(agreed)* across all four; revised again 2026-10-22: was *NAMS only for CHD/VTE* — NAMS's
  abstract lists CHD, stroke, VTE and dementia for initiation >60 / >10 y)
- **HRT is a symptom + bone treatment, not a preventive or anti-ageing drug** — not indicated for CVD,
  dementia, or muscle -> [[Menopause and the Shifting Levers]] (the lifestyle levers).
- **Out of scope (appraise, do not prescribe):** individual candidacy, contraindication screening (prior
  breast/estrogen-sensitive cancer, VTE, liver disease), dose, route selection, monitoring — prescriber
  acts needing this person's history and labs.


## Limits and gaps



- **One hard-outcome RCT, one regimen.** WHI tested only oral CEE+MPA; transdermal/estradiol/progesterone
  hard-outcome RCT data are largely absent — most route/formulation claims are observational (`type-G`;
  NICE itself flags research needs on progestogen type, mode of administration, and timing).
- **The timing hypothesis is unresolved, not settled either way** — held as a live tension, not a finding.
  A dedicated RCT of early-initiation hard outcomes would resolve it; `AWAITS` such a source.
- **Trajectory/quality-of-life under-measured** relative to event counts (-> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]).
- **POI / premature menopause is a distinct stratum** (NAMS: WHI does not apply; HT recommended at least to
  \~age 52) — noted, not developed here.
- **The loop is open.** This grades coherence and source-fidelity, never validity; no operation here checks a
  recommendation against a realized outcome.
