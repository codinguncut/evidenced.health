## 1. Verdict first

**Dairy behaves less like one food than like a family of foods that happen to
share a name.** Cheese, butter, milk, yoghurt and cream diverge even when their
saturated-fat content is matched, so every answer below attaches to a specific
category and a specific outcome. A whole-milk finding is not a yoghurt finding,
and a butter finding is not a cheese finding.

The bottom line, category by category and endpoint by endpoint:

- On the outcomes the evidence actually reaches — heart disease, death, type-2
  diabetes and bone — dairy is a small or neutral lever once its categories are
  read apart. None of it is a big rock.
- The frightening claim that milk shortens life does not survive the confounding
  checks. It rests on one unusual population, and that same population also
  generates the opposite, protective-looking signals — one confounded cohort
  manufacturing both poles of the debate.
- Preferring low-fat over full-fat dairy is not supported by the observed
  heart-disease and mortality data. Neither is the mirror claim that full-fat
  dairy is a health food.
- Fermented dairy — cheese and yoghurt — carries the one modestly favourable
  signal, but it is small, uneven across endpoints, and fragile enough that a
  single study can erase it.
- For bone, more dairy does not track fewer fractures once the stronger study
  designs are weighted.
- Whether a food's physical structure changes what its saturated fat does is a
  mechanism worth weighing, not an established finding.
- The cancer question cannot be answered from what is held: the direction
  differs by cancer site and no usable magnitude is available. It is a gap, not
  an answer.
- The realistic decisions are category swaps against a stated alternative, not
  *dairy or no dairy*. For someone whose big levers are already handled, dairy is
  not a lever to worry about.

**The certainty floor.** Every figure in this document is an *association*, never
a demonstrated cause. The evidence is entirely observational, built on
food-frequency questionnaires, and no dairy trial has ever tracked a hard outcome
such as a heart attack, a death or a fracture — because whole dairy cannot be
blinded or held constant over the decades such an outcome takes to appear. Read
each number as *tracks with*, not *reduces* or *causes*.

To read any number at all, the first move is to split *dairy* into the categories
that behave like different foods.

## 2. The category decomposition

*Dairy* is a category whose members diverge more than the label admits, so the
organizing move is to break it along three axes before any endpoint is read
-> [[Is the Food Category Doing Any Work]].

- **Matrix-intact vs matrix-stripped** — cheese and yoghurt keep an intact food
  structure; butter and cream are the fat pulled out of that structure. The
  question is whether the intact structure changes what the fat does.
- **Fermented vs unfermented** — yoghurt, kefir and cheese are fermented; milk is
  not. The favourable signals, such as they are, sit on the fermented side
  -> [[Fermented Foods and Health]].
- **Full-fat vs low-fat** — the fat-content axis, and the fault-line that current
  guidance draws (favouring low-fat on saturated-fat logic).

**Why the split is not optional: the aggregate hides null-and-opposite cells.**
The worked case splits dairy five ways, and *total dairy* is an average over
cells that do not agree. Total dairy (per 200 g/day) is null for both all-cause
mortality (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.96-1.03) and CVD (RR 0.97, 0.91-1.02)
[@guo2017]
— but that flat headline sits on top of divergent sub-categories:

- **Milk** is null (per 244 g/day, mortality RR 1.00, 0.93-1.07) but with extreme
  heterogeneity (I2 = 97.4%) — a single confounded cohort driving the spread, not
  a stable mean -> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]].
- **High-fat and low-fat dairy are both null** for CVD (high-fat 0.93, 0.84-1.03;
  low-fat 0.98, 0.95-1.01), so the observed contrast does not endorse the low-fat
  guidance default — it finds no difference to endorse.
- **Fermented dairy and cheese** carry the only inverse signals (fermented 0.98
  per 20 g/day for mortality and CVD; cheese 0.98 per 10 g/day for CVD), and both
  vanish when that same outlier cohort is removed
  [@guo2017]
  -> [[Fermented Foods and Health]].
- **Butter** — the near-pure-fat, matrix-stripped category — is weakly *positive*
  for mortality (per 14 g/day, RR 1.01, 1.00-1.03) yet *inverse* for diabetes
  (RR 0.96, 0.93-0.99): opposite-direction cells under one word
  [@guo2017].

So *dairy* is a category whose sub-boundaries carry real, sometimes opposite
signal. Never let a butter finding read as a cheese finding, or a whole-milk
finding as a yoghurt finding.

**The matrix-vs-component question — stated here, and OPEN.** The observation that
these foods diverge at *matched* saturated-fat content is what makes the axes
matter: at equal saturated fat, cheese, butter and milk do not behave alike, so
*saturated fat* is not by itself the exposure
[@astrup2020]. That diagnostic
point is held. What is *not* held is any demonstration that the intact food
matrix is the reason: the controlled-feeding matrix-mechanism source is not in the
collection. So the **matrix is a
candidate mechanism to weigh, not a finding** — a hypothesis for why the same
saturated fat might act differently inside cheese than inside butter, unconfirmed.

The saturated-fat verdict itself is not re-derived here; it lives on
[[Saturated Fat Intake and Replacement]].

With the categories in hand, the next move is to read each patient-important
endpoint through them, one at a time.

## 3. Cardiovascular events — the crux, and it lands neutral

This is the section the whole dairy question turns on, and the answer across
\~940,000 people in two overlapping gold observational meta-analyses is
*undramatic*: total dairy is neutral for cardiovascular disease, and decomposing
dairy by fat content and by category does not rescue a signal in either
direction. Every magnitude here is *observational* — FFQ-based, healthy-user
confounded, with no hard-outcome trial — so read it as *associated with*, never
*reduces* or *causes*. The one weakly-favourable cell (fermented dairy) is small
per-unit and artifact-fragile, and the one dairy-industry-funded source that
reads an inverse signal as *beneficial* is a distinction from the neutral
finding, not a contradiction of it.

### Total dairy, milk, and fat content: null across the board (Guo)

Guo 2017 (29 cohorts, 938,465 participants) ran a per-increment dose-response and
found no association at any level that matters:

- Total dairy, per 200 g/day, tracked neither all-cause mortality (RR 0.99, 95%
  CI 0.96-1.03) nor CVD (RR 0.97, 95% CI 0.91-1.02)
  [@guo2017].
- **High-fat and low-fat dairy were both null** for CVD (high-fat 0.93
  [0.84-1.03]; low-fat 0.98 [0.95-1.01])
  [@guo2017].
  Guo ran **no low-for-high substitution model**, so this is a *wash in the
  observed contrast* — it does **not** endorse the low-fat guidance default, and
  it equally does **not** license *full-fat dairy is health food*.
- Milk specifically, per 244 g/day, was null for CVD (RR 1.01)
  [@guo2017].

### Fermented dairy: a small, cheese-driven, artifact-fragile inverse (Zhang, bounded by Guo)

Fermented dairy is the *only* category carrying an inverse CVD signal, and it is
weaker than its headline. Zhang 2019 (10 cohorts, 385,122 participants) reports
an aggregate OR of 0.83 (95% CI 0.76-0.91)
[@zhang2019] —
but *do not headline that number*, because the subgroups show it is driven by
incidence and by cheese, and dissolves on the hard endpoints:

| Fermented-dairy subgroup (Zhang) | OR (95% CI) | Significant? |
|---|---|---|
| Overall CVD | 0.83 (0.76-0.91) | yes |
| Cheese | 0.87 (0.80-0.94) | yes |
| Yogurt | 0.78 (0.67-0.89) | yes |
| CVD mortality | 0.94 (0.80-1.11) | **no** |
| Stroke | 0.87 (0.75-1.01) | **no** |
| CHD | 0.85 (0.67-1.08) | **no** |

[@zhang2019]

Two facts bound the signal further. Heterogeneity is extreme (I2 = 94.0%), so the
pooled OR describes no single population
[@zhang2019].
And Guo's *per-unit* dose-response cuts Zhang's aggregate down (type-F
attenuation, not independent corroboration — shared cohorts, different contrast):
fermented dairy is RR 0.98 (0.97-0.99) per 20 g/day, cheese RR 0.98 (0.95-1.00)
per 10 g/day, yogurt null
[@guo2017].
The inverse is *fragile*: it vanishes on leave-one-out. Guo states «the inverse
associations of fermented dairy and cheese with all-cause mortality or CVD
disappeared after removing the study of Michaelsson et al.»
[@guo2017].
So the composite verdict is *weakly, fragilely inverse — cheese/incidence-driven,
and hostage to one cohort*.

### Butter: neutral for CVD

Even the near-pure-fat, matrix-stripped category does not carry the saturated-fat
worry to a hard endpoint. A butter meta-analysis cited through Guo (per 14 g/day)
found **no** significant association with CHD, CVD, or stroke
[@guo2017].
Butter is neutral for cardiovascular events (its weak mortality and
inverse-diabetes signals are taken up in Section 4). This is a figure reported
secondhand through Guo, so weight it accordingly.

### Guo-neutral vs Mishali-inverse: a distinction, not a tension

Mishali 2019 reads a modest inverse CVD association — pooled RR 0.942 (95% CI
0.892-0.994) on a highest-vs-lowest contrast
[@mishali2019].
Set against Guo's neutral 0.97, this is a **distinction, not a tension**: two
analyses of largely the same cohorts reaching different *headlines* via different
*contrasts*, not two conflicting findings.

| Parameter | Guo 2017 | Mishali 2019 | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure contrast | per-200 g/day dose-response slope | highest-vs-lowest category | **NO** |
| Total dairy -> CVD | 0.97 (0.91-1.02), NS | 0.942 (0.892-0.994), sig | point estimates compatible; only significance differs |
| Cohort set | 29 cohorts (938k) | 13 CVD cohorts (461k), overlapping subset | **NOT independent** |

Because the two run on overlapping data, their agreement is a *re-pooling*, not
two independent routes — so it is **not** type-E independent backing, and its
numeric closeness (0.97 vs 0.94) is a coincidence of scale between
non-commensurable units, not a same-observable convergence. Mishali is also
Israel-Dairy-Board-funded and does not stratify by fat content, so its inverse
reading is discounted, not deleted.

**Bridge to Section 4.** The same neutrality that holds for cardiovascular
*events* holds for *death* — which is exactly where the scariest public dairy
claim lives.

## 4. All-cause mortality and the milk-mortality artifact

The public fear attached to dairy — and to milk in particular — is a mortality
fear: that heavy milk drinking shortens life. The held evidence does not support
it. Total dairy and milk are null for all-cause mortality, and the one
frightening signal in the literature is a **single-cohort confounding artifact**
that manufactures *both* poles of the dairy debate at once. As in Section 3,
every figure is observational and absolute risks are unrecoverable.

### Total dairy and milk: null for death

- Total dairy, per 200 g/day, was not associated with all-cause mortality: RR
  0.99 (95% CI 0.96-1.03)
  [@guo2017].
  Guo's own summary of the pooled picture is «neutral associations between dairy
  products and cardiovascular and all-cause mortality»
  [@guo2017].
- Milk, per 244 g/day, was null for mortality (RR 1.00, 0.93-1.07) — but with
  extreme heterogeneity, I2 = 97.4%
  [@guo2017].
  That heterogeneity is the tell, and the next subsection is what it points to.

### The milk-mortality scare is a single-cohort confounding artifact

The *high milk drinking doubles mortality* claim comes from the Swedish cohorts,
where «higher milk consumption was associated with a doubling of mortality risk
including CVD mortality in the cohort of women»
[@guo2017].
Run the U-shaped-artifact checks -> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]], and the
signal fails every one:

- **Pooled estimate null.** Across all cohorts, milk -> mortality is RR 1.00; the
  Swedish cohort contributes only heterogeneity. Removing it drops I2 from 97.4%
  to 70.1% and leaves RR 0.99 (0.96-1.01)
  [@guo2017].
- **Lifestyle confounding named in-source.** The high-milk Swedish drinkers had
  low education, and «the highest milk drinkers had highest percentage of smokers
  and those living alone»
  [@guo2017]
  — the classic healthy-user (here, unhealthy-user) confounding structure.
- **The same cohort drives both poles.** The one confounded outlier that produces
  the milk-*harm* signal is also what produces the protective
  *fermented-dairy/cheese* signal of Section 3: «the inverse associations of
  fermented dairy and cheese with all-cause mortality or CVD disappeared after
  removing the study of Michaelsson et al.»
  [@guo2017].
  One cohort manufacturing both a harm and a benefit is about as clean a
  demonstration as one gets that neither has diagnostic value.
- **Collapses on leave-one-out.** The whole signal is a single-study artifact,
  adjudicated only by leave-one-out sensitivity plus Guo's confounder narrative.

*The reverse-causation and confounding reasoning is Guo's and the wiki's; the
artifact verdict does not rest on any mechanistic story about milk itself.*
Adjudication here is **weak** — there is no Mendelian-randomization or genetic
instrument in-source — so the arm is *dismissed as unsupported*, not positively
refuted. Either way, it is not a decision-change: milk is not a mortality lever.

### Butter: a weak positive for mortality

Butter is the one category with a non-neutral mortality figure, and it runs
slightly the *wrong* way: the butter meta-analysis cited through Guo reports RR
1.01 (95% CI 1.00-1.03) per 14 g/day for all-cause mortality, alongside an
*inverse* association with diabetes (RR 0.96, 0.93-0.99)
[@guo2017].
So the near-pure-fat category is at worst *weakly positive* for death and neutral
for CVD — a long way from the saturated-fat-in-butter alarm, and again a
secondhand figure through Guo.

### What we cannot recover: absolute risk

Every estimate above is a relative risk or odds ratio with no baseline event rate
attached, so the decision-relevant magnitude — the absolute risk difference —
**cannot be reconstructed** from what is held
-> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]. And the exposure itself is
self-reported dietary intake, whose measurement error is the binding constraint
on any dairy dose-response -> [[Measurement Error in Dietary Assessment]]: a null
gradient is weak evidence of *no* gradient, and the confounders that manufacture
the milk artifact are themselves measured with error. The honest statement is
*neutral, and imprecise about how neutral*.

**Bridge to type-2 diabetes.** Mortality and cardiovascular events both land
neutral. Diabetes is the one cardiometabolic endpoint where dairy tracks a modest
inverse association — and where that signal concentrates in a single subgroup.

## 5. Type-2 diabetes

Dairy tracks a **modest inverse association** with type-2 diabetes. Across 16
prospective cohorts (545,677 people), Mishali 2019 pooled a highest-vs-lowest RR
of 0.897 (95% CI 0.834-0.963)
[@mishali2019]
— about 10% lower risk in the highest dairy-intake band. All observational; no
hard-outcome RCT of dairy food on diabetes incidence exists, so read this as
*associated*, not *reduces*.

The paper's distinctive move is that **the signal is concentrated in women**. The
women-only T2D estimate is RR 0.868 (\~13% lower), while the pooled estimate for
men was not significant
[@mishali2019].

### Hold the sex split as hypothesis-generating, not a stratum rule

The women-only effect is a **route-(b) effect-modification claim** — the relative
effect itself differs by stratum — and route (b) is the false-positive-prone
route, the one that needs positive interaction evidence rather than mechanistic
plausibility. Here there is *no identified mechanism*: Mishali's candidate
moderators (menopause-age, region) came back null. So the sex difference is
**hypothesis-generating**, not an established basis for sex-specific advice. It
would need replication before it could move a recommendation.

### Two discounts, kept as labels not deletions

Symmetric standards apply, in both directions. Mishali is **financed by the
Israel Dairy Board, with all four authors financially tied to it (two are
consultants, one its Chief Health Officer, one paid by it)**
[@mishali2019],
and it **does not stratify by fat content** while leaning on an *exonerates-fat*
framing — a sponsor/halo tell and a directional-bias tell. That discounts the
effect estimate; it does **not** license deleting the finding (a labelled
weak/conflicted source is a legitimate, weighted input, not a silent zero).

### Butter is category-specific — an inverse diabetes signal too

Even the near-pure-fat, matrix-stripped category does not carry a diabetes
penalty. Guo reports a cited butter meta-analysis in which, per 14 g/day, butter
is *inversely* associated with diabetes at RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.93-0.99)
[@guo2017]
— a secondhand figure, but one that runs against the saturated-fat-in-butter
worry at the whole-food level. This is category-specificity in action: *dairy* is
a type-B label, and a butter result is not a milk result.

### A named gap

The **fermented-dairy -> T2D dose-response** is not held: the shape of the curve
(and any knee) cannot be read off the highest-vs-lowest contrasts above.

**Bridge to bone.** Diabetes is an endpoint where the association is at least
directionally consistent across the observational designs. Bone is where that
stops being true — there, *study design flips the sign*, and the flip is the
whole finding.

## 6. Bone and fracture

This is the crux endpoint, because the intuitive story fails on its own evidence.
*Dairy is rich in calcium and protein, so more dairy = stronger bones = fewer
fractures* is **not supported once studies are weighted by design.**

### Design-discordance is the whole finding

Malmir 2019 (34 studies, \~616k people for fracture) shows the same exposure
looking protective or null **depending on study design**
[@malmir2019]:

- **Total dairy -> osteoporosis:** 0.63 (0.55-0.73), significant, in
  cross-sectional and case-control studies; **0.82 (0.56-1.18), null**, in
  prospective cohorts.
- **Milk -> hip fracture:** 0.75 (0.57-0.99), significant (\~25% lower), in
  cross-sectional and case-control studies; **0.93 (0.75-1.15), null**, in
  cohorts — and the per-200 g meta-regression even trends to harm at RR 1.09
  (1.07-1.11).

**Design-discordance** — protective in cross-sectional/case-control, null in
prospective cohorts — is the reverse-causation signature: osteoporosis or
fracture may itself *lower* dairy intake, or recall may differ by disease status,
so the weak-design protective signal is the artifact-suspect one. Malmir
adjudicates by **design hierarchy**: *findings from cohort studies are closer to
the causal associations than those from cross-sectional and case-control studies*
[@malmir2019].
His overall verdict is that **a greater intake of milk and dairy products was not
associated with a lower risk of osteoporosis and hip fracture**
[@malmir2019].

The per-gram milk *harm* is the **same Michaelsson-driven Swedish cohort** that
manufactures the milk-mortality scare on the cardiometabolic side — the pooled
milk-cohort RR is itself null (0.93). Read the +9%/200 g signal as **confounded
and unadjudicated, not as evidence milk breaks bones**. The safe reading is
null-to-unproven, in both directions: *dairy is not a fracture-prevention lever.*

### The food-null and the supplement-null converge (independent routes)

The dairy-**food** fracture-null does not stand alone. It converges with the
supplemental-calcium/vitamin-D fracture-null in the replete adult
([[Vitamin D and Calcium Supplementation for Fracture Prevention]]: no fracture
benefit in community-dwelling, non-deficient adults). The two routes share **no
trials, no research group, no lineage**, and neither cites the other — Malmir's
author list (Malmir, Larijani, Esmaillzadeh) is disjoint from the supplement
evidence's, so the independence is genuine. `[E-independent]` on the **null
direction only** (the magnitudes and exposures differ — whole food matrix vs
isolate, observational vs RCT — so this is convergence on the shared null, not a
point-estimate agreement).

This **closes an escape hatch**. One could argue *supplements are the wrong form;
real dairy food, with its matrix, would protect bone*. Malmir shows dairy **food**
does not protect either, in cohorts — so both nutritional forms of *more
calcium/vitamin D* land on the same null for the enhancement stratum.

### The deficient arm, and the real lever, are held separate

The convergence is scoped to **enhancement** — increasing intake in the
generally-replete adult. The **deficient/osteoporotic arm is a different
question** (a route-(c) repletion effect that fires only in the genuinely
deficient stratum, where D+calcium does carry a small hip-fracture benefit).
Malmir does not screen calcium/vitamin-D status, so it cannot speak to that arm;
it stays separate.

For the generally-nourished older adult, the **better-evidenced modifiable
fracture lever is fall-prevention exercise** — balance and functional training,
high-certainty on falls with a downstream fracture signal — not the glass of milk
or the pill -> [[Exercise for Preventing Falls in Older Adults]].

**Bridge to cancer.** Bone is where design hierarchy settles the sign. Cancer is
the one endpoint the held fabric **cannot** answer — dairy runs in opposing
directions across cancer sites, and the magnitudes are not held here.

## 7. Cancer (a named gap, not an answer)

The honest answer for cancer is that *the held fabric cannot give one*. The wider
literature treats the dairy->cancer relationship as **site-specific and opposed
by site**: it is held as probable-decreased for colorectal cancer and
probable-increased for prostate cancer. But that extracted dairy->cancer evidence
is *not in this fabric*. The WCRF cancer report was ingested for the
red/processed-meat->colorectal question only; its dairy chapters were never
extracted. So there is no magnitude to state, no certainty grade to attach, and
no basis on which to net one site against the other.

Two facts hold even so, and they bound what the section can ever say.

**The two directions oppose, so they would stay un-netted regardless.** Even with
the evidence in hand — a colorectal association running one way and a prostate
association running the other — these do not collapse into a single dairy-and-
cancer verdict. *Un-netted* here means both directions are reported and no single
bottom line is drawn, because the weighting across two opposing site risks is the
person's (layer 3), not the fabric's. The rule is the one that governs any
cross-axis or cross-outcome divergence: **name the axis and stop**. So the gap is
double: the cells are *absent* from the fabric, and were they present they would
remain *opposing-and-un-netted*.

**The IGF-I mechanism is a candidate direction, not an outcome.** The usual
dairy->cancer pathway — insulin-like growth factor I — is assigned in this fabric
to dairy *specifically*, not routed through adiposity. That makes it a *candidate
mechanism*: biologically plausible, but with no held human-outcome source behind
it. A mechanism is not an outcome. It can point at a direction; it cannot supply a
magnitude, a certainty grade, or a patient-important effect. Hold it as candidate
direction only.

Resolving this is an **ingest/revisit task, not a compile task** — extract the
WCRF dairy chapters into the fabric before this section can carry any numbers.

Set cancer aside as unresolved, and ask the question the rest of the endpoints
have been circling: does *dairy* survive as its own lever at all?

## 8. Does *dairy* survive as its own lever?

Pull the threads together. Across the endpoints where the held evidence actually
reaches — cardiovascular disease, all-cause mortality, type 2 diabetes, bone — the
dairy signal is **small or null once the categories are read apart**. Milk is not
cheese is not butter is not fermented dairy, and the aggregate *total dairy*
estimate is an average over cells that do not agree; read at the category level,
the standalone effect mostly dissolves
-> [[Dairy and Cardiometabolic Health]], [[Is the Food Category Doing Any Work]].

**Where a real mechanism might still live is the matrix-vs-component question** —
does the food's intact structure change what its saturated fat does, so that the
same SFA behaves differently inside cheese than inside butter? That is the one
place a genuinely dairy-specific lever could hide. But it has **no held source**:
the food-matrix source is unheld and sits as an `[AWAITS]` item, so the matrix
reading stays a *candidate mechanism* — plausible, unbacked by a held
human-outcome source, not a finding. And the saturated-fat verdict it would lean
on is **cross-linked out**, not re-derived here
-> [[Saturated Fat Intake and Replacement]].

So the verdict is that ***dairy* mostly dissolves**, into two things that are
handled better elsewhere:

- **category swaps against a stated alternative** — the decision is *what replaces
  what*, not *dairy yes or no*; and
- **the SFA-source question**, which is answered on its own page, not inside
  *dairy*.

Neither of those is a standalone big rock. So the realistic decision is never
*dairy yes or no* but a handful of **swaps, each judged against its replacement** —
the saturated-fat-source swap, the added-sugar swap, and a marginal lean toward
fermented. That is what *dairy* reduces to once the endpoints are read apart. The
next section lays the ladder out by stratum.

## 9. Stratum ladder and the swaps that matter

**Population default — dairy is not a lever to worry about.** For an adult whose
big rocks (smoking, central obesity, inactivity, heavy alcohol, poor sleep) are
handled, dairy ranks **low**: the hard endpoints the evidence reaches are small or
null. CVD and all-cause mortality are broadly neutral across dairy's category
decomposition, the type-2-diabetes signal is modest and single-source, and the
bone/fracture endpoint is null in cohorts. None of this is a big rock — a dominant
exposure that outranks refinements — so it enters the hierarchy near the bottom
and stays there per [[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]]. *Attention
is an anti-signal* applies with force: dairy is discussed far out of proportion to
its established effect. The decision is never *dairy yes or no*; it is a set of
**swaps**, each judged against what replaces it.

### The swaps — each versus a stated alternative

- **Butter -> olive oil / non-tropical plant oil, where the saturated-fat channel
  matters.** This is a saturated-fat-source question, not a dairy question.
  Whether the swap pays, for whom, and on which outcome is settled at
  [[Saturated Fat Intake and Replacement]] — read it there; it is not re-derived
  here. At the whole-food level butter is at worst weakly-positive for mortality
  and neutral for CVD, so the swap is worth making where a stratum's total SFA
  load is the thing being managed, not as a blanket rule against butter.
- **Sweetened / flavoured dairy -> plain.** A flavoured yoghurt or a sweetened
  milk drink routes to the **added-sugar channel**, not to anything dairy is
  doing; the sugar is the exposure. Swap to plain and the dairy question and the
  sugar question separate cleanly.
- **Keep fermented (cheese, yoghurt) over unfermented, at the margin.** The only
  inverse cardiovascular associations sit in fermented dairy and cheese. The
  signal is weak and artifact-fragile, but where a choice between two dairy foods
  is already being made, fermented is the side the observational evidence leans,
  faintly.
- **Low-fat vs full-fat is a WASH on the observed heart and mortality data.** Both
  were null; the observed contrast finds no difference. So the fat level is chosen
  on **total-diet fit and calories**, not on a dairy-fat rule in either direction
  — neither a low-fat mandate nor a full-fat-is-health-food claim survives the
  pooled data.

### Strata where the picture shifts

- **The fracture-worried older adult.** Dairy is **not** the fracture lever — more
  milk or dairy to protect bone is not supported by the prospective evidence. The
  better-evidenced modifiable lever for this stratum is fall-prevention exercise
  (balance and functional training)
  -> [[Exercise for Preventing Falls in Older Adults]]. Calcium and vitamin-D
  repletion is a **separate route-c question** — a repletion effect that fires
  only in the confirmed-deficient or institutionalized stratum, not a reason for
  the replete older adult to consume more dairy.
- **The T2D-concerned adult.** The women-subgroup inverse association is
  **hypothesis-generating, not a prescription** — a single-source subgroup claim
  (route-b effect modification) with no identified mechanism, industry-funded, and
  not stratified by fat content. It does not license telling anyone to eat dairy
  to lower diabetes risk.
- **The lactose-intolerant, or by preference.** Fermented dairy and hard cheeses
  are lower in lactose and are the tolerable options. But there is **no health
  mandate to consume dairy at all**: no endpoint in the held evidence makes it a
  required food. If it is not wanted, nothing is lost by leaving it out and
  meeting protein, calcium and calories elsewhere.

**Bridge — what stays genuinely unknown.** These swaps rest on observational,
FFQ-measured evidence with no hard-outcome trial behind any of them, so the
absolute per-person effect of each is unrecoverable; whether the food matrix
changes what the saturated fat does is an open mechanism; and the whole cancer
axis — where the directions oppose — is not yet in the held fabric. Those are the
gaps §10 names honestly.

## 10. Gaps and bottom line

**Cancer is un-extracted — and the directions oppose.** The literature carries a
probable-decreased dairy -> colorectal association and a probable-increased
dairy -> prostate association, but **neither direction is in the held fabric**, no
magnitude is held for either, and the two run opposite ways and are **not netted**.
Name both, price neither; this is an ingest follow-up, not a resolved axis.

**The matrix mechanism is unheld.** Whether the food structure of cheese or
yoghurt changes what its saturated fat does to blood lipids — the dairy-matrix
hypothesis — is a **candidate**, held to weigh and not a finding, with no
controlled-feeding source in the fabric to move it from candidate to directional.

**There is no hard-outcome dairy RCT, and there structurally cannot be one.**
Whole dairy cannot be blinded over the decades a mortality or CVD-event trial
would need, so every endpoint above rests on observational cohorts with FFQ
measurement error -> [[Measurement Error in Dietary Assessment]]. Consequence that
binds: the absolute risks are largely **unrecoverable** — every figure is a
relative estimate with no stratum baseline rate attached.

**Kefir specifically is thin** — held as **insufficient-evidence, not null**. The
absence of a signal is the absence of study, not a demonstrated no-effect.

**One dose-response is owed:** — the
fermented-dairy -> type-2-diabetes dose-response that would put a shape under the
modest T2D association.

### Bottom line

Decompose before deciding: *dairy* names several near-different exposures, and
once the categories are read apart it is a **small or neutral lever** for the
adult whose big rocks are handled. The milk-mortality scare does not survive the
artifact checks — it is a single confounded cohort, not a finding. At the margin,
prefer fermented over unfermented; treat butter as a saturated-fat-source question
and settle it at [[Saturated Fat Intake and Replacement]], not here; and choose
the fat level on total-diet fit and calories, since low-fat versus full-fat is a
wash on the observed heart and mortality data. Dairy is **not a big rock**, and
the cancer question is **open**.
