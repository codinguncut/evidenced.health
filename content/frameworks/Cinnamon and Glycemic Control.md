Cinnamon **supplements** (powder or extract capsules — not the spice on food) produce **statistically
significant but small reductions in glycemic surrogates** in patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D) or
polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). The effect is largest on fasting glucose, **essentially trivial on
HbA1c (-0.10%)**, thinly-evidenced on insulin/HOMA-IR, and there is **no evidence at all on a
patient-important outcome** (CV events, complications, mortality) and **no safety appraisal** in the
source. The source's own framing is an **add-on**, not a replacement: «Cinnamon can be used as an
anti-diabetic agent and an add-on treatment to control some glycemic indices among T2D patients and
women with PCOS» [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon]. For the
decision, this is a **ceiling-is-a-finding** result — a small, uncertain, surrogate-only lever that is
dominated by established diabetes levers -> [[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]].

## The pooled estimates (umbrella MA of 11 interventional meta-analyses of RCTs)

Zarezadeh 2023 pooled **11 meta-analyses of RCTs** (7 in T2D, 3 in PCOS, 1 metabolic syndrome;
published 2008-2021), reporting both weighted (WMD) and standardized (SMD) mean differences vs
placebo/control [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon]:

| Outcome | WMD (95% CI) | SMD (95% CI) | n MAs pooled | I2 | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fasting plasma glucose | **-10.93 mg/dL** (-16.22, -5.65) | -0.86 (-1.19, -0.52) | 6 | 66% (high) | benefit (surrogate) |
| HbA1c | **-0.10%** (-0.17, -0.03) | — | 7 (8 ES) | 28% (low) | small benefit (surrogate) |
| Insulin | -2.01 IU/mL (-3.96, -0.07) | -0.61 (-0.93, -0.30) | 3 | \~0% | thin benefit |
| HOMA-IR | -0.61 (-0.91, -0.31) | -0.78 (-1.26, -0.30) | 2 | \~0% | thin benefit |

All four endpoints are **glycemic surrogates**, not patient-important outcomes -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]].
The dose ranged **0.12-14.4 g/day (median 3.76 g/day)**, duration 4-52 weeks; AMSTAR quality rated 8
of 11 MAs high, 3 low [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon].

## The FPG / HbA1c discordance is the decision-relevant signal

The FPG drop looks large (-10.93 mg/dL; SMD -0.86, a "large" standardized effect) while HbA1c — which
integrates \~3 months of glycemia and is far harder to game than a single fasting snapshot — barely
moves (-0.10%). A fasting-glucose effect that does not translate into the integrated marker is exactly
the pattern expected if the FPG signal is **transient, non-durable, measurement-inflated, or
heterogeneity-driven** rather than a real shift in chronic glycemic burden
[inferred from @zarezadeh2023cinnamon]. The high FPG heterogeneity (I2=66%) vs low
HbA1c heterogeneity (I2=28%) is consistent with this. Weight the **integrated** marker (HbA1c) over the
snapshot when they disagree -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]].

## The HbA1c benefit is fragile — most component MAs were individually null

The pooled HbA1c effect rests on a minority of the component meta-analyses. Four of the seven were
individually null and one showed the opposite sign:

- «The other four meta-analyses by Allne et al., Deyno et al., Leach et al., and Baker et al., showed
  no statistically significant associations between cinnamon supplementation and HbA1c reduction»
  [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon]; Akilen 2012 reported an
  HbA1c *increase* (Table 1) [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon].
- The pooled HbA1c effect concentrates in the **large-sample and T2D** strata: sample >=1000 -0.16%
  (-0.25, -0.06) vs <1000 -0.06% (-0.15, 0.03, **NS**); T2D -0.09% (-0.14, -0.05) vs non-T2D/PCOS
  "other" 0.01% (-0.51, 0.53, **NS**) [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon].

So even where "significant," the HbA1c effect is small and stratum-dependent — a benefit driven by a
few large T2D pools, not a robust cross-study finding.

## Umbrella non-independence — 11 MAs are not 11 independent estimates

The defining limitation of an umbrella-of-meta-analyses is that the included MAs **pool overlapping
sets of the same primary RCTs**, so the "11 meta-analyses" are not 11 independent tests — the pooled
umbrella estimate **re-counts the same underlying trials** across its inputs, and its precision is
overstated relative to a single MA of the deduplicated trial set. Volume of reviews is not
independence of evidence. The source does not flag this overlap
[inferred from @zarezadeh2023cinnamon]
-> [[Rating Certainty of Evidence]]. The insulin and HOMA-IR arms are additionally thin on their own
terms: «the included studies investigating the effects of cinnamon on insulin levels and HOMA-IR were
not sufficient» [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon].

## The exposure is an isolate, and it is under-specified

This is cinnamon **supplement** (powder/extract), not cinnamon-as-spice in a meal — a different
exposure (specify the exposure, not the label) -> [[Is the Food Category Doing Any Work]]. Worse, the
supplement itself is heterogeneous: species vary across trials (Cassia, *zeylanicum*/*verum*/Ceylon,
*aromaticum*, *burmanii*) and so does preparation:

- «the type of cinnamon and its prepration were different in the included studies, which might have
  affected the contents of active agents and their hypoglycemic activity»
  [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon];
- «it is not clear whether both the extract and powder of cinnamon are equally effective»
  [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon].

So "cinnamon lowers glucose" does not specify *which* cinnamon, in *what* form, at *what* dose — the
active-agent content is unknown and the pooled effect averages over incommensurable products.

## Dose-response — not estimable here

No dose-response curve can be read from this source: the authors «were not able to conduct subgroup
analyses according to supplementation dosage and study duration»
[@zarezadeh2023cinnamon], because the included MAs
did not report dose/duration consistently. A claimed dose-dependent insulin mechanism (Fig 6) is
asserted, not shown as an estimated curve. No **threshold, knee, plateau, or upper bound** is locatable
over the studied 0.12-14.4 g/day range; the median 3.76 g/day is a description of what was studied, not
a target. A further stated confound: baseline biomarker differences across MAs «might have diluted the
effect of cinnamon on glycemic control» [@zarezadeh2023cinnamon].

## Layer-1 sizing — a small, drug-dominated add-on

The marginal rock is small by construction. Cinnamon moves **surrogates only**, its most integrated
surrogate barely (HbA1c -0.10%), and there is **no patient-important-outcome evidence**. In the broader
diabetes domain, the dominant lifestyle and pharmacotherapy levers (intensive lifestyle modification,
metformin) carry both larger effects and hard-outcome / incidence evidence
-> [[Lifestyle vs Metformin for Diabetes Prevention]] (noting those DPP figures are *prevention*
incidence, a different quantity than cinnamon's within-patient surrogate deltas — not a numeric
comparison, a ranking one). Net of the best realistic alternative a T2D patient could reach, cinnamon
adds little; the source itself positions it as an **add-on**, not a substitute
[inferred from @zarezadeh2023cinnamon]. Compare
[[Resistant Starch and Glycemic Control]] — another small glycemic-surrogate add-on with the same
ceiling profile. **Reporting that this lever is small and uncertain is itself the decision-change**: it
licenses a reasonably-managed patient to not bother optimizing it.

## Evidence states and the open gaps (G)

- **Benefit (surrogate, moderate):** fasting glucose reduction in T2D/PCOS — but heterogeneous and not
  mirrored in HbA1c.
- **Small benefit / near-null (surrogate):** HbA1c -0.10% — statistically significant, clinically
  trivial, fragile across component MAs.
- **Insufficient evidence:** insulin, HOMA-IR (source says the studies «were not sufficient»); durable
  glycemic change; dose-response; species/preparation head-to-head.
- **Insufficient evidence — the load-bearing gap:** **no patient-important outcome** (CV events,
  microvascular complications, mortality, quality of life) is measured. The whole evidence base is
  surrogate-only.
- **Safety — unassessed here (G-gap).** The source appraises efficacy and is **silent on safety**
  (0 mentions of coumarin; "adverse"/"safety" appear only in reference titles). This matters because
  Cassia cinnamon — used in several included MAs and the common commercial form — carries **coumarin**
  (hepatotoxicity / anticoagulant interaction) concern at the \~3.76 g/day median dose. A dedicated
  adverse-events SR exists and is cited but not folded in — acquire to appraise the net effect
  before any recommendation [inferred from @zarezadeh2023cinnamon].

**The loop is open.** This page grades what the umbrella found and how confident to be in it; it does
not grade whether cinnamon-takers were better off — no operation here checks a surrogate against a
realized outcome.
