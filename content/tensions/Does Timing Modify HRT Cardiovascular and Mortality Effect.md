Orbiter-tension of [[Hormone Therapy After Menopause]] (which twice defers to "see tension" on the CHD and
mortality rows — this is that tension). **The same question — does starting HRT earlier (age <60, or <10 y
from menopause) change its effect on CHD and all-cause mortality? — gets different answers from two gold
guideline families, and the shape of the difference depends on the outcome.**

- **All-cause mortality — joined.** NAMS reports a reduction in younger initiators; NICE reports no significant
  age variation in its RCT analysis and concludes HRT is *unlikely to affect life expectancy*.
- **CHD — not benefit vs no-effect.** NAMS endorses lower CHD with younger initiation while asking for trials
  to *confirm or refute* it; NICE finds too little data to conclude on timing and makes a research
  recommendation. Both call for confirmatory trials; they differ in where they set the bar for endorsing.

The two read overlapping but differently-bounded evidence (NICE: RCT-only for mortality, RCT + observational
for CHD; NAMS: RCT + open-label + observational + meta-regression). The held NICE text does not list its
trials, but it names three reviews its CVD evidence review excluded as wholes — Boardman 2015 (the Cochrane
review; it pooled combined and oestrogen-only HRT), Kim 2020 and Salpeter 2006 (each held some off-criteria
study) — while re-screening their studies individually. Boardman and Kim are the favourable Cochrane review and
the 2020 meta-analysis NAMS cites as null on mortality, stroke and VTE (refs 236, 248); Salpeter 2006 is
probably NAMS ref 239, a meta-analysis of CHD events in younger and older women that NAMS cites, in its
block 232-242, for «fewer CVD events in younger versus older women» [@nams2022]
(that NICE's Salpeter 2006 is ref 239 is: NAMS also cites a 2006 Salpeter meta-analysis on
metabolic-syndrome components, ref 193, but 239 is the one that fits a CVD-review exclusion; counter-passage below). So overlap is
possible at the study level (matching studies were re-included; which ones is not stated) and breaks at the
level of pooled analyses. It is the guidance-null defeated by showing *why*
they disagree, not by picking a side. (corrected 2026-10-22: was *answered oppositely by two gold guideline
families reading a largely shared trial base*; on CHD NICE's position is insufficient evidence, not no
effect. Revised again 2026-10-22: was *the held NICE text does not name its trials, so the size of the
overlap is unknown* — NICE names the excluded reviews.)

[inferred from @nams2022; @niceng23]

## The two positions on the same question

**View A — NAMS 2022 endorses the window.** Younger initiation is associated with lower CHD and mortality:

> «A 2019 systematic review and meta-regression analysis of RCTs that examined the timing hypothesis of
> hormone therapy compared with controls or nonusers of hormone therapy found that younger hormone therapy
> initiation (participants aged <60 y) was associated with lower odds of CHD (OR, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.37-1.00),
> all-cause mortality (OR, 0.72; 95% CI, 0.57-0.91), and cardiac mortality (OR, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.37-1.00)»
> [@nams2022]

And in the WHI 50-59 subgroup specifically: «all-cause mortality was significantly reduced in the pooled
trials versus placebo (HR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.51-0.94)» [@nams2022].

NAMS grades the endorsement Level I in its key points, but its own Summary calls the benefit potential and
asks for trials (added 2026-10-22):

> «More clinical trial data are needed to confirm or re- fute the potential beneficial effects of hormone
> therapy on CHD and all-cause mortality when initiated in perimenopause or early postmenopause.»
> [@nams2022]

**View B — NICE NG23 2024 does not endorse it: no age effect on mortality, insufficient evidence on CHD
timing.** (corrected 2026-10-22: was *declines it*, which read NICE's CHD position as a no-effect finding.)

*Mortality.* A 50-59 mortality signal in its own RCT analysis does not survive its appraisal — but the signal
NICE discusses is the **oestrogen-only** 50-59 subgroup, not the pooled (combined + oestrogen-only) WHI 50-59
estimate NAMS reports above (corrected 2026-10-22: was *the same subgroup signal*; the held text shows two
different analyses):

> «A decrease in all-cause mortality was reported for 1 isolated subgroup (women starting oestrogen-only HRT
> aged between 50 and 59). In isolation, this was a statistically significant figure. But in a wider context,
> it cannot be interpreted as good evidence of a different effect in this group.»
> [@niceng23]

NICE's operative recommendation: «taking either combined HRT or oestrogen-only HRT is unlikely to affect
life expectancy» [@niceng23].

*CHD timing.* NICE does not find *no effect*; it finds too little data to tell, and turns the question into
a research recommendation (added 2026-10-22):

> «There was not a lot of data to check whether the following factors impact on risk of coronary heart
> disease:» [@niceng23]

> «In what little data was available, the risk did not have a clear tendency to decrease or increase with
> either of the above 2 factors (that is, with the age of the person when they start HRT or the time between
> a person's menopause and when they start HRT).»
> [@niceng23]

> «As a result, the committee decided to make a recommendation for research on the impact of timing of HRT on
> risk of coronary heart disease.» [@niceng23]

## Counter-passage — is the issue joined?

Checked NAMS's own text: on **mortality**, the window rests substantially on evidence NICE's RCT-only rule
excludes (observational data; and open-label trials enter NAMS's meta-regression — the held NICE text does
not mention open-label trials either way), and NAMS **itself records the fragility**:

> «the results for CHD, cardiac mortality, and all-cause mortality were all attenuated after excluding
> open-label trials in which the knowledge of active treatment may affect treatment options and outcomes.»
> [@nams2022]

NAMS's CV key points go further on CHD (added 2026-10-22): «Meta-analyses show a null effect of hormone
therapy on CHD after excluding open-label trials.»
[@nams2022]

(corrected 2026-10-22: was *the window rests substantially on evidence NICE excludes*, unscoped; NICE's
RCT-only restriction is stated for mortality only, and on CHD NICE admitted observational evidence — below.)

**The RCT-only meta-analyses NAMS cites split** (added 2026-10-22). A Cochrane subgroup by time since
menopause is favourable:

> «Meta-analyses of clinical outcomes A 2015 Cochrane review of RCT data found that hormone therapy initiated
> fewer than 10 years after menopause onset lowered CHD in postmenopausal women (RR, 0.52; 95% CI,
&gt; 0.29-0.96).236» [@nams2022]

> «It also found a reduction in all-cause mortality (RR, 0.70; 95%, 0.52-0.95) and no increased risk of
> stroke but an increased risk of VTE (RR, 1.74; 95% CI, 1.11-2.73).»
> [@nams2022]

The next sentence reports a null RCT meta-analysis in the same younger stratum:

> «A 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs pub- lished from 2000 to 2019 showed null effects of
> hormone therapy initiated fewer than 10 years after menopause or at an age youn- ger than 60 years on
> all-cause mortality, stroke, and VTE.248» [@nams2022]

Both meta-analyses are named by NAMS's reference list: ref 236 is the Boardman 2015 Cochrane review, ref 248
the Kim 2020 meta-analysis [@nams2022].

**On CHD, NICE admitted observational studies but excluded the Boardman, Kim and Salpeter reviews as wholes**
(added 2026-10-22; revised again 2026-10-22: was *On CHD, NICE did not exclude the wider evidence*): «The
committee based their recommendations on evidence from RCTs and observational studies.»
[@niceng23] Its CVD rationale names the
excluded reviews and the reasons:

> «• they combined data for participants who received combined HRT and oestrogen-only HRT (Boardman et al. 2015) or»
> [@niceng23]

> «• some individual studies included within a review did not match NICE's 2024 criteria and therefore the
> entire systematic review could not be included» (Kim 2020 and Salpeter 2006)
> [@niceng23]

> «The studies included in these systematic reviews were checked and any that matched the relevant criteria
> were included in NICE's 2024 evidence review.»
> [@niceng23]

NICE adds: «Although there were differences observed in certain areas, none of them challenged the overall
conclusion that HRT does not increase coronary heart disease risk.»
[@niceng23] That is about overall CHD risk,
not timing; the same passage recurs in the oestrogen-only rationale
[@niceng23]. On the time-since-menopause
subgroups: «As a result, they agreed they could not make any recommendations about the impact of either of
these factors on coronary heart disease risk in people taking HRT.»
[@niceng23] — the reasons listed being no
significant RCT subgroup differences and «• inconsistent results between different observational studies.»
[@niceng23]

And on the age gradient the two bodies cite **different analyses**. NAMS reports the pooled WHI trials with a
trend across age bands: «When data from the two WHI trials were combined and analyzed, a reduction in
all-cause mortality was shown in younger but not in older women; HRs in women aged 50 to 59 years, 60 to 69
years, and 70 to 79 years were 0.69 (95% CI, 0.51-0.94), 1.04 (95% CI, 0.87-1.25), and 1.13 (95% CI,
0.94-1.36), respectively ( Pfor trend = .01).31» [@nams2022]
NICE, reading its own RCT analysis, reports no gradient: «There was also no significant variation in this
difference depending on the age of starting HRT.» [@niceng23]
On the oestrogen-only arm — the subgroup NICE actually discusses — NAMS's CEE-alone follow-up text concedes
no significant age interaction for **overall CEE-alone mortality**: «In the 18-year intervention and
postintervention cumulative follow- up, the reduction in all-cause mortality was shown to be statistically
significant for the 50- to 59-year-old age group (HR, 0.79; 95% CI, 0.64-0.96),31 although interaction
between age and treatment was not significant.» [@nams2022]
But the same paragraph reports significant age interactions elsewhere — for MI in the 13-year follow-up:

> «Cumulative follow-up For CEE alone, in the 13-year cumulative intervention and post- intervention
> follow-up, significant age-treatment interaction was shown for MI such that only in the 50- to 59-year-old
> age group a reduction in MI risk was significant (HR, 0.60; 95% CI, 0.39- 0.91).9 Although a similar
> interaction was not significant for CHD and all-cause mortality, there was a significant reduction in CHD
> risk (HR, 0.65; 95% CI, 0.44-0.96) in this age group.»
> [@nams2022]

and in the bilateral-oophorectomy sub-analysis:

> «Additional analysis focusing on oophorectomy sta- tus in the CEE-alone trial revealed a significant
> age-treatment inter- action for all-cause mortality; younger women with BO assigned to CEE alone showed a
> significant reduction in all-cause mortality compared with placebo (HR, 0.68; 95% CI, 0.48-0.96).242»
> [@nams2022]

So NAMS does not concede an absent age interaction in general; its significant age interactions are on
another outcome (MI, in the whole CEE-alone trial at 13 years) and in one further subgroup (bilateral
oophorectomy, HR 0.68), which bears on the candidate-artifact reading below. (corrected 2026-10-22: was
*concedes the same point NICE makes*, quoted to the end of the 18-year sentence only, before NAMS's MI and
oophorectomy interactions. Revised again 2026-10-22: was *its significant interactions are in further
subgroups* — the MI interaction is a different outcome in the whole trial, not a subgroup.)

So the divergence rests on **which evidence and which analysis each body weighed** (reason 2), and NICE's
inclusion criteria are one candidate mechanism. *If* the CVD-review criteria also governed the
mortality analysis (the held text does not say so; it states only an RCT restriction there, which would not by
itself exclude RCT meta-analyses), they would remove the favourable Cochrane estimate and the null Kim estimate
alike. On CHD the held text gives no Kim estimate. The other removed reviews are the Cochrane review (CHD RR
0.52, 95% CI 0.29-0.96, for initiation under 10 years) and, probably, Salpeter's younger-vs-older CHD
meta-analysis, which NAMS cites without an estimate in a block supporting fewer CVD events in younger women;
both lean to the side NAMS takes. NAMS's 2019 meta-regression is not among the named exclusions, and NICE gives its own reasons for
declining a CHD timing recommendation: no statistically significant subgroup differences in the RCT evidence
and inconsistent results between observational studies [@niceng23],
plus, for oestrogen-only HRT, inconsistency between RCT and observational conclusions [@niceng23];
and for the research recommendation, limited data: «There was not a lot of data to check whether the following
factors impact on risk of coronary heart disease:» ... «This resulted in what is called 'lack of statistical
power'.» ... «In what little data was available, the risk did not have a clear tendency to decrease or
increase with either of the above 2 factors» [@niceng23]
So the criteria's effect on the timing verdict is not shown. On CHD the criteria are stated: no review pooling
combined with oestrogen-only HRT, no review holding any off-criteria study — which removes, as wholes, the
Cochrane review, the 2020 meta-analysis NAMS cites and Salpeter 2006; both bodies admit observational
evidence and NICE reads it as inconsistent. (corrected 2026-10-22: was *they remove the favourable Cochrane
estimate and the null Kim estimate alike*, stated for both outcomes; on CHD no Kim estimate is held, and on
mortality the criteria's reach is not stated.) On mortality NICE restricts to RCTs and reads its own formulation-specific, age-stratified
analysis (its subgroup is oestrogen-only), while NAMS admits observational + open-label + meta-regression and
leans on the WHI age trend pooled across both trials — an estimate the no-pooling criterion would not admit as
one figure. The held text states that criterion for the CVD review only; that it also shaped the mortality
analysis is the wiki's reading. The held text does **not** show the two bodies reading the same analysis and
disagreeing about it. Where their descriptions do coincide — an oestrogen-only 50-59 mortality decrease with
no significant age interaction (NAMS's HR 0.79 above; NICE's isolated subgroup) — they conclude differently:
NAMS's key point «Key points • For healthy symptomatic women aged younger than 60 years or within 10 years of
menopause onset, the favorable effects of hormone therapy on CHD and all-cause mortality should be considered
against potential rare increases in risks of breast cancer, VTE, and stroke.»
[@nams2022] vs NICE's *unlikely to affect life
expectancy* (View B). But NAMS's conclusion also rests on the pooled-WHI trend and the meta-regression, and
NICE does not say which trial analysis its subgroup came from, so the held text cannot show whether that
shared description, read alone, would move NAMS. It is still not a fake tension of different scopes — both
answer *does starting earlier change the CV/mortality outcome?* — so it stays a joined tension on the same
question, most sharply on mortality. Guidance-difference reason 2 (different evidence base / analysis)
fires; reason 3 (genuine disagreement on the same evidence) **cannot be separated from reason 2 in the held
text**; reason 5 (process defect) does not fire — both bodies apply a stated appraisal method, and NICE's
RCT-restriction is a defensible choice, not a lapse -> [[Certainty of Evidence vs Strength of Recommendation]].
(corrected 2026-10-22: was *partly a genuine disagreement on the same WHI subgroups* with reasons 2 and 3
both firing; NAMS's 0.69 is the pooled-trials 50-59 estimate, NICE's isolated subgroup is oestrogen-only
50-59 — different analyses, so reason 3 was withdrawn. Re-corrected 2026-10-22: *reason 3 not established*

## Hidden insight — the window rides on subgroup-level and softer evidence, and the RCT meta-analyses split

The critical-window hypothesis is **carried by the softer evidence** (observational, open-label, and
within-RCT age-subgroup estimates — the pooled-WHI 50-59 HR 0.69 (0.51-0.94) excludes 1, but it is one age
subgroup and the age interaction is contested between analyses, while the meta-regression ORs attenuate once
open-label trials are excluded).
(corrected 2026-10-22: was *a non-significant RCT-subgroup trend*; the NAMS 50-59 pooled CI excludes 1.)
The RCT level does not settle it either: the Cochrane time-since-menopause subgroup favours early initiation
on CHD and mortality, the 2020 RCT meta-analysis finds null mortality in the same stratum, and NAMS's own key
point calls CHD null once open-label trials are excluded (counter-passage above).
On **mortality**, a body that restricts to RCTs (NICE) does not endorse the window and a body that admits the
wider evidence (NAMS) does — but because RCT meta-analyses themselves split, RCT-weighting alone cannot be the
explanation. A candidate mechanism the held text shows is NICE's inclusion criteria, not shown to decide the verdict: the
favourable
Cochrane review is excluded as a whole for pooling combined and oestrogen-only HRT, and the null 2020
meta-analysis for holding off-criteria studies, with their studies re-screened one by one (counter-passage
above). That is stated for the CVD review; its reach into the mortality analysis is inferred. On **CHD**,
NICE admitted observational evidence, dropped the same pooled reviews, and still could not conclude, citing
inconsistent observational results; there the difference is what each body admits plus where it sets the
bar for endorsing over thin, inconsistent data. (corrected 2026-10-22: was *the window rides on the weakest
evidence in the stack. A body that weights RCT evidence hardest will not endorse it; a body that admits the
wider evidence will* — applied to the whole CV question, which NICE's CHD basis and NAMS's split RCT
meta-analyses do not support. Revised again 2026-10-22: was *RCT-weighting is one candidate explanation, not
a demonstrated one*, with no concrete mechanism named; NICE names its excluded reviews and why.)
This is the telos's case where *guidance families disagree because the
evidence does not determine the answer* — and the same discipline that governs a U/J-shaped protective arm
applies to a subgroup signal: NICE calls its oestrogen-only 50-59 mortality decrease statistically significant
in isolation and reports no age pattern; NAMS reports an age trend in the pooled WHI trials (P for trend .01),
so the age pattern itself is contested. A subgroup benefit without an agreed age gradient is a **candidate
artifact**, not an established effect modifier -> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]]. (corrected
2026-10-22: the verified-quote mark on *significant in isolation* did not locate verbatim; *no age gradient* was NICE's
reading stated as fact.) The
NAMS-endorsed magnitudes are real point estimates; what is contested is whether they are effect modification
(route (b)) or noise.

## Decision relevance

- **The disputed part is a *bonus*, not the core decision.** Neither body reports HRT raising all-cause
  mortality. NICE states it outright: «The committee noted that high-quality evidence showed no difference in
  mortality with either oestrogen-only or combined HRT compared with not taking HRT.»
  [@niceng23] NAMS states no overall verdict
  in the held text; it reports mixed RCT meta-analyses in younger initiators (a Cochrane reduction, a 2020 SR
  null — counter-passage above), while its key points grade a reduced absolute all-cause mortality in women
  <60 at Level I: «The absolute risks are reduced for all-cause mortality, fracture, diabetes mellitus (EPT and
  ET), and breast cancer (ET) in women aged younger than 60 years (Figure 1).9 (Level I)»
  [@nams2022] (added 2026-10-22); and, for
  initiation >10 y from menopause or
  &gt;60 y, cites a Cochrane analysis with «all-cause mortality (RR, 1.06; 95% CI, 0.95-1.18)»
  [@nams2022] — so *no increase overall* is the
  wiki's reading of NAMS, not its words (corrected 2026-10-22: was *both bodies agree HRT does
  not raise all-cause mortality overall*; no such NAMS sentence located. Re-corrected 2026-10-22: was *reports
  a reduction in younger initiators*, which left out the null 2020 SR NAMS cites beside Cochrane). Both
  support it for vasomotor symptoms and bone in a symptomatic woman <60 / <10 y
  from menopause. So the timing dispute is over a *possible CV/mortality benefit* (NAMS) vs *no effect on
  mortality, insufficient evidence on CHD timing* (NICE) — the symptom-relief-and-bone decision stands either
  way -> [[Hormone Therapy After Menopause]]. (corrected 2026-10-22: was *vs no effect (NICE)* for both
  outcomes.)
- **The harm side moves with timing regardless of the CV dispute** (NAMS, with NICE concurring on stroke and
  dementia): NAMS states that for initiation >10 y from menopause or >60 y «the benefit-risk ratio appears
  less favorable because of the greater absolute risks of coronary heart disease, stroke, venous
  thromboembolism, and dementia» [@nams2022].
  NICE ties stroke to age at starting combined oral HRT («This risk increased with age at starting HRT (that
  is, the older people were when they started taking HRT).»
  [@niceng23], for 5-9 y of use) and points
  to «a possible increased risk in dementia incidence if HRT is started at a later age»
  [@niceng23], by majority and worded as
  *might*; on CHD its timing finding is no clear tendency (View B), and no held NICE line ties VTE to timing.
  The wiki's reading: absolute risk rises with age-driven baseline risk, so late initiation is worse whether or
  not early initiation helps — a route-(a) baseline-risk fact independent of the contested effect modification
 -> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]. (corrected 2026-10-22: was *(agreed):
  starting >60 / >10 y raises absolute CHD/stroke/VTE/dementia risk*, untagged; NICE does not state the CHD
  or VTE part.)
- **Do not report a life-expectancy benefit from early HRT as established.** The honest statement is: HRT is
  a symptom-and-bone treatment; a cardioprotective early-initiation benefit is a live, unresolved hypothesis
  carried by the weaker evidence, and confidence on it stays `low`. A dedicated RCT of early-initiation hard
  outcomes would resolve it — and both bodies say as much (NAMS's Summary call for trials; NICE's CHD-timing
  research recommendation, View A/B).
