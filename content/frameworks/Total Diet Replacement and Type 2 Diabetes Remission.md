DiRECT (Lancet 2018) is the first registered trial to set **remission of type 2 diabetes as a
primary outcome** and to deliver the intervention in **routine primary care** rather than a research
centre. It supplies two things the fabric did not hold: a **quantified total-diet-replacement (TDR)
protocol with a structured maintenance phase**, and a **remission-by-weight-loss dose-response** —
the %-body-weight -> outcome gradient that grounds the weight-loss lever with a magnitude.

Its pathophysiological premise is Taylor's *twin cycle hypothesis* — that T2D of limited duration is
driven by excess fat in liver and pancreas and is reversible by negative energy balance. Earlier
mechanistic work had normalised liver insulin resistance within 7 days and pancreatic function over
8 weeks on a 600-700 kcal/day diet; DiRECT tests whether this is *practicable* at scale.
[@lean2018] The premise is now held directly:
«people with recent onset T2DM could regain normal glucose control and normal β-cell function when the
fat content of the liver and the pancreas was decreased by a weight loss diet- ary regimen» — the
depot-drawdown mechanism the remission gradient rides on -> [[Ectopic Fat and Depot-Specific Risk]].
[@taylor2015pft]

**The stratum DiRECT's BMI cut-off excludes — normal-weight T2D (a decision-change).** DiRECT required
BMI 27-45, so its evidence is silent on the normal-weight stratum (a third of the 1970s-80s UKPDS cohort; likely
smaller today, as higher BMIs are now more prevalent per Taylor), and such patients
are routinely *not* offered weight loss. Taylor's personal-fat-threshold frame extends the lever there: T2D
is one condition across BMI (36% of newly-diagnosed UKPDS patients had BMI <25), and reversal is
«achievable equally readily by people with lower initial BMI» — indeed at a *smaller* required loss (UKPDS:
\~13% at normal BMI vs \~21% for the whole cohort to reach normoglycaemia). Those UKPDS figures are *fasting
normoglycaemia* (FPG <6.0 mmol/l) after a 3-4 month diet-only run-in, in people presenting at 8-10 mmol/l —
not DiRECT-defined remission (HbA1c <6.5% off drugs), so they are not a remission dose for the lean stratum.
(corrected 2026-10-06: scope narrowed from reversal to fasting normoglycaemia, Taylor chunk 01) So a normal-weight person with
short-duration T2D is a candidate for the same energy-deficit lever, sub-threshold BMI notwithstanding — a
recommendation DiRECT's own population cannot license, which the hypothesised personal-fat-threshold
mechanism supports only directionally (mechanism-grade; Taylor states it as a hypothesis — «We hypothesize
that each individual has a personal fat threshold (PFT) which, if exceeded, makes likely the development of
T2DM.» — and the equal-readiness claim rests on Taylor's refs 30-31).
[@taylor2015pft]

## The intervention — an energy target, NOT a macronutrient one

The Counterweight-Plus programme, in three phases:

- **Total diet replacement:** a low-energy formula diet, "825-853 kcal/day; 59% carbohydrate, 13%
  fat, 26% protein, 2% fibre", for 3 months (extendable to 5). **Note the 59% carbohydrate** — this
  is high-carbohydrate *by share of energy*, but because energy is so low it is \~122-126 g/day of
  carbohydrate (825 x 0.59 / 4 to 853 x 0.59 / 4), just under (by 4-8 g) the <130 g/day low-carbohydrate cut-off
  Goldenberg uses. So during induction it is low in carbohydrate *grams* as well as in energy; the two
  cannot be separated in this phase. [inferred from @lean2018; @goldenberg2021]
- **Stepped food reintroduction:** 2-8 weeks, "about 50% carbohydrate, 35% total fat, and 15%
  protein".
- **Structured weight-loss maintenance:** ongoing monthly visits, behaviour-change methods (including
  CBT elements), a step-count target.

«All oral antidiabetic and antihypertensive drugs were discontinued on day 1», reintroduced only per
protocol if glucose or blood pressure required it.
[@lean2018]

**Population and design:** aged 20-65, T2D diagnosed within 6 years, BMI 27-45, **not on insulin**.
Open-label, cluster-randomised at 49 primary-care practices (the practice, not the patient, was the
randomisation unit); 149 per group in the ITT population. Co-primary outcomes, analysed
hierarchically: "weight loss of 15 kg or more, and remission of diabetes, defined as glycated
haemoglobin (HbA1c) of less than 6.5% (<48 mmol/mol) after at least 2 months off all antidiabetic
medications, from baseline to 12 months".
[@lean2018]

## The headline result

| Outcome (12 months, ITT) | Intervention | Control | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight loss >=15 kg | 36 (24%) | 0 | p<0.0001 |
| **Diabetes remission** | **68 (46%)** | **6 (4%)** | **OR 19.7 (95% CI 7.8-49.8)** |
| Mean weight change | -10.0 kg (SD 8.0) | -1.0 kg (3.7) | adj diff -8.8 kg (-10.3 to -7.3) |
| HbA1c change | -0.9% | +0.1% | adj diff -0.85% (-1.10 to -0.59) |
| QoL (EQ-5D VAS) | +7.2 | -2.9 | adj diff +6.4 (2.5 to 10.3) |

[@lean2018]

The 46% remission rate "greatly exceeded" the 22% deemed a-priori clinically important. On
medication: at 12 months **73.6% of the intervention group were on zero oral antidiabetic drugs**
(vs 18.2% of control), and **68% remained off antihypertensives** with no rise in mean blood
pressure.
[@lean2018]

## The jewel — remission scales with weight loss (dose-response)

**Pooling both randomised groups, remission rises monotonically with the weight loss maintained at 12
months.** "Remission varied with weight loss in the whole study population":

| Weight change at 12 months | Remission achieved | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Weight gained | 0 of 76 | **0%** |
| 0-5 kg loss | 6 of 89 | 7% |
| 5-10 kg loss | 19 of 56 | 34% |
| 10-15 kg loss | 16 of 28 | 57% |
| **>=15 kg loss** | **31 of 36** | **86%** |

[@lean2018]

The discussion states the composite the table implies: "86% of participants with at least 15 kg
weight loss, and 73% of those with weight loss of 10 kg or more" achieved remission.
[@lean2018]

### A different design reports the same *direction* of per-kilogram gradient (type-F corroboration, independence CAPPED)

DiRECT's dose-response is a **within-trial** association (responder confounding is live — see Limits). A
**separate observational design corroborates the same monotone gradient, reported across diets** — which
strengthens the *direction* without lifting the finding to `[E-independent]` (the independence is capped;
see below). The diet-independence leg is not DiRECT's: it is Churuangsuk's judgement, plus ADDITION's
per-kg association as relayed by Churuangsuk (no composition contrast described). DiRECT's gradient
pools a single formula programme with usual care, so it cannot separate weight from composition. Reported in the Churuangsuk umbrella review: ADDITION-Cambridge, a **population cohort of 867 newly-diagnosed T2D patients
across 49 UK primary-care practices**, recruited by screening (the study of «People with Screen-Detected
Diabetes in Primary Care», so earlier-detected disease than a clinically-presenting population), followed 5 years — overall 30% remission — found «Every 1 kg of
weight loss was associated with 7% higher chance of remission at 5 years, regardless of specific diet
regimens or lifestyle interventions», and «Loss of >10% of baseline body weight in the first year after
diagnosis was associated with 70% higher chance of remission at 5 years».
[@churuangsuk2022]

**Parameter table** (op-weave 2a — the two are matched on the claim they jointly back, NOT on magnitude):

| Parameter | DiRECT (Lean 2018) | ADDITION-Cambridge (via Churuangsuk 2022) | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design | within-RCT dose-response, both arms pooled (conditions on achieved weight) | **population cohort**, 49 practices | **both observational weight->remission associations; one nested in an RCT** |
| Population | T2D <6 yr, not on insulin, \~all white | newly-diagnosed T2D, UK primary care | overlapping, both early-disease |
| Exposure | weight-loss band at 12 mo | per 1 kg / >10% loss in yr 1 | different metric |
| Outcome / horizon | remission (HbA1c<6.5% off meds), 12 mo | remission (definition not in held umbrella text), **5 yr** | \~YES at best; different horizon |
| The claim backed | remission rises with kg lost (one trial, formula arm and usual-care controls pooled) | rises per kg lost, «regardless of specific diet regimens» | **PARTIAL — direction yes; diet-independence only as relayed by Churuangsuk for ADDITION (no composition contrast described)** |

The magnitudes are **not** pooled (12-mo band-rate vs 5-yr per-kg association); what is shared is the
**direction** of the gradient (diet-independence comes only from ADDITION as relayed by Churuangsuk, with no composition contrast described; not from DiRECT). There IS a real independence gain on one axis —
different datasets/institutions (Newcastle/Glasgow DiRECT vs Cambridge ADDITION). On design there is
none: both are observational weight->remission associations (DiRECT's is nested in an RCT but
conditions on achieved weight, a post-randomisation variable). But the `[E-independent]` token is **withdrawn**
(2026-08-08), for two reasons the earlier version missed:
- **Shared confounding.** Both gradients are driven by the SAME
  dominant threat — **responder confounding** (people who lose more weight differ biologically, so the
  weight->remission slope is partly confounded in the RCT *and* the cohort). A second design that
  shares the incumbent's main confounder does not neutralise it, so it cannot lift the finding the way a
  genuinely orthogonal design would.
- **The relay runs through DiRECT's own PI.** ADDITION-Cambridge is held ONLY via the Churuangsuk umbrella,
  whose byline is «...Simon J. Griffin ... & Michael E. J. Lean» — **Lean is DiRECT's joint PI/first
  author** and Griffin an ADDITION investigator. So the "independent corroboration" is read through a
  review co-written by the incumbent trial's own lead; that is a lineage tell E forbids, disclosed nowhere
  before. Same shared-authorship class as the Fibre SACN⟂Reynolds demotion.
So this is **type-F corroboration by a different design, with independence capped** — real but not
`[E-independent]`; and transport beyond UK early-disease weight-centric-remission populations is
corroborated by neither.
[inferred from @lean2018; @churuangsuk2022]

### Where DiRECT sits in the umbrella's certainty-graded remission map

Churuangsuk's GRADE table places **total diet replacement (the two low-RoB RCTs, DiRECT + DIADEM-I) at
the single GRADE-HIGH cell** of the whole remission map — median 54% remission (range 46-61) vs 4-12%
standard care, N=445 — above formula meal replacement (11%, moderate), Mediterranean (15%, low), and
ketogenic (20%, very low). [@churuangsuk2022] So
DiRECT is not an isolated strong trial: it anchors the one high-certainty conclusion in the graded T2D
remission literature (graded by a review co-authored by DiRECT's PI Lean — see the lineage note above), and that conclusion is a *format* (energy-controlling TDR), not a macronutrient
class -> [[Diets for Weight Management in Type 2 Diabetes]].

**Why this is the high-value finding.** A monotone gradient from 0% (weight gain) to 86% (>=15 kg),
in a *within-population* comparison, is evidence that, **within one trial, remission tracks kilograms
lost**. The gradient is drawn with «both randomised groups combined» [@lean2018],
and the formula arm supplied every large loss: 36 intervention participants lost 15 kg or more and «no
participants in the control group» did [@lean2018]. So weight
band and programme move together along it, and the gradient cannot separate weight from programme or
composition. The diet-independence reading comes from elsewhere: Churuangsuk's umbrella
(co-authored by Lean) concludes «The main contributor to HbA1c reduction and remission appears to be weight loss, irrespective of diet type.» [@churuangsuk2022]
— right after cautioning that «residual bias/confounding may remain» in the non-randomised studies — and
ADDITION's per-kg association is relayed «regardless of specific diet regimens» (no composition
contrast described). The DiRECT gradient is a cross-group association (not the randomised
contrast), so it carries confounding risk (a responder who loses more may differ), but the biological
gradient is steep and coherent with the mechanistic twin-cycle work — work from the same research
programme (Taylor is DiRECT's senior author, and the twin-cycle papers DiRECT cites, refs 4-5, are
Taylor's), so coherence here is not an independent leg. Read it as *name-the-curve*: the remission
rate is higher in each successive 5-kg band over the studied range (weight gain to >=15 kg), with **no
visible plateau below 15 kg**. As an association across five bands, it does not establish that each
added kilogram causes more remission. Whether it plateaus above 15 kg is unresolved (the top category
is open-ended and the mechanism has a ceiling — remission is bounded at 100%).
[inferred from @lean2018]

## The maintenance phase — the structure the fabric lacked

DiRECT is unusual in designing for maintenance "from the outset". Among engagers, weight fell sharply
in TDR then partly regained: **-14.5 kg** (95% CI 13.4-15.5) during TDR, then **+1.0 kg** during food
reintroduction and **+1.9 kg** during maintenance — phase means among engagers that sum to \~-11.6 kg
(not a single-cohort mean), against the -10.0 kg ITT mean.
[@lean2018]

Adherence: "79% completed the intensive total diet replacement phase"; the \~25% "dropout rate" (6
never engaged, 26 withdrew — 15 during TDR, 6 during reintroduction, 5 during maintenance) marks the
programme's non-acceptability ceiling. This is the missing-piece for the NICE TDR recommendation,
which names a low-energy TDR but not the reintroduction+maintenance scaffold
-> [[Diets for Weight Loss - What NICE Recommends]]. (DiRECT's 825-853 kcal formula sits at the bottom
of NICE's stated 800-1200 kcal band, as held on that page; no source on this page states the band.)


**If attempting it — two safety steps the sources state (trial protocol / author advice, not
trial-tested as separate interventions).** (i) *Review blood-pressure and glucose-lowering drugs with
the prescriber before starting an \~800 kcal diet.* DiRECT withdrew both on day 1 under protocol (above);
for the BP drugs the stated reason is: «Weight loss leads to a rapid and marked fall in blood pressure, with risk of postural hypotension if antihypertensive drugs are continued. The acute fall in blood pressure with a low energy formula diet is greater than anticipated from reduced salt intake alone.6 For that reason, all diuretic and antihypertensive medications were withdrawn at the start of the total diabetes replacement phase in participants in the intervention group, and only restarted if systolic blood pressure exceeded 140 mm Hg.»
[@lean2018]
Churuangsuk adds the glucose-lowering side: «Blood pressure falls with weight loss, and postural hypotension, common in older people and those with diabetes, is aggravated during rapid weight loss if diuretic or antihypertensive drugs are taken concurrently [73]. Hypoglycaemia is possible if hypoglycaemic drugs are also taken [74]. Diets other than nutritionally complete formula diets could incur vitamin and mineral deficiencies [75].»
[@churuangsuk2022]
(ii) *If retinopathy is present at baseline, rescreen at about 6 months:* «Even if diabetes recurs, there might be a legacy effect of a period of good glucose control, as suggested in the UK Prospective Diabetes Study.35 Sudden restoration of normoglycaemia can precipitate worsening of diabetic retinopathy, although this outcome is rare when early or no retinopathy is present.35 Nonetheless, if retinopathy is present at baseline, rescreening at 6 months is indicated.35 It will be possible to determine the effects of DiRECT intervention by future analysis of the national retinopathy screening databases.»
[@lean2018]
Both are review-with-the-clinician flags, not dosing; the drug changes themselves are the prescriber's.
[inferred from @lean2018; @churuangsuk2022]

## Durability at 24 months — remission tracks the weight kept off

The 24-month report of the same trial (same 298 ITT participants, same definitions, same team — so
**type-F durability, never type-E**) answers the 12-month paper's open question. About a third of the arm were in remission at 24 months
(not all the same people as at 12 months: 48 remitters held, some new), down by about a fifth from the
12-month level. The held text is the author-accepted manuscript (*Draft 7*, 7 Feb 2019), without main
Tables 1-2; figures should be checked against the published version before a deliverable quotes them:
«Findings: At 24 months, 53/149 (35·6%) of those commencing the intervention and 5/149 (3·4%) in the
control group (adjusted odds ratio 25·8, 95% CI 8·3,80·8; p<0·0001) had remission»
[@lean2019]

**Parameter table — 12 vs 24 months** (op-weave 2a):

| Quantity | 12 mo (Lean 2018) | 24 mo (Lean 2019) | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remission, intervention (ITT /149) | 68 (46%) | 53 (35.6%) | YES — same definition, same missing=not-remitted rule |
| Remission, control (ITT /149) | 6 (4%) | 5 (3.4%) | YES |
| Weight loss >=15 kg, intervention | 36 (24.2%) | 17 (11.4%) | YES |
| Adjusted between-arm weight difference | -8.8 kg | -5.43 kg (-6.87 to -3.99) | YES |
| Remission in the >=15 kg band (pooled arms) | 31/36 (86.1%) | 14/20 (70.0%) | **NO** — the band holds whoever is >=15 kg down *at that visit*, so it is a different, smaller set of people (true of every band row) |
| Remission, <5 kg band (pooled) | 6/165 (3.6%) | 8/154 (5.2%) | **NO** as people (bands re-sort at each visit); same bin edges — and this *<5 kg* bin includes weight gain (the 12-month table above splits it into 0/76 + 6/89) |
| Per-kg OR for remission (Fig 2C) | 1.32 (1.23-1.41) | 1.25 (1.16-1.35) | \~YES — same figure and model, but fitted to a different weight-change distribution at each visit |

[@lean2019]
12-month weight difference -8.8 kg (-10.3 to -7.3): [@lean2018]

- **Absolute effect.** The between-arm remission difference is about 32 percentage points at 24 months
  (35.6% vs 3.4%) against about 42 at 12 months — roughly 3 people started on the programme per
  additional 2-year remission vs usual care, in this stratum. The NNT inherits the remission definition's
  medication asymmetry (22/119 control participants on drugs had HbA1c <48 mmol/mol but count as
  non-remitters; see Limits). [@lean2019]
  The odds ratios swing with the model's adjustment set: imputed-outcome 25.8 in the results
  [@lean2019],
  vs 15.87 in Figure 2B [@lean2019].
  With only 5 control remitters, the counts are the stable quantity. [inferred from @lean2019; @lean2018]
- **The gradient survives.** «For the entire study population, remissions at 24 months were achieved by
  8/154 (5.2%) participants who lost less than 5 kg, 21/73 (28·8%) who maintained 5–10 kg loss, 15/25
  (60·0%) who maintained» [@lean2019] 10-15 kg, and
  14/20 (70%) at >=15 kg. Still monotone over the studied range (<5 to >=15 kg); the top band is lower
  than at 12 months, but that compares different people (row 5 above), not one person's falling odds.
  [inferred from @lean2019]
- **Relapse went with more regain (descriptively; the stayer-vs-relapser contrast is p=0.073).** Of the 63 intervention participants in remission
  at 12 months with weight at both visits, 48 stayed in remission and 15 relapsed; the stayers regained
  4.25 kg (SD 3.68) in year 2, the relapsers 7.09 kg (SD 5.42), t-test p=0.073. Over the full two years
  the stayers were -10.4 kg net, the relapsers -3.7 kg. [@lean2019]
  The authors read this as «achieving and maintaining weight loss is the dominant factor behind remission
  of type diabetes» [@lean2019] — consistent with the
  dose-response, though the regain difference itself does not reach p<0.05. A different analysis, the
  post-hoc model over all 298 participants with both arms pooled, modelling 24-month remission (not
  relapse among the 63 remitters), found remission varied
  significantly «with weight- change from 12 to 24 months (adjusted odds ratio per kg gained 1.11 (1·03, 1·21), p=0·010)» [@lean2019]
  — the sign is ambiguous as printed (an OR above 1 *per kg gained*, listed among factors remission
  «increased with»), so read it as a significant regain–remission association of unclear coding. The
  stated adjustment set is «All models were adjusted for treatment, practice list size, centre and a random effect for practice.» [@lean2019]
  Weight loss from baseline (OR 0.83 per kg) is reported in the same sentence; the text does not say
  whether the two weight terms share a model.
  [inferred from @lean2019]
- **Keeping it off took repeated rescue.** «about half required relapse management with brief total diet
  replacement and the offer of orlistat during the two years: 71/143 (49·7%) had not had any ‘rescue
  plan’, 49/143 (34·3%) had one, 15/143 (10·5%) had two and 8/143 (5·6%) had three or more rescue plan
  phases.» [@lean2019]
  The protocol behind that summary is two-tier: «In the event of weight regain >2kg, participants were
  offered a ‘rescue plan’ of 2-4 weeks partial meal replacement, and if >4kg a total diet replacement and
  food reintroduction, with the offer of orlistat treatment.» [@lean2019]
  Attendance averaged 7.7 of 12
  monthly visits in year 2, and the intervention arm regained 2.6 kg (SD 5.0) between 12 and 24 months
  while control lost 1.3 kg. [@lean2019]
- **Within the stratum, BMI did not predict 2-year remission; duration was imprecise.** «Likelihood of remission at
  24 months was not influenced by baseline BMI (adjusted odds ratio per kg/m2 0·99 (0·92, 1·06), p=0·77)
  or duration of diabetes within the 6-year range included (adjusted odds ratio per year 0·92 (0·76,
  1·11), p=0·39).» [@lean2019] Men (female OR 0.44) and
  older participants (OR 1.08 per year) remitted more often, and the arm-by-factor interaction tests were
  null (sex p=0.31, duration p=0.11). [@lean2019]
  These are post-hoc, pooled-arm *prognostic* associations (route (a)), not evidence of effect
  modification (route (b)), and not a basis to triage people in or out. The duration OR (0.92 per year,
  about 0.6 across the 6-year range) points the same way as the 2018 caution in Limits — insufficient
  evidence, not a null. Part of the age gradient may be built in: non-attenders were younger and the 26
  untraceable were counted as non-remitters. [inferred from @lean2019]
- **Secondary outcomes held, smaller.** HbA1c adjusted difference -4.82 mmol/mol with antidiabetic-drug
  use 39.5% vs 83.9% of control; SBP -3.43 mmHg with fewer on antihypertensives (47.3% vs 60.1%); QoL
  (EQ-5D VAS) adjusted difference 4.6 (0.4-8.9) [@lean2019],
  down from +6.4 (2.5-10.3) at 12 months [@lean2018].
- **Which bias hits which estimate.** In the ITT contrast, contamination and attrition push *against*
  the intervention; the remission definition's medication-withdrawal asymmetry pushes the other way
  (control participants kept their drugs, so some with HbA1c <48 mmol/mol count as non-remitters — see
  the NNT note above and Limits). Contamination shrinks it: «A proportion of the control
  group took personal action to lose weight (9 participants in the control group lost >10 kg during the
  second year compared to 2 during the first year).» [@lean2019]
  Attrition was unequal (77.9% vs 94.6% attended), handled by counting the 26 untraceable participants
  as non-remitters [@lean2019]; non-attenders were
  younger, with shorter diabetes duration and more microalbuminuria (HbA1c higher, p=0.075) (Table S1).
  [@lean2019]
  Counting the intervention arm's extra non-attenders as non-remitters also lowers its ITT rate. The
  selected analyses run the other way: the available-data rate (53/129, 41.1%, incl. GP-record data), the weight bands and the
  maintainer-vs-relapser contrast all condition on who stayed and how much they lost.
  [inferred from @lean2019]

**Decision consequence.** For the DiRECT stratum (age 20-65, BMI 27-45, T2D <6 years, not on insulin,
UK primary care), the 12-month 46% figure overstates what a person should expect: the 2-year ITT rate
is about one in three on the programme. Remission tracked weight kept off (post-hoc, pooled arms, among
those with data: 29/45, 64.4%, at >=10 kg), but it was neither guaranteed by it nor impossible without
it — 4 of the 50 who gained weight, each with a small (0-2 kg) gain and baseline HbA1c 6.5-6.63%, were
in remission at both 12 and 24 months. DiRECT's maintenance phase
included monthly visits and rescue TDR, used by about half the arm; whether remission lasts as well
without them is untested. The fabric holds no
evidence beyond 2 years for this route; the trial was funded to 3 years with consent for 5.
**The plain action:** for adults with T2D, overweight, and a recent diagnosis (the DiRECT stratum
above), a structured TDR programme with food reintroduction, maintenance visits and rescue for regain
is worth offering — expect about 1 in 3 in remission at 2 years, roughly 3 people started per extra
2-year remission vs usual care, with the safety steps above. Basis: one randomised ITT contrast at 12
and 24 months, its twin RCT (DIADEM-I) in Churuangsuk's GRADE-high cell; not tested outside this
stratum or beyond 2 years.
[inferred from @lean2019; @lean2018]

## Synthesis — is remission driven by WEIGHT LOSS or by CARBOHYDRATE RESTRICTION?

The fabric holds a *low-carbohydrate* remission result ([[Carbohydrate Restriction and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]], Goldenberg 2021). DiRECT achieves remission on a formula
diet that is **59% carbohydrate by energy** — so the two routes must be reconciled. Parameter table (op-weave 2a):

| Parameter | DiRECT (Lean 2018) | Goldenberg 2021 (BMJ MA) | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome | remission = HbA1c <6.5%, off antidiabetic meds >=2 months, 12 mo | remission = HbA1c <6.5% or fasting glucose <7.0 mmol/L, with or without medication (both reported), 6/12 mo | **YES only on Goldenberg's med-free definition** (pooled RD 0.05 NS at 6 mo; -0.04 NS at 12 mo; non-insulin-trial subgroup 0.20, 0.03 to 0.38, at 6 mo only); the RD 0.32 headline permits meds |
| Intervention | 825-853 kcal TDR formula, 59% carbohydrate = \~122-126 g/day | <130 g/day or <26% of energy from carbohydrate (of a 2000 kcal reference, where 26% = 130 g) | **NO as a share of the formula's own energy (59% vs 26%); on Goldenberg's 2000 kcal basis both are <130 g/day during induction (DiRECT just under)** |
| Mechanism claim | weight loss / negative energy balance (twin cycle) | carbohydrate restriction | **NO — the contested variable** |
| Weight loss | adj diff -8.8 kg vs usual care (-10.3 to -7.3; within-arm -10.0 kg), dose-response to remission | MD -3.46 kg vs comparator diets at 6 mo (18 RCTs); **+0.29 kg, NS, at 12 mo** (7 RCTs, 6 of them also in the 6-month pool) | NO — different comparators; DiRECT's difference much larger |

(remission definition, RDs, weight MDs); DiRECT weight difference [@lean2018];

**The fourth column is NO on mechanism and only partly matched on intervention and outcome — so this
is NOT a like-for-like tension to file. It is an F-refinement on the causal question, with a narrower
reach than a first reading suggests.** Both routes produce weight loss. On DiRECT's medication-free
definition, Goldenberg's pooled low-carb effect is non-significant at 6 and 12 months (RD 0.05, then
-0.04, GRADE low). In the trials that excluded insulin users, as DiRECT did, it is significant at six
months: «Studies that included patients using insulin had fewer remissions for both definitions of remission (HbA\~1c\~ \<6.5%; HbA\~1c\~ \<6.5% and no diabetes medication) at six months (risk difference 0.14, 0.03 to 0.25; 0.00, --0.07 to 0.07) compared with studies that did not (risk difference 0.51, 0.36 to 0.65; 0.20, 0.03 to 0.38) (test for subgroup difference P\<0.001; P=0.03).»
[@goldenberg2021]
Goldenberg counts this among its credible subgroups: «Most subgroup observations were not deemed credible; however, three credible subgroups were identified on the basis of meeting four of five credibility criteria.»
[@goldenberg2021]
(The sentence after the subgroup result concerns the very-low-carbohydrate weight subgroup, not
insulin.) The split is reported at six months only, and it compares trials, not patients within a
trial; the pooled 12-month medication-free estimate (-0.04) is the only long-term read, and it says
little about the non-insulin stratum: two of the three 12-month remission trials (Tay, Sato) included
insulin users [@goldenberg2021]. On the medication-free definition the insulin split may also coincide with Goldenberg's post-hoc
trial-length split, which gives the same 0.20 / 0.00 figures (0.20 in trials of 3 to under 6 months'
length, 0.00 in trials of 6-9 months), so it may be one finding, not two; with medication permitted the
figures differ (insulin split 0.51 vs 0.14, trial-length split 0.49 vs 0.25)
[@goldenberg2021], so the two
partitions are not identical. So low-carb shows a six-month drug-free remission signal in
trials that, like DiRECT, excluded insulin users, not an established effect
-> [[Carbohydrate Restriction and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]].
[inferred from @goldenberg2021]
But:

- DiRECT's **within-population dose-response** ties remission to *kilograms lost* within one trial,
  formula arm and usual-care controls pooled (it does not vary composition, so it cannot itself show diet-independence — that leg rests on
  Churuangsuk's judgement and its relay of ADDITION, which describes no composition contrast; above). **What DiRECT can show about carbohydrate:** remission was achieved on a
  formula with a high carbohydrate *share*, and it was measured at 12 and 24 months, after structured
  food reintroduction at «about 50% carbohydrate, 35% total fat, and 15% protein» and the maintenance
  phase [@lean2018]. **What it cannot show:** that remission
  occurs without carbohydrate restriction in grams. During induction the formula supplied \~122-126 g/day
  (59% of \~840 kcal), 4-8 g under Goldenberg's 130 g/day cut-off; energy and absolute carbohydrate
  restriction are confounded in this phase. Whether carbohydrate restriction
  adds a remission effect beyond the weight it produces is untested here — insufficient evidence, not
  shown absent.
- Goldenberg's own data are **consistent with weight-mediation but do not discriminate it**: its
  low-carb remission and weight advantage **are both non-significant by 12 months** (weight MD
  -3.46 kg at 6 mo -> +0.29 kg at 12 mo; remission on the medication-permissive definition RD 0.32 at
  6 mo -> 0.10, NS, at 12 mo; on the medication-free definition, pooled, RD 0.05 NS at 6 mo, 5 studies, and
  -0.04 NS at 12 mo, 2 studies, both GRADE low). [@goldenberg2021]
  The 12-month pools are smaller sets, largely nested in the 6-month pools (7 vs 18 RCTs for weight, 6
  of the 7 also in the 6-month pool, all except Yancy; 3 vs 8 for remission, all 3 in it), so this is a between-pool contrast, not
  observed regain. The two 12-month pools also barely overlap each other: only Tay is in both the weight
  and the remission pool, and two of the three remission trials (Morris, Sato) list 3- and 6-month
  interventions in Table 1. [@goldenberg2021]
  So weight and remission are not observed fading together in the same trials. At a near-zero 12-month
  weight gap, HbA1c, triglycerides and medication reduction still point toward low-carb, from pools of
  8, 7 and 3 trials whose membership the held text does not list, so not shown to match the weight pool
  (figures on [[Carbohydrate Restriction and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]]). A remission
  effect that fades alongside the weight advantage fits a weight-loss effect delivered via a low-carb
  route, and fits equally well a carbohydrate effect that fades as adherence to restriction fades; the
  fading pattern does not discriminate them.
  [inferred from @goldenberg2021]
  Goldenberg itself leaves the cause open: «Of note, the limited number of studies with 12 month outcome data providing differing levels of support and having highly adherent versus less adherent intervention arms precluded subgroup analyses that explicitly explored the effects of adherence at 12 months. Although improvements noted at six months diminished by 12 months, determining with any certainty whether this is related to intensity of intervention and/or dietary adherence beyond six months is difficult.»
  [@goldenberg2021]
  (The sentences before it report that very-low-carbohydrate diets underperformed less restrictive
  ones for six-month weight loss, a difference negated among highly adherent patients; that concerns
  restriction level and adherence, not the weight-vs-carbohydrate question.)
- **The closest held test of carbohydrate beyond energy is null and imprecise.** Goldenberg's
  prespecified calorie-matching subgroup: «For this reason, as part of our a priori planned subgroup analysis, we investigated the effect of calorically matched controls (as assessed by follow-up dietary questionnaires). On the basis of 18 studies providing adequate data, we identified no evidence of credible effect modification based on caloric matching or lack thereof. However, self-reported dietary intake data are prone to measurement error, particularly in dietary trials in which participants are not blinded.»
  [@goldenberg2021]
  The preceding sentence gives the reason for the analysis: because carbohydrate restriction tends to
  reduce hunger, it was unclear whether any benefit came from carbohydrate or from calorie restriction.
  The test matches calories, not weight, and rests on self-reported intake, so it fits *insufficient
  evidence*, not a shown absence.
- **One held RCT datum leans against a pure weight reading.** Churuangsuk relays Esposito: 15% vs 4%
  remission at 1 year with a Mediterranean diet vs low-fat, on mean weight loss of 6.2 vs 4.2 kg (GRADE
  low). [@churuangsuk2022]
  The contrast is Mediterranean vs low-fat, so carbohydrate is not isolated; it is weighed on
  [[Carbohydrate Restriction and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]] and does not move the verdict.

- **DiRECT's own authors leave composition open:** «Effective long-term weight management with a resetting of long-term energy consumption is clearly essential, but other factors contribute and there remain unanswered questions and debates about dietary approaches, and the optimal ratio of macronutrients.»
  [@lean2019]
  Their next sentences cite a non-randomised very-low-carbohydrate study with substantial weight loss,
  then state that meta-analyses of controlled trials show no important differences between high- and
  low-carbohydrate diets for weight control or HbA1c (their ref 31). [@lean2019]

**The defensible composite:** *T2D remission (of short-duration disease) appears to be driven mainly by
the magnitude of sustained weight loss. That is Churuangsuk's hedged judgement, which notes residual
confounding; DiRECT's gradient is confounded with programme; ADDITION's per-kg association is relayed
without a composition comparison. Carbohydrate restriction is one lever for achieving weight loss.
Whether it adds a remission effect beyond the weight loss it produces is insufficient evidence, and
DiRECT's authors list the optimal macronutrient ratio as unresolved.* This refines — does not
contradict — Goldenberg: the low-carb result holds at six months on HbA1c <6.5% with medication allowed (not significant by
12 months), and on the drug-free definition shows only a six-month signal in trials excluding insulin
users, but its
**framing** (*low-carb for remission*) is at least as well read as *weight loss for remission, low-carb
being one delivery route*. A person choosing a diet for remission should aim first at the **weight loss
they can sustain**; the held evidence does not show that the route matters beyond that, nor that it
does not ([[Low-Carbohydrate vs Balanced-Carbohydrate Diets]]).
[inferred from @lean2018; @goldenberg2021]

DiRECT states the same logic for surgery: «The essential mechanisms behind bariatric surgery are weight loss and decrease in body fat content, rather than any direct surgical effect.27,28,29,30,31 The very large weight losses targeted by bariatric surgery are not essential for achievement of remission of type 2 diabetes, as shown by the present data. Changes in intra-organ fat content and β-cell function in a subgroup of the DiRECT cohort will be reported separately.»
[@lean2018]
Lean 2018 does not discuss low-carbohydrate diets. Extending the point across three routes (formula
diet, low-carb, surgery), a common thread is weight/fat loss; the route-independence of that thread is
this page's generalisation, not a source's.

## Remission is a real benefit weight loss moves — but it is intermediate

DiRECT is a counterweight to the cardiovascular-event null ([[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]]): where Look AHEAD showed lifestyle weight loss did **not** cut CV events, DiRECT shows the
same lever delivers a **patient-important benefit it demonstrably does move** — freedom from diabetes
and its drugs, plus a measured QoL gain. Weight loss is indicated for the outcomes it moves; remission
is squarely one of them.

**But remission is an intermediate outcome, not a hard one.** HbA1c normalisation is expected (if
maintained) to reduce microvascular risk — Lean's words: «if maintained, can reasonably be expected to reduce microvascular complications» — but DiRECT was "not designed or powered to evaluate effects on
complications" and remission can relapse. DiRECT itself reaches for the hard-outcome bridge only by
citing a Look AHEAD post-hoc: "a 10% weight loss in the first year... was associated with a 21%
decrease in occurrence of cardiovascular outcomes" — the same >=10%-responder signal that page holds
as *reported, not proven* -> [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]].
[@lean2018]

## Limits and bias — read honestly

- **Open-label, and allocation was visible to participants** (cluster design). Baseline balance
  suggests limited bias, but blinding of a lifestyle programme is impossible.
- **Asymmetric medication handling:** antidiabetic drugs were stopped in the intervention arm but not
  control. The trial compares *the whole programme vs standard care*, so the remission definition
  (off-meds HbA1c) is favourable to the arm whose meds were deliberately withdrawn — a design choice,
  disclosed, that inflates the between-arm remission gap relative to a med-matched comparison.
- **12-month horizon**; durability is unaddressed here (follow-up planned to >=4 years). Remission of
  short-duration T2D only — "remission is less likely with longer durations of disease".
  (Superseded in part 2026-10-06: the 24-month report is held — see *Durability at 24 months* above;
  nothing beyond 2 years is held. [inferred from @lean2019])
- **No hard outcomes at 24 months either.** Year-2 serious adverse events were fewer in the intervention
  arm (9 in 6 participants vs 22 in 16), but «DiRECT was not powered to assess ‘hard’ clinical outcomes,
  but seeing fewer serious adverse events in the second year of weight management is reassuring, given
  the past anxiety over safety of older formula diets.» [@lean2019]
  Read it as a safety signal, not evidence of complication reduction.
- **Scottish/Tyneside population**; no unqualified transport to e.g. South Asian groups who develop T2D
  at lower weight: «The racial and ethnic characteristics, while typical of the populations of Scotland
  and Tyneside, do not allow for unqualified extrapolation to other groups» [@lean2018]
  Baseline Table 1 gives the share: «Ethnicity (White) 147 (98·7) 146 (98·0)» (control, intervention).
  [@lean2018]
  (corrected 2026-10-06: an earlier same-day note said no ethnicity percentage was located; Table 1 has it)
- **The dose-response is a within-population association**, not the randomised contrast — responder
  confounding is possible.
- **The twin-cycle / personal-fat-threshold mechanism is a HYPOTHESIS, not a settled cause.** Taylor's
  depot-drawdown account is mechanistically corroborated (liver/pancreas fat falls with weight loss and
  tracks glucose recovery) but the *personal fat threshold* — that each person has an individual fat
  level above which T2D triggers — remains an inference under test, not an established quantity; the
  remission gradient is consistent with it but does not prove it. That corroboration comes
  from the same research programme as DiRECT (Taylor is DiRECT's senior author; the held PFT paper is
  Taylor's own), so it is coherence within one lineage, not an independent leg.
- **Sponsor / vendor COI:** the programme (Counterweight-Plus) and the formula are commercial products.
  The trial was funded by Diabetes UK and «The formula diet was donated by Cambridge Weight Plan. Neither
  organisation had any input into the study design, data analysis or interpretation.» That disclaimer
  names Diabetes UK and Cambridge Weight Plan, not Counterweight Ltd — yet «HMR reports employment by
  Counterweight during the study» and «LM reports employment by Counterweight during the conduct of
  study», and «MEJL reports personal fees from Counterweight during the conduct of the study».
  [@lean2018]
  Disclosed here for symmetric standards — it does not overturn the RCT, but the honest-bias inventory
  must name it. (corrected 2026-10-06: generic vendor-stake line -> the source's funder /
  donor / employment disclosures)
- Adverse events during TDR were common, mostly mild or moderate, and transient (constipation, cold sensitivity, headache,
  dizziness); 9 serious AEs in 7 (4.5%) intervention participants, 2 possibly related (biliary
  colic + abdominal pain, same participant), none causing withdrawal.
- **Coherence, not validity (R1):** a strong single trial, not proof the effect transports or
  persists.
