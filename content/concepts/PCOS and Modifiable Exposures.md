Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a prevalent, insulin-resistance-linked reproductive/metabolic
condition — it affects 5-20% of women of childbearing age, insulin resistance is present in \~75% of
lean and \~95% of overweight women with PCOS, and 60-70% of women with PCOS are overweight
[@greff2023inositol]. It enters this wiki on the
**prevention-and-improvement-by-lifestyle-lever** terms (like MASLD / metabolic syndrome / T2D):
what an exposure does to a patient-important outcome for the stratum, not the fertility-clinic
management of a diagnosed case. The lever with gold SR/MA evidence held so far is **inositol**, a
nutraceutical insulin sensitiser positioned as the folk alternative to **metformin** (the standard
metabolic drug). This page is the nucleus; diet / weight-loss / exercise levers are named gaps below.

## Verdict — the surrogates move; the reproductive endpoint that matters does not

The single held gold source (Greff 2023, SR+MA of 26 RCTs / 1691 women; 24 in the quantitative pool)
concludes «Inositol is an effective and safe treatment in PCOS» and recommends it be added to PCOS
guidelines [@greff2023inositol]. Read against the
**surrogate-vs-patient-important** axis, that headline is a **surrogate-driven overclaim**: the
statistically significant effects sit almost entirely on **biochemical and anthropometric
surrogates** (androgens, SHBG, BMI, fasting glucose, AUC-insulin) plus a **very-low-certainty**
primary outcome (cycle normalization); the one **patient-important reproductive outcome the MA pooled
— clinical pregnancy — was null** vs both placebo and metformin, and **live birth and miscarriage
were never pooled** [INFERRED, from the outcome-by-outcome table below]. The decision-relevant finding
is narrower and different: inositol is a **plausible metformin substitute for the metabolic surrogates
with far fewer GI side effects**, on evidence that does not yet reach the outcome a woman seeking
fertility actually cares about -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]].

Two internal tells the headline glosses. **(1) The direct insulin-resistance markers did not move.**
The MA states: «In the case of fasting plasma insulin, HOMA-IR, and AUC-glucose, inositols compared
to placebo showed no favorable effect» [@greff2023inositol]. So the significant glycemic signal rests on **fasting glucose and AUC-insulin** — not on HOMA-IR
or fasting insulin, the canonical IR surrogates — which weakens the mechanistic *insulin sensitiser*
story on its own terms (the AUC-insulin subgroup benefit is myoinositol-specific and does not reverse
the HOMA-IR null). **(2) The primary outcome is very-low certainty.** Cycle normalization — the
registered primary outcome (down-switched from ovulation/cycle length for lack of data) — is
significant but GRADEd **very low** on only 2 studies / 118 women, so it carries little decision weight
-> [[Rating Certainty of Evidence]].

## The patient-important gap — pregnancy null, live birth never pooled

Pregnancy was the only patient-important reproductive endpoint pooled, and it is **null and
untrustworthy**: «The overall pregnancy outcome was heterogenous regarding its definition, carrying a
significant risk of bias. Only one study reported on the pregnancy rate for the inositol placebo
comparison without additional therapy and found no difference (RR = 3.3 CI: 0.4; 27.13)»
[@greff2023inositol]. The pooled pregnancy estimates lean
on trials where inositol was **followed by adjuvant fertility therapy** (letrozole, rFSH+HCG), so they
do not isolate an inositol effect, and the MA itself concedes «The generalization of pregnancy rate
results was problematic since only one study analyzed women who wanted pregnancy»
[@greff2023inositol].

**G-gap (patient-important, load-bearing):** **live birth and miscarriage were never pooled** — 0
occurrences across both chunks (searched: `live birth` / `miscarriage`, chunks 01-02, corpus zero over
a non-empty denominator). This is a named gap, **not** a null: the MA is silent on the two
outcomes that decide whether inositol helps a woman conceive and carry, so no fertility claim is
supported at any certainty. The MA's own next-step ask is «Further well-designed RCTs are needed to
assess the beneficial effect on pregnancy rate» [@greff2023inositol]. -> — a fertility-endpoint SR (live birth /
clinical pregnancy) would move this cell.

## Inositol vs metformin — the substitution/Layer-1 comparator

Metformin is PCOS's standard metabolic drug but induces GI side effects (nausea, diarrhea, vomiting,
flatulence) [@greff2023inositol]. Because a mature standard
drug is the realistic alternative, the decision-relevant question is not inositol-vs-nothing but
**inositol-vs-metformin** -> [[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]]. Head-to-head (Table 3):

| Outcome | n studies (pts) | Effect (95% CI) | GRADE | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HOMA-IR | 6 (419) | MD -0.18 (-0.41; 0.06) | high | non-inferior |
| BMI | 9 (593) | MD -0.11 kg/m2 (-0.25; 0.04) | high | non-inferior |
| Fasting insulin | 6 (427) | MD -0.37 (-1.52; 0.78) | high | non-inferior |
| Total testosterone | 4 (320) | MD 0.2 (-5.72; 6.12) | moderate | non-inferior |
| SHBG | 3 (220) | MD +2.78 nmol/l (0.02; 5.54) | moderate | slight inositol edge |
| **Ferriman-Gallwey / hirsutism** | 3 (220) | MD 0.6 (0.24; 0.96) | high | **metformin better** |
| Cycle normalization | 6 (424) | RR 1.42 (0.8; 2.53) | very low | non-inferior (NS) |
| Pregnancy rate | 5 (383) | RR 1.22 (0.84; 1.78) | very low | non-inferior (NS) |
| **Side effects** | 4 studies | RR 0.16 (0.09; 0.28) | — | **inositol far fewer** |

The one decision-changing head-to-head finding is **tolerability**: «four studies comparing inositol
to metformin showed a lower rate of side effects in the inositol group (7 vs. 53%, RR = 0.16, CI:
0.09; 0.28)» [@greff2023inositol]. On metabolic surrogates
inositol is broadly **non-inferior** to metformin (high certainty for HOMA-IR / BMI / fasting insulin),
but metformin is **better on hirsutism** (FG-score) and both are null on the reproductive endpoint. So
the net-of-alternative case is: for a woman who cannot tolerate metformin's GI effects, inositol
delivers similar metabolic-surrogate movement with a much lower side-effect burden — a genuine
substitution finding, bounded to surrogates. The choice stays the person's (layer 3); this only sizes
the marginal rock.

## Myo-inositol vs D-chiro-inositol — isomer and a dose caution

The two clinically-studied isomers behave differently, so *inositol* is not one exposure ->
[[Is the Food Category Doing Any Work]]. **Myo-inositol** carried the most-supported and best-GRADEd
benefits — BMI -0.71 kg/m2 (-1.00; -0.43, high), fasting glucose -4.03 mg/dl (-6.59; -1.47), AUC-insulin
-2034 (high), cycle normalization 1.76 (1.06; 2.92) — while its pooled pregnancy rate was null (3
studies, RR 0.92, 0.53; 1.61, very low) [@greff2023inositol].
**D-chiro-inositol (DCI)** drove the largest androgen/SHBG shifts (total testosterone -41.71,
free testosterone -0.58, SHBG +55.45, DHEAS -168.48; several high/moderate) but on very few, small
trials. Trial doses were typically **myo-inositol \~2000-4000 mg/day** or **DCI \~600-1200 mg/day** over
7-24 weeks [@greff2023inositol].

DCI carries a **dose/duration caution** (mechanism-flagged, route-c flavor): «DCI is an aromatase
inhibitor and promotes glycogen synthase, which inhibits the conversion of androgens to estrogens,
resulting in the accumulation of androgens and lack of estrogens. That is why long-term or high-dose
DCI administration will worsen the symptoms of PCOS» [@greff2023inositol]. The MA reports no adverse androgen effect at 6-8-week DCI dosing, but this is a
mechanistic/short-term claim, not a long-term safety finding — the pooled evidence cannot
confirm or refute the long-term-DCI-harm direction, so it stays a directional caution, not a settled
harm.

## Layer-1 placement and named gaps

Where inositol sits in the PCOS lever hierarchy: it is a **surrogate-moving, well-tolerated
metformin-alternative** whose patient-important reproductive benefit is unestablished — a modest,
adjunct-grade rock, dominated for metabolic risk by the same established levers that govern insulin
resistance generally -> [[Insulin Resistance Surrogates and Cardiovascular Risk]]. It is the PCOS
sibling of [[Cinnamon and Glycemic Control]]: a supplement with real but surrogate-only signal and no
held patient-important-outcome evidence.

Named gaps (G) driving the next PCOS source choices:

- **Weight loss / diet as first-line** — the standard first metabolic lever for overweight
  PCOS is weight loss, but no gold SR on diet/weight-loss -> PCOS incidence/remission or ovulation is
  held. — directional-only, `confidence: low`.
- **Low-GI diet vs weight-loss-does-the-work in PCOS** — separating the diet composition from the
  energy-deficit effect..
- **Exercise -> PCOS metabolic/reproductive outcomes** — unheld..
- **Fertility endpoint (live birth / clinical pregnancy)** for inositol — the reproductive cell above..

## Provenance, quality, and conflicts

Greff 2023 is a **gold** SR+MA (26 RCTs, RoB 2, GRADE, PROSPERO-registered CRD42021283275); most
outcomes were GRADEd **moderate**, the reproductive and cycle-normalization outcomes **very low**
[@greff2023inositol]. Its own stated limits: small
number of small, heterogeneous trials; differing follow-up and doses; heterogeneous pregnancy
definition; missing AUC forest plots; moderate-to-high RoB in some domains
[@greff2023inositol]. Funding was **public/academic**
(Hungarian NKFIH-FK129206, Semmelweis University, Hungarian Hypertension Society; funder had no role),
and the authors **declare no competing interests** [@greff2023inositol] — so the *meta-analysis* is not manufacturer-linked, though the underlying inositol
RCTs' funding was not appraised here.

## Synthesis

 This is a
**provisional-C** nucleus — a single gold source opens the PCOS cluster and the decision question; the banked cross-source A/F moves are OWED when a second PCOS lever lands. Within this one
source, the move beyond its own summary is a **surrogate-vs-patient-important re-sorting**: the MA
presents one flat *beneficial on all aspects* verdict; sorted by outcome tier it becomes a
**surrogate-strong, patient-important-null** result with a very-low-certainty primary outcome and a
live-birth/miscarriage hole the MA never names as such. That re-read uses the MA's own GRADE ratings
and tables against its headline — an intra-source critical reading, not a cross-source tension. The
genuinely decision-changing residue is the **metformin-substitution-on-tolerability** finding (7% vs
53% side effects, non-inferior surrogates), not the fertility framing the title invites.
`[E-independent]` is **not** claimed — this is a single source; the sorting and the gap are the value,
and both are reproducible-checkable against the MA's own tables.
