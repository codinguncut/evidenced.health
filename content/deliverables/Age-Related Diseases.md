**The diseases of ageing share a modifiable core, though not every lever reaches every disease.**
Cardiovascular disease, cancer, and dementia draw much of their modifiable risk from one short list
-- body fatness, tobacco, physical inactivity, and heavy alcohol reach across all three; blood
pressure, blood glucose, and blood lipids drive cardiovascular disease and dementia but are not
established cancer levers. Where a lever does overlap, pulling it buys protection against more than
one disease at once -- but those gains sit on different outcome scales and cannot be added into a
single number.

**Physical activity is the single lever that reaches almost every disease on the list.** It is the one
exposure with credible signal against cardiovascular disease, several cancers, dementia, Parkinson's,
frailty, and falls together. It is rarely the largest effect for any one disease, but it is the widest.

**The disease-specific add-ons are mostly small, contested, or absent.** Once the shared cardiometabolic
levers are pulled, the extra items that dominate public discussion -- eggs, red meat, individual
supplements, single "superfoods" -- move little, move uncertainly, or (for one supplement) do harm.

**For some diseases the honest finding is a low ceiling.** Parkinson's has few credible modifiable
levers and mostly low-quality evidence. Several prevention bundles move a marker without yet preventing
the disease. Reporting that there is little left to optimise is itself a result.

**The ranking shifts with age, not the levers.** In midlife the cardiometabolic levers carry the risk
for later disease; in later life falls, frailty, and muscle loss become the outcomes that decide
independence. Absolute benefit rises with age because baseline risk does.

## The diseases of ageing share a common core of levers

Assemble the modifiable-risk-factor lists the wiki holds for four leading age-related outcomes —
dementia, cardiovascular disease, cancer, and Parkinson's — and a small set of exposures recurs across
most of them in the *same* direction. The recurring core is physical (in)activity, adiposity,
diabetes/glycaemia, blood pressure, LDL, smoking, and excessive alcohol — the same cardiometabolic big
rocks the vault already ranks. No single source states this, because each disease body is
disease-specific by construction; the overlap itself is the finding.

The matched parameter is the **exposure and the direction of its association**, not a commensurable
magnitude. Each column's estimate is for a *different* outcome (dementia vs Parkinson's vs cancer
incidence vs CV events), measured in a different population, so a row is co-membership evidence — read
it across as *shared lever, same direction*, never as an equal-size effect. A blank cell is a `G`-gap
(not a credibly evidenced lever in the sources held), not a null.

Read the direction column, not a magnitude down a row. These relative risks are borrowed from
observational data and *assumed* causal — physical activity is the most robust leg (consistent across
all four, dose-response, some trial support), smoking-in-Parkinson's the least (a named reverse-causation
artifact, not a lever). The dementia body's own headline shows why the recurring numbers are not
per-person effects: the Lancet Commission estimates its 14 factors carry a population attributable
fraction of «nearly half of all cases of dementia being associated with 14 potentially modifiable risk
factors» — 45.3% [@livingston2024]. That figure is a
*modelled, communality-discounted* population quantity: the factors overlap so the per-factor PAFs do
NOT sum, and «this risk modification affects the population but does not guarantee any individual will
avoid dementia» [@livingston2024].

**Decision-change.** A person does not need a separate anti-dementia diet, an anti-cancer diet, and an
anti-heart-disease diet. Because the core is shared, pulling the big rocks *once* buys risk reduction on
several patient-important outcomes at the same time — which *strengthens* their Layer-1 ranking rather
than adding competing tasks: a lever that moves three outcomes outranks one that moves one at equal
per-outcome effect -> [[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]]. Ranked by how many
diseases it touches, physical activity is first.

## Physical activity is the widest lever across the diseases of ageing

Physical activity is the one exposure that recurs, protective and in the same direction, in every
disease body the wiki holds — and it earns its top place on **breadth across outcomes**, not on being
the largest single effect in any one of them.

- **Dementia:** activity is associated with a decreased risk of all-cause dementia, RR 0.80 (0.77-0.84,
  n=257,983), a midlife-and-later lever; the estimate is observational and assumed causal, at the
  Commission's consensus grade [@livingston2024].
- **Cancer:** WCRF grades physical activity a **Convincing** cause of decreased colon-cancer risk and
  **Probable** for endometrial and postmenopausal breast cancer, with «the greater the amount of
  physical activity, the greater the benefit» [@wcrf2018ter]. WCRF quantifies no per-person effect size — the grade, not a magnitude, is the object.
- **Parkinson's:** activity RR 0.79 (0.68-0.91), from prospective cohorts, dose-dependent by intensity
  and PD's most design-robust signal — but null in women
  [@chen2021pd].
- **Frailty, falls, and CVD:** activity is the cross-cutting function lever and an established CV lever
  -> [[Frailty]], [[Physical Activity Dose and Mortality]] (magnitudes re-derived there, not here).

Rank this by breadth, not by per-disease magnitude: physical activity is rarely the *largest* single
effect on any one outcome, and its cross-disease reach is what places it first. Two limits keep the
ranking honest. First, physical activity's **mortality** evidence is observational and softer than its
RCT-grade evidence for falls and frailty — a large hazard ratio from cohorts does not automatically
outrank a smaller trial effect, since reverse causation is not excluded by a between-quartile
observational design. Second, the effective **type** of activity is outcome-specific (aerobic dose for
mortality, resistance and balance work for falls and function), so *move more* is underspecified as a
prescription.

**Decision-change.** For any adult across these strata, make physical activity the default lever — one
act buys risk reduction on dementia, three cancer sites, Parkinson's, and function — and then match the
*type* of activity to the target outcome rather than treating *exercise* as a single lever. If
physical activity is the widest lever, the cardiometabolic cluster is the heaviest.

## The cardiometabolic cluster is the heaviest shared lever, and CVD adds no increment beyond it

Inside the dementia evidence, the heaviest shared exposures are the cardiometabolic cluster — blood
pressure, glucose, lipids, and body fatness — each acting in **midlife**: untreated hypertension HR
1.42 (1.15-1.76), diabetes HR 1.24 (1.06-1.46) per five years' *earlier* onset, high LDL-C +8% per 1 mmol/L
(1.08, 1.03-1.14) with >3 mmol/L at HR 1.33 (1.26-1.41), and midlife obesity RR 1.31 (1.02-1.68). All
are observational Commission estimates assumed causal, midlife-windowed
[@livingston2024].

These *are* the CVD risk factors. So cardiovascular disease has no distinct disease-specific lever left
to hunt: its answer to *what should I modify?* is the shared cluster itself. That is an informative null,
not an empty cell — the reason the CVD column above reads *established lever* across the cluster rows and
blank elsewhere is that CVD contributes the cluster and adds nothing outside it.

The lipid row carries a surrogate caution that the recurring *high LDL* cell must not launder. LDL /
apoB is a well-evidenced *causal* input to atherosclerotic CVD — genetic, Mendelian-randomization, and
RCT evidence together establish it -> [[LDL ApoB and Cumulative Exposure]] — but it is a **weaker proxy
for all-cause mortality**, and a marker moving the right way is not itself the patient-important outcome
-> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]. Pull the lipid lever for its *proven* CV-event benefit, not for a promised
mortality or cognitive payoff the surrogate does not carry.

The timing rule is Livingston's own: the risk is carried by *sustained midlife* exposure, so «decrease
risk factor levels early (the earlier, the better) and keep them low throughout life (the longer, the
better)» — «it is never too early or too late to reduce»
[@livingston2024]. Midlife diabetes and hypertension carry
the dementia risk; the same conditions arising in late life often do not.

**Decision-change.** For a midlife adult with elevated blood pressure or glucose, lowering it buys down
*two* diseases — cardiovascular and dementia — on a single action, and the earlier and longer it is
held, the more it buys; there is no separate CVD lever to chase beyond the cluster. Body
fatness is the shared lever that bites hardest on cancer.

## Body fatness drives cancer across a dozen sites — and alcohol has no cancer-safe floor

Body fatness is the broadest single diet-adjacent cancer exposure the evidence names, and WCRF's Third
Expert Report presents it first. The Continuous Update Project grades greater adult body fatness a
*convincing or probable* cause of many cancers: «As 12 of the 17 cancers reviewed by the CUP are linked
to greater body fatness» [@wcrf2018ter] — oesophagus
(adenocarcinoma), pancreas, liver, colorectum, kidney, endometrium and postmenopausal breast among them.
IARC extends the footprint by three sites beyond WCRF's set: it «concluded that greater body fatness is a
cause of thyroid cancer, multiple myeloma and meningioma»
[@wcrf2018ter]. WCRF calls maintaining a healthy
weight «one of the most important ways to protect against cancer»
[@wcrf2018ter], and warns that on current trends
«overweight and obesity are likely to overtake smoking as the number one risk factor for cancer»
[@wcrf2018ter]. This is a shared big rock (owned by
the Big Rocks deliverables) reading hard onto cancer specifically.

**The exposure is graded, not a threshold at BMI 25 — carry the shape.** WCRF states «For some cancers
the increase in risk is seen with increasing body fatness even within the so-called 'healthy' range.
Nevertheless, most benefit is to be gained by avoiding overweight and obesity.»
[@wcrf2018ter] So risk rises *inside* the studied
healthy-BMI range — there is no clean floor below which body fatness is cancer-neutral — while the
decision-relevant mass of benefit still sits in avoiding overweight and obesity. The certainty is WCRF's
convincing/probable grade; body fatness is indexed by BMI (with waist circumference / waist-hip ratio),
so no per-person effect size or CI attaches — WCRF does not quantify a per-person relative risk here, and
that absence is itself the honest reporting.

**Two caveats keep the lever honest.** First, the mechanisms — hyperinsulinaemia, elevated sex hormones,
and chronic inflammation — inform *direction*, not proof: the grades rest on cohort epidemiology, and the
pathways are marked as mechanism, not outcome evidence
[inferred from @wcrf2018ter]. Second, the causal grade is on body-fatness
-> *incidence* in *populations*; whether an individual's *weight loss* reduces *their own* cancer risk is
not established here — WCRF reports the survivor evidence (changing body fatness after diagnosis) as too
limited to grade. The weight-management decision, and the parallel open loop on whether losing weight cuts
hard events, is routed to [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]], where the lifestyle route was
null on cardiovascular events (Look AHEAD HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.83-1.09) yet weight loss stays strongly
indicated for the outcomes it demonstrably moves
[inferred from @wcrf2018ter].

**Alcohol's cancer feature is the outcome-specific one (T6): no lower threshold.** WCRF grades alcohol a
cause of many cancers and IARC classes alcoholic drinks a Group 1 (established) human carcinogen
[@wcrf2018ter]. On the shape,
alcohol behaves differently from the all-cause-mortality axis. Where mortality harm begins at a *dose*
(the pooled all-cause elevation on [[Alcohol and Mortality and Vascular Disease]] becomes statistically
unmissable around 45 g/day, and from \~25 g/day in women — read as *where the signal becomes unmissable,
not a permitted ceiling to drink up to*), the cancer risk has no floor: «Even small amounts of alcoholic
drinks can increase the risk of some cancers – there is no level of consumption below which there is no
increase in the risk of at least some cancers.»
[@wcrf2018ter] WCRF's blunt corollary:
«For cancer prevention, it's best not to drink alcohol»
[@wcrf2018ter].

**One protective arm, instantly outweighed — net effect decides.** WCRF records a genuine benefit:
«Drinking alcohol helps protect against kidney cancer (at least up to 30 grams or two drinks per day),
but this is far outweighed by the increased risk for other cancers.»
[@wcrf2018ter] A real single-site protective
association thus coexists with net whole-body harm — the *net-effect-not-the-intended-effect* rule: one
protective arm is not a reason to drink when the whole-organism cancer ledger is negative. The site
numbers WCRF cites (colorectal above \~30 g/day, liver/stomach above \~45 g/day) mark where the graded
evidence sits — the edge of the studied range — not safe drink-up-to levels; for breast cancer WCRF
identified no threshold at all. The full net ledger against mortality and vascular disease is held at
[[Alcohol and Mortality and Vascular Disease]].

**Decision-change.** Reducing visceral adiposity is the shared big-rock lever that reads hardest onto
cancer across a dozen-plus sites, and its risk gradient runs *inside* the healthy-BMI range rather than
switching on at a cutpoint — so an increment of fat loss pays even for someone nominally in range. Alcohol
carries no cancer-safe threshold: for cancer prevention the direction is *less, toward none*, and any
gram figure quoted elsewhere marks a mortality signal-detection point, never a cancer-safe allowance.
Late-life musculoskeletal decline is where the disease-specific evidence is richest and the levers
clearest.

## Measure function, not the scale — screen muscle strength, train it, and skip the bone pill

Three decades of practice sized up muscle on a DXA number; EWGSOP2 inverts that. In the revised
European consensus «muscle strength comes to the forefront, as it is recognised that strength is
better than mass in predicting adverse out- comes»
[@cruzjentoft2019] — mass drops to a
confirmatory role, physical performance to grading severity. The decision-change is upstream of any
intervention: screen an older adult on strength (grip dynamometer, chair-stand), not on the scale or
the scan.

Mass still carries an independent prognostic signal, so it is not disposable. de Santana's gold-tier
meta-analysis (9 cohorts, n=10,028, community-dwelling non-frail adults >=65, mean age 76) found
«A reduced pooled ASMI in individuals who died as compared to those who survived (ASMI SMD = −0.18,
CI95% −0.23 to −0.12, REM)» [@santana2021] — those
who died carried about 0.18 SD less appendicular muscle mass, a small but tightly-bounded difference
that «cannot be completely explained by differences in muscle strength»
[@santana2021].

That strength-independence is *consistent with* a real independent signal, not proof of one (T3). The
grip-strength moderator that failed to explain the mass-mortality link is a non-significant null with a
wide interval and a large point estimate (coefficient −0.451, 95% CI −1.470 to 0.564, on <=9 studies),
which de Santana concede may simply reflect underpowering
[@santana2021]. So the resolution holds both ways:
strength is the better single predictor and the better screen, while mass adds prognostic refinement —
strongest in the overweight/obese older adult (sarcopenic obesity), which is also the stratum with no
agreed operational cut-off, a standing gap.

The line that binds this cluster: low mass and low strength both *predict* death, but no RCT shows that
*raising* them cuts mortality — the loop stays open (predictor, not proven target). Grip
strength carries the same caveat at scale (all-cause HR \~1.16-1.20 per 5 kg lower, UK Biobank) — a
number to measure a stratum by, not one to steer toward -> [[Grip Strength and Mortality]]. The muscle
levers are still worth pulling, for **function, independence and sarcopenia** in their own right:
resistance training is the driver and adequate protein (\~1.6 g/kg/day, break-point 95% CI 1.03-2.20) a
modest RCT-grade adjunct on the surrogate -> [[Protein and Resistance Training for Muscle and Strength]],
and muscle-strengthening activity adds an independent, small-dose mortality association
-> [[Muscle-Strengthening Activity and Mortality]].

Osteoporosis fits the same shape, and here the intuitive lever is the wrong one. In generally-nourished,
community-dwelling adults >=50 who are not deficient, vitamin D (with or without calcium) does not
prevent fractures: the VITAL fracture trial found no reduction in total (HR 0.98, 95% CI 0.89-1.08) or
hip (HR 1.01, 0.70-1.47) fractures [@leboff2022], and
an independent USPSTF-commissioned pool (11 RCTs, N=51,419, sharing no VITAL trial) reached the same
enhancement-null — vitamin D + calcium total-fracture ARD -0.35% (95% CI -1.02 to 0.31), SoE low for no
benefit [@kahwati2018]. The repayment
is confined to the deficient or institutionalized stratum, where calcium is the active co-ingredient
(hip fracture RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.72-0.97) [@yao2019].
So for everyone else the bone-protective lever is **weight-bearing and balance exercise, not a
supplement**.

Decision-change: screen on strength and function, train resistance work plus adequate protein for
function and independence, and do not chase the DXA number or reach for a vitamin-D pill in the replete.
Frailty is the next handle — both a marker and a target.

## Treat frailty as a lever, not a fate — but dose the exercise down once someone is already frail

Frailty identifies a stratum with high baseline risk *and* a moving lever, which is what makes finding
it decision-changing. In identified prefrail/frail older adults, Racey's meta-analysis (26 studies, 24
RCTs, n=8022, adults >=65) found physical-activity interventions produced «Significant effects... for
mobility (standardized mean difference [SMD] 0.60, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.37 to 0.83),
activities of daily living (SMD 0.50, 95% CI 0.15 to 0.84), cognitive function (SMD 0.35, 95% CI 0.09
to 0.61), quality of life (SMD 0.60, 95% CI 0.13 to 1.07) and frailty (SMD –1.29, 95% CI –2.22 to
–0.36; risk ratio 0.58, 95% CI 0.36 to 0.93), with moderate certainty of evidence.»
[@racey2021frailtypa]

Three cautions bound that promise. The effective categories were muscle-strengthening and mixed
(aerobic + strength), not activity in general; some frailty-reversal subgroup figures rest on single
small trials, so the tight intervals read narrower than the evidence warrants. The general activity
guideline does not transport to this stratum — «recommended physical activity... may be too advanced or
too intense for a frailer population, put- ting them at risk for falls and injuries»
[@racey2021frailtypa] — the
effective programmes were supervised and dosed down (\~29 weeks, 30-60 min, 1-4x/week). And in the
already-frail, activity did *not* significantly reduce falls: «There were no significant effects on
measures of falls» [@racey2021frailtypa] (falls RR 0.80, 95% CI 0.51-1.26, very low certainty, 7 trials)
[@racey2021frailtypa]. The
high-certainty fall-prevention result is for the *general* older adult before deep frailty (balance +
functional exercise, RaR 0.77) -> [[Exercise for Preventing Falls in Older Adults]], [[Frailty]] — a
stratum-split limit, carried here rather than smoothed over.

Weight loss reverses direction in this stratum too (T4). Unintentional weight loss is itself a Fried
frailty criterion, and losing weight can *harm* an already-frail older adult by stripping lean mass and
reserve — so for the frail it is not a general function lever. This does not touch the
broad-incidence benefit of reducing excess body fat in the wider population (held elsewhere in this
deliverable); it is a stratum-specific contraindication, not a contradiction of it.

Decision-change: identify frailty early — high baseline risk plus a lever that moves mobility, ADLs and
frailty status makes case-finding worthwhile — then dose the exercise down, supervise it, favour
strengthening and mixed programmes, and do not prescribe weight loss to the frail. The most-studied
dementia-prevention strategy is a cautionary tale about surrogates.

## The dementia-prevention bundle moves the marker but has not yet prevented dementia

The levers that lower dementia risk in observational data are mostly the cardiometabolic big
rocks — blood pressure, glucose, weight, activity, smoking, lipids. Three randomised trials tested
whether pulling them *as a bundle* protects cognition, and the honest result is a cautionary tale
about surrogates: the marker moves a little, the disease does not.

FINGER (n=1260, at-risk CAIDE>=6 elderly, 2 years) moved a cognitive-composite **surrogate** by a
real but tiny amount: «The mean diﬀ erence between groups (group × time interaction) in change of
NTB total score per year was 0·022 (95%CI 0·002–0·042, p=0·030)»
[@ngandu2015], a Cohen's d of 0.13 at
2 years. Certainty is low: a single proof-of-concept RCT on a surrogate, its lower CI bound (0.002/yr)
near null.

MAPT tested a similar bundle on the same kind of surrogate over 3 years and was **null** at the
whole-population level: «The multidomain intervention and polyunsaturated fatty acids, either alone
or in combination, had no significant effects on cognitive decline over 3 years in elderly people
with memory complaints» [@andrieu2017]
(multidomain arm 0.079, 95% CI -0.012 to 0.170, adjusted p=0.179).

preDIVA is the one trial that measured the **hard endpoint** — clinical dementia incidence over
6.7 years — and found no reduction: HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.71-1.19, p=0.54), «A nurse-led, multidomain
intervention did not result in a reduced incidence of all-cause dementia in an unselected population
of older people» [@mollvancharante2016].

The responder hope — *it works if you target the high-risk* — was then tested directly and failed.
Coley pooled the MAPT+preDIVA individual-participant data (n=5205, up to 12 years, 486 dementia
cases): «there was no effect of multidomain interven- tion on the risk of all-cause dementia (HR
0.98, 95% CI 0.80–1.21)» [@coley2025]. No effect appeared in any of 11 pre-specified subgroups (CAIDE>=6 AD-dementia HR 1.03,
0.76-1.40; untreated-hypertension HR 0.72, 0.44-1.18 — both non-significant), and a data-driven
search found none either: «the SIDES algorithm did not detect any subgroup showing a differential
intervention effect on incident all-cause dementia»
[@coley2025]. The authors' verdict:
«the potential impact of multidomain interventions for dementia prevention at the individual level,
even among those at great- est risk of dementia, appears modest at best»
[@coley2025].

The one large *observational* diet signal points the same way, and does not add a fresh lever.
Mediterranean-diet adherence tracks lower Alzheimer's risk — «each one-point increase in the MD
scores was associated with an 11% reduced risk of developing AD (RR = 0.89; 95%CI, 0.84–0.93)»
[@garciacasares2021] — but every pooled study
is observational (low-to-moderate certainty, healthy-user and reverse-causation confounded), and the
effect is probably substantially **mediated** by the same cardiometabolic levers already counted,
not additive to them.

**Decision-change.** Pull the bundle's levers — they are the cardiometabolic big rocks, worth pulling
for their *proven* cardiovascular and metabolic outcomes. Do not promise a person a cognitive or
dementia payoff on top: one surrogate-positive trial, one surrogate-null trial, one hard-incidence
null, and a well-powered responder search that found no one who benefits. The marker is a
**predictor, not a validated target** here -> [[Multidomain Lifestyle Intervention and Cognitive Decline]], [[Surrogate Outcomes]]. Beyond the shared core, dementia carries specific levers the other
diseases do not.

## Correct hearing and vision, prevent head injury — the dementia-specific levers

Past the cardiometabolic core, dementia has genuine disease-specific **increments** — levers that are
not the shared big rocks renamed. Three are worth naming.

Hearing loss is associated with dementia in the 2024 Lancet Commission's new meta-analysis: «people
with hearing loss had an increased risk of dementia compared to those with normal hearing (HR 1.37,
1.00-1.87, I2 =80%, n=666,370)» [@livingston2024]. Read
the lower bound honestly — it touches the null (1.00), and the estimate is a meta-analysis of
high-quality cohorts, not a trial. Correction is available for most hearing loss, so the gap is
**uptake, not existence of a treatment**.

But the one hearing-aid RCT tempers the observational optimism. ACHIEVE found no overall cognitive
benefit at 3 years — «There was no overall effect on the primary outcome, cognition at three-year
follow-up (difference -0·002, –0·08 – 0·08)» — while a pre-specified analysis found benefit
concentrated in the higher-risk ARIC stratum: «substantial effects in the ARIC subgroup in cognition
at three years, (difference 0·19, 0·02 -0·36)»
[@livingston2024]. The ARIC participants were older, had
lower baseline cognition and more cardiovascular risk factors — so the benefit is a route-(b)
effect-modification signal (one pre-specified subgroup of one trial), not the overall effect
-> [[Hearing Loss and Dementia]].

The other two disease-specific increments the Commission carries: **uncorrected vision impairment**,
RR 1.47 (1.36-1.60) — correctable late in life; and **traumatic brain injury**, RR 1.66 (1.42-1.93)
— preventable [@livingston2024]. Both are genuine
increments over the cardiometabolic rocks, one a correction lever and one a prevention lever.

**Decision-change.** Correct hearing and vision — the treatment exists and uptake is the gap, with the
RCT benefit concentrating in the higher-risk stratum — and prevent head injury (protective equipment,
limiting high-impact collisions). These are levers dementia adds that the other age-related diseases
do not. Parkinson's is the deliberate contrast — few credible levers.

## Treat Parkinson's as the thin end — physical activity is the one credible lever

Parkinson's sits at the opposite pole from dementia: not a rich modifiable-risk map but a thin one,
and reporting that ceiling is itself the decision-change. The evidence base is the object of the
finding. Chen's umbrella of 46 systematic reviews grades its own field brutally: «none of the included
SRs were judged to be of high or moderate quality with the AMSTAR-2. Seven SRs (15.2%) were judged to
be of low method- ologicalquality,whiletheremaining39(84.8%)were judged to be of critically low
quality» [@chen2021pd]. And of the
50 statistically significant associations, «only 19 of these were supported by evidence from
prospective studies» [@chen2021pd].
Carry that as the certainty floor for everything below.

The one credible shared lever is **physical activity**: RR 0.79 (95% CI 0.68-0.91, from 8 cohort /
nested-case-control studies, I2=0)
[@chen2021pd] — the same lever that
already ranks high for mortality and cardiovascular disease, so it buys a *possible* Parkinson's
benefit as a rider, not a new lever.

Several headline *protective* signals are **artifact-suspect** and must not become actions. Parkinson's
has a years-to-decades prodrome that lowers activity, coffee and smoking *before* diagnosis —
manufacturing protective associations for exactly those exposures. Smoking's inverse association is a
reverse-causation curiosity, never a lever, and its net harm dominates any signal. The protective-drug
signals (statins, calcium-channel blockers) are confounded by indication. A protective exposure that
contradicts a well-established harm is a prompt to **run the artifact check**, never to act
-> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]]. The one dietary *risk* worth naming is dairy/milk (RR \~1.4-1.6,
cohort), of uncertain mechanism.

State the gaps at the strength held, not as a ranked menu [inferred from @chen2021pd]. The physical-activity effect-size meta-analysis and Mendelian-randomization
evidence for the reverse-causation arms are not held; Parkinson's has no population-attributable or
absolute-risk framing at all — a structural limit, so the absolute benefit of any lever at a stated PD
baseline risk is uncomputable from this source.

**Decision-change.** The honest reading is a low ceiling: pull physical activity, which is already
warranted for other outcomes; do not chase the pesticide or coffee folklore; and treat
smoking-to-protect as an artifact, never an action. Menopause changes which levers matter without
adding one.

## Menopause shifts which levers matter without adding new ones

**Menopause is a life-stage stratum, not a disease, and it changes which levers matter without adding
one the earlier sections did not already hold.** The intuitive story -- *menopause makes women gain
weight* -- puts the emphasis in the wrong place. On the largest meta-analysis of the question (201
cross-sectional studies, \~1.05 million women; 11 longitudinal, 2,472 women), total midlife fat gain
tracks *aging*: «The change in fat mass quantity was predominantly attributable to increasing age with
menopause having no significant additional influence»
[@ambikairajah2019]. What more likely
shifts is fat *distribution* -- centrally, toward the visceral depot -- but the source states this only
as a «possible» change, and its more-precise longitudinal estimates put the visceral-fat gain at
+12.95 cm2 (95% CI 8.65-17.25), under half the cross-sectional +26.90 cm2 (13.12-40.68). Central fat is
the cardiometabolic driver, so the diagnostic move is to measure the waist, not the BMI -- which is
confounded after menopause by bone and muscle loss [@ambikairajah2019].

**Hormone therapy is a symptom-and-bone treatment, not a body-composition, muscle, or anti-ageing
lever.** Systematic reviews find «hormone therapy had neither a beneficial nor harmful association with
muscle mass» [@nams2022]; on abdominal fat
it may help but «the effect is small» [@nams2022]. What HRT does well is relieve vasomotor symptoms and reduce fracture. Formulation is a real lever,
because the harms split by stratum: estrogen-plus-progestin carries a breast-cancer signal (\~20-year
HR 1.28, 95% CI 1.13-1.45) that estrogen-alone does not (HR 0.78, 0.65-0.93), and *oral* -- not
transdermal -- oestrogen carries the stroke signal -> [[Hormone Therapy After Menopause]]. Two gold
bodies, NAMS and NICE, disagree on whether early initiation adds a cardiovascular or mortality benefit,
but both agree HRT does *not* change all-cause mortality (WHI total mortality HR 0.98, 0.82-1.18
[@rossouw2002])
[inferred from @nams2022]. So the decision rests on symptom relief and bone against a rare harm, not on a longevity
gain.

**Decision-change: at menopause, watch the waist rather than the BMI, and use HRT for symptoms and bone
while weighing formulation against a rare breast-cancer or stroke harm -- not as a body-composition or
anti-ageing lever.** The most-discussed add-ons move little.

## Supplements are a near-null lever for the well-nourished -- and one does harm

**For a generally-nourished adult, supplementing vitamins, minerals, multivitamins, or marine omega-3
to prevent cardiovascular disease, cancer, fractures, or death is a null lever -- and for beta carotene
a harmful one.** The USPSTF recommends *against* beta carotene and vitamin E (grade D) and finds the
evidence insufficient for multivitamins and single nutrients (grade I): it concludes «there is no net
benefit of supplementation with vitamin E for the prevention of cardiovascular disease or cancer» and
«the evidence is insufficient to determine the balance of benefits and harms of supplementation with
multivitamins» [@uspstf2022supp]. The two
nulls are distinct -- vitamin E is a graded *no-effect*, multivitamins are *insufficient evidence* --
and neither is benefit.

**The harm is real and splits by stratum.** In two trials of people who smoke or were exposed to
asbestos, USPSTF reports «a significantly increased risk of lung cancer with beta carotene
supplementation (risk ratio [RR], 1.18 [95% CI, 1.03-1.36])», alongside a pooled increase in
cardiovascular-disease mortality (Peto OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.02-1.19) -- the *more-is-better* fallacy
realised on a hard outcome -> [[Vitamin and Mineral Supplements for Disease Prevention]] [@uspstf2022supp]. The load-bearing distinction is
between *enhancement in the replete* (the nulls and harm above -- adding to already-adequate people) and
*correcting a confirmed deficiency*, where repletion has real benefits: daily/weekly vitamin D cuts
respiratory infection in the deficient, iron and iodine raise cognition in the deficient, and
periconceptional folate cleanly prevents neural-tube defects -> [[Deficiency Repletion vs Enhancement]].
So supplements are a near-null *for the well-nourished adult on chronic-disease endpoints*, not
uniformly a distraction.

**Decision-change: do not supplement to enhance an already-replete body -- it buys nothing and, for beta
carotene in a smoker, does harm; correct a *confirmed* deficiency instead.** Two diet-specific items
attract attention out of proportion to effect.

## Eggs and red meat are small or contested disease-specific items

**Eggs are near a non-lever for the general population.** The largest dose-response meta-analysis to
date (39 cohorts, \~2 million people) concludes «There is no conclusive evidence on the role of egg in
CVD risk» [@godos2020]; every
general-population estimate sits within a few percent of 1.00 with confidence intervals kissing the
null (CVD at 4 eggs/week SRR 0.95, 0.90-1.00; at 1 egg/day 0.96, 0.92-1.01), and the body is GRADE
*low* [@godos2020]. Two
stratified exceptions matter (route-(b) effect modification): in cohorts of *diabetic* individuals the
direction «was substantially inverted for all outcomes», peaking at 1 egg/day (CVD SRR 1.22, 95% CI
1.08-1.39, low certainty), and heart failure shows a monotone risk signal rising to 1 egg/day HR 1.15
(1.02-1.30) [@godos2020].

**Red and processed meat carry a modest colorectal-cancer signal that two guideline families read
oppositely, and the disagreement is about grading and standpoint, not the numbers (tension T5).** On
the same cohort evidence, WCRF grades processed meat a *convincing* and red meat a *probable* cause of
colorectal cancer -- strong grades that «generally justify making public health recommendations»
(red-meat RR 1.12, 95% CI 1.00-1.25 per 100 g/day, the lower bound touching the null; processed 1.16,
1.08-1.26 per 50 g/day) [@wcrf2018]. NutriRECS grades that *same* evidence low-to-very-low certainty under strict GRADE and
re-pools it into absolute terms: «a very small and often trivial absolute risk reduction based on a
realistic decrease of 3 servings of red or processed meat per week» -- about 7 fewer cancer deaths per
1000 over a lifetime [@nutrirecs2019]. The
evidence does not force one answer: both bodies see the same small associations and diverge on the
standard of proof and on whether the threshold is set by population precaution or the individual's
values -> [[Should Adults Reduce Red and Processed Meat]]. Red meat is otherwise a shared big-rock
question treated in depth under the Big Rocks deliverables, and is not re-derived here.

**Decision-change: the general population can stop worrying about eggs on cardiovascular grounds -- the
diabetic stratum (and, at high habitual intake, heart failure) is the exception; red meat's absolute
cancer effect is small and its grade contested, so neither belongs above the shared big rocks, and the
attention both attract is an anti-signal.** The levers do not change with age; their ranking does.

## Re-rank the same levers by age stratum, not the levers themselves

The exposures that drive the diseases of ageing barely change across the life course; their
*ranking* does. Three facts fix the ordering for a given age stratum, and the decision-change is to
apply the ranking that matches the stratum rather than a single universal list.

**Midlife is when the cardiometabolic levers carry the future-disease risk.** Dementia's vascular
risk factors — hypertension, diabetes, obesity, high LDL-C, smoking — are counted as *midlife*
exposures, and the Commission's operative rule is to «decrease risk factor levels early (the earlier,
the better) and keep them low throughout life (the longer, the better)... it is never too early or too
late to reduce» [@livingston2024]. Late-life vascular care
may simply arrive too late to move the outcome: preDIVA, a 6.7-year multidomain vascular-care RCT in
*unselected* 70-78-year-olds, was null on dementia incidence (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.71-1.19; primary
endpoint) [@mollvancharante2016]. So
a younger adult's highest-value move is to pull the shared big rocks *early* and hold them low,
banking a benefit that compounds over decades -> [[Dementia Prevention and Modifiable Risk Factors]].
[inferred from @livingston2024; @mollvancharante2016]

**Later life is when falls, frailty, sarcopenia and osteoporosis become the deciding outcomes** —
function-and-independence endpoints, not length ones, carrying their own strong late-life levers
(balance training, resistance exercise, adequate protein). Low muscle mass is already a prognostic
marker by then: ASMI runs lower in older adults who die than in those who survive (SMD -0.18, 95% CI
-0.23 to -0.12, n=10,028 community-dwelling >=65) [@santana2021]
— though this is an observational *predictor*, not a proven treatment target
-> [[Low Muscle Mass and Mortality]]. The shared cardiometabolic levers still apply in later life, but
their marginal cognitive and CV payoff from *late* initiation shrinks (the preDIVA null is the worked
case), so the ranking tips toward the function levers.
[inferred from @santana2021]

**Absolute benefit rises with age because baseline risk does — route (a), no subgroup claim needed.**
The relative effect of a working lever is roughly constant across baseline risks, so the same
intervention averts *more* events per person in a higher-risk older stratum; that is legitimate
stratification requiring only prognostic data, not any positive interaction evidence
-> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]. It cuts the other way for surrogate-only levers:
a bigger absolute number on an intermediate marker is not a bigger patient-important effect.


**Decision-change:** match the lever ranking to the age stratum — younger, pull the shared rocks
early and keep them low; older, weight balance/resistance/protein and function/independence outcomes
alongside them. The diseases are counted as events, but many people weight the *trajectory* they
follow, which the next section takes up.

## Keep length and function as separate axes — do not collapse them

The diseases of ageing are measured mostly as death and disease *events*, yet the outcome many people
weight most heavily is the *trajectory*: full function followed by a rapid end (compressed morbidity)
versus a long decline. Falls, mobility, cognition and independence are patient-important outcomes in
their own right — often critical-rated, and for many people weighted above a marginal change in length
of life -> [[Dementia Prevention and Modifiable Risk Factors]], [[Low Muscle Mass and Mortality]].
[inferred from @livingston2024]

The dementia finding is partly a trajectory one, not only a length one: healthier lives push dementia's
*onset* back further than they extend life expectancy, compressing the years lived with dementia
-> [[Dementia Prevention and Modifiable Risk Factors]]. Trials rarely measure that trajectory (the
streetlight effect on the outcome side), so a clean mortality or incidence result can be *silent* on
the outcome a person actually values.
[inferred from @livingston2024]

**Decision-change:** this deliverable names *which* outcomes each lever moves and flags where the
trajectory is unmeasured, rather than letting a survival endpoint stand in for a life well-shaped. The
weighting of length against function stays the person's — there is no scalar maximand, and quality and
quantity are never collapsed into one number. The shared-core framing that runs through this cut hides
three real limits, named next.

## Know what this evidence still cannot tell you

Naming the limits is itself a result; each is structural, not a queue item awaiting one more source.

**(a) The cross-disease overlap is co-occurring separate effects, not one poolable number.**
BP-lowering against CVD, visceral-fat loss against cancer, and the dementia risk-factor bundle against
cognition are each estimated on their *own* outcome scale — a cardiovascular hazard ratio, a cancer
relative risk, a cognitive marker. The wiki holds no common metric to add them into a single
*diseases of ageing* effect, and no source supplies one. So *one lever, several diseases* is a
statement about *which* outcomes move, never a summed total — a G-limit that is structural, not
closable by aggregation.


**(b) The shared-core mechanism is not established here.** That the same exposures track several
diseases is epidemiological *co-membership* — evidence they cluster, not a tested common pathway. The
geroscience claim that a few ageing *hallmarks* are the shared upstream cause is a hypothesis this
corpus does not hold (no Hallmarks-of-Aging source is held), and it must not be read into the overlap.


**(c) Several of the largest post-tobacco modifiable cancer causes sit outside this corpus.**
Ultraviolet/solar exposure (skin cancer) and the oncogenic infections — HPV (cervical and other
sites), hepatitis B and C (liver), and *H. pylori* (stomach) — are modifiable and large, but the wiki
holds no source on any of them. Their absence here is a coverage gap, not a finding that they are
small.


**Decision-change:** do not treat *one lever, several diseases* as a summed total; do not read a common
mechanism into the overlap; and know the corpus's cancer blind spots when weighing what this cut can
and cannot rank.

## The bottom line

- **Pull the shared cardiometabolic levers first** -- blood pressure, body fat/visceral adiposity,
  blood glucose, blood lipids, not smoking, and holding alcohol down. Blood pressure, glucose and lipids
  buy risk reduction across cardiovascular disease and dementia; body fat, tobacco and alcohol reach
  several cancers too. Midlife is when they matter most.
- **Make physical activity the default lever** -- it is the one exposure with credible signal against
  the widest set of these diseases. Match the *type* to the target (balance for falls, strength for
  muscle, aerobic/mixed for cardiometabolic risk); "move more" alone is underspecified.
- **In later life, screen muscle *function* (strength, gait), not the scale**, and train resistance +
  adequate protein; identify frailty early and dose its exercise down.
- **Do not spend attention on eggs, red meat, or a supplement stack** for a well-nourished person --
  they are small, contested, or null, and beta carotene does harm. Correct a *confirmed* deficiency; do
  not supplement to enhance the replete.
- **Treat several "prevention" bundles as unproven on the disease** -- the multidomain dementia bundle
  moves a marker, not yet dementia incidence; pull its levers for their proven cardiometabolic outcomes,
  not for a promised cognitive payoff.
- **Menopause: watch the waist, not the BMI**; use hormone therapy for symptoms and bone, not as a
  body-composition or anti-ageing lever, and weigh formulation.

### Caveats that bind every claim above

- **The loop is open.** This deliverable grades coherence and source-fidelity, never validity. No
  operation here checks any lever against a realized outcome; a clean audit is not a validated
  recommendation.
- **It appraises evidence; it does not prescribe.** Selecting, dosing, screening contraindications, and
  managing interactions are prescriber acts needing this person's labs, history, and medications.
- **Estimates are population-level by default; stratify per person.** Personalize beyond the population
  estimate only on positive effect-modification evidence (as in the diabetic-egg reversal), not on
  mechanistic plausibility. Route-(a) baseline-risk stratification -- scaling absolute benefit by age or
  risk -- needs no subgroup claim.
- **One health axis only.** This holds a health axis (longevity, function, absence of deficiency). It
  carries no environmental, cost, or ethical axis and does not price one; where such a trade-off exists
  it is named, never netted against the health finding.
