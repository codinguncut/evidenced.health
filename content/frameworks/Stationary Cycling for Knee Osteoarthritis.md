Orbiter of the `osteoarthritis` cluster. **This page characterizes ONE exercise MODE** — stationary
cycling, a low-impact non-weight-bearing modality — where the nucleus
-> [[Knee Osteoarthritis and Modifiable Levers]] ranks exercise against weight loss, and the
pooled-all-modes effect lives on -> [[Exercise Therapy for Osteoarthritis Pain and Function]]. It
answers a decision neither of those does: *is this specific mode effective, and does any mode beat
another?* That is the mode-selection question Goh's pooled MA explicitly deferred to an unheld
companion network meta-analysis ->. **Scope
discipline:** a modifiable-lever appraisal (mode of exercise -> OA pain/function), not disease
management; patient-important QoL outcomes measured worst, so the register is honest uncertainty, not
confident advice -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]].

## Stationary cycling vs no exercise — real but modest; clinically meaningful only on pain and sport function

[@luan2020] Gold SR+MA: 11 RCTs / 724 participants with
knee OA found; 8 pooled. Effect measure is the **end-trial weighted mean difference (WMD)** in raw
points on the WOMAC and KOOS sub-scales (random effects), control = no exercise (education / usual
physical activity maintained). This is a different quantity from Goh's standardized mean difference
(see the saturation table below) — read the WMDs against each scale's own minimal clinically important
difference (MCID), not against Goh. The decision-legible readout is *does the gain clear MCID?*

| Outcome (scale) | WMD (95% CI) | I2 | P | MCID | Clears MCID? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pain (WOMAC) | 12.86 (6.90-18.81) | 0% | <0.0001 | 7.09 | yes at point; lower CI below |
| Pain (KOOS) | 6.87 (4.82-8.92) | 0% | <0.00001 | 6.05 | yes at point; lower CI below |
| Sport function (KOOS) | 8.06 (0.92-15.20) | 29% | 0.03 | 1.75 | yes at point; lower CI below |
| Daily living (KOOS) | 6.43 (3.19-9.66) | 0% | <0.0001 | 6.88 | no (below MCID) |
| Function (WOMAC) | 8.28 (2.44-14.11) | 11% | 0.005 | 11.25 | no (below MCID) |
| Stiffness (WOMAC) | 11.47 (4.69-18.25) | 0% | 0.0009 | 16.24 | no (below MCID) |
| Symptoms (KOOS) | 4.15 (-1.87-10.18) | 51% | 0.18 | 5.38 | no (NS and below) |
| Quality of life (KOOS) | 0.99 (-4.27-6.25) | 15% | 0.71 | 7.91 | no (NS, \~zero) |
| Mobility (6-min walk) | 13.37 (-26.43-53.18) | 0% | 0.51 | 36.98 | no (NS) |

- **The honest split:** cycling clears the clinical-meaningfulness bar only for **pain** and **sport
  function** at the point estimate — and even there «both the lower bounds of the 95% confidence
  intervals are less than their minimal clinically important difference values of 7.09 and 6.05
  respectively» [@luan2020], so the pain benefit is
  clinically meaningful on average but uncertain at the lower bound. Every other outcome is
  statistically significant (mostly) yet **below MCID**, or non-significant (symptoms, QoL, 6MWT).
  Luan's own conclusion: cycling «relieve[s] pain and improve[s] sport function ... but ... not ... as
  clinically effective for improving stiffness, daily activity, quality of life and mobility»
  [@luan2020].
- **Dose not characterized.** Pooled trials ran 4-12 weeks (most 8-12), 2-5x/week, 20-60 min, at
  40-80% MHR/HRR — heterogeneous, and no dose-response curve was estimated. Two included trials
  contrasted intensities (HIIT vs moderate-continuous; high vs low) but were not pooled, so no knee or
  threshold is located; treat intensity as an open sub-question, not a settled target.

## The mode-selection finding — no mode dominates on symptoms (the decision-change, type-C/F)

[@luan2020] Across every WOMAC and KOOS sub-scale and the
6-min walk, stationary cycling was «neither superior nor inferior to other exercises» (the head-to-head
comparators in the trials were swimming, treadmill, arm ergometer, Tai Chi, Baduanjin, land/water
treadmill) — all between-mode WMDs small and non-significant (e.g. WOMAC pain 2.37, CI -6.64-11.39,
P 0.61; WOMAC function -3.87, CI -11.52-3.78, P 0.32).

- **The clinical implication Luan draws:** «there are a number of exercises that may offer equivalent
  benefits ... those selected for exercise management can be the ones best able to accommodate the
  needs and preferences of the individual» [@luan2020].
  So mode selection is a **Layer-3 preference/constraint decision, not an efficacy decision** — choose
  by adherence, cost, access, and (the inference below) impact tolerance, because the symptom payoff is
  mode-agnostic over the modes tested.
- **This partially answers the mode-gap Goh deferred, without being the deferred source.** Goh pooled
  all modes and left *which mode is best* to an unheld network MA ->. Luan does not supply the full all-modes ranking, but it supplies the **null for one
  common mode vs the others** — a partial fill: the full relative-efficacy NMA is still owed, but the
  working answer *no mode clearly dominates on OA symptoms* now has direct backing.

## Low-impact angle for the obese / deconditioned / previously-injured knee (type-B, decision-relevant)

[inferred from @luan2020] Stationary cycling is non-weight-bearing and
low-impact — it loads the knee in **mid-range** motion with no ground-reaction impact. The nucleus and
-> [[Exercise Therapy for Osteoarthritis Pain and Function]] both flag the **obese, previously-injured
novice** as the explicitly-untested harm stratum and advise *favour lower-impact modalities there*
-> [[Exercise Interventions and Sports Injury Prevention]]. Luan's mode-equivalence null supplies the
missing half of that advice: because low-impact cycling is **neither superior nor inferior** to other
modes on the symptom outcomes, choosing it for the joint-limited / high-impact-intolerant knee costs
**nothing in efficacy** while avoiding the high-impact loading that could end the attempt (adherence /
route-(c) harm). This is the telos's exercise-programming taper for the untrained stratum made
concrete: modality choice gates injury-dropout, not marginal gains, and here the safe choice is
efficacy-equivalent. (The *causation* side — loading and incident OA — is a separate U-shaped question
-> [[Knee Osteoarthritis Incidence and Risk Factors]], [[The Physical Activity Paradox]].)

## Mechanism — why cycling moves pain but not stiffness or global function

[@luan2020] The within-mode outcome dissociation has a
proposed mechanistic account (the authors' hypothesis, not demonstrated):

- **Pain relief** is proposed to come from «the articular cartilage being repeatedly compressed then
  released during pedaling movements ... [which] may promote exchange of nutrients within the
  cartilage, increase the blood circulation in joints, and consequently be helpful in eliminating
  inflammation and relieving pain» -- a mechanism claim, not an outcome finding; the pain effect
  itself is the evidenced part.
- **Stiffness is NOT improved because the knee stays in mid-range.** The seat height keeps the joint
  off end-range flexion/extension, so cycling cannot train the ROM extremes stiffness lives at: «This
  finding challenges the traditionally-accepted clinical belief that stationary cycling undertaken as a
  therapeutic exercise can improve knee joint stiffness» [@luan2020]. A decision-change against a common clinical assumption — if the goal is stiffness/ROM,
  cycling is the wrong mode.
- **Global function / daily living / QoL under-respond** because cycling is a lower-limb-only
  closed-chain activity that does not build whole-body neuromuscular control, balance, or coordination
  — the capacities daily function draws on. This is why a mode can clear the pain bar yet
  miss the function/QoL bar: the trained capacity is not the limiting capacity for those outcomes.

## Saturation vs Goh — the parameter table (why this is a facet, not an echo)

[inferred from @luan2020] Before any
prose ranking Luan against -> [[Exercise Therapy for Osteoarthritis Pain and Function]] (goh2019), the
quantities must match. They do not — so the two are **not rankable against each other**, and Luan's
value is as a mode-specific facet, not a competing estimate:

| Parameter | Luan (this page) | Goh (pooled) | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Effect metric | WMD, raw WOMAC/KOOS points | SMD (Cohen's d) | **no** -- different scale |
| Intervention | stationary cycling only | all exercise modes pooled | **no** -- one mode vs all |
| Population | knee OA only | knee + hip OA | **no** |
| Comparator | no exercise / education | usual care (education/manual-therapy excluded) | \~similar |
| Pooled set | 8 cycling RCTs | 77 RCTs | overlapping -- cycling RCTs may sit inside Goh's 77 |
| Pain outcome | WOMAC 12.86 / KOOS 6.87 | SMD 0.56 | **no** -- cannot be compared directly |

- **Not type-E (independent backing).** Same field, same question-family, and Luan's 8 cycling RCTs
  plausibly **overlap** Goh's 77 all-mode trials — so the agreement is not independent routes; it would
  be laundered-E to claim convergence. The honest relation is **type-F refinement**: Luan narrows
  Goh's modality-agnostic pooled claim to a mode-specific estimate and adds the between-mode null, so
  the composite (Goh's pooled effect + Luan's mode resolution) says more than either alone.
- **Not a tension.** Cycling-vs-no-exercise positive is *consistent* with Goh's exercise-vs-usual-care
  positive; the between-mode null is *new structure on a deferred question*, not a disagreement
  (not-joined check (ii): different unit — one mode's effect vs all modes pooled).
- **Saturation verdict: KEEP** — Luan adds a modality-specific facet (mode-specific effect + outcome
  dissociation + the between-mode null that partially fills Goh's explicit mode gap), none of which a
  RAG over Goh reproduces.

## Limits and gaps (type-G)

[inferred from @luan2020]

- **Thin and short-horizon.** Only 8 small RCTs pooled (average PEDro 5.73, blinding unclear in
  several), GRADE moderate for most outcomes and low for some (heterogeneity + small samples);
  programs ran 4-12 weeks, so **nothing here speaks to durability** — and Goh shows exercise benefit
  for OA decays to no-better-than-usual-care by 9-18 months without maintenance. Luan itself notes
  «more large-scale trials are needed to clarify the short- and long-term effects»
  [@luan2020].
- **Harm / injury in the deconditioned knee is not directly measured.** The low-impact advantage above
  is inferred from the modality's biomechanics and the efficacy-equivalence null, not from a measured
  injury-dropout comparison — a named gap, not a demonstrated harm reduction.
- **Full mode ranking still owed.** The relative efficacy of all modes (aerobic / strengthening /
  aquatic / mind-body) remains the unheld NMA ->. Luan
  gives the cycling-vs-others null, not the ranking.
- **Coherence, not validity (R1):** the page reports effect sizes against MCID and a between-mode null;
  it does not grade cycling against a realized long-term disability or joint-replacement trajectory —
  the loop stays open.
