An **exogenous ketone supplement** — an orally-ingested ketone monoester or ketone salt — is a
distinct exposure from the ketogenic diet (see [[Ketogenic Diet and Endurance Performance]]) and from
carbohydrate restriction (see [[Carbohydrate Restriction and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]]): it raises blood
beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) *acutely* «without prolonged periods of fasting or adoption of a ketogenic
diet» [@falkenhain2022ketones]. The single held source
is a gold SR/MA (43 trials, 586 participants, 58 comparisons, published 2016-2021) — the first
systematic evaluation of what these supplements do to blood glucose. It is read here against the wiki's
own four-evidence-states discipline and the surrogate-vs-outcome rule, not at the source's own
enthusiasm (see *The source caveat* below).

**The decision this changes.** For someone considering a ketone supplement for glucose control /
metabolic health, the honest state is: a *robustly demonstrated movement of a surrogate* (acute glucose
lowering of about 0.5 mM) sitting on top of an **unbridged transmission gap** to every
patient-important outcome, with the evidence drawn almost entirely from the *wrong stratum* (healthy,
mostly young athletic men) and over the *wrong horizon* (single acute doses). Sorted by evidence state:

| Claim | State | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute BHB elevation** after ingestion | **established** (the exposure works) | within-group +1.73 mM; vs placebo +1.98 mM; monoester \~5x the salt |
| **Acute blood-glucose lowering** (a surrogate) | **benefit — on the surrogate only** | vs placebo -0.47 mM, I2=0% (consistent); within-group -0.54 mM (I2=94%) |
| **Durable glycaemic control** (HbA1c, mean daily glucose) | **insufficient evidence** | only 5 prolonged trials; fasting glucose unchanged; CGM/HbA1c signals in tiny studies |
| **Any patient-important outcome** (T2D incidence/progression, CV, mortality, function) | **insufficient evidence — not measured** | no trial reports one; the surrogate->outcome link is untested |
| **GI harm** (bloating, diarrhoea, nausea) | **harm — dose/form-dependent** | \~85% of salt comparisons vs \~40% of monoester comparisons reported AEs |

## The established surrogate movement — acute glucose lowering

The robust finding is the **between-condition** (vs a matched comparator) result: pooled glucose
MD = -0.47 mM (95% CI: -0.57, -0.36; P < 0.001) with **low, non-significant heterogeneity** (I2 = 0%),
a prediction interval of -0.90 to -0.04 mM
[@falkenhain2022ketones]. The within-group
(after vs before ingestion, fasted) estimate is larger, -0.54 mM (95% CI: -0.68, -0.40), but carries
very high heterogeneity (I2 = 94%) [@falkenhain2022ketones]. In magnitude terms, -0.5 mM is about -9 mg/dL
 — a modest acute dip.

**Form dominates dose.** «ketone monoesters increased blood BHB approximately 5 times higher and
lowered glucose approximately 3 times more than ketone salts»
[@falkenhain2022ketones]: between-condition glucose
-0.61 mM for monoesters vs -0.24 mM for salts (Table 2). What tracks the glucose fall is the **achieved
BHB concentration** (a significant study-level moderator, average-BHB-vs-glucose R2 = 59.2%), *not* the
administered dose — supplement dosage did not significantly moderate the glucose effect in
meta-regression (P = 0.86 monoester, P = 0.27 salt), though a coarse low-vs-high split did show a larger
fall at higher doses (low-dose MD -0.33 mM vs high-dose -0.63 mM across all comparisons, P < 0.01). So the
dose-response curve is **not cleanly characterised**: the lever is the BHB level reached, and the
supplement form is the main determinant of that.

## Why the surrogate does not carry the decision

**Blood glucose is a surrogate, and the transmission to a patient-important outcome is untested here.**
Per [[Surrogate Outcomes]], a target is legitimate only if its causal transmission to a named
patient-important outcome is *itself* evidenced — never assumed. Falkenhain measures the surrogate and
is explicit that the outcome link is not established: «more research is needed to determine if
supplementing with exogenous ketones is a viable therapeutic option for improving glycemic control»
[@falkenhain2022ketones]. Three gaps make the acute
surrogate finding decision-inert as it stands.

**Wrong stratum.** The population is overwhelmingly healthy-weight (and largely young, male, athletic):
«Fifty-one comparisons were conducted with participants at a healthy weight, 3 with participants with
overweight or obesity, 2 with participants with prediabetes, 1 with participants with T2D, and 1 with
participants with heart failure» [@falkenhain2022ketones]. The stratum where lowering glucose could plausibly matter (prediabetes / T2D) contributes 3 of 58
comparisons — a transportability hole, not a finding.

**Wrong horizon.** «The majority of evidence was obtained in acute, single-dose studies conducted in
controlled laboratory-based environments, and there was limited evidence on prolonged supplementation
with exogenous ketones, limiting our ability to draw conclusions about longer-term or clinically
relevant effects» [@falkenhain2022ketones]. Of the 5
prolonged (>1 day) trials: «no differences in fasting glucose following a period of exogenous ketone
supplementation were found, but trials assessing glucose via continuous or intermittent scanning glucose
monitoring found statistically significant decreases in mean daily glucose during thrice-daily
supplementation with ketone monoesters, which was also reflected in statistically significant decreases
in HbA1c in 2 studies and a decrease in fructosamine in 1 study»
[@falkenhain2022ketones]. That is an
insufficient-evidence signal on a downstream surrogate (HbA1c), not a patient-important-outcome result.

**Net effect vs intended effect.** The glucose-lowering «does not appear to be fully mediated by an
increase in insulin secretion, as infusion of ketones in the absence of insulin secretory function
lowers blood glucose as well»; the proposed mechanisms include suppressed hepatic glucose output and
BHB inhibiting lipolysis via GPR109A/HCAR2
[@falkenhain2022ketones].

## Harm, cost, and the realistic substitution

**GI distress is the consistent harm, and it is form-dependent** — «Of all comparisons using a ketone
salt and assessing adverse events, approximately 85% of comparisons reported adverse side effects, in
contrast to approximately 40% comparisons using a ketone monoester»; 3 participants across all studies
withdrew for supplement-related side effects
[@falkenhain2022ketones]. Salts additionally deliver a
sodium/calcium/potassium load.

## The source caveat

Read the source's optimistic framing against its own limitations. The between-condition glucose result
is genuinely consistent (I2 = 0%), but «Across analyses, we observed a large degree of heterogeneity
alongside wide prediction intervals ... This suggests uncertainty around the effect estimate, which
could indicate that certain populations may not experience the observed (BHB-raising and
glucose-lowering) effect» [@falkenhain2022ketones]. And
industry entanglement is pervasive: «many of the included studies may have had potential for a conflict
of interest (e.g., due to affiliation of authors to the ketone industry or provision of ketone
supplement free of charge to study authors)» [@falkenhain2022ketones] — the senior author also discloses founder shares in a metabolic-monitoring company.
`confidence: low` — single gold SR; the patient-important verdict is *insufficient evidence*, and the
surrogate result, though robust, is drawn from a population and horizon that do not transport to the
decision.
