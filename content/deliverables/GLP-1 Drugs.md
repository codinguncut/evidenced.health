The drug works. What it is worth to you depends almost entirely on who you are —
and that is the whole finding.

**A GLP-1 receptor agonist takes off more weight than any pill before it, and in the
sickest patients it also prevents hard events — heart attacks in those with established
heart disease, kidney failure and early death in those with diabetic kidney disease.** In
another group — someone near a normal weight taking it to look leaner — the same drug
delivers the weight change and *none* of the proven hard-outcome benefit, because that
benefit was never tested there. The lay question *do the benefits outweigh the risks?*
has no single answer: it splits into two decisions wearing one name.

**Treat weight and blood sugar as what these drugs reliably move, and treat a longer,
healthier life as a benefit that has to be earned in each group separately.** Weight
and HbA1c are markers; a marker moving is not an outcome changed. Where the evidence
follows the marker all the way to events (obesity with established heart disease;
diabetic kidney disease), the case is strong. Where it stops at the marker (cosmetic,
low-risk use), honesty stops there too.

**And the effect is a state you rent, not a cure you buy.** Stop the drug and most of
the weight returns, because the biology it overrides reasserts itself
-> [[Weight-Loss Maintenance and Metabolic Adaptation]]. The honest comparator is not a
68-week trial result but a strategy you can sustain for years, priced for cost, side
effects, muscle, and the exit.

## The same drug name hides two different decisions

**One molecule, two decisions — because the outcomes do not move together across
groups.** Separate the strata before anything else, establish each from its own
evidence, and ask whether they converge or diverge rather than assuming they behave
alike -> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]].

| | Stratum A — obesity with established CVD (or diabetic CKD) | Stratum B — near-normal BMI, low-risk / cosmetic |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline risk of a hard event | high | low |
| Proven hard-outcome benefit | **yes** (events, kidney, death — below) | **unproven** — not *disproven*, never tested |
| What is well-evidenced | events, glycaemia, weight, kidney, liver | weight and cardiometabolic **markers** only |
| The weight change itself | \~9-15% | \~15-21% (larger — younger, more to lose) |

**The tail that drives adoption is Stratum B, and it is exactly the group the hard
evidence does not reach.** Most people reaching for these drugs are closer to normal
weight than the trial populations that proved the outcomes. So the benefit they are
most likely to picture — a longer life, a heart attack averted — is the one least
supported for them.

## Weight is the lever these drugs reliably move

**On weight, the effect is large, fast and dependable — the best-evidenced thing the
class does.** In STEP-1, a primary-prevention obese population (no heart disease
required, mean age 46) lost, at 68 weeks, «−14.9%» of body weight versus «−2.4%» on
placebo, a difference of «−12.4 percentage points»; «50.5%» lost at least 15% and
«32.0%» lost at least 20% [@wilding2021].
Half the treated group lost a sixth of their body weight.

**Tirzepatide — a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist, a genuinely different molecule, not stronger
semaglutide — takes off more.** In SURMOUNT-1, doses of 5/10/15 mg produced «−15.0%»,
«−19.5%» and «−20.9%» versus «−3.1%» placebo, with «50%» and «57%» of the top two
doses losing at least 20% [@jastreboff2022].
The catch is that its evidence stops at weight: **tirzepatide has no cardiovascular-outcome
trial**, so its bigger number buys no proven event reduction.

**Weight is a surrogate, and a bigger surrogate is exactly where not to assume the
hard outcome follows.** The class resupplies a satiety signal the body loses after
weight loss, overriding the defended set-point from outside rather than persuading
anyone to eat less -> [[Weight-Loss Maintenance and Metabolic Adaptation]]. That is
why it works — and why it works only while taken.

## The heart-attack benefit is proven for one group only

**SELECT is the landmark: the first proof that treating obesity with a drug prevents
hard cardiovascular events — in secondary prevention.** Among 17,604 adults with
established cardiovascular disease and no diabetes, on top of standard care (90% on
statins), the primary composite of cardiovascular death, non-fatal MI and non-fatal
stroke fell to «569 of the 8803 patients (6.5%)» on semaglutide versus «701 of the
8801 patients (8.0%)» on placebo — «hazard ratio, 0.80; 95% confidence interval, 0.72
to 0.90; P<0.001» [@lincoff2023].
That is an absolute reduction of \~1.5 percentage points over \~3.3 years, a number
needed to treat of roughly 67 — real, and incremental to guideline therapy.

**Read the fine print: only the primary result is confirmed, and the population is
narrow.** The pre-specified hierarchy tested cardiovascular death next — «hazard ratio,
0.85; 95% CI, 0.71 to 1.01; P = 0.07» — which failed its gate, so «superiority testing
was not performed for the remaining confirmatory secondary end points»
[@lincoff2023]. All-cause
mortality «0.81 (0.71 to 0.93)» is directionally favourable but statistically
non-confirmatory; *semaglutide reduces mortality* is a common overreading of this
trial. Enrolment was «patients 45 years of age or older who had preexisting
cardiovascular disease and a body-mass index... of 27 or greater but no history of
diabetes» [@lincoff2023].

**The trialists draw the boundary themselves, and it is the load-bearing limit.** «An
important limitation of this trial is that we included only patients with preexisting
cardiovascular disease. The effects of semaglutide on primary prevention of
cardiovascular events in persons with overweight or obesity but without previous
atherosclerotic disease were not studied.»
[@lincoff2023] For a
low-risk person this is the *insufficient-evidence* state, not *no effect* — the drug
was not shown to stop working, it was never tested there.

**Even if the relative effect transported, the absolute benefit shrinks toward the
floor.** GRADE states the arithmetic: «Recommendations, however, may differ across
subgroups of patients at different baseline risk of an outcome, despite there being a
single relative risk that applies to all of them.» [@grade]
A person at a fifth of SELECT's baseline risk gets about a fifth of the absolute
benefit from the same hazard ratio -> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]].

**And weight loss by itself is not a guaranteed heart lever.** The largest lifestyle
trial, Look AHEAD, drove real weight loss in type 2 diabetes and still missed its
cardiovascular endpoint — «hazard ratio 0.95; 95% CI 0.83 to 1.09»
[@look2013]. The 54-trial
meta-analysis agrees on events — «risk ratio 0.93, 95% confidence interval 0.83 to
1.04» — while finding a separate, non-cardiac benefit: all-cause mortality «risk ratio
0.82, 95% confidence interval 0.71 to 0.95»
[@ma2017]. Ma's pool includes Look
AHEAD, so the two are consistent-with, not two independent confirmations. So no cardiometabolic
intervention the evidence holds delivers a large, *proven* cardiovascular-event
reduction for a genuinely low-risk person — a ceiling, and reporting it is a result,
not a failure -> [[Cardiometabolic Interventions and Hard CV Outcomes in Low-Risk People]].

## The drug prevents diabetes and protects failing kidneys

**In the prediabetic, it sharply slows the slide into diabetes.** In STEP-1's
primary-prevention population, «84.1%» of prediabetic participants on semaglutide
reverted to normoglycaemia by week 68 versus 47.8% on placebo
[@wilding2021]. This is a benefit on
a glycaemic threshold — a surrogate — not yet a demonstrated reduction in
diabetes-related events in this group, but a plausibly important one for an obese
person with impaired fasting glucose.

**In diabetic kidney disease it does something rarer — it moves hard kidney and
survival outcomes.** FLOW enrolled 3,533 adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic
kidney disease, already on maximal renin-angiotensin blockade, given semaglutide at
the 1.0 mg glycaemic dose. The primary composite of major kidney events fell — «hazard
ratio, 0.76; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.66 to 0.88; P = 0.0003», with «The number
of persons who would need to be treated over 3 years to prevent one primary-outcome
event was 20 (95% CI, 14 to 40)» — and all-cause death fell too: «hazard ratio, 0.80;
95% CI, 0.67 to 0.95, P = 0.01»
[@perkovic2024].

Read the endpoint precisely: it is a composite — hard kidney failure, a surrogate
eGFR-decline threshold, and death combined — and «the trial was not powered to separately
detect effects on kidney failure»
[@perkovic2024], so what is proven
is prevention of the *composite*, not of dialysis on its own.

Cardiovascular death was 0.71 (0.56 to 0.89), and the benefit was «unrelated to changes
in body weight» [@perkovic2024] —
a weight-independent kidney mechanism, with serious adverse events *lower* on the drug
(49.6% vs 53.8%).

**This is a third, distinct benefit — and it does not transport to the cosmetic user.**
FLOW's population is diabetic, albuminuric and old, with high absolute baseline risk;
its authors flag the result «may not be generalizable to other populations, such as
those at lower risk» [@perkovic2024].
It is also the same molecule and sponsor as SELECT and STEP — one programme adding a new
endpoint, not three independent proofs that *semaglutide works*
-> [[Semaglutide and Kidney Outcomes in Chronic Kidney Disease]].

## Fatty liver improves in step with the weight lost

**For metabolic fatty liver (MASLD), the drug's benefit is the weight it removes, dosed
to the stage you are treating.** EASL's ladder ties histology to sustained loss:
≥5% reduces liver fat, 7-10% improves inflammation (MASH), and ≥10% improves fibrosis —
«There is a dose-dependent association between the amount of weight loss and the extent
of improvement in biomarkers of liver damage.»
[@easl2024]. But the ladder stops short
of the worst disease: «evidence for an effect of weight reduction by lifestyle
modification on advanced fibrosis or cirrhosis is insufficient»
[@easl2024] -- it works on the reversible
stages, not established cirrhosis. GLP-1 weight loss is a pharmacological route onto that
same ladder, and the umbrella review below records a large MASH-resolution signal (odds
ratio 3.39).

**But the dominant risk MASLD carries is usually not the liver.** MASLD raises the risk
of «non-fatal cardiovascular disease (HR 1.40)» and «a more than two-fold increased risk
of an incident diagnosis of T2D» [@easl2024].
So for the metabolic-syndrome person, fatty liver adds a *reason and a target* to weight
loss already indicated for the heart — not a separate intervention
-> [[Fatty Liver MASLD and Weight Loss]].

## Most side effects are gut; the feared cancers have not surfaced in the trials

**The risk ledger is graded, and its two loudest fears do not survive scrutiny.** The
single gold-tier synthesis is an umbrella review — «A total of 60 meta-analyses
representing 116 unique adverse health outcomes were included, comprising 1751
randomized clinical trials and 3 580 616 participants.»
[@yang2026]. It grades each
outcome on two axes that can diverge — quality of evidence, and how robust the
association is to bias — so a headline quoting one is misleading.

**The one robust harm is gastrointestinal, and it is the real adherence tax.** Nausea
2.47 (1.84-3.34), vomiting 2.78 (1.91-4.06), and diarrhea 1.94 (1.52-2.49)
[@yang2026] — mostly transient,
dose-dependent, and the dominant reason people stop: in STEP-1, «(7.0% vs. 3.1%)
discontinued treatment owing to adverse events (mainly gastrointestinal events)»
[@wilding2021].

**But the serious-event balance flips by stratum — and the low-risk group nets more harm,
not less.** In FLOW's high-risk diabetic-CKD population serious adverse events were
*lower* on the drug (49.6% vs 53.8%); in STEP-1's lower-risk obese population they ran
*higher*: «Serious adverse events were reported in 9.8% and 6.4% of semaglutide and
placebo participants, respectively», the excess driven by «serious gastrointestinal
disorders (1.4% of participants in the semaglutide group and 0% in the placebo group)»
and «hepatobiliary disorders (1.3% with semaglutide and 0.2% with placebo)»
[@wilding2021]. Where there are few
events to prevent, the drug *adds* serious events rather than averting them — the mirror
image of the Stratum-A benefit, and a Stratum-B harm the headline weight number hides.

**The cancer scares fail the credibility bar.** «Cancer outcomes showed no associations
meeting the prespecified credibility thresholds despite several nominal signals.»
[@yang2026]. Thyroid cancer —
the rodent-derived worry — is 1.43 (0.95-2.13), not significant; the human signal is
absent, though absence in short-to-medium trials is not a long-latency all-clear.

**Several off-target signals point the *protective* way, suggestive not confirmed.**
Serious infections 0.89 (0.87-0.92), fracture 0.67 (0.52-0.87), all-cause dementia 0.55
(0.35-0.87) [@yang2026] — the
fracture reduction matters because it partly offsets the fall worry that fast weight loss
raises. Genuinely elevated but credibility-demoted: gallbladder/biliary disease 1.34
(1.16-1.55) and reflux 2.19 (1.65-2.90).

**Pancreatitis is the unsettled case — a question of causality, not of robustness.** Yang
finds «an elevated likelihood of pancreatitis and gallbladder or biliary diseases», but
flags that «the scientific consensus on the causal association between GLP-1 RAs and
pancreatitis remains unsettled» -- a 2025 propensity-matched US analysis reported no
increased risk after comorbidity adjustment while a 2026 UK safety update flagged rare
fatal cases [@yang2026]. These
are conflicting cohorts, not a prediction-interval technicality, so the posture is
targeted monitoring against a recognised regulatory signal, not dismissal.

**Grade before you fear — and know that most of this ledger is fragile.** In
leave-one-out reanalysis, «the direction of treatment effect flipped in approximately 9
of 39 outcomes, and statistical significance was lost in 6 of 39 outcomes after the
removal of a single trial» [@yang2026].
The class «should not be misconstrued as a panacea» — nor the poison its other narrative
claims -> [[GLP-1 Non-Cardiometabolic Effects and Safety]].

## Fast weight loss costs muscle — and who that harms depends on the person

**Rapid weight loss sheds lean mass, and the finding is two-faced — both faces true at
once.** As a proportion of total weight, lean mass *rises* — «an observed increase of
1.81% (95% CI: 1.1– 2.52...)» — because fat is lost faster than muscle. Yet in absolute
terms muscle falls — «a signiﬁcant decrease... in the absolute change in lean mass
(−1.74 kg; 95% CI: −3.04 to −0.45...)» and «−3.06%», with semaglutide the outlier at
«−5.44 kg» and «−9.9%» [@laverde2026].
On average the class loses about 30% of weight as lean — ordinary for fast loss — but
semaglutide runs «up to 45%», above the usual band
[@laverde2026].

**Which face matters depends on whose muscle it is.** For a young, muscular obese adult,
the ratio governs — composition improves and the loss is minor. For an older or
sarcopenia-risk adult, the absolute loss is a front-line harm (falls, fractures, lost
independence), and the drug's appetite suppression works *against* the defence, cutting
protein intake exactly when more is needed. That stratum is the one the trials did not
study: «relatively young populations with a low burden of comorbidities... the effects
in... older and multimorbid populations, should be evaluated in future studies»
[@laverde2026].

**Lean mass is not function, and function is what people care about — it was not
measured.** «the included studies did not evaluate functional outcomes, such as muscle
strength or physical capacity. As a result, the clinical interpretation of the observed
changes is limited» [@laverde2026]. This
is *insufficient evidence on function*, not proof of safety or harm — and it matters
because strength predicts adverse outcomes better than mass: modern sarcopenia guidance
puts it first, since «muscle strength comes to the forefront, as it is recognised that
strength is better than mass in predicting adverse outcomes»
[@cruzjentoft2019].

**The defence is not novel — it is the ordinary muscle-sparing toolkit.** Resistance
training plus adequate protein preserves muscle against any deficit: «Protein
supplementation beyond a total daily protein intake of \~1.6 g/kg/day during RET provided
no further benefit on gains in muscle mass or strength»
[@morton2018], with
resistance training the driver and protein a modest adjunct. Judge the *complete strategy*
— drug plus training plus protein — not the drug's naked body-composition number
-> [[GLP-1 and Lean Mass]].

## Stopping the drug gives most of the weight back

**The effect is contingent on continuation — this is a maintained state, not a one-time
correction.** When STEP-1 participants stopped both semaglutide and the lifestyle
programme, they «regained a mean of 11.6 percentage points (SD: 7.7) of body weight in
the semaglutide arm versus 1.9 percentage points (SD: 4.8) in the placebo arm» over the
following year — about two-thirds of the loss — and their cardiometabolic gains reverted,
so «ongoing treatment is required to maintain improvements in weight and health»
[@wilding2022]. One confound keeps this below a
clean drug-cessation reading: the extension stopped the lifestyle programme too, and its
«absence of structured lifestyle intervention following semaglutide withdrawal ... may
also have contributed to the trajectory of weight regain»
[@wilding2022], so part of the rebound is the
loss of both props at once.

**The regain is defended physiology, not a lapse of resolve.** After weight loss the
body's satiety signals stay suppressed and its hunger signal elevated for at least a
year; removing an exogenous satiety drug lets that deficit reassert
-> [[Weight-Loss Maintenance and Metabolic Adaptation]]. The honest comparator to a
lifestyle change is therefore a lifetime strategy — including the exit — priced for
cost, tolerability and reversibility, not a 68-week trial result.

## New molecules and higher doses reset the safety clock

**_Years of use without a scare_ reassures only for a *stable* exposure — and this one
drifts under a constant label.** The mature multi-year record sits on one older molecule
(semaglutide), while prescribing moves to newer agents adding new receptor targets
(tirzepatide's GIP), and the anchoring trials ran at lower glycaemic doses than obesity
use. The umbrella could not check either drift: agent-specific analyses «included few
studies and should be interpreted as exploratory», and «inconsistent reporting in primary
trials precluded detailed stratification by dose or treatment duration»
[@yang2026].

**Accrued years de-risk the specific compound at the specific dose that accrued them —
not a new molecule or a higher dose wearing the same class label.** The shared GLP-1
mechanism (the gut cluster, the recognised gallbladder signal) is near-mature and does
not restart; what resets is whatever just changed. So the honest reading is not *the
clock is at zero* but *at zero for what changed, near-mature for the shared mechanism*.


## Cost and access sit on a different axis this page will not price

**These drugs carry a real load on cost, supply and access — a *different axis* from
health, and this page names it without pricing it.** The wiki holds no cost,
affordability or equity data, and folding one silently into a health reading is the same
defect as an undisclosed weighting. That the trade-off exists, and runs against
affordability and durable access, is recorded; how to weigh it against the health effect
is the reader's call at the point of decision
-> [[Which Objective Moved This Recommendation]].

## Rank the drug by what it proves, and name where the evidence runs out

**Rank interventions by expected effect size times certainty, per stratum — and where a
big rock stands, it dominates.** For obesity with established cardiovascular disease or
diabetic kidney disease, a GLP-1 is a large, well-evidenced lever on patient-important
outcomes and ranks near the top. For a low-risk cosmetic user, its proven levers are
weight and cardiometabolic markers; the hard-outcome benefit is unproven, and *that
ceiling is itself the finding* -> [[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]].

**Three things the evidence cannot yet tell you — named, because silence is not
reassurance:**

- **Lifetime and long-latency outcomes are insufficient by construction.** Cancer, bone,
  cardiovascular risk after discontinuation, pregnancy and adolescent development sit in
  *insufficient evidence*, not *no effect*: the evidence horizon is a few years, the use
  horizon is decades. More searching now will not close this.
- **Whether the muscle and facial-adipose loss is drug-specific or generic to fast loss
  is open.** Settling it needs a same-magnitude-loss comparator (diet or bariatric vs
  drug) that is not held; the attribution stays open.
- **Unknown unknowns are unquantifiable.** The honest proxy is the evidence-horizon vs
  use-horizon mismatch, not an invented risk number.

## The bottom line

**If you have obesity plus established cardiovascular disease, or diabetic kidney
disease:** the case is strong. Semaglutide adds a proven, incremental reduction in hard
events (SELECT MACE HR 0.80; NNT \~67 over \~3.3 years) or in kidney failure and death
(FLOW NNT 20 over 3 years), on top of guideline therapy, with a net-favourable
serious-adverse-event profile. Treat it as a maintained therapy, defend muscle with
training and protein, and expect the gut side effects.

**If you are near a normal weight and want to look leaner:** expect the weight change
(\~15-21%) and the marker improvements — and know the proven heart, kidney and survival
benefits were established in sicker people and do *not* transport to you. That is
*unproven*, not *disproven*; act on the outcomes the drug demonstrably moves, not on a
longevity benefit the evidence has not promised you.

**In either case:** the effect lasts only while you take it — plan for years, or plan
for the regain. Pair any GLP-1 weight loss with resistance training and adequate protein,
and monitor function, not just the scale, wherever muscle is the binding constraint.
Cost, supply and access are a separate axis you weigh yourself.

**Mandatory caveats.**

- **The loop is open.** This page grades coherence and fidelity to its sources, never
  validity against realized outcomes. No operation here tested whether acting on it makes
  anyone better off.
- **It appraises, it does not prescribe.** Selecting, dosing, titrating, screening
  contraindications and managing interactions need your labs, medications and history.
  Not medical advice.
- **Findings are stratum-level, not personal.** An individual causal effect is not
  identifiable; each claim places you in the narrowest group the evidence supports and
  reasons about how you differ from it.
- **One health axis only.** Cost, access, supply and equity are named, never priced into
  the health reading.
- **The risk ledger is graded but not complete.** The tirzepatide cardiovascular-outcome
  trial is still awaited, and the
  long-horizon, lifetime and off-label exposures remain unknowable until time passes.
