*This page has been folded. Fermented foods are not one exposure — the lay question splits along the
food that was fermented, and each strand now lives with the food it belongs to.*

- **The gut / live-culture question** — is the effect the live cultures, and must they be alive? —
  now lives in [[Gut Microbiome]]. It carries the live-versus-pasteurized discriminator (no held source
  runs the dead-culture control), the strain- and dose-specific bar that a mixed live ferment does not
  obviously clear (WGO), the Wastyk diversity finding read as indirect remodeling rather than
  colonization, and the trendy ferments (kimchi, kombucha, sauerkraut, kefir) as a named
  insufficient-evidence gap — unstudied, not shown inert.
- **Fermented dairy** — the yogurt and fermented-milk cardiovascular and type-2-diabetes associations
  (Zhang, Guo, Gijsbers) — is a dairy question, worked in [[Dairy]] alongside the rest of the dairy
  subtypes, where the non-independence of the overlapping cohorts is handled in one place.
- **Fermented soy** (miso, tempeh, natto) is a distinct exposure appraised in [[Soy Products]], not
  covered by the dairy evidence.

The short answer is unchanged: **the fermented-food health halo runs well ahead of what has been
measured.** Fermented dairy carries a modest observational signal that is really a dairy signal; the
fashionable ferments have almost no hard-outcome human evidence (insufficient evidence, not a null);
and whether the *live cultures* are doing the work is untested. Treat the halo as a claim to check, not
a credential — and note this is a small lever, well below the big rocks.
