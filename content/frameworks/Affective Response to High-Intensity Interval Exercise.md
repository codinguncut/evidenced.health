**The decision this page answers.** HIIE is promoted as a time-efficient route to fitness
([[Measuring and Raising Cardiorespiratory Fitness]], [[HIIT vs Continuous Training for Type 2 Diabetes]]). The standing objection is that it *feels worse*, and an exercise not done has no effect
(the schema's *adherence is part of the effect* rule). So the question is a **substitution** one — HIIE
versus continuous moderate exercise (MICE), the realistic alternative — judged on in-task affect, not
on whether to exercise at all. This page holds the affective evidence; whether the worse feeling
actually lowers uptake is a separate, still-open endpoint (below).

**What the source is.** Niven 2020 is a gold systematic review and meta-analysis of **33 studies** of
*acute* affective responses, comparing HIIE against MICE and against vigorous-intensity continuous
exercise (VICE), with affect pooled by the single-item 10-point Feeling Scale (FS; Hardy & Rejeski
1989) at matched time points — during (\~50% duration), end, immediately post, post+ (>2 min after),
and the lowest value reached [@niven2020].

## The effect table — HIIE minus the continuous comparator

A **negative** affect MD favours the comparator (HIIE felt less pleasant); a **positive** arousal MD
means HIIE was more activated; a positive enjoyment MD favours HIIE. Affect and arousal are FS/FAS
points (roughly an 11-point bipolar scale); enjoyment is on a separate 0-100-type scale, so its
magnitude is **not comparable** to the affect points
[@niven2020].

| Outcome | Comparison | Time point | MD (95% CI) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Affect (FS) | HIIE vs MICE | during (\~50%) | -1.07 [-1.47, -0.67] | favours MICE |
| Affect (FS) | HIIE vs MICE | end (100%) | -0.72 [-1.64, 0.20] | no difference |
| Affect (FS) | HIIE vs MICE | post | -1.07 [-1.44, -0.70] | favours MICE |
| Affect (FS) | HIIE vs MICE | post+ (5-20 min) | -0.22 [-0.40, -0.04] | favours MICE (small) |
| Affect (FS) | HIIE vs MICE | lowest value | -1.18 [-1.96, -0.41] | favours MICE |
| Affect (FS) | HIIE vs VICE | all five points | 0.13 to 0.95, all NS | no difference |
| Arousal (FAS) | HIIE vs MICE | during (\~50%) | +1.08 [0.71, 1.44] | higher in HIIE |
| Arousal (FAS) | HIIE vs MICE | end | +1.68 [1.11, 2.25] | higher in HIIE |
| Arousal (FAS) | HIIE vs MICE | post | +0.62 [0.23, 1.00] | higher in HIIE |
| Arousal (FAS) | HIIE vs MICE | post+ | +0.24 [-0.05, 0.54] | no difference |
| Enjoyment | HIIE vs MICE | post | +6.73 [2.45, 11.01] | favours HIIE |

All analyses carried a large degree of heterogeneity [@niven2020]. HIIE-vs-VICE affect was non-significant at every one of the five time points (50%: 0.33
[-0.46, 1.12]; end: 0.36 [-0.99, 1.71]; post: 0.95 [-0.16, 2.06]; post+: 0.15 [-0.40, 0.69]; lowest:
0.13 [-1.08, 1.34]) [@niven2020].

## The load-bearing read: it is the intensity, not the intervals

The direction is consistent — HIIE is felt as **less pleasant than MICE** during, immediately after,
at its lowest point, and still (barely) at post+ — yet **HIIE and VICE were indistinguishable** on
affect throughout. Niven draws the inference the decision turns on: «Affective responses to HIIE are
similar to responses to VICE during and after exercise, perhaps suggesting that the intensity may
explain the responses, rather than the interval or continuous nature of the activity.»
[@niven2020]

The consequence for a recommendation: the aversion is a **dose effect of intensity**, not a property
of the interval format. The interval structure's recovery periods do not buy back the pleasantness —
so *do intervals instead of hard continuous work to feel better* is not supported, while *the
discomfort is what high intensity costs, by whatever route* is.

## No post-exercise rebound to positive — against Dual Mode Theory

Dual Mode Theory predicts that a decline in affect *during* hard exercise rebounds to *positive*
affect afterwards, which would rescue adherence. This MA finds the opposite: «It was unexpected that
at post and post+ exercise the findings indicated a response in favour of MICE, when DMT would suggest
that a decline in affect during exercise would rebound to positive affect following activity.»
[@niven2020] The MICE advantage shrinks over time (post+ MD
-0.22) but does not flip. The *you'll be glad you did it* rebound is not evident against the
continuous-moderate alternative.

## The enjoyment paradox — real, but discounted for publication bias

Post-exercise *enjoyment* (a retrospective affective judgement, distinct from in-task valence) ran the
other way, favouring HIIE (MD +6.73 [2.45, 11.01]). This is a **within-source distinction, not a
tension**: in-task displeasure and recalled enjoyment are different constructs measured on different
scales at different moments, and both can hold. Two cautions keep it from carrying weight on its own:
the enjoyment pool was heterogeneous with only 8/15 studies favouring HIIE, and «Inspection of the
funnel plot for post-exercise enjoyment (HIIE vs MICE) suggested some asymmetry in favour of positive
findings of HIIE» [@niven2020] — i.e. likely publication
bias inflating the pro-HIIE enjoyment signal.

## Arousal on its own is not a decision input

HIIE raised arousal (FAS) during and at end of exercise, as expected for harder work. Niven warns
against reading this as an affective verdict: «the assessment of arousal on its own is not indicative
of an affective response and therefore has limited value. Indeed, it is unsurprising that participants
working at a higher intensity report higher arousal.» [@niven2020] Arousal matters only paired with valence (the circumplex model) — high arousal with
unpleasant valence is tension, with pleasant valence is energy. The decision-relevant axis here is
valence, which favours MICE.

## The gap that blocks the adherence claim

The direction on *feeling* is settled; the step to *behaviour* is not. Niven is explicit that the
affect-to-future-behaviour link, established for moderate exercise, does not transfer cleanly to HIIE:
«A recent study reported that in-task affect during MICE was related to subsequent MICE behaviour in
the next 4-weeks, but this relationship was not evident for two HIIE conditions (Stork et al., 2018).»
[@niven2020] So *HIIE feels worse, therefore people will do
less of it* is **plausible but unevidenced** — the affective substrate is confirmed, the behavioural
consequence is a named gap.

Three further limits keep confidence **low**:

- **No moderator analysis for the target stratum.** The MA does not separately estimate the
  sedentary/novice person — the very stratum for whom the inactivity rock is present and adherence is
  the binding constraint. The pooled effect averages across fitness levels, so the sedentary-specific
  affect gradient is a type-G gap. A staged companion on the adjacent adherence lever —
  whether letting people *self-select* intensity softens the affective cost versus an *imposed* dose —
  may bear on this but does not stratify by fitness.
- **Acute and lab-bound.** Every comparison is a single or short-run bout, mostly cycle-ergometer, in
  the laboratory — not free-living, repeated, self-selected exercise over months, which is where
  adherence actually plays out.
- **Heterogeneity is large throughout**, so each pooled MD is a direction with a wide real spread, not
  a precise number.

## Where this sits in the lever stack

For the sedentary person the big rock is doing *any* activity at all ([[Physical Activity Dose and Mortality]]); modality and intensity are refinements below it. What this page adds at the margin: if
the choice is HIIE versus continuous moderate work and the person is intensity-averse, **the discomfort
is real, intensity-driven, and not rescued by a post-exercise rebound** — so prescribing hard intervals
to a reluctant novice spends an adherence budget that an easier sustained dose may not. It does **not**
establish that HIIE loses on long-run adherence; that endpoint is unheld. The affective substrate feeds
the open adherence question on [[Measuring and Raising Cardiorespiratory Fitness]] (Challenge #11) and
the adherence caveat on [[HIIT vs Continuous Training for Type 2 Diabetes]].
