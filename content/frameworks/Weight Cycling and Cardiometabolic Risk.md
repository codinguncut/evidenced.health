A very common belief — *yo-yo dieting is worse than staying heavy, so don't bother trying to lose weight*.
Three sources now anchor the answer, across two outcome families: a **narrative review** (Montani 2015,
tier moderate) foregrounding the controversy; a **gold SR/meta-analysis** of 23 cohort studies / 441,199
participants (Zou 2019) pooling the CVD-and-mortality magnitude Montani could not; and a second **gold
SR/meta-analysis** of 14 cohorts / 253,766 participants (Zou 2020) adding the **type-2-diabetes-incidence**
arm. The converging verdict is deflationary in the direction that matters for the decision: body-weight
fluctuation *is* associated with higher all-cause and CVD mortality and with incident T2D across cohorts,
but the association is **observational, inconsistent, and — for the CVD/mortality outcome — absent once
fluctuation is restricted to the INTENTIONAL kind** that an obese person choosing to diet would incur, and
**null in the frankly obese (BMI>=30) for the T2D outcome** — the very stratum the decision is about. No
source can license deterring an obese patient from attempting weight loss out of fear of cycling; Zou 2019's
authors say so in as many words.
[inferred from @zou2019fluctuation; @montani2015cycling; @zou2020cycling]

The two Zou meta-analyses are from the **same Wuhan / Tongji-Huazhong research group** (six shared authors;
Zou 2020 cites Zou 2019 as ref 46), so they are **complementary by outcome, never independent confirmation**
— the agreement below is NOT a type-E robustness claim (see the same-group note in the T2D section and in
Limits).

</div>

## The evidence is contested, and the associations attenuate on adjustment

Montani foregrounds the controversy rather than asserting a harm: «the topic of health consequences of
weight cycling has been the source of considerable controversy ... with ongoing debates about whether it
is prudent to even recommend that over- weight or obese adults should try to lose weight».
[@montani2015cycling]

- Early epidemiological studies suggested weight-fluctuation harm; **later studies did not confirm it**.
  In NHS II and a Framingham subset the weight-cycling -> type 2 diabetes association was no longer
  significant after adjusting for overall weight status; several large studies failed to confirm a
  weight-cycling -> CV-mortality link.
- There is **no universally accepted definition** of weight cycling (amplitude, number of cycles,
  intentional vs unintentional), so cross-study comparison is weak — a measurement problem, not a clean
  null.
  [@montani2015cycling]

## Who the signal is actually in — normal-weight, not obese

The decision-relevant nuance: «some of the potentially negative health consequences of repeated dieting
and weight cycling are more readily seen in people of normal body weight rather than in those who are
overweight or obese». The metabolic-syndrome / hyperinsulinaemia / dyslipidaemia associations reached
significance **only in BMI<25 subgroups** and were lost above BMI 25; the author's summary is that it is
«weight cycling resulting from repetitive intentional weight loss in the young and primarily normal-weight
population groups that seems to be more strongly associated with risks».
[@montani2015cycling]

This population split matters because the driver differs: normal-weight cyclers diet for **body image**
(media/social pressure, weight-sensitive sport), not for a metabolic indication — a confounding structure
quite unlike an obese patient losing weight on medical advice. It also predicts future weight gain in the
lean (dose-dependent with cycle count).

## The pooled magnitude — a gold SR/MA that confirms the SHAPE and cashes the confounding

Zou 2019 pools 23 cohort studies (441,199 participants) and gives the numbers the narrative could not
[@zou2019fluctuation]:

| Outcome | RR (95% CI) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| All-cause mortality | 1.41 (1.27-1.57) | I2=78% (high heterogeneity); **corrected to 1.18 (1.05-1.32)** after trim-and-fill for the one outcome with publication bias |
| CVD mortality | 1.36 (1.22-1.52) | I2=32% |
| CVD morbidity | 1.49 (1.26-1.76) | secondary outcome |
| Hypertension | 1.35 (1.14-1.61) | secondary outcome |
| Cancer mortality | 1.01 (0.90-1.13) | NULL — a specificity signal against a generic frailty confounder |

Three features carry the decision, and all three deflate the naive *cycling-is-dangerous* reading:

- **Publication bias inflates the headline.** All-cause mortality was the ONLY outcome flagging bias
  (Egger P=0.001, Begg P=0.014); trim-and-fill deflates it from RR 1.41 to «RR, 1.18, 95% CI 1.05-1.32»
  — the authors note this «suggested the existence of potentially negative studies.»
  [@zou2019fluctuation] State the corrected figure,
  not 1.41, at point of use.
- **The inconsistency is in the data, not just the framing.** 21 of 28 reports were positive, but the 7
  null reports held >60% of participants and events — so the pooled point sits on top of a genuinely
  split literature. [@zou2019fluctuation]
- **Intentional cycling shows NO association — the decision-relevant subgroup is null.** Restricted to
  intentional fluctuation, the associations vanish: all-cause mortality P=0.49, CVD mortality P=0.12.
  «Limited studies demonstrated that intentional weight ﬂuctuation was not associated with an increased
  risk of all- cause and CVD mortality or hypertension in our study.» The positive signal concentrates
  in **unintentional** fluctuation (all-cause P=0.016) — but from only 3 reports / 2,975 participants,
  which the authors say «limited the conclusion.»
  [@zou2019fluctuation] This is exactly Montani's
  confounding-by-indication concern cashed out: illness-driven (unintentional) loss, not chosen dieting,
  carries the mortality signal — the prior MAs Zou cites show intentional loss lowering mortality and
  unintentional loss raising it. [inferred from @zou2019fluctuation; @montani2015cycling]

### Montani (narrative) vs Zou (SR/MA) — matched parameters

| Parameter | Montani 2015 (narrative) | Zou 2019 (SR/MA) | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall harm verdict | controversial, attenuates on adjustment, inconsistent | pooled RR 1.36-1.49 significant BUT 7 null reports hold >60% of events; corrected all-cause RR 1.18 | **PARTIAL** — both foreground inconsistency; Zou adds a magnitude and confirms the inconsistency Montani asserted qualitatively (F-refinement, not a clash) |
| Confounding by intentionality | main threat named; body-image dieting vs medical loss | intentional subgroup NULL; unintentional carries the signal (small n) | **YES, same axis** — Zou is the pooled subgroup test Montani's hypothesis predicted |
| Reverse causation | foregrounded | «the ﬂuctuation in weight could be the consequence, and not the cause» | **YES** — both name it |
| Where the signal sits by BMI | normal-weight (<25); metsyn/insulin/lipid markers lost above BMI 25 | all-cause: at ALL BMI; CVD mortality: normal/overweight and <60y, NOT BMI>=30 | **NO — different outcomes/designs** (Montani = cross-sectional metabolic-syndrome markers; Zou = cohort MORTALITY). A distinction, not a joined tension; they partially converge in that neither locates the strongest signal in the frankly obese |
| Decision for the obese patient | fear of cycling not a reason to avoid intentional loss | «we cannot conclude whether obese individuals should be deterred ... by concerns about the hazards of weight cycling» | **YES, same decision** — Zou adds gold warrant |

The BMI-split row is a **distinction, not a tension**: Montani's normal-weight signal is on cross-sectional
metabolic-syndrome markers, Zou's is on cohort mortality — different outcomes on different designs, so they
are not joined (they cannot be the same quantity). They do converge weakly in that neither places the
strongest harm signal in the frankly obese, which is the population the decision is about.
[inferred from @zou2019fluctuation; @montani2015cycling]

## The T2D-incidence arm — a second gold SR/MA (same group, different outcome)

Zou 2020 pools **14 cohort studies / 253,766 participants / 8,904 new-onset diabetes events** (21 reports,
since Yokomichi 2017 supplied 8) and asks the diabetes question the CVD/mortality MA did not
[@zou2020cycling]:

| Stratum | RR (95% CI) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Overall (all reports) | 1.23 (1.07-1.41), P=0.003 | I2=73.9% (high heterogeneity) |
| Overall, omitting Yokomichi | 1.41 (1.25-1.59), P<0.001 | Yokomichi's 8 null reports used a small (>4%) cycle amplitude that «might not have shown any adverse effect» |
| Overweight (25<=BMI<30) | 1.62 (1.31-2.00), P<0.001 | I2=0 — where the T2D signal concentrates |
| Obese (BMI>=30) | 1.47 (0.96-2.24), P=0.55 (full); P=0.08 omitting Yokomichi | **NULL — the decision-relevant stratum** |
| Intentional weight loss | 1.23 (1.16-1.31), P<0.001 | I2=0 but only **3 reports** — «the small number of studies limits the conclusion» |

Three features carry the decision:

- **The obese stratum is NULL — the cell the decision is actually about.** «the association between weight
  cycling and the risk of developing diabetes was not observed in obese participants (body mass index >=30
  kg/m2; P = 0.08)», and the null «was still detected in obese individuals (BMI >=30 kg/m2), even after
  removing the Yokomichi et al. study (P = 0.08)».
  [@zou2020cycling] The authors read this as obesity itself
  saturating the diabetes risk so cycling adds nothing detectable on top. So for an obese person choosing
  to diet, this MA shows no measurable weight-cycling penalty on T2D incidence. State the obese-null, not
  the pooled 1.23, at point of use for that stratum.
- **No publication bias — unlike the CVD arm.** «Egger's (P = 0.53) and Begg's (P = 0.81) tests suggested
  that there was no significant publication bias.» [@zou2020cycling]
  (Contrast Zou 2019, where all-cause mortality flagged bias and needed trim-and-fill.) The T2D point
  estimate therefore needs no headline correction, but heterogeneity stays high (I2=73.9%).
- **Reverse causation is left open by the authors themselves.** «weight cycling might be a marker of
  potential illnesses that have worse prognoses ... weight cycling could be the early sign and consequence,
  and not the cause, of the health endpoints, including diabetes», with depression / bipolar disorder named
  as disorders that both drive cycling and raise diabetes risk.
  [@zou2020cycling] The conclusion is explicitly non-causal:
  «Future physiological studies are required to show the causal links».
  [@zou2020cycling]

**Symmetric-standards note:** Zou 2020's own summary language is *stronger* than the CVD paper's — its
abstract calls weight cycling «a strong independent predictor of new-onset diabetes».
[@zou2020cycling] That verdict is not wrong on its own terms
(the pooled association is real and survives sensitivity analysis), but it is **observational, causally
disclaimed by the same authors, and null in the obese** — so it does not transport to the decision as a
deterrent. Report the association *and* its obese-null and reverse-causation caveats together.

### Intentional cycling: T2D DIFFERS from CVD/mortality — do not merge them

The intentional-weight-loss subgroup runs the **opposite way** across the two Zou outcomes, and the
different outcome means this is a distinction, not a contradiction to reconcile. For **CVD/mortality**
(Zou 2019) intentional fluctuation was NULL (all-cause P=0.49, CVD P=0.12). For **T2D** (Zou 2020) the
intentional subgroup is *positive* — «individuals with intentional weight cycling had a higher risk of
diabetes (P < 0.001) ... however, the small number of studies limits the conclusion».
[@zou2020cycling] Two guards keep this from over-reading:
**(i)** it rests on only 3 reports (I2=0), drawn from mixed-BMI, not-predominantly-obese populations —
not the obese stratum, which is null; **(ii)** it is a different endpoint from the CVD-arm intentional-null,
so the two cannot be pooled or read as a single *intentional cycling is safe/unsafe* verdict. The honest
composite for the obese decider is the **obese-stratum null on T2D**, not the small-n intentional-positive.

### Zou 2020 vs Zou 2019 — matched parameters (same group, different outcome; NOT type-E)

| Parameter | Zou 2019 (CVD/mortality SR/MA) | Zou 2020 (T2D SR/MA) | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary outcome | all-cause + CVD mortality/morbidity, hypertension | new-onset type-2 diabetes | **NO — different endpoints** |
| Pooled overall effect | all-cause RR 1.41 -> 1.18 corrected; CVD mort 1.36 | T2D RR 1.23 (1.07-1.41) | **NO** — not comparable across endpoints |
| Publication bias | present on all-cause (Egger P=0.001), trim-and-fill applied | none (Egger P=0.53, Begg P=0.81) | NO (different outcome); both tested, opposite result |
| Obese subgroup (BMI>=30) | CVD mortality NOT in BMI>=30 | T2D null (P=0.08) | **PARTIAL** — convergent DIRECTION (obese-null) on different outcomes; not one quantity |
| Intentional subgroup | NULL (all-cause P=0.49, CVD P=0.12) | POSITIVE 1.23 (1.16-1.31), small n | **NO** — different outcome, opposite direction |
| Reverse causation | foregrounded | foregrounded («consequence, and not the cause») | **YES, same axis** — both name it |
| Research group | Wuhan / Tongji-Huazhong | SAME group; Zou 2020 cites Zou 2019 (ref 46) | **SAME — non-independent (NOT type-E)** |

**Same-group NOT-E guard (load-bearing).** Zou 2019 and Zou 2020 share the Wuhan / Tongji-Huazhong group
(six overlapping authors) and Zou 2020 cites Zou 2019 as its own antecedent (ref 46), so the two agreeing
on obese-nulls and on reverse causation is **shared-lineage self-consistency, not independent
corroboration** — no type-E robustness claim spans them. They are a **different-outcome coverage extension**
(type-F/coverage: CVD/mortality + T2D), which is why the "same quantity?" column above is almost entirely NO.
A T2D meta-analysis from a non-overlapping group would be needed before any confidence gain from
convergence. [inferred from @zou2020cycling; @zou2019fluctuation]

**Absolute magnitude.** The MA reports relative risk only; crude pooled incidence is 8,904 / 253,766 = 3.5%
over a median follow-up ranging 2.5-32 years, so RR 1.23 implies on the order of +0.8 percentage points at
that (very heterogeneous) baseline — a rough figure, not a per-year absolute risk, and undefined for the
obese stratum where the effect is null. [inferred from @zou2020cycling] No dose-response
curve is estimable: weight cycling is categorical with «no single definition», and the one low-amplitude
(>4%) cohort (Yokomichi) drove the null reports — a hint that amplitude matters, but not a locatable knee.

## The proposed mechanism — *repeated overshoot* (a theory, not an outcome)

Montani's mechanistic frame: weight cycling drives **fluctuations of BP, heart rate, sympathetic activity,
renal filtration, glucose and lipids, with overshoots above normal during the regain phase** that may not
be compensated by the loss-phase decreases (the oscillations are asymmetric), adding cardiac load and
possible glomerular / vascular injury — invisible in a single cross-sectional snapshot.
[@montani2015cycling] This is a physiological
hypothesis with small human corroboration, not an outcome finding.

A separate, firmer strand: older dieters regain fat but not fat-free mass, so repeated cycling risks
**relative sarcopenic obesity** — a function/muscle concern that transports better than the CV one
-> [[Weight-Loss Maintenance and Metabolic Adaptation]].

## Decision relevance

- **Fear of weight cycling is not a good reason for an obese person to avoid intentional weight loss.** Now
  backed by TWO gold SR/MAs whose own authors decline the deterrent conclusion. Zou 2019 (CVD/mortality):
  «we cannot conclude whether obese individuals should be deterred from eﬀorts to control their body weight
  by concerns about the hazards of weight cycling.» [@zou2019fluctuation]
  Zou 2020 (T2D incidence): the diabetes association is **null in the obese stratum (BMI>=30, P=0.08)** — the
  very population the decision is about — and its authors disclaim causation. Across both outcomes the harm
  signal is observational, and for the obese decider it is either intentional-null (CVD/mortality) or
  obese-stratum-null (T2D), while the benefits of sustained loss in the obese/metabolically-ill are
  established (glycaemia, remission, MASLD, function)
  -> [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]], [[Total Diet Replacement and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]], [[Lifestyle vs Metformin for Diabetes Prevention]]. The honest framing: pursue *sustainable* loss;
  the cost of trying-and-partly-regaining is not shown to exceed the benefit of the loss achieved.
  [inferred from @zou2019fluctuation; @zou2020cycling]
- **The population where caution is better warranted is the opposite of the intuitive one:** lean, young,
  body-image-driven dieters (and weight-cutting athletes) — for whom repeated dieting predicts weight gain
  and carries the clearer metabolic signal, and for whom there is no metabolic indication to diet at all.
- **Prioritise maintenance design over loss magnitude.** Since the plausible harm is in the *cycling*, the
  lever that matters is a structured maintenance phase that prevents regain, not a bigger initial deficit
  -> [[Weight-Loss Maintenance and Metabolic Adaptation]].

## Limits

- **Confidence moderate — two gold SR/MAs now anchor the page, but the loop stays observational.** Zou 2019
  pools 441,199 participants (CVD/mortality) and Zou 2020 pools 253,766 (T2D incidence), together lifting the
  page well off `low`; it does NOT reach `high` because both are observational (no causal inference possible),
  Zou 2019's all-cause estimate is publication-bias-inflated (corrected RR 1.18) with high heterogeneity
  (I2=78%), Zou 2020's overall estimate carries I2=73.9% and its own authors disclaim causation, the two are
  from the SAME group (no independence gain — see NOT-E guard below), and the decision-relevant subgroups are
  thin (CVD intentional = 3 reports; T2D obese = 3 reports, null). The magnitude that WOULD settle it —
  fluctuation vs maintenance-of-obesity vs stable loss, head to head — «no cohort studies have directly
  compared.» [@zou2019fluctuation]
- **The *repeated overshoot* mechanism is a hypothesis**; no hard-outcome trial isolates weight cycling from
  the confounders (baseline health, unintentional loss from illness, dieting motivation). Zou explicitly
  keeps reverse causation open alongside the metabolic-disturbance pathway.
- **Lean-mass loss is unmeasured.** Zou tracked only weight, not waist circumference or body composition, so
  the sarcopenic-obesity strand -> [[Weight-Loss Maintenance and Metabolic Adaptation]] is not addressed by
  its pooling. [@zou2019fluctuation]
- **Coherence, not validity (R1):** the loop is open on whether cycling per se changes any patient-important
  outcome.
- **Same-group NOT-E guard:** Zou 2019 (CVD/mortality) and Zou 2020 (T2D) share the Wuhan / Tongji-Huazhong
  research group (six overlapping authors), and Zou 2020 cites Zou 2019 as its own antecedent (ref 46). They
  are therefore **complementary by outcome, not independent corroboration** — their agreement on obese-nulls and
  on reverse causation is shared-lineage self-consistency, NOT convergent confirmation, and no type-E
  robustness claim spans them. A T2D meta-analysis from a non-overlapping group is the gap.
- **T2D obese-null is small-n, not a proof of no effect (R1 / four-states).** BMI>=30 rests on 3 reports with
  I2=88.8% and P=0.08 (omitting Yokomichi); this is *insufficient-evidence-of-harm in the obese stratum*, not
  established *no-effect*. It licenses *no measurable T2D deterrent for the obese decider*, not a claim that
  *cycling is proven safe*. [@zou2020cycling]
