Acne vulgaris is a near-universal (lifetime incidence \~100%), puberty-associated skin disorder whose
lesions are patient-important on the physical-outcome menu — visible appearance, permanent scarring,
and the psychological load through the physical intersection [@meixiong2022acne]. It enters this wiki on the **modifiable-exposure -> patient-important-outcome** terms: does
a dietary lever change acne incidence/severity for an identified stratum. IN scope = the diet -> acne
question; OUT = per-patient dermatologic treatment (topical/systemic drug selection, isotretinoin
management — prescriber zone). This page is the nucleus of the diet-dermatology cluster (single gold-
adjacent source so far); it is a **provisional-C** opener, not a banked cross-source synthesis.

## Verdict — one exposure has RCTs, the other has none; do not read them at equal strength

The single held source (Meixiong 2022, a high-tier **narrative** SR of 34 studies — Newcastle-Ottawa
LOE scoring, **no pooled meta-analysis / no pooled effect estimate**) reaches a two-part conclusion
that a flat *diet affects acne* reading flattens. Sorted by the design behind each exposure:

- **Glycemic content (high-GI / high glycemic-load / sugar): the stronger, RCT-supported lever.** 77%
  (14/18) of observational studies found a positive association, and the positive ones were the
  higher-quality ones (LOE 6.5 vs 5.5 for null); crucially, the signal is «backed by numerous RCTs»
  showing low-GI diets improve acne [@meixiong2022acne]. Directional,
  bounded to teens/twenties, magnitude uncertain.
- **Dairy: observational-only, mixed, region-conditional.** **No RCT exists.** 70% of observational
  studies found a positive dairy signal, but positive and null studies were of **equal quality** (LOE
  6.1 vs 6.4), the null studies clustered outside the West, and one population showed milk *protective*.
  Low confidence, Western-stratum-conditional at most.

Both exposures act through the **same hormonal channel** (insulin / IGF-1 / androgens -> sebaceous
activation), which is why they are appraised together — but the evidence is asymmetric, and the
asymmetry is the decision-relevant finding.

## The mechanism through-line — insulin / IGF-1 / androgen

The unifying pathway makes both levers biologically plausible and gives *direction* (not an outcome
number): a Western diet raises insulin, IGF-1 and androgens, which activate sebaceous glands and drive
acnegenesis [@meixiong2022acne].

- High-GI diets raise postprandial insulin and IGF-1; «low-GI diets have been shown to decrease fasting
  IGF-1 concentrations» [@meixiong2022acne].
- Dairy consumers have higher IGF-1 and insulin; whey and casein each raise IGF-1 (a 2-y whey RCT raised
  serum IGF-1 by \~8%) [@meixiong2022acne].
- Hormonal anchor: exogenous androgens/GH cause acne; PCOS and acromegaly raise it; Laron syndrome
  (low IGF-1) shows near-absent acne [@meixiong2022acne]. This is the
  shared insulin->androgen mechanism that also drives [[PCOS and Modifiable Exposures]].

This is mechanism-with-human-corroboration -> admit **directionally**, discounted. The ecological *diseases-of-Western-civilization* observation (Kitavans, Inuit) is weaker
still — an observed-healthy-population pattern, not a component contrast.

## Glycemic content — the RCT-supported lever, bounded

Observational effects (all associations, self-report-heavy): soft-drink sugar >=100 g/d -> moderate-
severe acne aOR 3.12 (1.80-5.41; Huang, China, n=8197); frequent sugar aOR 1.3 (1.06-1.82; Koku Aksu,
Turkey); chocolate+sweets OR 2.38 (1.31-4.31; Wolkenstein); chocolate OR 1.48 (1.24-1.76; Karadag,
3826 cases + 759 controls) [@meixiong2022acne]. Several small/early studies null.

The interventional evidence — **all 6 trials are on GI/GL, none on dairy** — is what lifts glycemic
above dairy:

- Smith 2007 RCT (Australia, n=43 males, 12 wk): both arms improved, low-GL improved **more** —
  «the low-GI diet group experienced a significantly greater improvement compared with the control-diet
  group (inflammatory lesion counts: 16.0 with low GI vs 8.5 with control, P = .04; total lesion counts:
  22.0 ... with low GI vs 10.9 ... with control, P = .02)» [@meixiong2022acne] (numbers are lesion-count *reductions* — larger = greater improvement).
- Kwon 2012 RCT (Korea, n=32, investigator-blinded): low-GL improved lesions/severity to 70.9% of
  baseline vs control.
- Smith 2008: low-GL 59% vs control 38% lesion decrease (P=.046).
- The sole clearly-null RCT (Pavithra 2019, India, n=84) was confounded: all arms improved \~100% under
  concurrent topical benzoyl peroxide + trial counseling, plausibly masking a diet effect
  [@meixiong2022acne].

**Two caveats keep this at directional, not magnitude, confidence:** (1) three of the *6* trials
(Smith x3) reuse the same cohort, so the independent-RCT count is smaller than it looks (volume !=
independence); (2) the RCTs are small, short, mostly young **males**, while the observational base is
mostly **women** — a sex mismatch the source flags as problematic given sex-specific hormone effects.
Author verdict: high-GI/high-glycemic-content diets have a «modest yet signiﬁcant proacnegenic effect»
and a low-GI diet «may benefit» acne, «irrespective of the geographic region or country»
[@meixiong2022acne].

## Dairy — mixed, region-conditional, and the skim-beats-whole paradox

No RCT exists: «we could not identify any RCTs on the topic and, instead, had to base our conclusions
on the observational studies» [@meixiong2022acne]. The vote-count
(70% positive) hides two features that undercut it:

- **Symmetric quality.** «both positive and negative studies had similar LOE scores (6.1 vs 6.4,
  respectively)» [@meixiong2022acne] — unlike glycemic, the null dairy
  studies were as good as (slightly better than) the positive ones. The signal does not track quality.
- **Region/culture dependence.** Null studies clustered outside the US/Europe; one Singapore study
  found frequent milk *protective* (OR 0.572 [0.360-0.910]) and another linked more milk to *less*
  scarring. Verdict: dairy «may exacerbate acne among young people in nations with a prevailing Western
  diet ... In contrast, dairy is unlikely to exacerbate acne among youths from non-Western countries»
  [@meixiong2022acne].

**The skim-beats-whole paradox — *dairy* is not one exposure** -> [[Is the Food Category Doing Any Work]]. In the two Adebamowo cohorts the signal sat on **skim/low-fat** milk while **whole milk was
null**: NHS-II (n=47,355) skim milk PR 1.44 (1.21-1.72) and total milk 1.22 (1.03-1.44) but whole and
low-fat milk **not associated**; the boys' cohort found **only skim milk** associated (PR 1.19,
1.01-1.40). This inverts the usual *full-fat dairy is worse* intuition and points at a milk-component
(hormonal/whey) rather than fat as the candidate agent — whey protein carried a large signal in one
weak survey (Dreno, OR 3.94). Which dairy fraction, if any, is causal is unresolved.

## Causal fragility — why this is `confidence: low` and not a recommendation

The source's own limitations are the reasons to hold this loosely, and one is self-indicting:

- **Positive-classification overestimation (author-declared):** a study was counted *positive* if
  **any single** food item hit significance «even if many others in the study were not associated, which
  created biases in our summary statistics toward the overestimation of a potential association» — the
  cited example being Adebamowo's skim-milk-only hit [@meixiong2022acne]. So the 70%/77% vote-counts are inflated by construction.
- **Measurement error / recall bias** dominates the observational base (self-report, some recalling
  teenage diet years later) -> [[Measurement Error in Dietary Assessment]] — the binding constraint
  here as elsewhere in nutrition.
- **Reverse causation is live:** acne sufferers plausibly change their diet, and cross-sectional
  designs cannot separate that from diet -> acne.
- **Underpowered, sex-mismatched, age-bounded, no pooled estimate / no GRADE.**

The outcome is measured by partial surrogates — lesion counts (reasonable), heterogeneous grading
scales, and self-reported severity (weakest) -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]. Scarring, arguably the most
patient-important endpoint, appears in only two studies.

## Layer-1 placement and named gaps

For the reasonably-healthy teen/young-adult stratum **with acne**, a **low-GI / lower-glycemic-load
diet** is a plausible, low-harm, directionally-supported adjunct lever — but a *modest* rock: the RCTs
show it augments improvement that also occurs on standard topical therapy, and the marginal benefit
over an effective, mature topical/systemic regimen (the realistic alternative in the prescriber zone)
is unquantified. Dairy restriction is weaker still and
Western-stratum-conditional; it is a candidate to trial individually, not a general recommendation.

Named gaps (G) driving the next diet-acne source choices:

- **A pooled meta-analysis / GRADE of low-GI diet -> acne lesion counts** — the vote-count SR cannot
  give a magnitude or certainty. — the cell this
  page most needs to move from directional to magnitude.
- **Any dairy RCT** — the entire dairy arm rests on observational data..
- **Which dairy fraction (skim vs whole vs whey/casein)** carries the signal, if any — the
  food-category question..
- **Acne + the shared insulin/IGF-1/androgen mechanism** as a cross-cluster synthesis with
  [[PCOS and Modifiable Exposures]] and [[Insulin Resistance Surrogates and Cardiovascular Risk]] — a
  banked A/E move OWED when a second insulin-axis-dermatology source lands.

## Synthesis

 This is a
**provisional-C** nucleus: one source opens the diet-acne cluster and its decision question; the banked
cross-source moves are OWED. The move beyond the source's own summary is a **design-stratified re-sort**
(type F / B): the SR presents a flat *diet affects acne within select populations*, but sorted by the
design behind each exposure it becomes **GI/GL = RCT-supported and directional** vs **dairy =
observational, equal-quality-both-ways, region-conditional** — an asymmetry the headline blurs, and one
that changes what a reader should do (try low-GI first; treat dairy as an individual trial, not a rule).
The **skim-beats-whole paradox** is a type-B food-category disambiguation (*dairy* names several
exposures with different signals), and the author's own positive-classification bias plus the
measurement-error constraint bound the whole thing to low confidence. `[E-independent]` is **not**
claimed — single source. The loop stays open: no acne outcome here is graded against a realized result.
