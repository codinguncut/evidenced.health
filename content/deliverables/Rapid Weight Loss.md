*This page has been folded into [[Losing Fat and Keeping It Off]].*

[[Losing Fat and Keeping It Off]] now covers the rate-of-loss question — *how fast is too fast?* — in its
treatment of how the deficit is created and what happens to muscle as the fat comes off. The short answer
is unchanged: rate is a minor knob. Two things protect a person while fat comes off — preserving lean mass
(adequate protein plus resistance training) and choosing a method they can sustain; much of what people
fear about *speed* tracks the *method* (protein-poor, unsupervised regimens), not the number on the scale.
Supervised rapid loss — as in DiRECT's 825-853 kcal/day total diet replacement — is safe and delivered
the largest diabetes-remission signal in the corpus. The rate-vs-regain and speed-vs-muscle magnitudes
remain named gaps.

See [[Losing Fat and Keeping It Off]] for the method-and-durability treatment (which method moves which
outcome, lean mass, and whether the loss lasts). See [[Body Fat]] for whether a person's fat is actually
raising their risk and whether losing it reaches hard outcomes.
