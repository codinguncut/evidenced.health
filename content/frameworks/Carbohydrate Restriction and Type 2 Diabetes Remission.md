## The answer depends entirely on what "remission" means

| Definition | 6 months | 12 months |
|---|---|---|
| **HbA1c <6.5%** | **RD 0.32** (0.17 to 0.47), RR 1.87 (1.18 to 2.97), 8 RCTs, n=264, **Moderate** | RD 0.10 (-0.02 to 0.21), 3 RCTs, n=171, Moderate |
| **HbA1c <6.5% AND no diabetes medication** | RD 0.05 (-0.05 to 0.14), 5 RCTs, n=199, Low | **RD -0.04** (-0.16 to 0.09), 2 RCTs, n=126, Low |

[@goldenberg2021]

**This is the finding.** The headline **32 per 100 more reaching HbA1c <6.5%** -- an NNT of about 3 -- counts
that threshold **with medication allowed to continue**: the review's protocol defines remission «with or without the use
of diabetes medication» [@goldenberg2021], so
this is glycaemic *control below the diagnostic threshold*, not remission in the off-drug sense DiRECT
measures -> [[Total Diet Replacement and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]]. Under the definition
that also requires coming off medication, the pooled effect is **never statistically significant at any
timepoint, and the 12-month point estimate is negative**.

**But the stratum matters, and this is where the headline needs qualifying.** In trials that
*excluded* insulin users, medication-free remission **is** significant: **RD 0.20 (0.03 to 0.38), NNT
5**, against **0.00 (-0.07 to 0.07)** where insulin users were included (test for subgroup difference
**P=0.03**). For the medication-permitted definition the split is starker still -- **0.51 (0.36 to
0.65)** vs 0.14 (0.03 to 0.25), P<0.001.
[@goldenberg2021]
So "never significant" holds for the **pooled** medication-free estimate and **not** for the
non-insulin stratum, the narrowest reference class Goldenberg's credible subgroups support. It is
not the only stratum in which low-carb is offered: ADA's endorsement also covers adults «where reducing
antiglycemic medications is a priority» [@evert2019],
which includes insulin users. The split is also between trials, and on the medication-free definition
it may coincide with the post-hoc trial-length split below (on the medication-permitted definition the
two splits' figures differ).

**But this rescue is a six-month result only:** the insulin subgroup split is reported at six months,
and Goldenberg reports no 12-month insulin subgroup.
[@goldenberg2021]
With only three trials carrying 12-month remission data, a split was probably not feasible; Goldenberg
gives no reason. So the NNT-5 stratum carries **no durability estimate**. The pooled 12-month
medication-free row above (RD -0.04, 2 RCTs) is the only long-term read, and it says little about the
non-insulin stratum: two of the three 12-month remission trials (Tay, Sato) included insulin users, and
only Morris excluded them [@goldenberg2021].


The two definitions also differ in trial mix. The medication-free row (5 RCTs) is a subset of the 8
six-month remission trials, so it is partly a stricter reading of the same evidence and partly a
design-selected set. Goldenberg ties the shrinkage to medication permission: «Finally, the limited number of trials allowing patients to reduce their medication use impeded our ability to assess remission of diabetes when defined as HbA\~1c\~ below 6.5% without diabetes medication.» «Only 7/23 (30%) of eligible trials permitted reduction of medication and reported usable medication data.»
[@goldenberg2021]
That 7 matches the count for the separate medication-reduction outcome (7 studies at six months, Table 3),
not the 5-trial remission row.
[inferred from @goldenberg2021]

## Most benefits attenuate by 12 months — but not all

| Outcome | 6 months | 12 months | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight | **MD -3.46 kg** (-5.25 to -1.67), 18 RCTs | **MD +0.29 kg** (-1.02 to 1.60), 7 RCTs | Moderate |
| HbA1c | **MD -0.47%** (-0.60 to -0.34), 17 RCTs | MD -0.23% (-0.46 to 0.00), 8 RCTs | **High** -> Moderate |
| Fasting glucose | MD -0.73 mmol/L (-1.19 to -0.27) | MD 0.06 mmol/L **higher** (-0.37 to 0.48), 6 RCTs | Moderate |
| Medication reduction | **RD 0.24** (0.12 to 0.35), 7 RCTs | RD 0.33 (-0.00 to 0.66), 3 RCTs | Moderate -> Low |

**On medication reduction and triglycerides the point estimate holds or rises** (medication RD 0.24 ->
0.33, but the 12-month CI touches the null, -0.00 to 0.66, Low, 3 RCTs; triglycerides MD -0.30 -> -0.32
mmol/L, High, 7 RCTs at 12 months), so the source's own wording is the accurate one: *"most benefits
diminished at 12 months."*

[@goldenberg2021]

**Diet effects on weight shrink by 12 months across diets generally (corroboration, F, on a related
but different quantity).** Across 121 RCTs of overweight/obese adults, Ge reports: «All named diets, except a paleolithic diet, decreased their estimated effects at the 12 month follow-up by, on average, 1.5 kg compared with the six month follow-up (appendix tables S36 and S37).»
[@ge2020]
Cardiovascular risk-factor gains «largely disappear» by 12 months -> [[Named Diet Programs Compared]]
[@ge2020].
Ge's estimates are each diet against usual diet. Goldenberg's quantity is the gap between two active
diets (LCD vs comparator) closing from -3.46 to +0.29 kg, which can vanish even if neither arm regains.
So Ge shows that diet effects generally decay; it does not show that the LCD advantage decays for the
same reason. Ge measures no remission outcome; it enters as an F-refinement on the weight-decay
reading.
[inferred from @ge2020; @goldenberg2021]

The HbA1c estimate at 6 months is the only High-certainty row **among the primary outcomes**. Table 3
carries five more (LDL at 6 months, HDL and triglycerides at both timepoints).

**Attenuation is already visible inside the six-month window — but this is a POST HOC analysis and
carries less weight than the a-priori subgroups above.** The review is explicit about its provenance:
«On the basis of comments from peer reviewers, we did a post hoc analysis on remission at six (±3) months.»
It hedges its reading: «Evidence suggested larger treatment effects for LCDs in shorter term trials (3 to \<6 months), suggesting that shorter term trials may be an effect modifier.»
[@goldenberg2021]
Trials of 3 to <6 months give RD **0.49**
(0.30 to 0.68); trials of 6 to 9 months give **0.25** (0.08 to 0.42). Medication-free: 0.20 (0.03 to
0.38) vs 0.00 (-0.07 to 0.07).
[@goldenberg2021]

**Note a coincidence worth not over-reading:** the medication-free figures here (0.20 / 0.00) are
numerically identical to the insulin-exclusion subgroup's. That is consistent with the two splits being
near-coextensive — the non-insulin trials largely being the short ones — in which case this is one
finding presented twice, not two. The match holds only on the medication-free definition: with
medication permitted, the insulin split gives 0.51 vs 0.14 and the trial-length split 0.49 vs 0.25
[@goldenberg2021], so the two
partitions of the pool are not identical. The paper's supplementary tables would settle it; the wiki does not
hold them.

## Medication is handled four ways at once, and this is the transferable move

Not adjusted for -- **co-reported**. The review's stated reasoning:

> *"Reductions in medication may blunt the effect on mean HbA1c levels, biasing results towards the null
> and masking any effect; however, any improvement can still be captured if reduction of medication is
> included as an outcome of interest."*
[@goldenberg2021]

So medication appears (1) inside two of the four remission definitions, (2) as a standalone outcome,
(3) as a named bias mechanism, and (4) as a subgroup variable. **The HbA1c mean difference and the
medication-reduction risk difference can be read as two projections of one underlying effect, neither
complete alone.** In the trials that permitted medication reduction, a claim built on the HbA1c figure
alone *may* understate the glycaemic effect, by an amount the review does not quantify; only 7 of 23
trials permitted it and reported usable data, so the bias is trial-dependent.
[inferred from @goldenberg2021]

**Generalizes past diabetes:** wherever a treatment lets people reduce a co-intervention, the outcome
measured while the co-intervention is allowed to fall in one arm is biased toward the null, and the fix
is to report the co-intervention change as its own outcome rather than to adjust it away.

</div>

## Harms, and how firm they are

- **LDL at 12 months: MD 0.14 mmol/L** (-0.00 to 0.28), 6 studies, Moderate -- above the review's own
  0.10 mmol/L threshold, so labelled a clinically important worsening, though P=0.05. At 6 months LDL is
  flat (0.02) at **High** certainty.
- **Quality of life at 12 months: MD 3.10** (-2.03 to 8.23), threshold 1, Low certainty. **Rests on a
  single trial of 116 people**, with an interval spanning a benefit twice the threshold and a harm eight
  times it, judged important on the point estimate alone. The 6-month estimate points the other way and
  comes from 4 trials -- so the apparent reversal is partly a change of study set, not a trajectory.
  [inferred from @goldenberg2021]
- Adverse events: «Although no significant or clinically important increase in total or serious adverse events was identified, these outcomes were poorly reported among trials and the certainty of evidence for safety ranges from low to very low.»
  [@goldenberg2021]

## What the review tells clinicians to do

> *"clinicians might consider short term LCDs for management of type 2 diabetes, while actively
> monitoring and adjusting diabetes medication as needed."*
[@goldenberg2021]

Two conditions attached -- **short term**, and **active medication management**. Note the reason for the
time limit is partly *external* to this review's own evidence: it cites a cohort review suggesting
long-term low-carbohydrate diets are associated with increased mortality.

## The summary box vs the body — reconciled on weight and adverse events, not on insulin resistance

The key-messages box claims *"large and clinically important improvements in weight loss, triglycerides,
and insulin resistance were also seen, without adverse events."* The Discussion's own threshold tally
says something narrower: «Among 10 continuous outcomes, two showed improvements that met or surpassed the MCID at six months (triglycerides, insulin resistance) with no detrimental effects. At 12 months, two had improvements that surpassed the MCID (triglycerides, insulin resistance) and two had a clinically important worsening (quality of life, low density lipoprotein cholesterol), although neither was statistically significant (P=0.24 and P=0.05).»
[@goldenberg2021]

**On weight and adverse events this reconciles rather than conflicting.** The Methods
pre-specify that *"if the risk of bias sensitivity analysis was credible, we focused our results on
those studies at low risk"* -- and weight was the outcome where that applied, giving **MD -7.41 kg**
(-9.75 to -5.08) in low-risk-of-bias trials, which does surpass the 4.4 kg threshold. And *"adverse
events"* is a named outcome class in this review (total and serious AEs), on which nothing significant
was found; the 12-month LDL and quality-of-life worsening are continuous outcomes, and the Conclusions
state them plainly. On those two clauses the box is the review applying its own stated method, not
drifting from its body.
**The insulin-resistance clause does not reconcile the same way.** HOMA-IR clears its MCID (0.05) on
the point estimate, but at both timepoints the interval crosses zero and the certainty is very low:
MD -0.14 (-0.51 to 0.23) at 6 months and -0.13 (-0.39 to 0.13) at 12 months, GRADE very low.
[@goldenberg2021]
So *"were also seen"* overstates by the review's own GRADE; the box follows the point-estimate-vs-MCID
tally, not the certainty rating.


**On the HbA1c threshold.** The main text prints thresholds for eight continuous outcomes and not for
the two glycaemic ones; the full table is in a supplement not held here. But the question is
**answerable anyway**, from the review's own tally: of **ten** continuous outcomes, only triglycerides
and insulin resistance met or surpassed their threshold at six months. HbA1c is one of the ten. **So
-0.47% did not clear the review's own bar** -- a decision-relevant fact, and one recoverable by
counting the outcomes rather than concluding it unknowable because the main text does not say so
directly.
[inferred from @goldenberg2021]

## The mechanism this review does not isolate — weight loss appears first; carbohydrate's added role unresolved

Low-carbohydrate diets cause weight loss, and this review's remission effect **is non-significant by
12 months alongside its weight advantage** (weight MD -3.46 kg at 6 months -> **+0.29 kg at 12
months**; the 12-month pools are smaller sets, largely nested in the 6-month ones — 7 vs 18 RCTs for
weight, 6 of the 7 also in the 6-month pool, all except Yancy; 3 vs 8 for remission, all 3 in it).
[@goldenberg2021]
The two 12-month pools barely overlap each other: of the 7 weight trials and the 3 remission trials, only
Tay is in both. Two of the three remission trials, Morris and Sato, list intervention durations of 3 and
6 months in Table 1.
[@goldenberg2021]
So their 12-month data may be follow-up after the diet ended; the review says only that the 12-month
trials were all reported at that time point.
[inferred from @goldenberg2021]
Not every advantage closes with the weight gap. At 12 months, with the between-arm weight difference at
+0.29 kg, HbA1c (MD -0.23%, -0.46 to 0.00, Moderate), triglycerides (MD -0.32 mmol/L, -0.51 to -0.12,
High) and medication reduction (RD 0.33, -0.00 to 0.66, Low) still point toward LCD, from pools of 8,
7 and 3 trials whose membership the held text does not list.
[@goldenberg2021]
So the *co-fading* is a cross-pool comparison, not weight and remission observed fading together in
the same trials. A remission effect that fades as the weight advantage fades would be consistent with a
*weight-loss* effect delivered via a low-carb route, and equally with a carbohydrate effect that fades
as adherence to restriction fades. A pure weight reading would predict the HbA1c and triglyceride gaps
closing with the weight gap, which they only partly do: the HbA1c difference roughly halves (MD -0.47%
at six months to -0.23% at 12) while the triglyceride difference holds (MD -0.30 to -0.32 mmol/L)
[@goldenberg2021]. With trial sets
not shown to match the weight pool, that is weak evidence either way. The fading pattern does not discriminate the two readings, and
Goldenberg leaves the cause open (Durability, below).
[inferred from @goldenberg2021]

DiRECT (Lean 2018) achieves **46% remission** on a total diet replacement phase, with remission rising
monotonically with kilograms lost (0% at weight gain -> 86% at >=15 kg).
[@lean2018]
That gradient is drawn with «both randomised groups combined», and every loss of 15 kg or more was in
the formula arm («no participants in the control group») [@lean2018],
so it is pooled across both arms and confounded with programme -> [[Total Diet Replacement and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]].
Lean describes the formula: «Weight loss was induced with a total diet replacement phase using a low energy formula diet (825–853 kcal/day; 59% carbohydrate, 13% fat, 26% protein, 2% fibre) for 3 months (extendable up to 5 months if wished by participant), followed by structured food reintroduction of 2–8 weeks (about 50% carbohydrate, 35% total fat, and 15% protein), and an ongoing structured programme with monthly visits for long-term weight loss maintenance.»
[@lean2018]
**A high carbohydrate *share* is not a high carbohydrate *amount*.** Goldenberg defines low-carb on
a 2000 kcal/day basis: «For the purposes of this review, we refer to diets with less than 130 g/day or less than 26% of calories from carbohydrates based on 2000 kcal/day as a low carbohydrate diet (LCD).»
[@goldenberg2021]
At 825-853 kcal/day, 59% of energy is about 122-126 g/day of carbohydrate (825 x 0.59 / 4 = 121.7;
853 x 0.59 / 4 = 125.8) — 4-8 g under the 130 g/day arm of that definition and, on Goldenberg's
2000 kcal basis, under the equivalent 26% arm (26% of 2000 kcal = 520 kcal = 130 g; 122-126 g is
\~24-25% of 2000 kcal). Only as a share of the formula's own \~840 kcal is 59% far above 26%.

So DiRECT does **not** show remission *without* carbohydrate restriction in absolute terms: its
formula was low-energy *and* just under the gram cut-off, so it cannot separate weight loss from
carbohydrate amount. The deeper point is structural: on any \~840 kcal formula, grams of carbohydrate fall
with energy by construction, so neither yardstick can discriminate. By share of actual intake, DiRECT's
59% would sit on Goldenberg's comparator side («higher in carbohydrates (≥26%)»); by grams it sits just
inside the LCD side. The gram arithmetic above shows the classification is ambiguous, not that DiRECT
was a low-carbohydrate diet.
[@goldenberg2021] (comparator criterion)


The weight-first reading rests mainly on Churuangsuk's umbrella review, which hedges it after cautioning
about the non-randomised studies: «Despite extracting baseline data for ITT analysis, residual bias/confounding may remain with these study designs. The main contributor to HbA1c reduction and remission appears to be weight loss, irrespective of diet type. From the high-quality studies with high GRADE certainty, structured programmes with an intensive induction phase with total diet replacement were effective.»
[@churuangsuk2022]
That umbrella's byline includes DiRECT's PI Lean [@churuangsuk2022],
so it is not independent of DiRECT. Goldenberg's co-fading (a cross-pool comparison, above), the
caloric-matching null (below) and the umbrella's placement of TDR (next section) are each partly
consistent with the weight-first reading, but none of them discriminates weight from a carbohydrate
effect.

One held RCT datum leans the other way. Churuangsuk relays Esposito: «A single RCT of Mediterranean diet over 12 months report- ed a remission prevalence of 15% (15/102), with mean weight loss 6.2 kg, compared with 4% (4/97) with weight loss 4.2 kg in the control LFD arm (N = 215; GRADE low certainty of evidence; some concern over risk of bias) [59].»
[@churuangsuk2022]
That is roughly 10 more remissions per 100 on 2 kg more weight loss. ADA describes the same trial's arm as
«low-carbohydrate Mediterranean-style» and its outcome as «at least partial diabetes remission»
[@evert2019], and Churuangsuk's evidence table lists
it as an ITT ancillary analysis [@churuangsuk2022].
Three things limit it: GRADE low; the contrast is Mediterranean vs low-fat, so carbohydrate is not
isolated; and the endpoint is at least partial remission. It is the held datum least favourable to a
pure weight reading, and it is not strong enough to move the verdict.

On the held evidence, weight loss appears to be the main driver and carbohydrate restriction **one lever
for that weight loss**; whether it adds a remission effect *beyond* the weight loss it produces is
**insufficient evidence** — not shown present, not shown absent.
[inferred from @churuangsuk2022; @goldenberg2021]
The carb-vs-calorie question is **weakly probed, not untested**: Goldenberg ran an a-priori caloric-matching subgroup across all five primary outcomes
(remission included) and, on 18 studies, «identified no evidence of credible effect modification based on
caloric matching or lack thereof», while hedging that «self-reported dietary intake data are prone to
measurement error»
[@goldenberg2021].
But that subgroup matches *reported intake*, not the prescribed goal — its iso-caloric controls are «per intake not per goal»
[@goldenberg2021] — not weight loss
*achieved*, so it under-identifies a weight-mediated effect and cannot cleanly separate a carb-specific
contribution from the weight-loss one.
[inferred from @goldenberg2021]
The added glycaemic contribution is therefore **null on a weak test, not cleanly tested and not
excluded**. A person should optimize for the weight loss they can *sustain*, by a route they will adhere to;
whether the route adds a remission effect beyond the weight is unresolved -> [[Total Diet Replacement and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]].

### Durability: Goldenberg 6 -> 12 months vs DiRECT 12 -> 24 months — not the same quantity

Goldenberg's 12-month attenuation and DiRECT's 24-month gap look like a contrast in durability. They are
not a like-for-like comparison:

| Parameter | Goldenberg 2021 (low-carb MA) | DiRECT 24 mo (Lean 2019) | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comparator | comparator diets (e.g. low-fat, Mediterranean) | usual primary care (control lost 2.3 kg) | **NO** |
| Horizon | 6 vs 12 months | 12 vs 24 months | NO |
| Remission definition | the attenuation (RD 0.32 at 6 mo) is on the medication-permitted definition; drug-free definition was never significant pooled (0.05 at 6 mo), though significant at 6 mo in trials excluding insulin users (0.20, 0.03 to 0.38; above) | HbA1c <48 mmol/mol (6.5%) off all antidiabetic drugs for >=2 months (Lean 2019 Figure 2 legend, chunk 02) | **NO** |
| Remission estimate | between-diet RD at 12 mo, both non-significant: 0.10 (-0.02 to 0.21; 3 RCTs) if remission ignores medication, -0.04 (-0.16 to 0.09; 2 RCTs) if drug-free required | 35.6% vs 3.4% (ITT) at 24 mo, down from 46% in the intervention arm at 12 mo | NO — diet-vs-diet vs programme-vs-usual-care; DiRECT requires drug-free (off all antidiabetic drugs) |
| Maintenance support | varies by trial; adherence beyond 6 mo not separable | monthly visits + rescue TDR (used by \~half) | NO |
| Population | insulin users included in 14 of 23 trials (excluded in 7, not stated in 2; Table 1) | «not receiving insulin» | **NO** — the closer Goldenberg comparator is the non-insulin subgroup (drug-free RD 0.20 at 6 mo) |
| Medication handling | «Only 7/23 (30%) of eligible trials permitted reduction of medication and reported usable medication data» | oral antidiabetic and antihypertensive drugs discontinued on day 1 | **NO** — DiRECT's drug-free remission is remission under protocol withdrawal |

[@lean2019] (DiRECT column)
[@lean2018] (DiRECT population and medication rows)
[@goldenberg2021] (Goldenberg column)

Goldenberg does not attribute its fade to the diet: «Although improvements noted at six months diminished
by 12 months, determining with any certainty whether this is related to intensity of intervention and/or
dietary adherence beyond six months is difficult.»
[@goldenberg2021]
DiRECT's 2-year data are consistent with durability tracking weight kept off: 12-month remitters who
stayed in remission regained 4.25 kg between 12 and 24 months vs 7.09 kg in those who relapsed (p=0.073,
not significant), and in post-hoc pooled bands remission at 24 months rose with kilograms lost (5.2%
below 5 kg to 70% at >=15 kg).
[@lean2019]
So this is a distinction, not a tension: neither source tests whether a low-carb route holds remission as
long as an energy-controlled one with the same maintenance support. Both readings are consistent with
durability tracking maintained weight loss; neither tests carbohydrate share against it.
[inferred from @goldenberg2021; @lean2019]

## The umbrella review sharpens the reading — on Churuangsuk's count, no low-carb RCT pre-specified remission as its outcome `[2026-08-04]`

Goldenberg pools low-carb weight and glycaemic outcomes in T2D, and reports remission under its four
definitions, mostly derived from data the trial authors supplied: «Although only three included studies previously published HbA\~1c\~ threshold criteria and medication use to determine diabetes remission, our successful contact with authors yielded trial data from five additional studies to determine remission rates, increasing the precision and overall certainty of the effect estimates.»
[@goldenberg2021]
The gold-tier T2D umbrella review (Churuangsuk 2022) states the design gap that this disclosure implies
but does not foreground: «Only two RCTs had remission as the pre- specified outcome, both relatively large and using almost identical designs and diets, with very similar results, but in very different populations, notably with different durations of diabetes [51, 53, 89].»
Its refs 51, 53 and 89 are DiRECT (2018 and 2-year reports) and DIADEM-I. In its 1-year remission
synthesis it adds: «No RCT has evaluated LCDs/ketogenic diets for type 2 diabetes remission.»
[@churuangsuk2022]
The two sources count differently: Goldenberg counts three included low-carb trials with published
remission criteria; Churuangsuk counts no low-carb RCT evaluating remission. Read together, low-carb RCTs
did measure remission-defined endpoints (mostly derived afterwards from author data), but on
Churuangsuk's count none was designed with remission as its pre-specified outcome.


- The much-cited keto-remission figure is a **single non-randomised controlled study** (Virta, 20%
  remission, mean −13.8 kg) graded **GRADE very low, serious risk of bias** — the **bottom GRADE tier of
  the umbrella's remission map** (tied very-low with a food-based VLED, which is graded on *critical* risk
  of bias from a single n=9 uncontrolled study — so keto is joint-weakest by certainty, not uniquely so),
  below Mediterranean (15%, low) and far below total diet replacement (54%, high).
  [@churuangsuk2022]
- Read against DiRECT (**46% remission at 1 year** on an **825-853 kcal, 59%-carbohydrate** formula
  [@lean2018] — one of the two RCTs in the total-diet-
  replacement class the umbrella grades **GRADE high** at a median 54% remission, range 46-61%
  [@churuangsuk2022]), the placement says where the
  evidence sits: the strongest remission evidence is on a *low-energy total-diet-replacement* format, and
  the keto remission figure in the umbrella's map rests on the weakest design available. It does not by itself settle the causal
  question — that formula's high carbohydrate share is still only \~122-126 g/day, below Goldenberg's
  130 g/day cut-off (mechanism section above), so TDR does not separate weight from carbohydrate amount.
  This is consistent with, and the umbrella-level companion to, this page's mechanism reading — weight
  loss appears to be the main driver, carbohydrate's added role unresolved ->
  [[Diets for Weight Management in Type 2 Diabetes]], [[Total Diet Replacement and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]].
- **Not independent backing (F, shared-evidence).** Churuangsuk's LCD-weight conclusion pools the same
  MA class as Goldenberg (and lists Goldenberg 2021 among its high-quality LCD meta-analyses), so its
  agreement on *low-carb holds no overall advantage* is F-broadening, not a second independent route.
  Its distinct contribution here is the *remission-design gap*, which Goldenberg discloses but does not
  foreground.
- **A second umbrella (Szczerba 2023) re-reports Goldenberg's low-carb estimates and rates remission
  evidence low/very-low.** Its low-carb (<26%E) figures are Goldenberg's own (its ref 37): HbA1c −0.47%
  (high), triglycerides −0.30 mmol/L (high), and medication use — low-carb «reduced the use of drug
  treatments by an additional 24 per 100 individuals» (risk difference 0.24, 0.12 to 0.35; moderate
  certainty), the same RD 0.24 as Goldenberg's medication row above. Szczerba ranks that last as its one
  robust clinical outcome: «For clinical outcomes, robust evidence exists only for a low carbohydrate diet and reduction in the use of drug treatments.»
  So it is the firmest low-carb clinical outcome Szczerba grades, not the firmest in either umbrella
  (Churuangsuk grades TDR remission high). Szczerba places remission among the low/very-low outcomes: «For other outcomes, including kidney function parameters, inflammatory markers, liver enzymes, and patient relevant outcomes (eg, remission of diabetes, health related quality of life, and incidence of cardiovascular disease), meta-­ analyses are already available, but the certainty of evidence was rated as low or very low, and definitive conclusions on these findings cannot be drawn.»
  Its remission rating may itself rest on Goldenberg; nothing held shows otherwise. It also notes the
  low-carb estimates may be **under**-stated by poor adherence («especially for low carbohydrate and
  ketogenic diets»). Shared MA base -> a restatement (F/corroboration at most), not [E].
  [@szczerba2023] [@szczerba2023]

## What this means for a decision

- **If on insulin or other glucose-lowering drugs, arrange a medication review with the prescriber
  before cutting carbohydrate.** Goldenberg: «LCDs seem to promote important reductions in HbA\~1c\~, potentially increasing risk for hypoglycemic episodes, including severe syncope, if the dosage of diabetes medications is not adjusted accordingly.»
  [@goldenberg2021]
  ADA, on very-low-carbohydrate plans: «Adopting a VLC eating plan can cause diuresis and swiftly reduce blood glucose; therefore, consul- tation with a knowledgeable practitioner at the onset is necessary to prevent dehydration and reduce insulin and hypoglycemic medications to prevent hypoglycemia.»
  [@evert2019]
  The dose changes themselves are the prescriber's.
- **To lower HbA1c and medication use over about six months**, a low-carbohydrate diet is a reasonable
  option (HbA1c High certainty, medication reduction Moderate); most benefits diminish by 12 months.
- **For drug-free remission**, low-carb shows a six-month signal only in trials that excluded insulin
  users, with no durability estimate; the best-evidenced route is a total-diet-replacement programme ->
  [[Total Diet Replacement and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]].
- **Whatever the route, aim at weight loss you can sustain**; whether carbohydrate restriction adds a
  remission effect beyond that weight loss is insufficient evidence.

[inferred from @goldenberg2021; @evert2019]

## Limits

- Small numbers behind the headline: the 6-month remission estimate is **8 trials, n=264**; by 12 months
  it is 3 trials, n=171. [@goldenberg2021]
- Only 30% of trials permitted medication reduction, which constrains the definition that matters most.
- The mortality caution is imported from cohort evidence, not tested here.
- **Same population, adjacent condition:** this stratum overlaps heavily with fatty liver — insulin
  resistance is the shared hub (MASLD carries about a 2-fold risk of incident T2D: pooled HR 2.22,
  attenuating to HR 1.85 in the fully-adjusted studies, sourced on the linked page), and the
  weight-loss and glycemia levers here are the same ones that reduce liver fat ->
  [[Fatty Liver MASLD and Weight Loss]].

- Guidance benchmark: both ADA 2019 and EASD-DNSG 2023 are held (below). The cross-body emphasis
  type-D on very-low-carb is filed on [[Diets for Weight Management in Type 2 Diabetes]].

## The US guidance benchmark — ADA 2019 `[2026-09-16]`

The ADA consensus report is the first guidance body the wiki holds on this question (EASD-DNSG 2023
is the paired second, for the cross-body comparison). **It corroborates the page's core mechanism
reading and adds nothing that shifts the effect estimates.**

- **Remission is framed weight-first, carbohydrate second.** ADA: «The DiRECT trial showed that at 1
  year, weight loss associated with the lifestyle intervention resulted in diabetes remission in 46%
  of participants. Remission rates were related to magnitude of weight loss, rising progressively
  from 7% to 86% as weight loss at 1 year increased from <5% to >=15%» [@evert2019]. ADA's bands read as percent of weight; DiRECT's own bands are in
  kilograms («31 (86%) of 36 participants who lost 15 kg or more»
  [@lean2018]), so the kg version is the one to carry.
  Look AHEAD gave at-least-partial remission in 11.5% vs 2%
  control. Diet composition «may also play a role» (Esposito's low-carb Mediterranean pattern:
  partial remission 14.7% yr 1 / 5% yr 6 vs 4.7% / 0% low-fat, on only a 2-kg weight-loss
  difference). ADA's 4.7% is not the year-1 control figure in Churuangsuk's evidence table, which gives
  the low-fat arm 4.1% (4/97) at year 1 and 4.7% (3/64) at year 2
  [@churuangsuk2022]; on either figure the year-1
  gap is about 10 points. **The weight-first half rests on DiRECT and Look AHEAD, trials the page already cites,
  so it is F/corroboration, NOT independent type-E. The composition half is Esposito, the one held datum
  against a pure weight reading, weighed in the mechanism section above.**
- **On carbohydrate as a lever, ADA is measured, not enthusiastic.** «Reducing overall carbohydrate
  intake for individuals with diabetes has demonstrated the most evidence for improving glycemia», and
  low/very-low-carbohydrate plans are «a viable approach» for «select adults with type 2 diabetes not
  meeting glycemic targets or where reducing antiglycemic medications is a priority»
  [@evert2019] — a conditional, select-stratum
  endorsement, not a remission claim for carbohydrate restriction per se.
- **ADA states the durability caveat itself:** in one MA «A1C benefits were more pronounced in the
  VLC interventions (where <26% of calories came from carbohydrate) at 3 and 6 months but not at 12
  and 24 months» [@evert2019] — the same
  attenuation-by-12-months the low-carb evidence shows elsewhere.

## The EU guidance benchmark — EASD-DNSG 2023, and the emphasis type-D resolved `[2026-09-16]`

EASD-DNSG 2023 is the paired EU guidance body. On **remission specifically it agrees with ADA and the
fabric** — the mechanism is weight-first, the route is total diet replacement:

- EASD grades **High** that «Remission of type 2 diabetes... can be achieved through sustained weight
  loss», and recommends a **low-energy total diet replacement programme (\~3500 kJ/840 kcal/day for 12-20
  weeks)** to deliver 10-15%+ weight loss to induce remission
  [@dnsg2023]. It states over 80% of people with
  T2D can achieve remission by losing >15 kg
  within 6 years of diagnosis. That figure cites DiRECT (EASD refs 44, 45)
  [@dnsg2023], so it is the pooled-arm
  gradient flagged above as confounded with programme. This is the DiRECT-anchored, low-energy
  total-diet-replacement route — the same evidence and same causal reading held here, so
  **F/corroboration, not independent type-E**.

**The emphasis type-D is real but narrow, and its home is the diet-choice question, not remission.** On the
**shared** evidence base, ADA calls low/very-low-carb «a viable approach» for «select adults... not meeting
glycemic targets», while EASD grades **Moderate** that «Very low carbohydrate intakes, such as with
ketogenic diets, are not recommended» [@dnsg2023]. But both accommodate the individual who *chooses* very-low-carb with professional support — EASD:
«If people with diabetes themselves choose to reduce carbohydrate to very low intakes, it is important this
is done with health professional support» [@dnsg2023]. So the clash is on the **population default** and on the **weight given to the observational U-shaped
mortality + LDL-rise harm signal**, not a flat contradiction — filed with the full parameter table on
[[Diets for Weight Management in Type 2 Diabetes]]. Neither bears on remission, where no low-carb RCT
pre-specified remission as its outcome (Churuangsuk, above).

**Guidance-null verdict (both bodies held).** ADA and EASD reach the same weight-first remission reading
from the same trials — so on the remission question the guidance null is **not defeated by divergence**; it
would be defeated (if at all) by better-warranted agreement. `[E-independent]` does NOT apply between them
(shared trial base — DiRECT, Look AHEAD, the same low-carb MAs). More broadly, the umbrella and guidance
support for weight-first traces largely to one trial programme: DiRECT itself; Churuangsuk, with DiRECT's
PI Lean on the byline; EASD, which cites DiRECT (refs 44, 45) and Churuangsuk (ref 27), with Churuangsuk
among its authors; and ADA, which cites DiRECT. The trial and three bodies citing it are one line of evidence, not four.
[inferred from @dnsg2023; @churuangsuk2022]
