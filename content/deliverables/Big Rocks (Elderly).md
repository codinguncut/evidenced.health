> [!warning] Applies to a specific group
> This appraisal is written for the **obese older adult, \~70, with *sarcopenic obesity*** (extra visceral
> fat plus muscle loss) — *not* the frail or institutionalised elderly, and *not* the fit 70-year-old
> (different strata). If you are not in this reference class, the recommendations here may not transfer.

## 1. The verdict

At around 70 with sarcopenic obesity, the big levers are not the younger-adult list
re-graded. Two things move at once: the outcomes that matter shift toward function,
muscle, bone, falls and independence, and the runway for pure-longevity plays shortens.
Together they reorder the page onto function first.

The lever that rises most is protecting and rebuilding muscle — resistance training with
enough protein, eaten across the day, because old muscle needs a bigger and more even
protein stimulus than a younger body does. Right beside it sits the balance work that
prevents falls, which carries the strongest evidence on the page. Building muscle and not
falling are different levers with different active ingredients, so do both.

Losing visceral fat still helps the liver, blood sugar, blood pressure and joints, but here
it is a conditional lever: done with training and enough protein to defend muscle and bone,
and not in a hurry. Treating blood pressure keeps real benefit because the underlying risk
is high — but over-treatment becomes its own harm, and several younger levers (aggressive
lipid and glucose targets, the vitamin-D pill, testosterone) weaken or change sign with age.
If a smoker, quitting still dominates everything else here.

The honest end: once the muscle-and-balance rocks are placed, most of what remains is
small — and saying so is a result, not a failure to find more.

Two things change at 70, and together they reorder the page.

## 2. What changes at 70 — the outcome menu shifts and the runway shortens

Two independent shifts reorder this page, and neither is a re-grading of the underlying
effect sizes. The younger big-rock list is not wrong; it is answering a question this stratum
no longer asks.

### The outcome menu shifts — function and independence become endpoints in their own right

At younger ages, muscle, bone, mobility and falls read mostly as waypoints toward mortality —
things worth defending because they feed the survival number. At 70 with sarcopenic obesity
they are patient-important outcomes on the one health axis in their own right, and often
*critical*-rated: for many people, keeping mobility, staying out of a care setting and
avoiding a hip fracture weigh above a marginal change in length of life. This is an expansion
of the outcome menu, not a new axis — falls, gait, ADLs and pain are health, rated like any
other outcome, and their weighting stays the person's to set (layer 3)
-> [[The Weighting Problem - Why Population Guidance Is Ill-Posed and Individual Advice Is Not]].

The consequence for ranking is direct. A lever is no longer scored only by what it does to
death or disease events; it is scored by what it does to the function this stratum is actively
losing. That changes which levers sit at the top before a single effect size is re-examined
-> [[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]].



### The runway shortens — competing risk buys fewer disease-free years per lever

The second shift is that a longevity-oriented lever buys less at 70 than at 40, and a guidance
body says so about its own recommendations. In ESC's lifetime-benefit construction, the time
gained free of cardiovascular disease from treating a risk factor is lower in older people —
a person who will die of something else first cannot be given CVD-free years — so ESC raises
its own treatment thresholds at ages >=70
[@esc2021]. The
lifetime-benefit endpoint is itself a composite of a CVD event *or* death, not pure CVD-free
time, which is why the age grids fall so steeply. It is also a modelled quantity, not a
measured one — no trial followed anyone for a lifetime
-> [[Lifetime Benefit - The Frame for Younger Adults]].

The visible edge of this in guidance: at >=70, ESC's lipid-lowering recommendation drops to a
*may be considered* class (Class IIb) even in the very-high-risk band
[@esc2021]. That is a recommendation weakening with
age, not proof the drug's effect vanishes — appraise, don't prescribe.

The runway *caps* these levers; it does not zero them. Because this stratum's baseline
cardiovascular risk is high, a constant relative effect still buys real *absolute* benefit
(route-a) — the blood-pressure and lipid levers keep genuine benefit here that they lack in a
low-risk younger adult. What competing risk shortens is the horizon over which that benefit
accrues, and the shorter horizon is exactly why the menu no longer sorts on mortality alone
-> [[SCORE2 Baseline Risk and the ESC Treatment Thresholds]].

Put together, the outcome menu tilts toward function and the runway trims the pure-longevity
plays — so the ranking sorts function-and-independence first.

Start with the lever that defends what this stratum is actively losing.

## 3. Muscle first — resistance training with adequate protein

This is the lever that rises most at 70: it defends exactly what this stratum is losing.
Two inputs drive one adaptation — resistance training is the driver, dietary protein the
adjunct — and the older, sarcopenic-obese body needs *more* protein, eaten *more evenly*,
than a younger one, because aging blunts the muscle's response to a meal
([[Anabolic Resistance]]). None of what follows is a proven mortality lever; the warrant
is function, muscle and independence.

### The elderly protein target — higher and more meal-distributed than the RDA

PROT-AGE (a four-society consensus, so a target set on surrogates — nitrogen balance,
lean mass — not a hard-outcome trial) gives a daily ladder by catabolic load, all as g
protein per kg body weight per day:

- healthy older adult: 1.0-1.2 g/kg/d
- exercising / active: 1.2 g/kg/d
- acute or chronic illness: 1.2-1.5 g/kg/d

These sit above the general-adult RDA of 0.8 g/kg/d, which is a deficiency floor, not a
muscle-maintenance target [@bauer2013].

### Distribution matters as much as the total

Anabolic resistance raises the *per-meal* trigger, so hitting the daily total is not
enough if it is uneven. Clear roughly 25-30 g protein (about 2.5-2.8 g leucine) at each
of three meals, rather than back-loading it at dinner — the typical Western pattern that
wastes the sub-threshold meals
[@bauer2013]. For healthy kidneys
higher protein is not a hazard; the one flip *down* is a genuine sub-stratum
contraindication — severe kidney disease (eGFR <30, not on dialysis), where protein must
be limited. That is a prescriber act: this person's labs decide it, not the wiki
[@bauer2013].

### \~1.6 g/kg is a FLOOR for an older trainee, not a ceiling

For an older adult training for hypertrophy, the familiar \~1.6 g/kg figure is a floor,
not a cap. It comes from Morton's supplementation MA as a break point of 1.62
g/kg-total-body-mass — but with a *wide* 95% CI of 1.03-2.20 and a non-significant fit
(p=0.079), measured in a largely young, non-energy-restricted, non-obese sample over a
sampled band of \~1.0-2.2 g/kg-total
[@morton2018]. The
supplement's own effect *falls* with age, and anabolic resistance means old muscle needs
at least as much per stimulus — so a non-significant young knee is no reason to aim low
in the old. Read it as a region and a direction, not a point optimum
-> [[The Underivable Optimum]].

### In an energy deficit the target rises — but the obese are OFF-SUPPORT

Weight loss is on this stratum's menu (section 6), and a deficit raises the protein
requirement to hold fat-free mass: \~1.9 g/kgBM/d (or \~2.5 g/kgFFM/d) is where the average
FFM change crosses from loss to hold in Refalo's meta-regression
[@refalo2025]. But that
evidence is in *nonobese*, resistance-trained adults (BF% <=27.8% men / <=39.7% women),
the authors call it exploratory, and the sarcopenic-obese are explicitly off-support. So
for *this* stratum the number does not transport: the direction (a deficit raises the
target) holds, the exact figure is unobserved, and the g/kg denominator is itself
undefined until you name total-versus-lean mass in an obese body.

### Resistance training is the driver; protein is the adjunct

The training does the building; protein lets it build slightly more. Currier's Bayesian
network MA (178 strength / 119 hypertrophy RCTs) shows the decision that carries the
effect is *train versus not-train*: every prescription beat no-exercise, and «The 95% CrI
contained zero for a striking 91% (101/111) of all between-­RTx comparisons»
[@currier2023] — so «adults
should engage in RT, even if they cannot meet existing recommendations»
[@currier2023]. Where
prescription does matter it splits by goal — strength is load-driven (heavy, multiset:
SMD 1.60, 95% CrI 1.38-1.82 versus no exercise), hypertrophy is volume-driven — and a
floor of \~2 sets, \~2x/week captures most of the gain. Programming specifics are
appraisal-level guidance, not a prescriber act.

### The mortality signal is real but weak — not the reason to do this

Muscle-strengthening activity *tracks* lower mortality independent of aerobic exercise
(any versus none: all-cause RR 0.85, 95% CI 0.79-0.93), but the evidence is observational
and GRADE *very low*
[@momma2022]. So mortality is
not the warrant for this lever. The strength case runs through what it preserves —
function, staying off the floor — not through a proven death-rate change.

### The prognostic backbone — grip and mass predict, and the stratum sharpens it

Grip strength and muscle mass each *independently predict* mortality, and this is where
the sarcopenic-obese stratum bites hardest:

- Grip: lower grip *predicts* higher all-cause mortality (per 5 kg lower, HR \~1.16-1.20;
  \~1.48 per 1 SD), a monotone gradient with no located knee over the studied range
  [@celismorales2018].
- Mass: those who died carried \~0.18 SD less appendicular muscle (ASMI SMD -0.18, 95% CI
  -0.23 to -0.12), and — the stratum twist — the mass-mortality link is *strongest at
  higher BMI* (BMI moderator coef -0.044, p=0.031: the sarcopenic-obesity synergy)
  [@santana2021].

### Absolute stakes rise with age — but predictor is not target

Two disciplines govern how to use those numbers. First, absolute benefit: the relative
gradients transport, but the *absolute* stakes scale with baseline risk (route-a), so a
signal that looks small in a middle-aged cohort is far larger in a high-risk 70-year-old
— no subgroup claim needed [inferred from @celismorales2018].

Second, and firmer: these are *predictors, not targets*. That low grip or low mass
*predicts* death (observational) is not that *raising* them *cuts* death — no RCT closes
that loop. So measure grip to place
the person in a risk stratum (the EWGSOP2 weakness screen is a *normative* cut, <27 kg men
/ <16 kg women, set at -2 SD against a healthy-young reference, not outcome-validated)
[@cruzjentoft2019, 04] — but do
not *squeeze harder to live longer*. Pull the muscle lever for function, independence and
sarcopenia; the mortality rationale is associational.

*Building muscle is not the same lever as not falling — they have different active
ingredients, and both must be pulled.*

## 4. Falls and fractures — balance and functional exercise

This is the best-evidenced lever on the whole page — the one place the wiki holds a
HIGH-certainty, RCT-based effect on a patient-important outcome. At least a third of
community-dwelling people over 65 fall each year, and fall-related fracture (hip above
all) is a direct driver of lost independence and death, so a fall is not a surrogate for
anything.

### Exercise reduces falls — and this is HIGH-certainty

In a gold-tier Cochrane review (108 RCTs, 23,407 participants, mean age 76), exercise
versus a control not expected to reduce falls *cut* the rate of falls to RaR 0.77
(95% CI 0.71-0.83; 59 RCTs; HIGH GRADE)
[@sherrington2019]. In absolute terms,
at an illustrative control rate of 850 falls per 1000 people per year, that is \~195
(144-246) fewer falls per 1000 per year — a large absolute effect at this stratum's high
baseline fall risk. Removing the high-risk-of-bias trials left the result essentially
unchanged, so non-blinding did not downgrade it.

### The active ingredient is BALANCE, not volume or lifting

The decision is not *exercise*, it is *balance*. Balance-and-functional training carries
the effect (RaR 0.76, 95% CI 0.70-0.81; 39 RCTs; HIGH), while resistance training *alone*
shows no clear fall benefit — its point estimate sits above 1 (RaR 1.14, 95% CI
0.67-1.97; 5 RCTs; very low)
[@sherrington2019]. «Exercise programmes
that reduce falls primarily involve balance and functional exercises, while programmes
that probably reduce falls include multiple exercise categories (typically balance and
functional exercises plus resistance exercises)»
[@sherrington2019]. And *walk more* is
not fall-prevention advice — walking programmes have insufficient evidence. This is why
section 3's muscle lever and this one stay distinct: falls are a balance/gait problem, and
strength is a *component* of the effective multi-category programmes, not the lever alone.

### Fractures — the downstream signal, honestly lower-certainty

The outcome people most fear is the worst-measured. Exercise carries a fracture signal
downstream of the fall reduction — fall-related fractures RR 0.73 (95% CI 0.56-0.95; 10
RCTs) — but only at LOW certainty (downgraded for imprecision and likely publication
bias), because fractures are rarer events needing far larger trials, not because the
effect is absent
[@sherrington2019]. So the chain
*exercise -> fewer falls* is HIGH-certainty; the downstream *exercise -> fewer fractures*
is promising but LOW.

### The counter-lever — the vitamin-D/calcium pill fails in the community

The intuitive bone-protective move — supplement vitamin D and/or calcium — does *not*
prevent fractures in the generally-nourished community-dwelling older adult. The USPSTF
evidence review (Kahwati 2018, gold SR, 11 RCTs, N=51,419, with the deficient /
institutionalized / osteoporotic / prior-fracture strata excluded) found no fracture
benefit (vitamin D + calcium: total fracture ARD -0.35%, 95% CI -1.02 to 0.31, HR 0.96)
and a *harm* — kidney stones rose (RR 1.18, 95% CI 1.04-1.35, moderate strength of
evidence)
[@kahwati2018].

This is not *supplements don't work* — it is the *enhancement* arm of a two-arm curve. The
pill's benefit lives in the *excluded* strata (confirmed-deficient, osteoporotic,
institutionalized), where it is a deficiency-repletion lever, not a community-wide one
-> [[Deficiency Repletion vs Enhancement]]. So the two legs of fracture prevention run
opposite ways here: for the non-deficient, non-osteoporotic older adult the movement leg
(balance + functional exercise, fracture RR 0.73) is the better-evidenced bone move,
reversing the common intuition that the pill is protective. The pill earns its place only
in the confirmed-deficient sub-stratum — a prescriber act needing this person's labs
[inferred from @kahwati2018; @sherrington2019].

*Aerobic capacity and frailty round out the movement levers.*

## 5. Aerobic activity and frailty

Aerobic activity earns a place here for mortality, but at 70 the headline number is not
the reason to do it. Device-measured cohorts bank most of the all-cause mortality benefit
at roughly 24 min/day of moderate-to-vigorous activity — read as a flat-region central
estimate (about 20-40 min/day), not a threshold to clear — after which the curve flattens
[@who2020]. The steepest
drop is the first step off the floor: getting out of the least-active quartile roughly
halves mortality (HR 0.48)
[@ekelundacc2019]. *For this
stratum the falls-and-function payoff outweighs the mortality figure* — the balance work of
the previous section, plus the frailty gains below, are what the movement is for here.


**Frailty is a lever, not a fate.** The older-adult syndrome of reduced physiological
reserve predicts a wide set of patient-important outcomes (mortality, disability, falls,
fractures), but it is also *modifiable* — the decision-relevant refinement a prognosis-only
reading misses. A GRADE-rated meta-analysis of physical-activity RCTs in identified
pre-frail/frail adults >=65 found:

> «Significant effects were found for mobility (standardized mean difference [SMD] 0.60,
> 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.37 to 0.83), activities of daily living (SMD 0.50, 95% CI
> 0.15 to 0.84), cognitive function (SMD 0.35, 95% CI 0.09 to 0.61), quality of life (SMD
> 0.60, 95% CI 0.13 to 1.07) and frailty (SMD –1.29, 95% CI –2.22 to –0.36; risk ratio
> 0.58, 95% CI 0.36 to 0.93), with moderate certainty of evidence.»
> [@racey2021frailtypa]

**Train the measurable components; read *frailty* as the composite that follows.** This
refines rather than overturns the older view that frailty *cannot itself be targeted* — a
concern grounded in its definitional slipperiness (the source states no verbatim such
claim; the phrasing is this deliverable's):

> «A lack of consensus regarding the definition of frailty, and an absence of core measures
> to assess this means any attempt to create an optimal intervention will be impeded.»
> [@who2020]

The effective interventions *are* component training (resistance and mixed aerobic-plus-
strength), and frailty status itself was measured in only a handful of the trials (4 of 23)
— too rarely to serve as a treat-to-target endpoint
[@racey2021frailtypa]. So
aim at strength, balance and aerobic capacity directly; the frailty-status improvement (RR
0.58) is the downstream composite, read with that thin-base caveat, not as a number to chase.

**Dose the activity DOWN for the already-frail — the general guideline does not transport.**
The same review flags a sub-stratum boundary that binds inside this cut:

> «recommended physical activity may be too advanced or too intense for a frailer population,
> put- ting them at risk for falls and injuries»
> [@racey2021frailtypa]

And the falls payoff is *route-dependent by frailty depth*: the high-certainty fall reduction
holds in general community-dwelling older adults, but in the *already-frail* the falls signal
is uncertain on thin data (RR 0.80, 0.51-1.26, very low certainty)
[@racey2021frailtypa]. So
the pre-frail patient gets the full aerobic/component prescription; the deeply frail one gets a
supervised, dialled-back version whose falls benefit is not assured — an in-context condition,
not a new whole-cut caveat.
[inferred from @racey2021frailtypa]

The headline younger lever — lose the visceral fat — needs the most re-grading.

## 6. Weight and visceral fat — conditional, muscle-and-bone-protective, exercise-weighted, never rapid

At 40 this is the unqualified top rock. At 70 with sarcopenic obesity it keeps genuine
benefits but changes character: it becomes a **muscle-and-bone-protective, exercise-weighted,
modest-not-rapid** lever. This is a route-(b) age-inversion — the *sign becomes conditional*,
and (as below) the conditionality rests on mechanism plus partial data, so it is stated as
*conditionality, never as evidenced elderly harm.*


**Reframe first: steer by depot, not the scale.** Cardiometabolic risk tracks *where* fat is
stored more faithfully than total mass. Visceral fat is not the villain it is treated as — it
is a marker for the intra-organ (liver, pancreas) fat doing the damage:

> «Extent of visceral fat accumulation is a surrogate marker for intra-organ fat ex- cess, but
> is not pathophysiologically related to adverse metabolic consequences»
> [@taylor2015pft]

So the operative variable is depot / metabolic status, not the number on the scale — and some
lean patients are above their personal fat threshold while some obese ones are, for now, less
so.

**But metabolically-healthy obesity is not durably benign.** Observational cohorts show the
excess risk is real, though small and slow to appear:

> «there is no healthy pattern of increased weight»
> [@kramer2013mho]

The honest framing is *a small, time-latent, non-zero excess that emerges only after about a
decade* — it licenses continued, not urgent, attention
[inferred from @kramer2013mho]. Whether *moderate overweight is protective at advanced age* (the
obesity paradox in the very old, via reserve or competing risk) is a separate, **unheld**
question — a named gap handled in the age-inversions section; do not infer a sign from it here.

**Benefits that are kept — because they sit on outcomes weight loss demonstrably moves.**

- **Fatty liver (MASLD).** Sustained loss draws down liver fat on a dose-dependent ladder —
  the guideline names >=5% to reduce steatosis, 7-10% for inflammation, >=10% for fibrosis:
  «There is a dose-dependent association between the amount of weight loss and the extent of
  improvement in biomarkers of liver damage»
  [@easl2024]. Read those percentages
  as *name-the-curve* guidance, not precise cutpoints: they trace to a single histology-endpoint
  trial (no pooled interval), the studied basis is thin, and few sustain even >=5%.
- **Type 2 diabetes remission.** An energy-restricted total-diet-replacement programme put
  nearly half of short-duration patients into drug-free remission in a primary-care RCT:
  «Diabetes remission was achieved in 68 (46%) participants in the intervention group and six
  (4%) participants in the control group (odds ratio 19·7, 95% CI 7·8–49·8; p<0·0001)»
  [@lean2018]. Remission rose monotonically with
  kilograms lost — from 0% among those who gained weight to 7% (0-5 kg) and 34% (5-10 kg),
  higher still above — with no visible plateau across the studied range up to >=15 kg
  [@lean2018].
- **And the remission lever reaches the normal-weight T2D patient** DiRECT's BMI floor
  excludes: reversal is «achievable equally readily by people with lower initial BMI»
  [@taylor2015pft],
  at a *smaller* required loss (about 13% at normal body weight vs \~21% for the whole cohort
  to reach normoglycaemia)
  [@taylor2015pft].
- **Blood pressure, glycaemia and mobility** improve alongside; and losing the adiposity
  restores testosterone naturally in the obese hypogonadal man — though the effect is *«modest»*
  by the realistic non-surgical route
  [@corona2013]. That
  natural route is the one to prefer: testosterone *therapy* is rejected as an anabolic here on a
  surrogate inversion (bone density up, fractures up), covered in the age-inversions section.

**What it does NOT reliably buy: fewer heart attacks.** The largest, longest lifestyle
weight-loss RCT was null on hard cardiovascular events — intensive lifestyle intervention
«did not reduce cardiovascular events in overweight or obese adults with type 2 diabetes»
(HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.83 to 1.09)
[@look2013]. The 54-RCT meta-analysis
generalises the null on events (RR 0.93) while finding a *modest all-cause mortality* benefit
(«risk ratio 0.82, 95% confidence interval 0.71 to 0.95»)
[@ma2017]. Since the events are null,
*that mortality benefit runs around the heart, not through it* — a moved bundle of surrogates
is not the cardioprotection it lacks.


**How to lose it without losing muscle — weight the deficit toward exercise.** At a *matched*
weekly energy deficit, a meta-regression of 40 RCTs found «exercise had a superior dose–response
effect on reducing visceral fat compared with caloric restriction (ES −0.18 (−0.33 to −0.04);
p=0.012)», and only exercise was dose-dependent (−0.15 per 1000 kcal/week)
[@recchia2023]. The
mechanism is muscle-sparing: «during caloric restriction both fat and muscle mass are reduced»
whereas «exercise might stimulate fat loss while maintaining muscle mass»
[@recchia2023]. So the
prescription is a deficit *weighted toward activity*, paired with resistance training and the
raised protein target of the muscle section, kept modest.

**The muscle-and-bone cost of an aggressive deficit is conditionality, not evidenced elderly
harm.** The mechanism is real and partially measured — a diet-only deficit spends part of
itself on lean tissue (Recchia, above), and the elderly-specific fat-vs-lean/bone trade-off is
reasoned from mechanism plus partial trial data on the muscle and energy-restriction pages
(-> [[Protein Intake During Energy Restriction]], [[Knee Osteoarthritis and Modifiable Levers]],
[[Protein and Resistance Training for Muscle and Strength]]). No muscle-and-bone-preserving
weight-loss trial in older adults yet exists — the central trade-off is *unmeasured in this
stratum* (a named gap). So the warrant is route-(b) mechanism-plus-partial: it justifies *how*
to lose (exercise-weighted, resistance-paired, protein-supported, modest and slow), not a claim
that losing weight harms the elderly.


The cardiovascular drug levers are age-adjusted, not switched off.

## 7. Blood pressure, glycaemia and lipids — age-adjusted, not switched off

The disease-number levers do not retire at 70. They *re-rate*, and the direction of the
re-rating is not obvious. Baseline cardiovascular risk is *high* in this stratum, so a
constant relative effect buys **more** absolute benefit here, not less — the same arithmetic
that makes these levers small for a low-risk 40-year-old makes them real for an obese
70-year-old. The reframe is [[Cardiometabolic Interventions and Hard CV Outcomes in Low-Risk People]] read in reverse: where that page finds no large *absolute* lever because
the baseline is low, this stratum sits at the other end of the same curve. This is
**route-(a) stratification — absolute benefit scaling with baseline risk, no subgroup
effect-modification claim required** [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]. What
shortens with age is the *runway*: competing mortality caps how many disease-free years any
longevity-oriented lever can still buy.

**Blood pressure stays a real lever.** The pooled trial evidence is that lowering systolic
blood pressure by 5 mmHg cut major cardiovascular events by «about 10%, irrespective of
previous diagnoses of cardiovascular disease, and even at normal or high-normal blood
pressure values» — HR 0.91 (0.89-0.94) with no prior CVD, and «in no subgroup was the HR
above 1.0» across baseline strata from <120 to >=170 mmHg
[@bplttc2021].
The effect is roughly constant; the *absolute* gain rises with this stratum's high baseline
risk. Per 5 mmHg the components are stroke 13%, heart failure 13%, ischaemic heart disease
8%, cardiovascular death 5%
[@bplttc2021].

**But treat on absolute risk, not a BP number — and the harm side is real here.** BPLTTC's
own rule is to «emphasise its importance on reducing cardiovascular risk rather than focusing
on blood pressure reduction itself»
[@bplttc2021].
The one RCT that pushed an intensive target, SPRINT, cut the primary composite (HR 0.75,
0.64-0.89) and all-cause mortality (HR 0.73, 0.60-0.90), «number needed to treat ... 61» over
3.26 years — but intensive treatment raised «hypotension, syncope, electrolyte abnormalities,
and acute kidney injury or failure, but not ... injurious falls»
[@sprint2015].

So a lower target buys events at a real adverse-event cost. *How low to go, which agent, and whether to
deprescribe are prescriber acts — the wiki appraises, it does not titrate.* This
appraise-not-prescribe line binds hardest in this stratum, where orthostasis and polypharmacy
turn over-treatment into its own harm.

**Glycaemia: control matters, tight control less so.** *Glycaemic control is a route-(a)
lever like blood pressure — the higher this stratum's baseline complication risk, the more a
given relative reduction is worth. But two things reweight it downward at 70: the runway
buys fewer complication-free years for the slow microvascular endpoints, and the harm of
tight control — hypoglycaemia, and its downstream falls and cognitive cost — rises with age
and frailty. So the target loosens as the person ages, and the choice of agent and glucose
target is again a prescriber act, not something to read off a number here.* —
this stratum-specific reweighting is the wiki's synthesis; no glycaemia effect-size feeder is
carried in this section.

**Lipids: the relative effect holds, the competing risk shrinks what it buys.** Lowering LDL
still works — statin efficacy is roughly constant per unit LDL reduction, composite CVD RR
0.72 (0.64-0.81) in primary-prevention trials
[@uspstf2022] — and «the absolute benefit of
lowering LDL-C depends on the absolute risk of ASCVD ... even a small absolute reduction in
LDL-C may be beneficial in a high- or very-high-risk patient»
[@esc2021]. So at high absolute risk the
lipid lever is still worth pulling.

**What changes is the guideline's own confidence with age:** at >=70 ESC drops lipid-lowering to **Class IIb («may be considered»)** even in the
very-high band [@esc2021], and USPSTF finds
the evidence for *starting* a statin insufficient at >=76 (its rule covers 40-75)
[@uspstf2022]. This is [tension 4]: the benefit
does not *vanish* at 70 — it is a **diminishing, competing-risk-capped lever, still worth it
where absolute risk is high**, not an absent one. The fewer disease-free years the runway can
buy, the smaller the case, but a high enough baseline risk keeps it positive.

Beyond the disease numbers sit the levers that defend daily life.

## 8. Quality-of-life and function levers — pain, mobility, mood

Pain, mobility and mood are not soft outcomes bolted on beside the disease numbers. They are
**patient-important endpoints on the one health axis, often critical-rated at 70** — for many
people weighted above a marginal change in length of life
[[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]. This is an expansion of the outcome menu,
not a separate axis: the weighting of function-versus-longevity stays the person's, elicited
per case (layer 3). What makes these levers rank high in *this* stratum is that the movement
lever which defends function is frequently the *same* lever that defends muscle, joint and
metabolic health — one intervention, several critical outcomes.

**Knee osteoarthritis — a large, high-certainty QoL lever that double-counts.** In the IDEA
RCT (454 overweight/obese adults >=55, 18 months), weight loss and exercise acted through
different mechanisms and were **additive**: the diet-plus-exercise arm «had less pain relative
to the E (mean score, 1.02; 95% CI, 0.33-1.71; P = .004) and D (1.13; 95% CI, 0.44-1.82;
P = .001) groups», with 38% reporting little or no pain versus \~20% for diet-alone and
exercise-alone, and function, 6-minute walk and physical HRQL all best in the combined arm
[@messier2013idea].

The weight-loss leg here is the *same* visceral-fat lever the cardiometabolic sections rest on, so
for the overweight knee-OA patient one intervention buys joint **and** cardiometabolic benefit
-> [[Knee Osteoarthritis and Modifiable Levers]]. And the common fear that movement damages an
arthritic joint is answered directly: across 49 arthritis RCTs, «No detrimental effects of PA
were reported in any study», and exercise «should be an integral part of standard care»
[@rauschosthoff2018exercise].

**Chronic pain generally — safe, probably a small help, evidence genuinely weak.** The
gold-tier Cochrane umbrella (21 SRs, 264 exercise-vs-control studies) found physical function
«significantly improved ... in 14 reviews» though with «small-to-moderate effect sizes», an
inconsistent low-certainty pain signal, and — the load-bearing finding — «none of the physical
and activity interventions assessed appeared to cause harm»
[@geneen2017chronicpain]. The low certainty is a structural weakness (tiny trials, mild baseline pain,
short follow-up), *not* evidence of no effect -> [[Chronic Pain and Physical Activity]].
Reporting *safe, probably a small function benefit, evidence weak* is the honest output, a
result rather than a hedge — and against the realistic comparator of drugs or surgery, a safe
self-managed lever is an attractive adjunct even at low certainty.

**Mood — peripheral but admitted, through the physical intersection.** Depression enters this
wiki via its physiological intersection with physical health (the HPA / inflammation /
metabolic channels) and as a lever that sustains adherence to the movement levers above, not
as an end in itself. Exercise is the better-warranted of the mood levers: a network
meta-analysis of 218 RCTs found walking or jogging «−0.63 (−0.80 to −0.46)» and strength
training «−0.49 (−0.69 to −0.29)» in Hedges' g versus active control, with the dose being
**intensity, not volume or energy expenditure**
[@noetel2024exercise]. Certainty is low
(unblindable behaviour, self-reported scale).

The diet-quality route is weaker still —
observational, OR «0.77 (0.69 to 0.84)» for incident depression, and fragile to reverse-
causation and diagnosis-based checks
[@molendijk2017diet]. So
exercise is a defensible adjunct here, diet a thin one -> [[Depression and Modifiable Exposures]]. Mental health stays peripheral; it earns its place through the physical
intersection.

A handful of smaller, cross-linked levers remain.

## 9. Small or contested levers — cross-linked, not re-derived

These levers each have their own deliverable; here they earn one line and a link out, not a
re-argument. Every one of them ranks LOW for this stratum on the same test — a small effect
crossed with thin certainty, and mostly on outcomes (CV events, dental) that competing risk
caps or that sit off the function-first menu. The verdict lives on the linked page.

- **Saturated / poly-unsaturated fat replacement** — events-only and small; it moves CV
  events (competing-risk-capped here), not function, muscle or falls, so it sits low on this
  page -> [[Saturated Fat Intake and Replacement]].
- **Free sugars** — essentially a dental-caries recommendation with little bearing on this
  stratum's function-first menu -> [[Free Sugars Intake]].
- **Sodium** — double-edged in the old: over-restriction can worsen appetite and total
  intake in a frailty-prone older adult, blunting the protein target that matters more
  -> [[Sodium Intake and Blood Pressure]].
- **Red / processed meat** — a small effect, and the protein-quality COUNTER-axis pulls the
  other way here: meat is a dense leucine source for anabolic-resistant muscle, so the
  general "reduce" verdict is weighed against §3's protein need -> [[Should Adults Reduce Red and Processed Meat]].
- **Alcohol** — the protective J-curve is an artifact; polypharmacy and MASLD sharpen the
  case for keeping intake low -> [[Alcohol and Mortality and Vascular Disease]].
- **Sleep** — aim for a normal range (roughly 7-8 h); a NEW long-sleep pattern is an illness
  MARKER to investigate, not a duration to cut toward -> [[Sleep Duration and Mortality]].
- **Inflammation** — CRP is a MARKER, not a target; the evidenced anti-inflammatory levers
  are drugs, out of lifestyle reach -> [[Inflammation as a Modifiable Lever]].
- **Menopause-era levers (historical for this stratum)** — the sarcopenic-obesity, bone-loss
  and visceral-fat levers here trace back through the post-menopausal transition this stratum
  is \~20 years past; note only that estrogen's bone-density -> fracture chain runs the *right*
  way where testosterone's inverts (§10) -> [[Menopause and the Shifting Levers]],
  [[Hormone Therapy After Menopause]].

Two of the younger levers do more than shrink here — they change sign.

## 10. Where age flips the sign — the inversions

A sign-inversion is the most expensive Layer-1 error available: it spends effort in the
wrong DIRECTION, not merely on a small lever. So each entry below carries its warrant
explicitly. Every one is a route-(b) effect-modification claim — age changes the sign or the
weight — and the wiki assigns that change ONLY from held age-moderation evidence, never from
mechanism alone. Where the warrant is mechanism plus partial data, the entry states
CONDITIONALITY, not evidenced harm.

### Weight loss — the sign becomes conditional, not reversed (the obesity-paradox gap)

At 40, *lose the visceral fat* is the unqualified #1 rock. At 70 with sarcopenic obesity it
does not reverse — the liver, glycaemia, BP and joint benefits are real (see §6) — but its
sign becomes CONDITIONAL on defending muscle and bone.

The epistemic root is a NAMED GAP, and it is left OPEN, not resolved. BMI's association with
mortality attenuates, and in some elderly cohorts reverses (the "obesity paradox"). The wiki
does NOT infer a protective sign from this — the association is heavily confounded:

- **reverse causation** — illness lowers weight before death, dragging the low-BMI arm's
  mortality up;
- **sarcopenic-obesity heterogeneity** — one BMI hides opposite body compositions (muscle
  vs fat), so a BMI contrast is not a fat contrast;
- **collider bias** — conditioning on established disease can manufacture an apparent
  protective slope.

 — the confounding read is the wiki's, via [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]].

What the wiki DOES hold runs the other way at the depot level: the muscle-mass -> mortality
association is STRONGER at higher BMI (the sarcopenic-obesity modifier)
-> [[Low Muscle Mass and Mortality]], and metabolically-healthy obesity is not durably
benign — RR 1.24 (1.02-1.55) vs healthy normal-weight in cohorts with >=10 y follow-up
[@kramer2013mho]
-> [[Ectopic Fat and Depot-Specific Risk]]. So the weight-loss sign is CONDITIONAL (pursue
it muscle-and-bone-protectively, §6), never reversed; the very-old paradox stays unheld
-> §12.

Warrant class: route-(b) via the BMI-modified mass-mortality association + Kramer's MHO
signal (held); the paradox itself is mechanism-plus-unheld -> conditionality, not evidenced
harm.

### Testosterone — the surrogate inversion (mass up, fractures up)

Testosterone therapy (TRT) raises muscle MASS — a surrogate — by roughly 1.6 to 3.6 kg
[@lopezhilario2026]. But the
patient-important outcome runs the OTHER way. In TRAVERSE's bone substudy — a trial powered
to detect a 30% fracture REDUCTION — clinical fractures were higher on testosterone than
placebo: 91/2601 (3.50%) vs 64/2603 (2.46%), HR 1.43 (95% CI 1.04-1.97, unadjusted)
[@snyder2024].

So the mass gain does NOT license TRT: the credentialed surrogate (mass, and separately bone
density) moved the right way while fractures moved the wrong way -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]].
Function gains are modest and do not reliably translate. The preferred route is natural
restoration — in an obese man a low T reading is largely a readout of the adiposity
(secondary hypogonadism), and weight loss is associated with a rise in T (dose-dependent,
though modest by the realistic diet route), while delivering benefits TRT does not
-> [[Testosterone Adiposity and Muscle]]. Treat the fat, train the muscle; TRT is a narrow
option for confirmed hypogonadism only.

Warrant class: route-(b) from a powered RCT (Snyder / TRAVERSE) — therapy- and
outcome-specific, held, not mechanism.

### Tight glycaemic and lipid control — more-is-better stops holding (-> §7)

The *lower is better, treat-to-target* logic that governs a 50-year-old weakens at 70.
Guidance itself is age-graded: statin initiation drops in strength with age (the ESC/USPSTF
reading is derived in §7). Intensive glycaemic and BP targets trade their benefit against
falls, orthostasis and polypharmacy. These are inversions of WEIGHT, not full sign flips —
the levers still buy absolute benefit at this stratum's high baseline risk (route-a), but
the competing-risk runway caps it and over-treatment becomes its own harm. Target-setting
and deprescribing are PRESCRIBER acts: appraise, don't prescribe. Full treatment is in §7.

Warrant class: route-(b) age-graded guidance (held) + the prescriber-act boundary.

### The vitamin-D / calcium pill — a sign flip by stratum (-> §4)

In the confirmed-deficient or osteoporotic older adult, repletion earns its place. In the
generally-nourished community older adult the SAME pill flips to net harm (fracture null plus
kidney-stone excess) — a stratum-conditional sign flip, derived in §4. Here the exercise leg,
not the pill, is the fracture lever.

Warrant class: route-(b)/route-(c) — stratum-specific, held (Kahwati, §4).

### Counter-note — grip's RELATIVE signal is if anything stronger younger

One apparent inversion runs against intuition and is stated so it is not over-read: grip
strength is not a pure old-age screen. With 500,000+ participants Celis-Morales found the
grip-mortality association was «moderately stronger, not weaker, in the younger age groups»
[@celismorales2018]. The ABSOLUTE stakes
still rise with age via baseline risk (route-a), but the RELATIVE signal does not — so grip
stays a stratification metric to MEASURE, not a number to STEER, and its role does not
amplify simply because the person is older -> [[Grip Strength and Mortality]].

One big rock does not invert — it only grows.

## 11. Smoking — the big rock that does not invert

If this stratum smokes, cessation dominates the entire ranking at any age — no nutrition or
activity lever on this page approaches it. Current smoking is associated with about three
times the never-smoker mortality rate (HR \~3), and cessation pays at every age: quitting at
45-54 is associated with roughly 6 years of life gained and removal of about two-thirds of
the excess risk [@jha2013]. The
elderly RELATIVE effect attenuates, but the ABSOLUTE benefit stays large — a large relative
effect on this stratum's high baseline risk yields a big absolute gain, and competing risk
shortens but does NOT erase the runway -> [[Smoking and Mortality]],
[[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]].

If this stratum does NOT smoke, the lever is already pulled — and that is itself a FINDING,
not a blank. The single largest exposure is off the table, so the achievable ceiling is set
by the muscle, balance and weight rocks below it; a pulled big rock is reported, not skipped
-> §12. Cessation METHODS (pharmacotherapy, behavioural support) are prescriber/support acts,
out of scope — appraise, don't prescribe.

Once these rocks are placed, the honest finding is how little is left.

## 12. The ceiling, the gaps, and the bottom line

### The ceiling is a finding

Once resistance training with adequate protein (§3), balance and falls-prevention (§4), and
the conditional weight / visceral-fat lever (§6) are handled — and any smoking addressed
(§11) — the remaining gains are small BY CONSTRUCTION. Each big exposure has a plateau
established earlier in this cut (aerobic benefit near \~24 min/day of moderate activity, §5;
muscle-strengthening giving most of its benefit at \~30-60 min/week, §3/§5; protein around
1.6 g/kg as a FLOOR that flattens above it, §3 — each carrying the studied-range caveat there).
Beyond them the long tail is small and uncertain.

Reporting *your remaining levers are small and uncertain* is itself a decision-change: it
licenses a reader to STOP optimizing rather than chase refinements that cannot move a
patient-important outcome much. That is delivered as a result, not as a failure to find more.

### The named gaps — stated, routed, not filled

- **Obesity paradox in the oldest-old** — whether moderate overweight is protective via
  physiological reserve or competing risk at advanced age is UNHELD; do not infer a sign
  (§10). Route to acquisition.
- **Muscle-and-bone-preserving weight-loss RCT in the elderly** — the central trade-off (§6)
  is reasoned from mechanism plus IDEA / TREAT / Refalo (with the obese off the studied
  range), not measured in this stratum; still unheld.
- **Elderly-specific RCT scarcity / transportability** — most levers here are transported
  from younger or mixed-age trials; the effect estimate AND the baseline-risk estimate each
  carry a transportability caveat.
- **QoL-trajectory under-measured** — compressed morbidity (full function then a rapid end)
  is what this stratum most wants and what trials least measure; a clean mortality or
  incidence result can be silent on the trajectory a person actually values.
- **Off-support for the OBESE in the muscle-sparing-deficit evidence** — the
  protein-during-restriction targets (Refalo) were derived in leaner trainees; the obese sit
  off the studied range, so the number is stated as conditionality, not a held target.
- **No agreed operational cut-off for sarcopenic obesity** — the stratum rests on an
  undefined construct (EWGSOP2 leaves it out of scope).
- **Bone-density as a steering target is unheld** — the fracture lever here runs through
  balance/functional exercise (§4), not through a bone-density-dosed loading protocol or a
  protein-for-bone target; whether BMD can be *steered* to a fracture benefit in this stratum
  is not held, only that the pill does not do it in the non-deficient (§4).
- **Deprescribing / polypharmacy** — unheld and a prescriber act; named, not appraised.

The prior cut's #1 named gap — elderly protein dosing — is now CLOSED (the PROT-AGE target
is held, §3).

### The bottom line

Overall `confidence: low`. This is NOT the younger big-rock list re-graded. Two things move
at once — the outcome menu shifts toward function, muscle, bone, falls and independence, and
the competing-risk runway shortens for pure-longevity plays — and together they reorder the
whole page onto **function-and-independence first**. The ranking is stratum-specific (the
obese \~70 with sarcopenic obesity), not a universal ordering and not a re-graded list for a
younger person.

Against the guidance null, this page mostly *agrees* with mainstream geriatric advice on the
big movers — resistance training, protein, balance work, treating high absolute risk — so the
value is not divergence. Where it goes further is in *naming how thin the elderly-specific
evidence is*, holding the function-first reordering explicitly, and marking the age-inversions
(testosterone, the vitamin-D pill, tight targets) that a population guideline states less
sharply.

The loop is open. No lever on this page is graded against a realized outcome: the wiki grades
coherence and source-fidelity, never validity, so a clean internal audit is not a validated
recommendation. The weighting of length-versus-trajectory, and of the competing outcomes,
stays the person's (layer 3); target-setting, deprescribing and fall-risk titration are
prescriber acts. This page appraises — it does not prescribe.
