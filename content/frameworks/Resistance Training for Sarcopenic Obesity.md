The decision: an older adult **already has sarcopenic obesity** (SO — coexisting excess fat and
age-related low muscle mass/strength/function) and is deciding whether resistance training (RT) alone,
*without* a prescribed diet or weight-loss program, is worth doing — and for which outcome. Polo-Ferrero
2025 is a gold-tier SR-MA (11 RCTs met inclusion; 9 pooled; 513 subjects) answering exactly this: RT
versus **no treatment / no exercise**, no caloric restriction in either arm.
[inferred from @poloferrero2025]

This is the sibling of [[Resistance Training During Caloric Restriction]] (Sardeli) but a **different
decision** — see the parameter table below; the two are not in tension and must not be read as one
estimate.

## Population and design

[@poloferrero2025]

- **Who:** older adults meeting an operational SO definition — obesity (BF% > 30% or BMI > 30 kg/m²)
  **and** sarcopenia (SMI ≤ 7.76 kg/m², gait speed ≤ 1 m/s, or hand grip ≤ 21 kg). Mean age 68 ± 5.94;
  the studies «primarily focused on women with SO», one included both sexes. SO «affect[s] approximately
  11% of older adults globally».
- **Exposure:** RT, «typically over a 12-week period» (one 10-week), three sessions per week (one twice
  weekly). **No dietary / caloric-restriction co-intervention** — searched: `caloric restriction` /
  `weight loss` / `energy restriction` return zero across both source chunks.
- **Comparator:** no treatment / without exercise (not diet, not another modality).
- **Quality:** PEDro overall good (1 fair, 7 good, 3 excellent). GRADE moderate-to-very-low by outcome.

## What RT does — three outcomes, three verdicts

[@poloferrero2025]

**Body composition — small benefit, GRADE moderate (the best-graded of the three outcomes).** Pooled across SMI, ASM,
BF%, BMI, BMD (7 studies): «favorable effects on body composition ... (standardized mean difference
[SMD] = 0.35, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 0.12; 0.57, p = 0.003, n = 324)», heterogeneity I² = 0%.
Funnel asymmetry flags possible publication bias. «Moderate-quality evidence supports improvements in
body composition (SMD = 0.35; 95% CI: 0.12, 0.57).»

**Physical performance — small benefit, GRADE low.** 4 studies: «positive effects on physical
performance in older individuals with sarcopenia and obesity compared to those without exercise,
demonstrating a low effect size (SMD = 0.36; 95% CI = 0.03; 0.69; p = 0.003; n = 155)», I² = 0%. The
only significant performance subgroup was single-leg stance (SLS): «SMD = 0.88; 95% CI = 0.09; 1.68;
p = 0.03; n = 29» — a wide interval on 29 people, so a fragile, hypothesis-generating signal, not a
reliable magnitude.

**Blood biomarkers — null, GRADE low/very-low.** 3 studies (LDL, HDL, TG, CRP): «no significant
differences in biomarker concentrations between older individuals with SO undergoing exercise programs
compared to those without exercise (SMD = 0.1; 95% CI = −0.28; 0.49; p = 0.6; n = 111)». A 12-week RT
program did not move the lipid/inflammatory panel in this pool — insufficient evidence rather than proof
of no effect (small n, short horizon; authors note baseline inflammation/insulin status may modify
response).

## The benefit is fat-loss-weighted, not demonstrated muscle gain

[@poloferrero2025]
The composite body-composition SMD is carried by **fat**, not by shown muscle accretion. The only
significant body-composition subgroup was BF%: «exercise interventions led to a significant reduction in
BF% among older individuals with sarcopenia and obesity when compared to those without exercise
(SMD = 0.52, 95% CI = 0.19; 0.86, p = 0.002, n = 150)». The muscle-mass subgroups (SMI, ASM) did not
reach significance. The authors state the nuance plainly: «While RT is known to enhance lean mass [29],
some studies, similar to our own, have reported reductions in BF% without a corresponding increase in
muscle mass [67–69]», and «one trial found that RT did not significantly increase muscle mass in older
adults with SO, but led to improvements in physical function».

**Decision consequence.** In the SO stratum, the realistic near-term RT return is **lower body fat and
better function, not bigger muscles**. That inverts the naive *RT builds muscle* expectation for this
population — a refinement, not a contradiction, of
[[Protein and Resistance Training for Muscle and Strength]] (where muscle/strength gains are shown in
leaner or diagnosed-sarcopenic — but not specifically sarcopenic-*obese* — pools).

## Surrogate-to-outcome transmission (the open loop)

[inferred from @poloferrero2025]
Every endpoint here is a **surrogate**: BF%, SMI, SMD body-composition and performance-test scores, not
falls, disability, independence, or mortality. The patient-important link is asserted, not measured in
this MA: the authors argue «even modest improvements in these variables can significantly reduce the
risk of disability and falls in older adults», and that balance/postural gains matter «for fall
prevention». Treat function (SLS, gait, chair-stand) as the closer-to-patient target and BF%/muscle
indices as the farther surrogate -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]. The loop stays open: no endpoint here is a
realized patient outcome.

## Distinction from Sardeli (RT during caloric restriction) — not a tension

This page resolves the open thread left on [[Resistance Training During Caloric Restriction]]
(`[AWAITS]` now struck). The two MAs answer **different questions** on every axis; the parameter table is
the evidence, built before the prose (no cell, no claim):

| Parameter | Sardeli — RT During Caloric Restriction | Polo-Ferrero — RT for Sarcopenic Obesity | Same quantity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Population | obese **elderly** (mean > 57 y) undergoing a weight-loss diet; not required to be sarcopenic | **sarcopenic obesity** (coexisting low muscle + excess fat; mean 68, mostly women); **not dieting** | **NO** |
| Exposure | RT **added to caloric restriction** (RT during an energy deficit) | RT **alone**, 12 wk, 3×/wk; **no dietary co-intervention** | **NO** |
| Comparator | caloric restriction **alone** (CR without RT) | **no treatment / no exercise** (no deficit in either arm) | **NO** |
| Outcome | **lean-body-mass retention during weight loss** — absolute RMD in kg; % of CR-induced LBM loss prevented | **composite body-composition SMD** (SMI/ASM/BF%/BMI/BMD pooled); performance SMD; biomarker SMD | **NO** (retention fraction vs standardized composite) |
| Effect | \~93.5% of CR-induced LBM loss prevented; RMD 0.819 kg [0.364–1.273], p < 0.001 | body-comp SMD 0.35 [0.12, 0.57]; performance 0.36 [0.03, 0.69]; biomarkers null | **NO** |

[inferred from @poloferrero2025; @sardeli2018]

**Not-joined check (ii) fires decisively** — different scope/population/exposure/outcome-unit, consistent
once matched: *RT preserves muscle a diet would otherwise strip, during a deficit* (Sardeli) and *RT
lowers fat and improves function in sarcopenic-obese people who are not dieting* (Polo-Ferrero) are both
true and do not clash. So this is a **distinction, not a `[[tension]]` and not type-E** (different claim,
not the same claim by independent routes; author lists are also fully disjoint).

**The \~50% referent was mis-attributed.** Sardeli's open thread contrasted its 93.5% against «50% LBM
loss attenuation when different types of exercise were added to CR in sarcopenic obese individuals»,
reading the gap as evidence RT is the active modality. That \~50% figure is from **Sardeli's own cited
ref [10]** — a different, mixed-modality MA of exercise *added to CR* — **not** from Polo-Ferrero.
Polo-Ferrero reports no lean-mass-retention-under-deficit fraction at all (it has no deficit), so it
cannot confirm or refute the 93.5%-vs-50% comparison. That comparison remains Sardeli's unresolved
internal contrast with an **un-held source** -> named gap:.

## Decision relevance

[inferred from @poloferrero2025]

- **For an older adult with sarcopenic obesity, resistance training is worth doing — mainly for fat and
  function, modestly, on moderate-to-low evidence.** Expect a small composite body-composition gain
  (SMD 0.35) driven by lower body fat (BF% SMD 0.52) and a small function gain (SMD 0.36), over \~12 weeks
  at 3×/week. Do **not** expect it to normalize lipids or inflammation (null biomarker panel), and do not
  promise muscle growth — the muscle-mass signal was not significant here.
- **RT over aerobic in this stratum — author-asserted, not head-to-head here.** The authors conclude RT
  «has been shown to be superior to aerobic exercise in terms of functionality and body composition in
  adults with SO», citing other work; this MA did not itself compare modalities (RT vs no-treatment only).
  Hold it as a directional lead, not a within-MA finding -> [[Exercise Modality for Body Composition in Obesity]].
- **Safety is in RT's favour.** RT was «generally safe», with only minor exercise-typical effects (muscle
  soreness) in two trials — relevant given the deconditioned, higher-injury-risk SO population.
- **Pairs the diet lever, does not replace it.** The authors note a «low-calorie, high-protein diet has
  been shown to reduce BF% in older adults with SO, while supplementation has not». RT here is the
  no-deficit lever; the diet-plus-RT combination (and RT-during-deficit for muscle retention) is the
  Sardeli / [[Protein Intake During Energy Restriction]] question.

## Limits

[inferred from @poloferrero2025]

- **Surrogates only; open loop at the patient level** (above).
- **Short and small.** 9 pooled RCTs, 12-week horizon, n in the low hundreds per outcome (n = 29 for the
  only significant performance subgroup). Durability and long-term function/mortality are unstudied here.
- **Mostly women** — transportability to older men with SO is a stated gap.
- **SO definition is not yet unified** — Polo-Ferrero applies one operational criterion («recent expert
  recommendations»); «clinical data on SO are still insufficient to support a unified definition» — the
  same `type-G` gap flagged on [[Sarcopenia Definition and Diagnosis]].
- **Publication-bias signal** on body composition and biomarkers (funnel asymmetry).
