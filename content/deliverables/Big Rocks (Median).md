# Big Rocks (Median)

**A general stratum — not a person, and not everyone.** This applies the general fabric to one reference
class; the leaner and the more-impaired tails need the conditionality flags below, not this exact list.

## Who this is for

A Western adult in their **late 40s** around the metabolic-drift median (roughly the 3rd quintile), with
**obesity-associated metabolic impairment**: central adiposity, borderline hypertension, borderline
dysglycemia (prediabetes-leaning, not diabetic), elevated triglycerides, low-grade systemic inflammation,
a modestly raised resting heart rate, leaning toward metabolic syndrome and fatty liver (MASLD).
**Drifting, not diseased.**

## Read this first — two framings that reorder everything

- **Movement and energy are the headline; the carbs-vs-fat-vs-protein wars are the small print.** The
  biggest *quantified* levers are activity and weight; nutrient composition is second-order — an
  isoenergetic sugar swap is null on weight, low- vs balanced-carb differs by ~1 kg (judged not
  clinically important), and cutting saturated fat buys a small effect on events and none on death.
- **Sorted by robustness, because guidance for the drifting median must not harm the leaner ~40%.** Each
  lever below is graded: **(a)** helps this stratum and harms no one; **(b)** helps the impaired but is
  needless or counterproductive for the metabolically healthy; **(c)** small and/or contested.

## (a) Robust big rocks — pull these; they help this stratum and harm no one

1. **Move (aerobic).** The largest well-measured lever: total activity **HR 0.34 (0.27-0.43), high
   certainty**, with most of the mortality benefit reached by **~24 min/day of moderate-to-vigorous
   activity**, then the curve flattens — you do not need to be an athlete
   ([[Physical Activity Dose and Mortality]]). Sit less as a *separate* lever (risk rises above ~6-8 h/day
   total sitting, ~3-4 h/day TV).
2. **Add strength training — mainly for function and to protect muscle while you lose fat.** Its
   claim on this tier is **RCT-grade but on a *surrogate*:** it preserves lean mass during the
   weight-loss deficit ([[Protein and Resistance Training for Muscle and Strength]]) — you want to lose
   visceral fat, not muscle — and builds strength and function; and it harms no one. Its *independent*
   all-cause-mortality signal (~RR 0.85; with aerobic, a larger ~RR 0.60) is real but **very-low
   certainty** (observational, self-reported) — a weakly-warranted bonus, **not** what earns it a place
   here ([[Muscle-Strengthening Activity and Mortality]]). *(So it sits in this tier on different grounds
   than aerobic above: aerobic on high-certainty mortality, strength on RCT-certain function/lean-mass +
   safety — by effect x certainty its mortality claim alone would not qualify.)* A modest weekly volume
   (~2 sessions) captures it; do not chase high volume.
3. **Lose the visceral fat (eat less).** The dominant *metabolic* lever — it acts on the blood pressure,
   the glucose, the fatty liver and the drift at once. **Honesty, refined by the meta-analysis:** dietary
   weight loss did **not** reduce cardiovascular *events* (Look AHEAD null, and a 54-RCT meta-analysis
   confirms CV events null), but it **does** lower **all-cause mortality** (RR 0.82, ~6 fewer per 1000) by
   a *non-cardiovascular* route ([[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]]) — so pursue it for the
   liver, glucose, blood pressure, function *and* a real (non-CV) mortality benefit, just not a promised
   heart-attack reduction. Targets that exist: **>=5%** loss clears liver fat, **7-10%** improves liver inflammation
   ([[Fatty Liver MASLD and Weight Loss]]); the method matters less than a sustained deficit — the one
   diet with a positive recommendation is defined by *energy* (an 800-1200 kcal total diet replacement,
   max 12 weeks, [[Diets for Weight Loss - What NICE Recommends]]).
4. **Eat enough protein (~1.6 g/kg/day).** Preserves muscle during the deficit and augments strength
   training; benefit *plateaus* near 1.6 g/kg — more is not better ([[Protein and Resistance Training for Muscle and Strength]]).
5. **Sleep ~7-8 h.** Avoid chronic short sleep; *more* is not a lever, and the long-sleep risk arm is a
   marker of ill-health, not a target ([[Sleep Duration and Mortality]]).

## (b) Stratum-dependent — conditional on metabolic status, NOT universal

- **Carbohydrate restriction — for the dysglycemic arm only.** In the insulin-resistant / prediabetic it
  lowers **HbA1c ~0.47%** and drives short-term type-2-diabetes remission (**NNT ~3 at 6 months**) — but
  the effect **attenuates by 12 months** and it **raises LDL (~+0.14 mmol/L)**
  ([[Carbohydrate Restriction and Type 2 Diabetes Remission]]). For the metabolically *healthier* member
  of this stratum it is unnecessary and the LDL rise is a net negative — this is the clearest
  *helps-the-60%-could-harm-the-40%* lever. Between low- and balanced-carb for weight the difference is
  ~1 kg, not clinically important ([[Low-Carbohydrate vs Balanced-Carbohydrate Diets]]).

## (c) Small / over-published / contested — do not lead with these

- **Saturated fat -> unsaturated:** a small effect on cardiovascular *events* (RR 0.83; ~15 fewer per
  1000 at a high baseline, less at this stratum's) and **none on mortality** ([[Saturated Fat Intake and Replacement]]). The channel is real, though — cutting SFA lowers LDL/apoB, which *causes* ASCVD
  cumulatively ([[LDL ApoB and Cumulative Exposure]]) — so a *sustained* small reduction is worth more
  than the short-trial RR; for this metabolically-impaired stratum track **non-HDL/apoB**, not LDL-C.
- **Free sugars:** the lever is *energy*, not sugar itself — an isoenergetic swap is null on weight
  ([[Free Sugars Intake]]).
- **Alcohol:** moderate drinking is **not** protective — the apparent J-curve is an artifact — so there
  is nothing to seek upward, and for this fatty-liver-leaning stratum keep intake low, as alcohol
  directly loads the liver ([[Alcohol and Mortality and Vascular Disease]]).
- **Sodium:** a small blood-pressure lever, worth relatively **more here** (hypertensive-leaning: ~4-6
  mmHg vs ~1.4 in normotensives) ([[Sodium Intake and Blood Pressure]]). Note that *pharmacological* BP
  lowering has proven hard-outcome benefit even in primary prevention ([[Blood Pressure Lowering and Cardiovascular Events]]),
  but at borderline BP the lifestyle route (weight, sodium, activity) is the move, not a drug.
- **Red / processed meat:** genuinely *contested* — one guidance family says reduce it, another says
  current intake is defensible, on the same evidence; the clash is the standard of proof and whose
  values set the threshold, not whether meat causes cancer. Presented as the open question, not a verdict
  ([[Should Adults Reduce Red and Processed Meat]]).

## The frame — why the diet composition is second-order for this person

This stratum is higher-risk than a lean adult but still **not high-risk**, so most levers act on
metabolism, liver and function; the hard cardiovascular-event payoff of any single cardiometabolic
intervention is modest at this risk level, with blood-pressure lowering the partial exception
([[Cardiometabolic Interventions and Hard CV Outcomes in Low-Risk People]]). Absolute benefit scales with
baseline risk, so a big *relative* effect on a modest risk is a modest *absolute* one
([[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]).

## Gaps (stated honestly; routed for later acquisition / adjudication)

- **Pulses / plant-protein quality: the wiki holds almost nothing** — the top acquirable gap for a
  nutrition guide (routed to `ingest-queue.md`).
- **The *harms-the-40%* sort is mostly reasoned, not measured** — the conditionality above rests on mechanism +
  held stratum-dependence (carb->LDL, remission-only-in-dysglycemia), not on quantified subgroup harm.
- **No population statistics** — the stratum is a stated hypothetical, by design.
- **No evidenced resting-heart-rate or anthropometric outcome targets** — treat waist and resting HR as
  progress markers that move the right way with the levers above, not as cutoffs.

## The honest limits

- **Open loop:** this grades what the evidence holds and how faithfully, never whether acting on it
  improves a real outcome.
- **Appraise, do not prescribe:** carbohydrate restriction in particular needs medication management this
  cannot do.
- **A stratum, not a person and not everyone:** outcome priorities and constraints are the individual's,
  elicited per case — the tails need the conditionality flags, not this exact ordering.
