Ask whether a GLP-1 drug's benefits outweigh its risks and the honest answer splits in two, because the benefit and the harm do not move together across the people taking it. For someone with class II or III obesity who already has established cardiovascular disease, the SELECT trial shows semaglutide delivers a proven reduction in major cardiovascular events — a heart attack, stroke, or cardiovascular death averted, an outcome the person feels, not a marker — on top of standard secondary-prevention care, alongside large weight loss, diabetes prevention, and fatty-liver benefit. For that person the proven benefit outweighs the known risk on today's evidence; the gastrointestinal tax and a lifelong dependence on the drug are the real costs ([[Semaglutide for Cardiovascular Risk in Obesity]]).

For the near-normal-weight person taking the same drug to shed cosmetic weight — the tail that drives the scale of adoption — what is proven shrinks to surrogates: large weight loss, better metabolic markers, and, if they are prediabetic, improved glucose. The hard cardiovascular-event benefit there is unproven and, even if real, small by construction; the risk side is a graded near-term ledger plus a lifetime, off-label exposure no trial has yet seen. That stratum's answer is dominated by a gap, not a number — and naming the gap is the finding, not a hedge ([[Cardiometabolic Interventions and Hard CV Outcomes in Low-Risk People]]).

Two disciplines run underneath both answers. Weight, HbA1c, and eGFR are surrogates: they carry decision weight only where their transmission to an outcome a person actually feels is itself evidenced ([[Surrogate Outcomes]]). And the honest comparator is a lifetime strategy including its exit, because the effect reverses on stopping. Hold both reflexes to one bar — the weight-loss number does not license *the benefits obviously outweigh* for the lean user, and the sheer scale of use does not license *we know it is harmful*, because most of the feared long-horizon signals are unproven, not established.

## The two strata, and why they diverge

Why one drug yields two verdicts is arithmetic, not opinion, and it begins with baseline risk. The absolute benefit any therapy delivers is its relative risk reduction multiplied by the person's own baseline risk of the event ([[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]). The relative effect is a property of the drug; the absolute benefit is a property of the drug applied to a particular person — so the same one-fifth reduction removes a fifth of a large risk and a fifth of a negligible one. GRADE draws the consequence outright: «Recommendations, however, may differ across subgroups of patients at different baseline risk of an outcome, despite there being a single relative risk that applies to all of them.» [@grade]

The two strata sit far apart on that baseline. SELECT — the one trial that proved a hard-outcome benefit — enrolled a high-risk group: «patients 45 years of age or older who had preexisting cardiovascular disease and a body-mass index...of 27 or greater but no history of diabetes» [@lincoff2023]. Its placebo group ran an 8.0% event rate over a mean 3.3 years [@lincoff2023] — roughly 2.4% a year [inferred from @lincoff2023]. This is stratum (i): obesity with established atherosclerotic disease, and a substantial yearly risk of a cardiovascular event.

Stratum (ii) is the person most GLP-1 prescriptions now reach: near-normal weight, no established disease, taking the drug mainly to lose weight. Their annual cardiovascular risk sits on the order of 0.5% — roughly five-fold below SELECT's enrolled population — and no trial has ever measured a hard cardiovascular outcome in anyone like them on this drug [inferred from @lincoff2023].

Same drug, two populations whose baseline risk differs about five-fold — enough, on its own, to make one relative effect worth acting on for one person and barely measurable for the other. With the strata fixed, take the drug's benefit side at face value first: what is actually proven, and on which endpoint.

## The benefit side: what is proven, by outcome

With the two strata defined, take the benefit side at face value first — what these drugs actually
move, on which endpoint, and how firmly. The pattern to watch for as the outcomes accumulate: every
proven *hard*-outcome benefit was won in a high-risk, secondary-prevention population, while everything
proven in the lean, primary-prevention population is a **surrogate** ([[Surrogate Outcomes]]).

### Weight and appetite

Semaglutide takes off weight reliably and dose-dependently, and by more than any prior non-surgical
agent — but weight is a **surrogate**, not an outcome anyone feels directly. STEP-1 ran semaglutide 2.4 mg
in obese adults without established disease: «The mean change in body weight from baseline to week 68 was
−14.9% in the semaglutide group as compared with −2.4% with placebo, for an estimated treatment difference
of −12.4 percentage points» [@wilding2021]. Half
the treated group lost at least 15% (50.5% vs 4.9%) and a third lost at least 20% (32.0% vs 1.7%) — the
latter approaching post-bariatric-surgery loss.

Tirzepatide — a **dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist**, a related-but-different exposure that must not be
pooled under "GLP-1" — takes off more. SURMOUNT-1: «−15.0%», «−19.5%» and «−20.9%» at 5/10/15 mg vs
«−3.1%» placebo, with «57%... a reduction in body weight of 20% or more, as compared with 3%» at 15 mg
[@jastreboff2022].

The larger number does **not** carry a larger cardiovascular benefit with it. The weight-to-event
transmission step was never trialled for tirzepatide — its dedicated CV-outcome trial — so more weight buys *less* outcome evidence,
not more. A record-breaking surrogate is precisely where not to assume the hard outcome follows
([[Surrogate Outcomes]]).

### Cardiovascular events

In one high-risk stratum semaglutide **cut hard cardiovascular events** — the first obesity drug shown to
do so — but only in secondary prevention, and only the primary composite is a confirmed result. SELECT
(n=17,604, obese with established CVD, non-diabetic): «A primary cardiovascular end-point event (death from
cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke) occurred in 569 of the 8803
patients (6.5%) in the semaglutide group and 701 of the 8801 patients (8.0%) in the placebo group (hazard
ratio, 0.80; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.72 to 0.90; P<0.001)»
[@lincoff2023].

That is an absolute reduction of \~1.5 percentage points over \~3.3 years — **NNT \~67**
[inferred from @lincoff2023]
 — and it is *incremental*, on top of intensive guideline therapy (90.1%
on lipid-lowering drugs, 86.2% on antiplatelets) [@lincoff2023].

Read the rest of SELECT carefully — only that composite is confirmatory. The pre-specified gatekeeping
hierarchy tested cardiovascular death next: «hazard ratio, 0.85; 95% CI, 0.71 to 1.01; P = 0.07»
[@lincoff2023], which **failed the
gate**, so «superiority testing was not performed for the remaining confirmatory secondary end points»
[@lincoff2023]. Two misreadings must
both be foreclosed:

- **Not "no cardiovascular effect."** The composite reduction is real and confirmatory; the point estimates
  below it are favourable. All-cause mortality 0.81 (0.71-0.93) and the heart-failure composite 0.82
  (0.71-0.96) point the right way, and non-fatal MI 0.72 (0.61-0.85) carries most of the composite.
- **Not "semaglutide proven to cut mortality."** CV death crossed the gate at P=0.07, so everything downstream
  is a point estimate, not a confirmed effect; non-fatal stroke 0.93 (0.74-1.15) was not reduced.

Demanding significance for the read you dislike while granting it to the read you like is the same error
run in two directions — both are ruled out here.

### Kidney and survival

In a *different* high-risk stratum — type 2 diabetes with chronic kidney disease — semaglutide **cut hard
kidney events and all-cause death**, through a mechanism that does not run through weight. FLOW gave the
1.0 mg glycaemic dose (not the 2.4 mg obesity dose) on top of maximal RAS inhibition: primary composite
«...331 first events [5.8 per 100 patient-years of follow-up] vs. 410 first events [7.5 per 100 pa- tient-
years]), which resulted in a 24% lower rela- tive risk of the primary outcome in the semaglu- tide group
(hazard ratio, 0.76; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.66 to 0.88; P = 0.0003)»
[@perkovic2024]. «The number of persons who
would need to be treated over 3 years to prevent one primary-outcome event was 20 (95% CI, 14 to 40)»
[@perkovic2024].

The survival signal is the unambiguously patient-important part: «The risk of death from any cause was 20%
lower in the semaglutide group than in the pla- cebo group (227 vs. 279 events; hazard ratio, 0.80; 95%
CI, 0.67 to 0.95, P = 0.01)» [@perkovic2024] —
NNT \~34 over the trial [inferred from @perkovic2024].
CV death fell 0.71 (0.56-0.89) and MACE 0.82
(0.68-0.98).

Two disciplines bound the headline. First, the primary endpoint is a **composite** that mixes hard kidney
failure with a surrogate eGFR-decline threshold and death — and «the trial was not powered to separately
detect effects on kidney failure» [@perkovic2024],
so the honest claim is *reduced a composite of kidney events and reduced death*, not *prevented
dialysis* as a standalone ([[Surrogate Outcomes]]). Second, the benefit was «unrelated to changes in body
weight» [@perkovic2024] — a weight-independent
kidney effect, not merely weight loss lowering serum creatinine ([[Semaglutide and Kidney Outcomes in Chronic Kidney Disease]]).

One caution on how much confidence FLOW adds. It is the **same molecule and the same sponsor** as SELECT and
STEP, so it is *not* an independent line of evidence for "semaglutide works" — three sponsor-run semaglutide
trials are one programme, not three routes. What it adds is genuinely new *content*: a filled gap — a hard
kidney-and-survival outcome in a stratum none of the other trials measured.

### Diabetes prevention

Semaglutide sharply **cut progression to diabetes** and reverted most prediabetics — a large effect, on a
glycaemic surrogate. In SELECT, progression to diabetes fell to a hazard ratio of 0.27 (0.24-0.31)
[@lincoff2023]; in STEP-1's younger
population, 84.1% of prediabetics reverted to normoglycaemia versus 47.8% on placebo
[@wilding2021]. The endpoint is a **glycaemic
threshold, not a patient-important outcome**; whether preventing the label lowers hard diabetes-related
outcomes inherits the same transmission caveat ([[Surrogate Outcomes]]) — though for an obese, impaired-
fasting-glucose person it is a distinct and plausibly relevant benefit.

### Fatty liver (MASLD)

Weight loss improves fatty-liver histology in a **dose-dependent staircase** — but only for the reversible
stages, and the effect fades as weight returns. EASL: «There is a dose-dependent association between the
amount of weight loss and the extent of improvement in biomarkers of liver damage... A stringent
interventional trial with histological end- points suggested a bodyweight reduction of >−5% is required to
reduce liver lipid content, 7-10% to improve inﬂammation, and >−10% to improve ﬁbrosis»
[@easl2024]. Read the 5% / 7-10% / 10% cutpoints as
EASL's studied dose-*range* from a single histology-endpoint RCT — a name-the-curve staircase, not precise
thresholds ([[Fatty Liver MASLD and Weight Loss]]).

Two caveats bound the lever. Lifestyle weight loss has **no proven effect on advanced fibrosis or
cirrhosis** — «evidence for an effect of weight reduction by lifestyle modiﬁcation on advanced ﬁbrosis or
cirrhosis is insufﬁcient» — so it works on the reversible stages only. And the loss does not hold:
12-24-month follow-up shows «a gradual weight regain to a net weight loss of about 5%... and partial regain
of liver lipid» [@easl2024].

## The stratum flip: do not transport the benefit

Every hard-outcome benefit above was won in a high-risk stratum — which is precisely why it does not reach the lean person taking the drug to lose weight. Two independent reasons stop the transport, and either one alone is decisive.

First, the arithmetic. Apply SELECT's own relative reduction — about 20%, HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.72-0.90) [@lincoff2023] — to a baseline roughly five-fold lower and the absolute benefit shrinks about five-fold with it, from the trial's \~1.5-percentage-point reduction over 3.3 years toward a fraction of that [inferred from @lincoff2023]. The relative effect is the generous assumption here; grant it in full and the absolute gain for a low-risk person is still small by construction ([[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]).

Second, the relative effect itself may not transport. SELECT measured its benefit in people whose arteries already carry atherosclerotic plaque; a drug that stabilises existing disease need not do the same work — or any work on events — where that disease is absent. The mechanism differs between secondary and primary prevention, and SELECT cannot speak to it ([[Semaglutide for Cardiovascular Risk in Obesity]]).

This is an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence — and the distinction is load-bearing. The trialists say so themselves: «An important limitation of this trial is that we included only patients with preexisting cardiovascular disease. The effects of semaglutide on primary prevention of cardiovascular events in persons with overweight or obesity but without previous atherosclerotic disease were not studied.» [@lincoff2023] Nothing here suggests the drug stops working in a low-risk person; only that the question has never been a trial's endpoint. Read it as insufficient evidence, not as no effect — collapsing the two, in either direction, is the error ([[Framing a Decision Question]]).

And the ceiling is itself the finding. No intervention the wiki holds — GLP-1 drug, statin, or lifestyle weight loss — has a proven large reduction in hard cardiovascular events for a genuinely low-risk, primary-prevention person ([[Cardiometabolic Interventions and Hard CV Outcomes in Low-Risk People]]). The hard-outcome trials were run where the events are, in high-risk populations, because that is the only place a trial can accrue them; so the missing benefit for the lean user is not peculiar to this drug, it is the structural shape of the whole evidence base.

Reporting that a large, proven cardiovascular lever does not exist for this person is a result — it licenses them to stop expecting one — not a failure to find something ([[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]]). So far, though, this is only the benefit side; the alarm that we may not know enough for a drug used at this scale has to be cashed into specific harms — and much of that ledger has now been filled.

## The risk side: a graded ledger, no longer a blank

The alarm that adoption has outrun the safety data now meets an actual ledger. One gold-tier synthesis grades the non-cardiometabolic harms and benefits of the class: an umbrella review pooling «A total of 60 meta-analyses representing 116 unique adverse health outcomes were included, comprising 1751 randomized clinical trials and 3 580 616 participants.» [@yang2026] It re-pooled each outcome itself rather than vote-counting the source reviews, in populations that were mostly type-2-diabetic and obese ([[GLP-1 Non-Cardiometabolic Effects and Safety]]).

Read each row on **two axes that can point opposite ways**, and a headline quoting one alone misleads. GRADE rates the *quality of evidence* for the effect (high to very low); the umbrella-review *credibility class* rates how robustly the association resists bias — convincing (I), highly suggestive (II), suggestive (III), weak (IV). The worked case sits inside the GI cluster: nausea is «GRADE: high quality of evidence; weak association [class IV]» while vomiting is «GRADE: moderate quality of evidence; highly suggestive association [class II]» [@yang2026]. A well-estimated effect can still be single-trial-fragile ([[Rating Certainty of Evidence]]). Everything below is an umbrella-pooled *association*, not a proven cause.

### Gastrointestinal events — the one robust, established harm

The gastrointestinal signals are the sturdiest thing in the ledger: «The most consistent signals were for gastrointestinal AEs» [@yang2026], and this is where GRADE quality is highest. GLP-1 use is associated with roughly 2.5-fold higher odds of nausea (OR 2.47, 95% CI 1.84-3.34), 2.8-fold vomiting (OR 2.78, 1.91-4.06), and near-doubled diarrhea (OR 1.94, 1.52-2.49) [@yang2026].

These corroborate the tolerability tax already visible in the trials, where GI intolerance drove the excess
treatment discontinuation in STEP-1 — «discontinued treatment owing to gastrointestinal events (59 [4.5%]
vs. 5 [0.8%])» [@wilding2021] — against the
placebo group ([[Semaglutide for Cardiovascular Risk in Obesity]]). This is the harm that is *established*
rather than exploratory — dose- and titration-dependent, mostly transient, and the dominant real-world
adherence cost. It is also the one harm already priced into the drug decision.

### The net serious-event balance is itself stratified

The single most decision-relevant harm number is not any one organ signal but the *net* serious-adverse-event
tally — and it flips sign across the two strata, for the same baseline-risk reason the benefit does. In
high-risk SELECT, serious adverse events were *lower* on the drug: «(33.4%) in the semaglutide group and
3204 patients (36.4%) in the placebo group (P<0.001)»
[@lincoff2023]
— the events the drug prevents (cardiovascular, renal) outnumber the GI and biliary ones it causes.

In healthier STEP-1, the balance reverses: «Serious adverse events were reported in
9.8% and 6.4% of semaglutide and placebo participants, respectively»
[@wilding2021]
— the excess «due primarily to a difference between the groups in the incidence of serious gastrointestinal
disorders (1.4% of participants in the semaglutide group and 0% in the placebo group) and hepatobiliary
disorders» [@wilding2021]. Same drug: in a
population with events to prevent it nets *fewer* serious events; in one without, its own GI/biliary harms
have nothing to offset them and it nets *more*. The safety verdict, like the benefit, is a property of the
stratum, not the molecule.

### Cancers — no robust human signal, which is not a long-latency all-clear

The two harms that most shape public fear — thyroid and pancreatic cancer — do not clear the bar. The umbrella states it plainly: «Cancer outcomes showed no associations meeting the prespecified credibility thresholds despite several nominal signals.» [@yang2026]

- **Thyroid cancer:** an imprecise, non-significant estimate — OR 1.43 (0.95-2.13), P=.08. The rodent C-cell origin is named and its human relevance denied: «the relevance of these findings to humans remains unconfirmed» [@yang2026].
- **Pancreatic cancer:** the nominal signal ran the *other* way, toward lower odds — OR 0.51 (0.30-0.85), P=.01 — and stayed exploratory.
- **Colorectal cancer:** a bare nominal signal — OR 1.24 (1.00-1.54), P=.049, weak (class IV).

So the most-feared harms show no robust human signal in short-to-medium-term trials. That is not proof of safety: absence of a signal over a few years is not a long-latency all-clear, and the cancer question stays open on the horizon-mismatch grounds the known-unknowns section develops below.

### The unsettled middle — gallbladder, pancreatitis, and reflux are distinct states

These three carry real regulatory attention and each *failed* the stringent credibility bar — but "failed the bar" means something different for each, and lumping them is the error to avoid. The umbrella treats the biliary effects as real: «Our study shows an elevated likelihood of pancreatitis and gallbladder or biliary diseases.» [@yang2026]

- **Gallbladder or biliary disease:** a *statistically significant* association — OR 1.34 (1.16-1.55), P<.001. It is "exploratory" only because its prediction interval sits close to the null (a robustness caveat), not because the harm is doubtful.
- **Reflux (GERD):** OR 2.19 (1.65-2.90), P<.001 — same posture as gallbladder: significant, credibility-class-exploratory.
- **Pancreatitis:** here the demotion is about *causality*, not robustness — «the scientific consensus on the causal association between GLP-1 RAs and pancreatitis remains unsettled» [@yang2026]. A 2025 propensity-matched US analysis found no increased risk after comorbidity adjustment; a 2026 UK regulatory update flagged rare fatal cases. Conflicting cohorts, not a prediction-interval technicality.

The decision posture is targeted monitoring calibrated to which state applies — a significant biliary association, versus an unresolved pancreatitis question — not one blanket attribution ([[GLP-1 Non-Cardiometabolic Effects and Safety]]).

### Protective signals — suggestive, and single-trial-fragile

Several *off-target benefits* appear, all directionally favourable and mechanistically arguable, all below the stringent bar. These are **associations, not proven reductions** — GLP-1 use *tracks* fewer of these events; no trial was built to prove it *prevents* them.

- **Serious infections:** OR 0.89 (0.87-0.92) — the only convincing (class I) association among the review's highlighted outcomes.
- **All-cause dementia:** OR 0.55 (0.35-0.87), P=.01 — a large protective *association*, certainty limited.
- **Fracture:** OR 0.67 (0.52-0.87), P=.003. This one matters for the lean-mass question: rapid weight loss raises the sarcopenia and fall worry, and a *lower* fracture association partly offsets it — the two musculoskeletal facts must be read together, neither reassuring the other away ([[GLP-1 and Lean Mass]]).
- **MASH resolution:** OR 3.39 (2.63-4.36), P<.001 — more than threefold higher odds, from a single review, exploratory.

None licenses a recommendation; the umbrella calls them «hypothesis-generating» [@yang2026]. They belong in the ledger as directionally protective, not as unmeasured blanks.

### Why most of it stays exploratory

The reason nearly everything outside the GI cluster stays "suggestive" is mechanical, and the umbrella measured it. In leave-one-trial-out reanalysis, «the direction of treatment effect flipped in approximately 9 of 39 outcomes, and statistical significance was lost in 6 of 39 outcomes after the removal of a single trial» [@yang2026]. An association a single trial can flip is not one to dose a recommendation on.

Add passive adverse-event capture, heterogeneous definitions, and the source reviews' AMSTAR-2 limitations, and the umbrella's own conclusion follows: the class «should not be misconstrued as a panacea» [@yang2026]. Grade before you fear — a regulatory warning and a rodent finding are not the same evidence state as a class-I umbrella association. The risk column is now a graded ledger rather than a blank, but its verdict is *sized signals*, not settled ones ([[GLP-1 Non-Cardiometabolic Effects and Safety]]).

## Lean mass, function, and sarcopenia

The umbrella ledger has no muscle arm, and muscle is the cost patients and clinicians fear most from
rapid pharmacological weight loss. The dedicated evidence has now landed: a meta-analysis of 7 RCTs at
obesity doses (821 patients), graded high-certainty [@laverde2026].
Its finding has two faces, and reading only one of them is the error -> [[GLP-1 and Lean Mass]].

### Two faces, both true, both high-certainty

As a *proportion* of the weight lost, lean mass improves — fat goes faster than muscle, so body
composition gets better as a ratio: «GLP1-RA signiﬁcantly improved lean mass as a proportion of total
weight, with an observed increase of 1.81% (95% CI: 1.1– 2.52; p < 0.00001; I² = 7%). However, a
signiﬁcant decrease was observed in the absolute change in lean mass (−1.74 kg; 95% CI: −3.04 to −0.45;
p < 0.00001; I² = 98%)» [@laverde2026]. Both are
real at once. Semaglutide is the extreme: it drove the largest absolute loss, −5.44 kg (−7.07 to −3.81),
about −9.9% of lean mass [@laverde2026].

Which face governs is a stratum question, not a contradiction. For a young, muscle-rich adult with
obesity the ratio is what matters — composition improves and the absolute loss is minor and recoverable.
For anyone whose muscle is already the binding constraint — older, sarcopenia-risk, multimorbid — the
absolute kilograms are the front-line harm, because falls, fractures and lost independence run off muscle
mass, not off a ratio -> [[Sarcopenia Definition and Diagnosis]].

### Is the drug the culprit, or is this what fast weight loss does?

Largely the latter, on average. Across the class the lean-mass share of total loss is «a proportion
comparable to that observed after bariatric surgery» — i.e. generic to losing weight quickly rather than
drug-special [@laverde2026]. By agent, «with
liraglutide, lean mass accounted for 14–22% of total weight loss; with semaglutide, this proportion
reached up to 45%; and with tirzepatide, approximately 26%»
[@laverde2026]. So the muscle loss tracks the
route (rapid loss), not the molecule — with **semaglutide the drug-specific exception** whose share up to
45% runs above the others and earns the extra worry. The class picture is not drug-special; one agent is.

### Function was not measured — insufficient evidence, not reassurance

Every number above is lean *mass*, and mass is a surrogate for what a person actually cares about —
strength, and the ability to stay on their feet -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]. Laverde measured none of it:
«the included studies did not evaluate functional outcomes, such as muscle strength or physical capacity.
As a result, the clinical interpretation of the observed changes is limited»
[@laverde2026]. This cuts both ways: a reassuring
ratio does not prove function is spared, and a scary kilogram figure does not prove it is lost. The
outcome is held as **insufficient evidence on function**, neither safe nor harmful.

It matters that function is the missing measurement, because function fails *first*. Sarcopenia's modern
case definition puts strength ahead of mass precisely because «strength is better than mass in predicting
adverse out- comes» [@cruzjentoft2019], and
after 50 strength declines faster than mass (1.5-5% vs 1-2% per year)
[@cruzjentoft2019]. A mass number
under-reads the functional loss — so where muscle is the concern, monitor strength and performance
directly, not the scale -> [[Muscle-Strengthening Activity and Mortality]].

### The stratum inversion, and a mechanism that fights the fix

Laverde's population is the wrong one for the worry: mostly «relatively young populations with a low
burden of comorbidities», with «the effects in patients with diabetes, as well as in older and
multimorbid populations» left to future study — the exact people «at high risk for muscle deterioration»
[@laverde2026]. In that stratum the drug's own
mechanism works against muscle defence: a GLP-1 receptor agonist suppresses appetite and total intake, yet
defending older muscle needs a *higher* per-meal protein dose to clear the raised anabolic-resistance
threshold -> [[Anabolic Resistance]]. The drug cuts intake just where the muscle-sparing protein target
has gone up, not down — hardest to hit exactly where it matters most.

### The defence is held, and it is decisive — modify, don't discard

GLP-1 muscle loss is not a novel hazard needing a novel remedy; it is ordinary weight-loss muscle loss,
and the levers that defend muscle against any deficit defend it here. Laverde reaches the muscle cluster's
own answer from the drug side: lean-mass loss «should not be considered a limitation for the use of these
drugs in patients with obesity. However, it is essential to accompany drug treatment with nutritional and
physical exercise interventions» [@laverde2026].

- **Resistance training** is the primary muscle-sparing lever under any energy deficit — the driver, with
  protein a modest adjunct -> [[Protein and Resistance Training for Muscle and Strength]].
- **Adequate protein**, roughly 1.6 g/kg/day of total body weight, is where the muscle-building benefit
  plateaus in healthy resistance-trained adults (break-point 95% CI \~1.0-2.2, measured in a non-obese,
  non-energy-restricted sample) [@morton2018].
  An energy deficit points the requirement *higher*, not lower; a deficit-specific figure of \~1.9 g/kg is
  a direction, not a transportable target for the obese, untrained GLP-1 user (off-support on both
  counts) -> [[GLP-1 and Lean Mass]].

The complete strategy — drug plus resistance training plus adequate protein — is what gets judged, never
the drug's naive body-composition number in isolation. Muscle can be defended while the drug is taken.
The deeper problem is what happens when it is stopped.

## Discontinuation and the lifetime net

The effect reverses on stopping, and the STEP-1 off-treatment extension quantifies it. When both
semaglutide and the lifestyle programme were withdrawn at week 68, «Participants regained a mean of 11.6
percentage points (SD: 7.7) of body weight in the semaglutide arm versus 1.9 per- centage points (SD: 4.8)
in the placebo arm» over the following year — «participants regained two-thirds of their prior weight
loss, with similar changes in cardiometabolic variables»
[@wilding2022]. The cardiometabolic gains went with
it: «Cardiometabolic improvements seen from week 0 to week 68 with semaglutide reverted towards baseline
at week 120 for most variables» [@wilding2022], and
the trialists conclude «ongoing treatment is required to maintain improve- ments in weight and health»
[@wilding2022].

One caveat holds the rebound below a clean drug-cause reading: the extension also withdrew the lifestyle
support, so «the absence of structured lifestyle intervention following semaglutide withdrawal ... may
also have contributed to the trajectory of weight regain»
[@wilding2022]. Part of the regain is the loss of
*both* props. The underlying physiology is not a failure of resolve — removing the exogenous satiety
signal lets the defended post-loss appetite-hormone deficit reassert -> [[Weight-Loss Maintenance and Metabolic Adaptation]].

This makes the drug **low on structural leverage**: it substitutes for the behaviour rather than reshaping
the physiology or environment so future choices are easier. That is not a verdict against it. Adherence is
part of the effect, and a maintained substitute can beat an unachieved change — a maintenance phase, not
abandonment, is what the regain argues for -> [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]]. What it
does change is the comparator: the honest unit is a *lifetime* strategy, drug plus cost plus GI tax plus
its eventual exit, not a course that banks a permanent gain. And if the comparator is a lifetime, it must
be judged against the realistic lifetime alternatives, not against nothing.

## Net effect against the realistic alternatives

The alternative to the drug is not zero risk. Obesity itself carries large, sustained risk, so *no drug*
is not *no harm* — the comparison is between complete strategies, each with its own costs.

- **Sustained lifestyle weight loss** — large if achieved, but with low adherence and, crucially, an
  unproven hard-cardiovascular benefit *via this route*. The largest, longest trial was null on events
  (Look AHEAD, «hazard ratio 0.95; 95% CI 0.83 to 1.09»)
  [@look2013], and
  a 54-RCT meta-analysis of weight-loss interventions [@ma2017]
  generalises it: weight-loss diets were null on cardiovascular events («risk ratio 0.93,
  95% confidence interval 0.83 to 1.04») while cutting all-cause mortality («risk ratio 0.82, 95%
  confidence interval 0.71 to 0.95») — a benefit that does not run through the heart
  [@ma2017]. Note the two are not independent:
  Ma's pool *includes* Look AHEAD, so the trial and the meta-analysis are consistent-with, not two
  separate confirmations -> [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]].
- **Bariatric surgery** — a larger and more durable loss with a proven all-cause mortality benefit, but
  its own surgical and nutritional costs; a comparator here, not appraised in its own right.
- **Tirzepatide** — the strongest weight lever short of surgery (a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist, a
  related-but-different exposure), but with **no cardiovascular-outcome trial**: more weight, less outcome
  evidence, and the surrogate-to-outcome step untested
  -> [[Semaglutide for Cardiovascular Risk in Obesity]].

The preferred move follows from all of the above: **modify, don't discard.** Drug and lifestyle are the
same weight lever by different routes, combinable rather than either/or. The complete strategy pairs the
drug with resistance training, adequate protein, and a planned exit built in from the start — judged on
net effect across the outcomes that matter. Everything to this point is what the trials could see. The
alarm about the scale of adoption is really about what they structurally could *not*.

## The known-unknowns, sized honestly

You cannot quantify an unknown unknown. What you *can* size is the mismatch between what the evidence
covers and how the drug is used, and that mismatch is itself the estimate of the exposure being run. The
**evidence horizon** is short and narrow — roughly 1-4 years, in defined trial populations (obesity with
established cardiovascular disease; type 2 diabetes with kidney disease; non-diabetic adults with
obesity). The **use horizon** is a lifetime, increasingly off-label, reaching people no trial enrolled:
near-normal-BMI cosmetic users, adolescents, the peri-partum, the psychiatric. The gap between the two is
where the honest uncertainty lives.

The horizon is also *moving*: dedicated hard-outcome trials in heart failure with preserved ejection
fraction and in obstructive sleep apnoea have been reported but are not yet held in this wiki, so the
benefit column may firm up on further indications as they are ingested
. That is a coverage gap on the benefit side, distinct from the long-latency
harm gap below; both are *insufficient evidence*, not settled answers.

Inside that gap sit the long-latency and rare outcomes: cancer, bone health, pregnancy exposure,
adolescent development, non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy, psychiatric effects, and the
reward/anhedonia signal some users describe as a loss of joy. On each, the correct state is **insufficient
evidence, not no effect** — the expectancy test asks whether, if a long-latency harm existed, we could
plausibly have seen it in 1-4 years of trials, and for these we could not -> [[Framing a Decision Question]].
Silence from an unstudied question is not a null result -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]. This is a limit no
search can close today: it lifts only as time passes and the horizons converge. One force, though, is
pushing this whole debate that the health evidence cannot speak to at all.

## One axis only: health

Everything above weighs one thing: what the drug does to a health outcome. But the force driving
adoption hardest is not on that axis at all. Cost runs to hundreds of dollars a month, insurance
coverage is patchy and shifting, supply has been intermittently short, and access divides sharply by
income and geography. Those are real, and for most people they decide the question more than any hazard
ratio does — but they are a **different axis**, and this appraisal holds no cost, coverage, or equity
data to weigh them against health.

So the discipline is to name that the trade-off exists and stop there. Pricing access into the health
verdict — letting "it's expensive and rationed" quietly move the benefit-risk call — is the same defect
as a guideline that folds an undisclosed objective into an evidence rating: a non-health consideration
absorbed without being shown [[Which Objective Moved This Recommendation]]. The health verdict below is
health only. What a given person can afford, and whether the supply and coverage will hold for the years
this drug must be taken, is theirs to weigh alongside it — named here, not netted in.

## Bottom line, by stratum

One drug name, two decisions — because the proven benefit and the graded risk do not move together as
baseline risk falls. Layer-1 ranking applied to a single molecule: rank by effect size times certainty,
and the ranking inverts across the two strata below [[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]].

### Class II/III obesity with established cardiovascular disease, non-diabetic

**Benefit outweighs known risk on current evidence.** This is the one stratum where the drug has a
**proven reduction in a hard outcome**: in secondary prevention it cut major cardiovascular events by
about a fifth, hazard ratio 0.80 [@lincoff2023]. In absolute terms that is roughly one avoided event per 67 people treated over
3.3 years, on top of guideline statin and antiplatelet therapy. The benefit sits on a **net-favourable serious-adverse-event
profile** and comes with large weight loss, strong diabetes prevention, and MASLD benefit on top
[[Semaglutide for Cardiovascular Risk in Obesity]].

For this person the drug is a **strong incremental option**, not a first move that displaces the big
rocks but a real lever once they are addressed. The costs are concrete and worth stating plainly: the
gastrointestinal tax of nausea and vomiting, and lifetime dependence — the effect reverses on stopping,
so this is a commitment measured in years, not a course.

### Near-normal-BMI, taken for cosmetic weight loss

**Neither "the benefits clearly outweigh" nor "this is dangerous."** What is proven here shrinks to
**surrogates** — large weight loss, better metabolic markers, and, if the person is prediabetic,
diabetes prevention [[Semaglutide for Cardiovascular Risk in Obesity]]. The **hard-outcome
cardiovascular benefit is unproven** in a low-risk person and small by construction even if real: no
held cardiometabolic intervention has a proven large hard-CV benefit at low baseline risk
[[Cardiometabolic Interventions and Hard CV Outcomes in Low-Risk People]].

The risk side is a graded near-term ledger **plus a lifetime, off-label exposure that is not yet
knowable** — sized honestly by the mismatch between a 1-to-4-year evidence horizon and a lifetime of
use. This stratum's answer is dominated by that gap, not by a number, and the gap is the honest finding,
not a reassurance.

So the drug earns its place here, if at all, on the **weight and metabolic levers**, weighed by that
person against costs the evidence cannot yet quantify — and only with **resistance training, adequate
protein, and a discontinuation plan built in from the start**, since muscle must be defended while the
drug is taken and two-thirds of the weight returns within a year of stopping it.

Both reflexes fail the **same bar**. The weight-loss magnitude does not license "benefits obviously
outweigh" — weight is a surrogate, and the proven hard-outcome benefit is confined to the high-risk
stratum. And the scale of adoption does not license "we know it's harmful" — most feared long-horizon
signals are insufficient-evidence, not established harm.

## Mandatory caveats

- **Appraisal, not prescription.** This document appraises what the drug does, for whom, and how
  certainly. It does not select, dose, titrate, or screen anyone — those are prescriber acts requiring a
  person's labs, medications, and history, and they are out of scope.
- **Stratum-level appraisal, applied per person.** The two verdicts above are reference-class estimates;
  which one a given person is in, and how they weight the outcomes, is settled with them, not here.

- **Health axis only.** Cost, access, supply, and equity are named above and deliberately left unpriced;
  the verdict weighs health outcomes and nothing else.
- **Open loop.** This appraisal grades coherence and fidelity to its sources — never validity. No step
  here checks a claim against a realized outcome; a clean audit is not a validated recommendation.

- **The risk ledger is graded but not complete.** The tirzepatide cardiovascular-outcome trial is still
  awaited, and the long-horizon, lifetime, and
  off-label exposures remain unknowable until time passes.
