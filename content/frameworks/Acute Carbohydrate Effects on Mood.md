**The *sugar rush* is a myth.** A gold SR+MA of acute carbohydrate (CHO) challenge trials in healthy
adults finds **no** mood-boosting effect at any time-point — and the only signals that reach significance
run the *other* way: within the first hour CHO leaves people *less* alert and *more* fatigued than
placebo. This refutes the widely-held belief that sugar or a sugary drink gives a quick lift in mood or
energy. [@mantantzis2019sugar]

This is an **acute** mood/alertness question (minutes-to-hours after ingestion, healthy adults). It is a
different decision from chronic depression (-> [[Depression and Modifiable Exposures]], a scope/horizon
distinction, not a tension) and from how-much-sugar-to-eat (-> [[Free Sugars Intake]], the cost side).

## The finding

The meta-analysis pooled **176 effect sizes from 31 RCTs (1259 participants)**, comparing carbohydrate
against placebo on subjective mood across three time windows (0-30, 31-60, 61+ minutes)
[@mantantzis2019sugar].

- **No mood benefit, anywhere.** No positive effect of CHO on any mood construct (alertness, calmness,
  contentedness, vigor, or the reverse-scored negatives) at any time-point. The pooled effects were
  **homogeneous** across constructs and windows (I2 = 0% in most cells; the exceptions are a few 61+ min
  constructs) — a consistent null, not a washed-out average of clashing studies.
  [@mantantzis2019sugar]
- **The serotonin prediction failed.** The mechanistic case for a mood lift (CHO -> tryptophan ->
  brain serotonin) predicts an effect **beyond the first hour**; the 61+ min window is exactly where no
  facilitation appeared. The outcome-level null is the check the peripheral-biomarker mechanism did not
  pass. [@mantantzis2019sugar]
- **The only significant effects are decrements (a mild HARM direction):**
  - **Fatigue rises, 0-30 min:** CHO -> significantly *higher* fatigue than placebo (9 studies, p=.011).
    The same direction held (non-significantly) at 31-60 min. [@mantantzis2019sugar]
  - **Alertness falls, 31-60 min:** alertness was lower on CHO than placebo in all three windows,
    significant in the second (12 studies, p=.020). [@mantantzis2019sugar]
- **The one whiff of benefit is the least trustworthy number.** Overall-mood at 61+ min showed a marginal
  positive *trend* (p=.051) — but it was flagged for publication bias (Begg/Egger p=.058/.089) and built
  partly from **selectively reported** subscales, so the authors explicitly discount it.
  [@mantantzis2019sugar]

## The narrow carve-out — physical exertion

The single context where CHO reduced fatigue was **during/after physically demanding tasks** (the only
moderator that approached significance for fatigue at 61+ min: b=0.474, 95% CI [0.04, 0.91], p=.037) —
NOT after cognitive tasks or at rest [@mantantzis2019sugar].
This is the sports-nutrition case (fuelling strenuous exercise), not a general everyday mood/energy boost.
CHO dose (>/< 25 g), CHO type, and fasting interval did **not** move mood — so *which sugar, how much,
fasted or not* is not the lever people imagine it is.

## Decision relevance

- **The claimed benefit is a phantom.** Reaching for a sugary snack or drink *for energy* or *to pick
  myself up* does not deliver, in the healthy-adult stratum, on the acute timescale people expect it to —
  and within the first hour it slightly *worsens* alertness and fatigue. Anyone using this heuristic can
  drop it. This is a **confirmed refutation** of a common belief, as reportable as a confirmation.
- **It removes an offset against the free-sugar cost.** The acute *lift* was the main *benefit* people put
  on sugar's side of the ledger; the MA empties it. With no acute upside, the free-sugars cardiometabolic
  and dental costs (-> [[Free Sugars Intake]], whose beverage limb carries the SSB dose-response) stand
  unopposed for this use-case, and a lower-sugar substitute loses nothing on mood/energy.
- **The substitute is water or nothing** for the mood/energy motive; keep sugar for the palatability or
  the exertion-fuelling motive, judged on its own terms.

## Limits + scope

- **Surrogate, acute outcome.** Mood is self-reported on scales (Bond-Lader VAS, POMS) measured within
  hours — a [[Surrogate Outcomes|surrogate]], not a patient-important physical endpoint, and silent on
  chronic mood. Mental wellbeing is peripheral to the physical-health telos; this page earns its place by
  killing a belief that drives *consumption*, not by mood as an end in itself.
- **Healthy adults only.** Clinical/subclinical populations (diagnosed depression, high stress-sensitivity,
  obesity, luteal phase) were out of scope; the authors note their mood «could be more sensitive to CHO
  manipulations» [@mantantzis2019sugar]. So this does NOT speak
  to *carbohydrate-craving* self-medication in depression — that is [[Depression and Modifiable Exposures]]'
  chronic question, a different scope and horizon.
- **Carbohydrate in isolation.** CHO+caffeine energy-drink synergies and CHO+protein combinations were
  largely excluded — the null is about sugar/CHO alone.
- **Single-source page.** One gold MA; the refute-of-benefit direction is well-supported (31 RCTs, low
  heterogeneity, consistent, mechanism-prediction failed), the harm-direction (worse alertness/fatigue) is
  weaker (significant in only some windows). No second SR/MA is held on the acute question.
