## Verdict

**The diseases of ageing mostly do not need separate answers.** One small set of modifiable
exposures -- the cardiometabolic **big rocks** (adiposity, physical inactivity, glycaemia,
blood pressure, LDL, smoking, excess alcohol) -- recurs across dementia, cardiovascular
disease, cancer and Parkinson's in the *same* direction. Pulling those levers once buys risk
reduction on several patient-important outcomes at the same time, so there is no separate
anti-dementia, anti-cancer and anti-heart-disease diet to run in parallel. That shared core
is appraised in [[Big Rocks (Median)]] and [[Big Rocks (Elderly)]] and cross-linked here, not
re-derived; the recurrence itself is the synthesis
-> [[Shared Modifiable Levers Across Age-Related Diseases]].


- **The disease-specific increments beyond the big rocks are the product of this appraisal,
  and they are few and mostly modest.** Two endpoints add **nothing** meaningful -- CVD and
  the function diseases (frailty/sarcopenia/osteoporosis) are informative nulls, because the
  big rocks *are* their risk factors. Cancer and dementia carry the genuine per-site levers;
  Parkinson's is honestly the thinnest.
- **Cancer's increments are real but small:** alcohol has no lower threshold on the *cancer*
  axis (distinct from its mortality dose), red/processed meat is a small, mostly-colorectal
  association graded differently by different bodies, and high-dose beta-carotene supplements
  are a documented *harm* in smokers -- not a lever to pull.
- **Dementia is the richest increment set and the sharpest lesson.** Hearing and vision
  correction, cognitive engagement, head-injury avoidance are all **observational** and
  assumed-causal; the bundled lifestyle *trials* (FINGER/MAPT/preDIVA, pooled by Coley 2025)
  do **not** demonstrate dementia prevention, and found **no responder subgroup** even on a
  data-driven search. The 45.3% attributable fraction is a *population* figure, never a
  per-person prevention promise.
- **The ranking shifts by age stratum, not the menu.** Younger adults act on a lifetime
  horizon; midlife is where the cardiometabolic levers bite hardest (including for dementia);
  the elderly are dominated by the function big rocks. Menopause is a worked mid-life shift
  -- route (a), a shifted baseline risk, not a new effect-modifier.
- **Function is a separate axis from length, and under-measured.** Several levers act on the
  *trajectory* of decline -- falls, independence, strength -- more than on lifespan; trials
  count events, rarely trajectory, so a clean mortality result can be silent on the outcome a
  person most values. Named, not scored into a scalar.
- **The honest gaps are load-bearing:** the aging-biology (geroscience) mechanism behind the
  common-soil pattern is not held; a full Parkinson's risk-factor menu is not held; and the
  aggregate cross-disease effect is **not poolable** -- each lever acts on a different outcome
  scale, so no combined *total risk reduction* number is derivable.

The ceiling is itself the finding. For someone whose big rocks are already handled, "the
disease-specific increments are few, mostly modest, and several are gaps" is a decision -- it
licenses stopping the hunt for a disease-specific miracle -- delivered as a result, not a
failure to find one. And the loop is open: nothing here has been graded against a realized
multi-disease outcome.

The rest answers it disease by disease, because the increments -- where they exist -- do not
transfer across endpoints.

## The shared core — one regimen, several diseases

**The diseases of ageing mostly do not need separate answers.** Assemble the modifiable-risk-factor
lists the wiki holds for dementia, cardiovascular disease, cancer and Parkinson's, and one small set of
exposures recurs across most of them in the *same* direction: adiposity, physical inactivity,
diabetes/glycaemia, blood pressure, LDL, smoking, excess alcohol. These are the same **big rocks** the
cardiometabolic cluster already ranks — appraised in [[Big Rocks (Median)]] and [[Big Rocks (Elderly)]]
and cross-linked here, never re-derived. No single source states the overlap — each disease body is
disease-specific by construction — so the recurrence itself is the synthesis
-> [[Shared Modifiable Levers Across Age-Related Diseases]].

**What it changes.** A person does not need a separate anti-dementia diet, an anti-cancer diet and an
anti-heart-disease diet. Because the levers are shared, pulling the big rocks *once* buys risk reduction
on several patient-important outcomes at the same time.

This **strengthens** the Layer-1 ranking rather than adding competing tasks: a lever that moves three
outcomes outranks one that moves one at equal per-outcome effect -> [[Layer 1 - Ranking Interventions for a Stratum]].

**The matched-exposure table.** The matched parameter is the exposure and the *direction* of its
association, NOT the magnitude. Each column is a relative effect for a *different* outcome (dementia vs
PD vs cancer incidence vs CV events), estimated in a different population — so the numbers are
**co-membership evidence, not commensurable magnitudes**: do not read across a row as a same-size
effect. All are observational and assumed causal (physical activity the most design-robust leg). Blank =
not a credibly evidenced lever for that disease in the sources held (a gap, not a null).

| Exposure | Dementia (Livingston) | Parkinson's (Chen) | Cancer (WCRF) | CVD (vault) | Direction shared? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physical inactivity | activity assoc. 0.80 (0.77-0.84) | activity 0.79 (0.68-0.91) | protective cause | established lever | **yes — all four** |
| Adiposity / obesity | midlife 1.31 (1.02-1.68) | — (not credible) | convincing/probable, 12 of 17 sites | established lever | yes (3 of 4) |
| Diabetes / glycaemia | HR 1.24 per 5-yr earlier onset | 1.31 (1.10-1.57) | (via adiposity) | established lever | yes |
| High blood pressure | untreated 1.42 (1.15-1.76) | — | — | established lever | yes (dementia + CVD) |
| High LDL | 1.08 per 1 mmol/L (1.03-1.14) | — | — | established lever | yes (dementia + CVD) |
| Smoking | midlife 1.30 (1.18-1.45) harm | **0.64 (0.60-0.69)** *protective* | a cause (ungraded by WCRF) | established harm | **NO — flips in PD (artifact)** |
| Excess alcohol | >21 u/wk 1.22 (1.01-1.48) | — | a cause | J-curve, arm artifact-suspect | yes (dementia + cancer) |

[@livingston2024]
[@chen2021pd]
[@wcrf2018ter]

Two rows carry a warning the rest do not. Smoking's PD column runs *opposite* to every other disease;
the source attributes it to reverse causation over PD's long prodrome, not neuroprotection — so it is
the one place the shared-lever logic would license a harmful act, and exactly where the signal is an
artifact (handled under Parkinson's). Alcohol's protective vascular arm is likewise artifact-suspect.
A protective/plateau arm contradicting an established harm is a prompt to run the artifact check, never
to act -> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]].

**Common soil — a mechanism hypothesis, not a finding.** The most parsimonious reading of the overlap is
that the shared core damages a **common cardiometabolic-vascular substrate** — hypertension, diabetes,
adiposity, LDL and smoking all injure the vasculature and drive systemic metabolic/inflammatory
dysfunction, feeding several endpoints at once. This is corroborated by the co-membership
above but not independently tested here, and it privileges no single exposure as *the* cause — an effect
arrives only when its whole condition set is present. The aging-biology (geroscience / hallmarks-of-ageing)
backbone that would ground this mechanistically is NOT held — mark it the gap it is.

**How to read the table.** The magnitudes are population-attributable, not per-person; the factors
overlap, so their attributable fractions do not sum, and removing one does not remove that share of an
individual's risk. And the loop is open: no operation here has graded the shared-core recommendation
against a realized multi-disease outcome.

## Cardiovascular disease

**Verdict: CVD is the pure common-soil case — there is no meaningful disease-specific increment beyond
the big rocks, because the big rocks *are* the cardiovascular risk factors.** That informative null is
the finding: once blood pressure, LDL/apoB, not smoking, visceral adiposity, physical activity and an
unremarkable whole-food pattern are handled, the cardiovascular tail is thin by construction.

Big rocks for CVD are blood pressure, LDL/apoB, not smoking, visceral adiposity, physical activity and
dietary pattern -> see [[Big Rocks (Median)]]. None is re-derived here.

**One route note — the weight lever splits by route, and it is a cross-link, not a re-derivation.** For
the visceral-adiposity/weight lever, the *route* to weight loss changes what is proven on hard CV events:

- **Lifestyle route — null.** Look AHEAD, the largest, longest lifestyle-weight-loss RCT, did not reduce
  hard CV events in overweight/obese type-2 diabetics (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.83 to 1.09); the 54-RCT
  meta-analysis generalises the null (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.83 to 1.04) while all-cause mortality falls
  non-CV (RR 0.82, 0.71-0.95) -> [[Does Weight Loss Reduce Cardiovascular Events]].
- **Drug route — cut events, but only in secondary prevention.** Semaglutide cut MACE by 20% (HR 0.80,
  0.72-0.90; ARR \~1.5 pp over 3.3 yr, NNT \~67) in obese adults *with established CVD*; the trialists
  state the primary-prevention gap themselves -> [[Semaglutide for Cardiovascular Risk in Obesity]].

**This is not a clean contradiction — route, delivered dose and population all differ** (lifestyle vs
drug; \~2.5 pp vs \~8.5 pp sustained loss; primary vs secondary prevention). The defensible joint reading:
a hard-CV-event benefit of weight loss is **unproven by the lifestyle route** and **proven only in
secondary-prevention obesity by the drug route** — neither shows that shedding weight *per se* prevents
events in a low-risk person. So the CVD increment stays null: the cardiovascular gains live in the big
rocks themselves, not in a weight-loss lever with an independent hard-outcome effect.

## Cancer

Where cardiovascular disease was pure common soil — its risk factors *are* the big rocks, with no
meaningful increment — cancer is the first endpoint that carries genuine disease-specific increments
beyond them. They are few, and mostly modest.

**Big rocks for cancer, in order: not smoking — tobacco dwarfs every diet-adjacent lever — then body
fatness, alcohol, and physical activity.** These are appraised in [[Big Rocks (Median)]] and not
re-derived here. WCRF ranks the leading *diet-adjacent* exposures after tobacco as body fatness,
alcohol and activity — not the *superfoods* and *carcinogens* that fill public discourse
(*attention is an anti-signal*) -> [[Diet Physical Activity and Cancer Prevention]]. Adiposity's rank
is rising: «If current trends continue, overweight and obesity are likely to overtake smoking as the
number one risk factor for cancer.» [@wcrf2018ter]
The increments below are what those big rocks leave on the table; all are causal *judgements* on
observational cohorts, and the loop stays open (coherence and fidelity, never validity).

### (a) Alcohol — no lower threshold on the cancer axis

Alcohol is a big rock, appraised for mortality and vascular disease in [[Big Rocks (Median)]] and
[[Alcohol and Mortality and Vascular Disease]]. The cancer axis carries a distinct, decision-relevant
feature: **it behaves differently from the mortality axis on threshold.** IARC classes alcoholic
drinks a Group 1 (established) human carcinogen, and WCRF grades alcohol a convincing/probable cause
of mouth/pharynx/larynx, oesophagus (SCC), breast, colorectum, liver and stomach cancers
[@wcrf2018ter].

Where the all-cause-mortality harm begins at a *dose* (Zhao's pooled \~45 g/day, \~25 g/day for women —
in [[Alcohol and Mortality and Vascular Disease]]), the cancer risk has no such floor: «there is no
level of consumption below which there is no increase in the risk of at least some cancers.»
[@wcrf2018ter] The
site-specific dose labels in the matrix (breast: no threshold identified; colorectal above \~30 g/day;
liver/stomach above \~45 g/day) mark **where the graded evidence sits, not a safe level below it.**

A single genuine protective arm exists and is instantly outweighed: alcohol «helps protect against
kidney cancer (at least up to 30 grams or two drinks per day), but this is far outweighed by the
increased risk for other cancers.» [@wcrf2018ter]
That is the *net-effect-not-the-intended-effect* rule: one favourable site does not license drinking
when the whole-body cancer ledger is negative.

### (b) Red and processed meat — colorectal, small in absolute terms, and a live cross-body clash

Two guideline families read the *same* cohort evidence and issue opposite instructions. WCRF grades
processed meat a **convincing** and red meat a **probable** cause of colorectal cancer; NutriRECS,
applying strict GRADE to the same data, rates it low-to-very-low certainty and tells adults they may
continue current intake (a weak recommendation) -> [[Should Adults Reduce Red and Processed Meat]].
The relative associations both bodies work from are modest:

| Exposure | WCRF dose-response (colorectal) | Grade | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Processed meat | RR 1.16 (95% CI 1.08-1.26) per 50 g/day | convincing | statistically significant |
| Red meat | RR 1.12 (95% CI 1.00-1.25) per 100 g/day | probable | CI touches 1.00 — dose-response not significant |

[@wcrf2018]

In **absolute** terms the lever is small. NutriRECS computes that a realistic 3-servings/week cut
yields «a very small reduction of overall lifetime cancer mortal-ity (7 fewer events per 1000 persons
with a decrease of 3 servings/wk)», at low certainty
[@nutrirecs2019]. That reduction is
framed against a *studied* typical intake of «2 to 4 servings per week in North America and Western
Europe» — the range the evidence covers, not an optimum
[@nutrirecs2019]. WCRF's own goal —
«limit consumption to no more than about three portions per week» (about 350-500 g cooked) — is a
studied-category label marking where the graded evidence sits, not a knee in a curve
[@wcrf2018ter].

**The disagreement is not about the numbers.** Both bodies see the same small, mostly-colorectal
associations; they diverge on the *grade* (precautionary "probable" vs strict-GRADE "low-certainty")
and the *standpoint* (population cancer-prevention vs individual decision-making). This deliverable
adopts neither pole — it reports the modest absolute effect and the low certainty, and leaves the
weighting to the person. Processed meat, with the convincing grade and a significant dose-response,
rests on firmer footing than red meat, so the two should not be treated as one exposure.

### (c) The beta-carotene supplement trap — a lever that runs the wrong way

Supplementation is not a cancer lever, and one agent is actively harmful. **High-dose beta-carotene
supplements *raise* lung cancer in smokers.** WCRF states it as a formal grade: «Taking high-dose
beta-carotene supplements is a cause of lung cancer in current and former smokers.»
[@wcrf2018ter] The
pooled RCT magnitude is a lung-cancer «risk ratio [RR], 1.18 [95% CI, 1.03-1.36]» in smokers/
asbestos-exposed, which earns beta carotene a USPSTF grade **D (recommend against)**
[@uspstf2022supp].

This is the *more-is-better fallacy* realised on a hard cancer outcome: a dose above repletion moved
incidence in the wrong direction -> [[Vitamin and Mineral Supplements for Disease Prevention]],
[[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]]. For general-population cancer prevention supplements are a null
lever at best; here they are a documented harm in an identifiable stratum (smokers).

### (d) Young-adult adiposity and breast cancer — a U-arm warning, not a lever

WCRF grades body fatness in *young adulthood* a **probable** protective factor against breast cancer —
direction *opposite* to adult body fatness, which convincingly *raises* postmenopausal breast-cancer
risk [@wcrf2018ter]. Read this
as a **unit-of-analysis / U-shaped-artifact caution, not an intervention.** A protective arm pointing
against the dominant adiposity signal is exactly the pattern the diagnostic flags: a protective or
plateau arm must survive a referent-correction or a genetic/objective check before it is believed, and
one equally consistent with the artifact explanation has no decision value
-> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]]. Nobody should be advised to carry more weight in early
adulthood to lower later breast-cancer risk; the finding is a warning about interpreting the curve,
not a lever to pull.

### Named gaps — the large non-diet cancer levers the corpus does not hold

The biggest modifiable cancer causes after tobacco are largely **outside** the held diet/activity
evidence base. Stated as gaps (absence asserted, no direction inferred):

- **UV / solar radiation** (skin cancers) — not held.
- **HPV** (cervical, oropharyngeal, anogenital) — not held.
- **HBV / HCV** (hepatocellular carcinoma) — not held.
- **H. pylori** (gastric cancer) — not held.

These are not judged null; they are simply not in the diet/activity corpus this deliverable is built
from, and a reader whose stratum turns on any of them will not find the lever here. Their omission is a
scope boundary, not a finding.

With cancer's genuine-but-thin increments in hand, dementia is where the disease-specific set is
richest — sensory, cognitive-engagement and vascular levers layered on the same cardiometabolic big
rocks, and the one place a bundled lifestyle *intervention* has been put to the randomized test.

## Dementia and Alzheimer's

Cancer gave a few genuine per-site increments beyond the big rocks; dementia gives the **richest** such
set — and the sharpest lesson in keeping association apart from demonstrated prevention.

**Big rocks for dementia are the midlife cardiometabolic levers** — hypertension, diabetes, midlife
obesity, high LDL-C, smoking, physical inactivity, excessive alcohol -> see [[Big Rocks (Median)]]. Seven
of the 2024 Lancet Commission's 14 modifiable risk factors *are* those big rocks, so pulling them buys
cognition as a **second** patient-important outcome on levers already ranked high, not a new regimen
[@livingston2024]. The Commission's headline — the 14 factors
carry a **population attributable fraction of 45.3%**
[@livingston2024] — is a modelled, **population, not per-person** figure: it is what theoretically would not occur
if the whole population's exposure were removed, not the share of *one* person's risk any lever removes,
and the per-factor PAFs are communality-discounted so they do **not** sum to 45.3%.

The disease-specific increments — the levers the cardiometabolic big rocks miss — are all **observational
and assumed-causal**, so read them as *associated with*, and all obey the Commission's timing rule:
act **earlier and keep it up longer** (midlife exposure carries the risk; the same condition arising in
late life often does not) [@livingston2024].

- **Hearing correction — the largest single dementia-specific lever.** Hearing loss is associated with
  higher dementia risk (new meta-analysis HR 1.37, 95% CI 1.00-1.87, lower bound touching the null), with
  a graded \~4-24% risk rise per 10 dB worse hearing across the four dose-response studies
  [@livingston2024]. Correction is available for most hearing
  loss, so uptake is the gap -> [[Hearing Loss and Dementia]]. Observationally, aid users show a 19%
  (0.76-0.87) lower risk of cognitive decline and a 17% (0.77-0.90, 4 studies) lower risk of dementia
  versus uncorrected impairment [@livingston2024]. But the one
  hearing-aid RCT (ACHIEVE) was **null overall** on 3-year cognition; its 48% protective effect was a
  pre-specified *high-risk* subgroup (route-a: absolute benefit largest where baseline dementia risk is
  highest), so the aided-benefit claim rests on observational evidence, not RCT proof.
- **Vision** — uncorrected vision impairment (new 2024 factor) is associated with dementia (RR 1.47,
  1.36-1.60), a late-life, correctable exposure like hearing
  [@livingston2024].
- **Cognitive/social engagement and depression** — less education and low cognitive/social engagement
  track higher risk (early- and late-life windows): high (vs low) cognitive stimulation at work carries a
  10-year HR 0.79 (0.66-0.95), and less frequent social contact an RR 1.57 (1.32-1.85). Depression is
  associated (RR 2.25, 1.69-2.98) but is **partly reverse-causal** over the long prodrome, so it is a weak
  lever [@livingston2024].
- **TBI protection** — traumatic brain injury is associated with dementia at any age (RR 1.66, 1.42-1.93),
  making head-injury avoidance a genuine increment [@livingston2024].
- **Air pollution** — late-life PM2.5 exposure tracks a small per-unit risk (HR 1.03, 1.02-1.05 per
  1 ug/m3), an environmental rather than personal lever
  [@livingston2024].

**Diet caveat — do not double-count.** Diet is not one of the headline 14 as a standalone; a Mediterranean
pattern is associated with lower Alzheimer's risk (dose-response RR 0.89, 0.84-0.93 per adherence point),
but the source frames the effect as running partly *indirectly* through cardiometabolic and vascular
disease — themselves already-counted risk factors
[@garciacasares2021]. So a MedDiet dementia benefit is largely a *route to pull the cardiometabolic
big rocks*, not a 15th additive lever -> [[Dementia Prevention and Modifiable Risk Factors]].

### The interventional evidence — the increments above are associations, and the trials do not close them

The whole per-factor map is observational: relative risks assumed causal. The randomised tests of acting
on those levers *as a bundle* land on **surrogates and hard endpoints that must be kept disjoint** — a
cognitive-composite surrogate is not dementia incidence, and the surrogate-to-incidence link is itself
unproven -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]. Four trials, and they do not converge on demonstrated prevention:

- **FINGER (Ngandu 2015) — surrogate-positive, small.** A 2-year double-blind multidomain RCT (n=1260,
  at-risk CAIDE>=6 elderly) **improved** a cognitive-composite Z-score (between-group 0.022/yr, 95% CI
  0.002-0.042, p=0.030; **Cohen's d 0.13**) — real, significant, but small, on a **surrogate**; dementia
  incidence was not measured [@ngandu2015].
- **MAPT (Andrieu 2017) — surrogate-null.** The closest same-endpoint replication (a lifestyle bundle on
  the same kind of composite) was **null** at the whole-population level even against a weaker comparator
  that should have made a difference easier to detect
  [@andrieu2017].
- **preDIVA (Moll van Charante 2016) — hard-incidence null.** A 6.7-year multidomain vascular-care
  cluster-RCT (n=3526, unselected elderly) measured clinical **dementia incidence** directly and **did not
  reduce it** (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.71-1.19)
  [@mollvancharante2016].
- **Coley 2025 — pooled responder NULL.** Pooling the MAPT+preDIVA individual-participant data (n≈5205,
  up to 12 yr, 486 dementia cases), the intervention **did not reduce** all-cause dementia (HR 0.98, 95% CI
  0.80-1.21), with no effect in any of 11 pre-specified subgroups and **none found by a data-driven SIDES
  search** free to combine variables
  [@coley2025]
  -> [[Multidomain Lifestyle Intervention and Cognitive Decline]].

**So the bundle is NOT demonstrated dementia prevention, and there is NO evidenced responder subgroup.**
The honest evidence state is a **small-to-absent population effect** on the surrogate and a hard endpoint
that is **not-yet-shown** rather than shown-absent — preDIVA was underpowered for the plausible 2-9%
reduction that late-life vascular care could deliver
[@mollvancharante2016]. The 45.3% PAF must never be read as validated prevention: the
observational map identifies **who is at risk** (a route-a prognostic use); the RCT bundle has not shown
that intervening converts that risk into prevented dementia.

**The responder tension — evidence runs both ways.** Coley supplies a well-powered route-(b) *null* on the
hard endpoint: targeting the high-risk did not rescue the bundle — the authors conclude «the potential
impact of multidomain interventions for dementia prevention at the individual level, even among those at
greatest risk of dementia, appears modest at best»
[@coley2025]. Elsewhere, diabetes prevention supplies a positive route-(b) interaction (metformin
by baseline risk) -> [[Lifestyle vs Metformin for Diabetes Prevention]]. Per the `[PRIOR]`, report this as
evidence-both-ways: **route-a absolute-benefit scaling by baseline risk still holds** (the safe workhorse),
but do **not** assert a route-(b) modifier for multidomain dementia prevention without positive in-stratum
interaction evidence, which Coley's search could not find.

Cutting the other way, the Commission holds that
«Multicomponent interventions addressing several risk factors potentially benefit individuals with either
high or low genetic dementia risk» [@livingston2024] — i.e. risk
is modifiable irrespective of APOE status, which argues against gating the lifestyle levers on a genetic
stratum at all.

**A distinction, not a contradiction.** FINGER moving a cognitive surrogate and preDIVA/Coley finding no
hard-incidence effect are **not** a filed tension — they are different quantities (surrogate vs dementia
incidence; selected vs unselected populations; 4-component vs vascular-only bundles), and the surrogate's
transmission to incidence is itself unproven -> [[Surrogate Outcomes]]. Neither result refutes the other.

Dementia is the richest increment set the diseases of ageing offer; Parkinson's is the honestly thinnest.

## Parkinson's disease

**The only credible Parkinson's lever is physical activity — and it is already a big rock** (ranked high
for mortality, CV disease and cognition), so pulling it buys a *possible* PD benefit as a rider, not a new
competing lever -> see [[Big Rocks (Median)]]. There is **no PD-specific big rock** comparable to the
cardiometabolic ones, and no population-attributable-fraction estimate exists for PD at all
[@chen2021pd].

**The headline is the thin field, not a lever menu — this is insufficient-evidence, not a ranked list of
things to do.** In the held 2021 umbrella review of 46 systematic reviews, under AMSTAR-2 **none rated
high or moderate; 39 of 46 (\~85%) were critically low quality**; and although significant associations
appeared for 50 factors, **only 19 were supported by prospective evidence** (the other 31 rested on
case-control or mixed designs, no reliable conclusion)
[@chen2021pd]. Low-quality reviews do not launder into confidence by their count.

The disease-specific residue, each **observational and heavily hedged**:

- **Pesticides / occupational exposure** — the most-discussed PD risk factor, yet it rests largely on
  **case-control** evidence (pesticides OR 1.76, 1.56-2.04), which is bias-prone and reported as odds
  ratios that may exaggerate effect size — real but lower-certainty than its prominence implies (attention
  is an anti-signal) [@chen2021pd].
- **Coffee and tea** — inverse associations (coffee RR 0.67, tea RR 0.64) that are **reverse-causation-
  suspect**: PD's years-to-decades prodrome lowers consumption *before* diagnosis, manufacturing apparent
  protection, so a protective arm must survive a lag/lead-time or genetic check before it is believed
  -> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]]
  [@chen2021pd].
- **Dairy** — the one dietary *risk* signal worth naming (milk/dairy RR \~1.40-1.56), a modest
  observational association of uncertain mechanism, not a demonstrated cause
  [@chen2021pd].
- **Smoking's inverse association is an artifact, never a lever.** No causal consensus exists; the leading
  reading is reverse causation (PD patients quit more easily), the nicotine-neuroprotection evidence is
  animal-only, and smoking's net harm dwarfs any PD signal — frame it as a U-shaped artifact to explain,
  not a recommendation -> [[The U-Shaped Association Artifact]]
  [@chen2021pd].

**Named gap (assert the absence).** The corpus holds the Chen umbrella review only. The Fang
physical-activity-magnitude meta-analysis and a fuller ranked modifiable-risk menu are **not held**
(acquisition-gated). State PD at the strength actually held — a thin, mostly critically-low field with
physical activity as its one credible lever — and do **not** infer a ranked menu the evidence base does
not support.

The function diseases — frailty, sarcopenia, osteoporosis — are the next stratum, where the levers again
collapse back onto the big rocks.

## Frailty, sarcopenia and osteoporosis

For frailty, sarcopenia and osteoporosis the big rocks are resistance training plus
adequate protein, balance and functional exercise, and vitamin-D/calcium *only where a
deficiency is confirmed* -> [[Big Rocks (Elderly)]]. Beyond those there is **no
disease-specific increment** — the same informative null the cardiovascular section
reports. The levers that move these conditions are the shared elderly big rocks, not a
separate anti-frailty, anti-sarcopenia or anti-osteoporosis regimen; reporting how thin
the tail is *is* the product here.

Frailty is a lever, not a fate — but the lever is the same component training. It is
both a potent prognostic marker (high baseline risk) *and* modifiable: physical-activity
programmes improved frailty status at moderate certainty (risk ratio 0.58, 95% CI 0.36
to 0.93)
[@racey2021frailtypa]
-> [[Frailty]]. What works *is* resistance and mixed exercise, dosed **down** for the
frail (general activity guidance can be too intense for this stratum). So frailty raises
the *stakes* of the big rocks (route-a, absolute benefit scales with baseline risk), it
does not add a new lever.

For sarcopenia, measure **strength, not mass**. EWGSOP2 puts strength first because «it
is recognised that strength is better than mass in predicting adverse outcomes»
[@cruzjentoft2019] —
probable sarcopenia is flagged on low grip or a slow chair-stand alone, and imaging only
confirms -> [[Sarcopenia Definition and Diagnosis]]. Mass is demoted, not dismissed: low
appendicular muscle mass still tracks all-cause mortality (pooled ASMI SMD -0.18, 95% CI
-0.23 to -0.12), and the signal is not merely a shadow of low strength
[@santana2021]
-> [[Low Muscle Mass and Mortality]]. But mass is a **predictor, not a proven target** —
no RCT shows raising it cuts mortality, so the loop stays open. **GAP:** sarcopenic
obesity (the stratum where the mass-mortality link is strongest) has no agreed
operational definition — EWGSOP2 sets no cut-off.

For osteoporosis the same null holds. Supplementing a replete, community-dwelling adult
with vitamin D (with or without calcium) does not prevent fractures
-> [[Vitamin and Mineral Supplements for Disease Prevention]]; the pill repays only in
the deficient or institutionalized high-risk stratum, and calcium is the active
co-ingredient there. The community-dwelling fracture levers are the big rocks —
strength/balance exercise and repletion where deficiency is confirmed — not a supplement
stack.

## How the hierarchy shifts by age stratum

**The big rocks are universal; their *ranking* is stratum-dependent.** The shared core does not change
with age — what changes is which levers bind hardest and how large their absolute payoff is. This is
route (a) — baseline-risk scaling of a roughly constant relative effect — not a new menu
-> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]].

- **Younger adult — the cumulative-exposure / lifetime frame.** Most big rocks act over decades (LDL/apoB
  and blood-pressure exposure accumulate; visceral adiposity and inactivity set a trajectory), so the
  young-adult payoff is a *lifetime* one even when the 10-year absolute risk — and therefore the
  near-term absolute benefit — is small. The lever is the same; the horizon is long.
- **Midlife — where the cardiometabolic levers bite hardest.** This is the window in which blood
  pressure, glycaemia, adiposity and LDL are both elevated and still modifiable *and* the dementia risk
  they feed is being set (the interventional dementia evidence suggests late-life vascular care can come
  too late — see the dementia section). Midlife is where the common-soil levers earn their top ranking.
- **Elderly — the function big rocks dominate.** Incidence prevention recedes and strength, muscle,
  balance and bone become the binding levers -> see [[Big Rocks (Elderly)]].

**Menopause — a worked mid-life stratum shift (route a, not a new effect-modifier).** Menopause changes
*which levers matter and how the risks are distributed* without adding a lever the wiki does not already
hold -> [[Menopause and the Shifting Levers]].

- **Attend to central redistribution, not the scale.** Total fat gain across midlife tracks aging and
  would happen regardless; the *plausible* menopause-specific change is **central/visceral
  redistribution** — the meta-analysis frames it as «indicative of a possible change in fat distribution
  after menopause» [@ambikairajah2019],
  not established. Central fat, not total fat, is the cardiometabolic driver (\~2% CVD-risk rise per 1 cm
  waist), and this redistribution helps explain why women's CVD advantage over same-age men erodes after
  menopause.
- **Measure the waist, not BMI.** Because the menopause signal is *distribution*, BMI and scale weight —
  confounded post-menopause by bone loss, sarcopenia and height shrinkage — are the wrong instruments;
  waist circumference, waist-to-hip ratio and trunk-fat % are more sensitive
  [@ambikairajah2019]. A normal BMI does
  not clear a menopausal woman of central-adiposity risk.
- **The lean-mass and bone levers are resistance training + protein, and weight-bearing/balance work.**
  The menopause transition raises the fat-to-lean ratio and accelerates bone loss
  [@nams2022]. HRT is the bone adjunct (it
  prevents fracture here), but is **not** the muscle lever — the retention of muscle needs interventions
  other than hormone therapy [@nams2022]
  -> [[Big Rocks (Elderly)]].

So menopause raises the central-fat and fracture baselines; the *interventions* are the ones already
ranked, applied at a shifted baseline — route (a), not personalization on an effect-modifier.

**The route-a / route-b discipline that governs every stratum shift here.** Route (a) — scaling absolute
benefit to a stratum's baseline risk while the relative effect stays fixed — is the safe workhorse and
holds everywhere; it needs prognostic data only, no subgroup claim. **Do not upgrade a stratum to a
route-(b) effect-modifier without positive in-stratum interaction evidence.** The corpus shows route (b)
runs both ways: DPP supplies a genuine positive (metformin's relative effect is *modified* across BMI and
fasting-glucose strata -> [[Lifestyle vs Metformin for Diabetes Prevention]]), while the multidomain
dementia bundle supplies a well-powered *null* — no responder subgroup even on a data-driven search (see
the dementia section). Effect modification is real *sometimes*; the discipline is that it must be shown,
not assumed, in either direction.

## Healthspan versus length — a separate axis, under-measured


Several of the levers above act on the **trajectory** of decline — falls avoided,
function and independence retained, sensory correction, strength — more than on length of
life. Keep **length** and **function** as *separate axes*: there is no scalar maximand
that collapses them, because the weighting of a good end against extra years is
irreducibly the person's (elicited at the point of use, not estimated here).

Compressed morbidity — full function then a rapid end — is, for many, strongly preferred
over a slow multi-decade loss of ability. Yet trials count *events* (death, incident
disease) and rarely the *trajectory* or the disutility of prolonged decline: the
streetlight effect on the outcome side. A clean mortality or incidence result can be
silent on the outcome a person most values.

The dementia evidence supplies a rare direct
read on this axis: the Commission reports that those with a healthy lifestyle «not only have
a lower risk of dementia, but dementia onset was also pushed back further than life
expectancy prolongation» [@livingston2024] — the
big-rock levers compress morbidity, not merely extend life, on the one endpoint where the
trajectory was actually measured. Dementia is the clearest shape case (a slow
loss of self over years), and the function diseases are the next: the frailty, sarcopenia
and strength levers are worth pulling for independence and function **in their own
right**, even though no RCT shows they extend life (predictor != target; the loop is
open) -> [[Frailty]], [[Low Muscle Mass and Mortality]].

The discipline that follows: name **which axis** a lever moves, and flag an unmeasured
trajectory as a **GAP, not a zero**. Absence of trajectory data is not evidence the
trajectory is unchanged.

## What is NOT a disease-specific lever


The second product of this appraisal is naming what does **not** earn a per-disease
answer. **Attention is an anti-signal**: the exposures with the most content, products
and debate around them are mostly the small, contested ones — the large effects are
settled and boring. Once the big rocks are handled, the following are not disease-specific
levers:

- **Supplements beyond confirmed-deficiency repletion** — for a replete adult, null across
  CVD, cancer, fracture and death, and for one named agent (beta carotene) actively
  harmful -> [[Vitamin and Mineral Supplements for Disease Prevention]].
- **Meal timing, micronutrient fine-tuning, and the superfood hunt** — heavily published,
  small-effect, and not shown to move a patient-important age-related-disease outcome
  beyond what the big rocks already reach.

**Eggs are the worked de-escalator.** For the general population egg intake is near a
non-lever: do not restrict on cardiovascular grounds and do not count as protective (every
general-population estimate sits within a few percent of the null). The one stratified
result that moves a recommendation runs the *other* way — in diabetic cohorts the
direction inverts, peaking at **\~1 egg/day, summary RR 1.22 (95% CI 1.08 to 1.39)**, a
\~22% relative CVD-risk rise
[@godos2020]
-> [[Eggs Dietary Cholesterol and Cardiovascular Risk]].

This is a genuine **route-b** (effect modification) subgroup, but observational and
GRADE-**low** — a *credible flag, not an established interaction*. It is the contrast case to
the dementia multidomain route-b null: route-b evidence here *exists* but is fragile and
observational -> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]]. Decision: a watch-item
for type-2 diabetes, a non-lever for everyone else.

## Gaps and named limits


Three named gaps are load-bearing and must not read as silent zeros:

- **Parkinson's ranked modifiable-risk menu is NOT fully held.** The Chen umbrella review
  is held -> [[Parkinsons Disease Modifiable Risk Factors]], but a Fang physical-activity
  magnitude meta-analysis and a fuller ranked-factor menu are acquisition-gated. State the
  case at the strength held (physical activity the only credible lever, itself already a
  big rock; the rest insufficient-evidence) — do NOT infer a menu.
- **The geroscience / common-soil MECHANISTIC backbone is NOT held.** The *epidemiological*
  common-soil leg IS held — the shared cardiometabolic-vascular levers moving several
  endpoints at once -> [[Shared Modifiable Levers Across Age-Related Diseases]]. What is
  absent is the aging-biology mechanism (hallmarks-of-aging / geroscience); any
  aging-biology mechanism claim must be marked as the gap it is, never asserted as an
  outcome finding.
- **The aggregate cross-disease effect is NOT poolable.** Each lever's per-disease effect
  sits on a *different outcome scale* — CV events, cancer incidence, fracture, dementia
  incidence, mortality — with no common metric. This is a **G-type structural limit**:
  name it, and do NOT fabricate a combined DALY or *total risk reduction* number the
  evidence cannot support.

Four further limits:

- **Non-diet/activity cancer levers are unheld** — UV/sun, HPV, HBV/HCV, H. pylori sit
  outside this corpus; large levers, named gaps, not appraised here.
- **Per-factor dementia PAF weights are not recoverable** — the modelled fraction is
  aggregate and communality-discounted, so the individual factor weights cannot be read off
  it.
- **Trajectory is systematically under-measured** (the healthspan-vs-length gap above) —
  an absence to name, not a zero.
- **Sarcopenic obesity has no agreed operational definition** — the strongest-modifier
  stratum is the least operationally defined.

**Scope note — autoimmune disease is out of set.** Rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory
bowel disease are NOT among this deliverable's seven diseases, and their lesson is a
*contrast*: their modifiable levers are disease-specific, opposite-direction and
distinct-mechanism (smoking and adiposity RAISE RA risk; fibre/produce LOWER IBD risk via
a different pathway) — the **opposite** of the cardiometabolic common-soil case, and not a
shared *inflammation lever* -> [[Autoimmune Disease and Modifiable Risk]].

## Mandatory caveats

- **This appraises; it does not prescribe.** It reports which exposures move which age-related
  disease, for whom, how much and how certainly -- not a per-person regimen, screening
  schedule, or drug selection. Selecting, dosing, screening for a condition and managing
  interactions are prescriber acts needing this person's history, labs and medications.
- **Applied per person.** Baseline risk, age stratum, sex/menopausal status, comorbidity and
  constraints vary; the population-level reading here is the starting point for a
  recommendation, not the recommendation itself. The absolute benefit of a fixed relative
  effect scales with baseline risk -> [[Baseline Risk and the Relative-Absolute Split]].
- **Association is not demonstrated prevention.** Most per-factor magnitudes here are
  observational relative risks assumed causal; where a bundled intervention was randomized
  (dementia), it did not convert the risk map into prevented disease. Keep the two apart.
- **The loop is open.** These are appraisals of coherence and source-fidelity, not outcomes
  measured against a realized result -- nothing here has been graded against what actually
  happened to anyone.
